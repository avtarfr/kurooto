Skills and routines

A skill is a ready-made set of instructions for your AI, run with one command: /journal my day saves your day into today's note, /pack linear algebra midterm builds a revision PDF. A routine runs a skill, a command or a request on a schedule (/every monday 9:00 /journal plan my week). You can write your own skills, copy and change the ones Kurooto ships, and use them in other AI programs too.

Turn it on

Dashboard → Features → Skills. It needs Chat (skills are requests to the AI you picked). With Skills off, typing /journal says "Skills is off: Features → Skills → Turn on" instead of sending it to the AI.

Running a skill

From Telegram. Type /<skill> and what it's about, in one message:

/journal finished the problem set, stuck on question 4, read two papers for the reading group
  • Files go along: send photos, PDFs or slides with the command as the caption (/study-log with your slides).
  • The answer comes back as a reply. Answers longer than 7,000 characters come as the first part plus the full answer as a file.
  • Reply to the answer to carry on: answer the questions it asked, or ask for a change ("make the plan lighter after 4pm"). Your reply runs the same skill again, with its previous answer attached.
  • Line breaks are fine: /journal on the first line and your text below it works.
  • The "/" menu lists your skills. Telegram's menu can't show a dash, so a skill with one shows with _: /term_setup runs term-setup, /study_log runs study-log. Both spellings work when typed.
  • A command of the same name wins: a skill can't take a name a feature's command already uses (/pack, /note, /quiz...). /skills <name> says when that's the case.

Effort. Each skill says how hard the AI should think (low, medium or high), so a quick /goodnight stays quick and /assignment gets the strongest model.

What you get back. "On it." at once, then the answer as a reply, labelled with the AI and effort that answered, e.g. [Gemini key · medium] /journal finished the problem set.

Related commands that aren't skills: /studypack <subject> [what it's for] sends a zip of a subject's notes and course files with a prompt for another AI (Books and study); /note saves a quick note (Notes).

Revision packs (/pack)

What it does. /pack linear algebra midterm friday reads that subject's notes (course page, exam prep, lecture notes, solutions) and builds a 2 to 6 page pack: what's on it, definitions, theorems with their hypotheses, formulas, worked problems, pitfalls, and a self-test with answers. It's sent to you on Telegram and saved:

  • <subject>/revision-pack-<date>.tex and .pdf in your notes (in your files folder when files.folder is set);
  • revision-packs/ when the subject has no folder;
  • linked from today's daily note (under ## Added) and from the course page (under ## Revision packs).

The subject is found from the first words: a subject's name or short name from Subjects, or a folder in your notes. The rest says what it's for; without it, the pack is for the next quiz or exam.

Making the PDF. Kurooto uses Tectonic (a small LaTeX program) if it's installed, else LaTeX already on your computer (latexmk or pdflatex, e.g. TeX Live, MiKTeX or MacTeX). Either way the LaTeX can't run commands on your computer (shell escape is off), since an AI wrote it. With neither:

  • the pack comes as a .tex file, and a message asks Install Tectonic / Not now;
  • Install Tectonic downloads it (about 25 MB, from Tectonic's official releases) and then makes the PDF of the pack that was waiting. The first PDF takes a few minutes while it fetches about 50 MB of LaTeX packages into Kurooto's cache; later ones take seconds;
  • Not now: packs come as LaTeX, and it asks again after 30 days at the earliest.

If the LaTeX doesn't compile, the AI gets one try to fix it. If that fails too, you get the .tex with the first error line, and it stays in your notes.

Packs before exams. With Deadlines on, the evening before a quiz or exam that has a subject (12 to 36 hours before it, not before 16:00), Kurooto asks once per quiz or exam: "Linear Algebra midterm is tomorrow 10:00. A revision pack for it tonight?" with Build it / No thanks. Build it makes the pack (it comes in a few minutes); No thanks drops it. skills.auto_pack:

  • ask (default): the question above;
  • auto: builds it without asking (it comes like a /pack);
  • off: never offers.

This offer counts as a message that came by itself (Messages it sends): its Less of this → Stop sets skills.auto_pack to off.

Listing and changing skills

CommandWhat it does
/skillsEvery skill you can run, Kurooto's first, then yours (marked "(yours)") and your add-ons' ("(add-on)"), each with what to type after it
/skills journalOne skill: what it does, its effort, and where it comes from
/skills copy journalMakes your own copy in kurooto/skills/journal/SKILL.md in your notes, to edit there

Your copy wins. A skill in your skills folder with the same name as Kurooto's replaces Kurooto's for you; delete your copy to go back. Updates replace Kurooto's own skills, never your copies.

Where skills come from, later ones replacing earlier ones with the same name:

  1. the skills that come with Kurooto;
  2. your add-ons' skills and prompts (Add-ons);
  3. prompts in your prompt library marked as skills (below);
  4. your own skills folder (kurooto/skills/<name>/SKILL.md, or master/skills/ in the zettelkasten layout).

What a skill file looks like. Markdown with a short header, then the instructions to the AI. $ARGUMENTS is replaced by what you typed after the command (if it's missing, what you typed is added at the end):

---
name: weekly-review
description: Turn this week's daily notes into a short review.
effort: medium
args: anything to focus on
---
1. Read this week's daily notes (journal/<YYYY-MM-DD>).
2. Write a note journal/<date>-week-review with what went well, what didn't, and next week's top 3.
Focus: $ARGUMENTS

A name is lowercase letters, digits and dashes, 2 to 40 characters.

Your own skills (/newskill)

/newskill <what it should do>, for example /newskill every Sunday, turn my week's daily notes into a short review.

  1. Kurooto says "Writing it; you'll see the whole skill before anything is saved."
  2. The AI writes the skill file, and Kurooto sends it to you in full with Save and Discard.
  3. Save puts it in kurooto/skills/<name>/SKILL.md and tells you how to try it ("Saved. Try it: /weekly-review ..."). Discard throws it away. Nothing is saved without your tap.

If the name is taken (by a command or another skill), it gets a free one (weekly-review-2). If the AI's answer isn't a usable skill, you're told to try again with more detail. New skills may use only the AI's tools; they're told never to run shell commands, delete or reword your text, or send anything to anyone else. Edit the file in your notes any time.

Skills Kurooto learns by itself. With the optional Learns from its own work on, a request of yours that took many steps can become a skill without a Save tap ("Learned a skill: /..., from today's request", with Undo), a skill can gain tips ("Learned from experience") or lessons ("Lessons") from your corrections. Their header says learned: true; they're your skills like any other.

Prompts that are skills

A prompt in your prompt library (prompts/*.md, see Quick notes and prompts) becomes a skill when its header says so:

---
title: Summarise a paper
skill: true
---
Summarise this paper in 5 bullets for a first-year student: $ARGUMENTS

skill: true names it after name: or the file; skill: paper-summary names it outright. /prompts marks these "(also /name)".

Routines (/every)

What it does. Runs a skill, a command, or a request for the AI on a schedule.

From Telegram.

ExampleWhat runs
/every monday 9:00 /journal plan my weeka skill
/every weekdays 7:30 /morning-prepa skill on Monday to Friday
/every friday 18:00 /weekany command
/every daily 21:00 how did today go?a request for the AI
/every mon,thu 8pm /quizstatuson several days

The day part: daily (or day), weekdays, weekends, a day (monday, mon), or several (mon,thu, mon and thu). The time: 9:00, 21:30, 9, 8pm, 7.30am. "at" between them is fine.

In plain English: "every friday at 6pm send me a summary of my week", "every weekday at 7 plan my day". It has to be a request (send, remind, tell, give, make, run, plan, summarise, check, quiz, review, prepare, draft...) or a /command; "every monday at 9 I have a lab" is a fact, not a routine, so it isn't taken as one.

/routines lists them, each as its own message with a Cancel button ("Cancelled." or "Already gone.").

When it runs.

  • The result comes as a message starting with the routine's label, e.g. "Mon at 09:00: /journal plan my week".
  • Routines run in the background lane, so they never hold up your own requests.
  • One that came due while the computer was off or asleep runs once when it's back, if that's still one of its days.

Limits. At most 30 routines. A routine can't set up routines (/every ... /every is refused). A /command that doesn't exist is refused when you set it up ("There's no /x command or skill").

Settings and storage. Routines live in Kurooto's data folder (skills/routines.json), are kept in Profile sync, and start again when you turn Skills back on.

The rules every skill follows

Every skill request carries these rules (yours and add-ons' too):

  • It only creates notes or adds to them: never deletes, reorders or rewords what you wrote. Your own words go in verbatim.
  • Every link it adds carries a short reason, and never points at a note that doesn't exist.
  • It keeps health and medical details, money, passwords and other people's private details out of your notes, and says what it left out.
  • It sends nothing to anyone but you: no messages, mail or sharing, no spending, no deleting.
  • It doesn't run shell commands or write programs. If a step needs a tool the AI doesn't have (a feature that's off), it skips that step and says so in one line.
  • Its answer is short and says what it did and where.

Sending you files

With Skills on, the AI can send you files, on Telegram, in Chat and on calls ("send me my linear algebra pack", "send me everything about the statistics quiz"):

  • one or two files come as they are; more come as one zip;
  • only files from your notes folder, your files folder, the course files folder and Kurooto's Classroom copies; nothing from anywhere else;
  • up to Telegram's 50 MB. With Drive upload on, anything bigger goes to your Google Drive and you get a link instead (a temporary upload, deleted after drive_upload.temp_days, 7, unless you move it out of the Kurooto folder). Without it, you're told the file is too big;
  • during focus time, a file waits until focus ends.

The AI is told always to send files this way and never to answer with file paths.

Skills in agy, Claude Code and other agent programs

What it does. Copies every skill you can run here (Kurooto's, your add-ons', your own, and prompts marked as skills) into the skills folder of the agent programs you use, so /journal my day typed in agy runs the same instructions as on Telegram. Each copy says where your notes folder is and carries the same rules.

From a terminal.

CommandWhat it does
kurooto export-skills onInto agy (~/.gemini/antigravity-cli/skills)
kurooto export-skills on --to agy,agentsAlso ~/.agents/skills (GitHub Copilot CLI and other programs that read it)
kurooto export-skills on --to claudeInto Claude Code (~/.claude/skills), only when you ask for it
kurooto export-skills statusLists the copies
kurooto export-skills offRemoves every copy Kurooto made

How it stays tidy.

  • Kurooto only changes or removes the copies it made (each carries a .kurooto-skill marker).
  • If you have a skill of your own with the same name in that program, yours stays and Kurooto's goes in as kurooto-<name>.
  • A copy you edit by hand is yours from then on.
  • They're brought up to date when Kurooto starts (so after every update) and every hour; copies of skills that are gone are removed.
  • agents.prefix (e.g. k-) puts a prefix on all of them: /k-journal.

Settings. agents.export (where: agy, agents, claude; empty = off) and agents.prefix.

Turn it off. kurooto export-skills off (or empty agents.export). kurooto uninstall also removes the copies. Your own skills in those programs stay.

Settings, privacy, turning it off

Settings that matter (Dashboard → Settings → skills):

SettingDefaultEffect
skills.auto_packaskRevision packs before exams: ask, auto or off
skills.auto_pack_after16:00Not offered before this time of day
agents.export[]Agent programs your skills are copied into (set by kurooto export-skills, or Settings → Skills in other AI programs)
agents.prefixemptyPrefix for the copied skills' names

What leaves your computer. What you send with a skill (text and files) and the notes it reads go to the AI you picked. Your own skills, routines and packs are files on your computer.

Turn it off. Features → Skills → Turn off. Your own skills, routines and packs stay; routines start again when you turn it back on. Packs already made stay where they are; skills.auto_pack = off stops the evening offers.
