Face presence: greet you at your desk

Kurooto can learn your face, on your computer only, and greet you by voice when you sit down at your desk after a while: what's next today and what's waiting. Then it listens for your answer, so you can say "what's on today?" without its name. Someone else's face never triggers it and is never kept. Off until you turn it on, and nothing watches until you've enrolled your face yourself (0.8.10).

It's a convenience, never a lock: a face match never approves, unlocks or allows anything.

Setting it up

Features → Face presence → Turn on, then Set up (on the card or the Face page). It downloads once, and says how much first:

PartWhat it's forAbout
opencv-python-headless and numpythe camera, finding faces, recognising yours0.14 GB installed
YuNet (face_detection_yunet_2023mar.onnx)finding faces in a picture0.23 MB
SFace (face_recognition_sface_2021dec.onnx)turning a face into 128 numbers to compare38.7 MB

The two model files come from OpenCV's model zoo (pinned to one version and checked against their fingerprints), the package from PyPI. After that it works without the internet, on your computer's processor (no graphics card needed, so it never competes with a local AI model for it).

No internet on this computer? Put the two .onnx files and the package's wheels in a folder, then kurooto face install-from FOLDER (or Install from a folder on the Face page). On another computer:

  • the models: YuNet and SFace;
  • the wheels: pip download opencv-python-headless numpy --only-binary :all: -d FOLDER (for the same system and Python version as this computer).

Files whose fingerprint doesn't match are skipped and named; it then says what's still missing.

The camera: the first ordinary webcam is used (infrared ones are skipped). face.camera.device picks another, by number or part of its name. On macOS and Windows, the program that runs Kurooto needs the system's camera permission (see Troubleshooting); kurooto face test-camera checks the camera for 3 seconds and prints its size, speed and light, never a picture.

Enrolling your face

Open the Face page (the dashboard's face tab, Alt+E, or "Open the Face page" on the card) and press Start. The camera's light comes on; the preview is drawn on the page only, never saved. Nine short steps:

#StepWhat to doSamplesCan skip

4uptilt your chin up a little4no

6nearlean in closer5yes
7farsit back5yes
8glassesput your glasses on (or take them off); "I don't wear glasses" skips it8yes
9lightchange the light: the room light off, a desk lamp, the window behind you8yes

That's 52 samples. Finish and train works once the first five steps are done (26 samples); the last four raise the grade, and Add samples later picks up the steps that are short. Each step has its own meter, and the top one says "34 of 52 samples · step 5 of 9". Tick Say the steps aloud (with Voice on) and Kurooto reads each step and the odd nudge. Pause and Discard do what they say; Discard drops every sample at once.

Samples are kept in memory only while you enrol; if you don't train within 30 minutes, they're dropped. The camera closes when you leave the page (after 15 seconds) and after 10 minutes of capturing in all.

Start over (on the Face page once you're enrolled) runs all nine steps again from nothing. Your current face model keeps working until you press Finish and train; Discard leaves it as it was. After a training (Start over or Add samples), Undo the last training puts the previous model back, until Kurooto restarts (the old one is held in memory only, never written down).

What "training" means here

Honestly: Kurooto doesn't train a neural network. Face detection (YuNet) and recognition (SFace) are pretrained models, used as they are. "Training" is three small things done on your computer in under a second:

  1. Your template. Each sample becomes SFace's 128 numbers. Your template is their average (the centroid), one for each condition: your usual look, with glasses, and in other light, so glasses on and off both work.
  2. Your threshold. Kurooto scores each of your samples against a template built without it (held out, five rounds), and scores a bundled set of made-up faces against your template. It then picks the score above which a face counts as you, so that about 1 in 1,000 of those made-up faces would pass (face.recognise.strictness). It never goes below 0.363, the threshold OpenCV publishes for SFace, chosen on real people.
  3. A grade and advice: how often you'd be recognised at that threshold, and what would help.

Why not a classifier or a fine-tune on top? There's one person (you) and about 50 samples. A classifier would mostly learn how the made-up impostor faces differ from real ones, not how you differ from other people, and fine-tuning SFace would need a much bigger toolkit and real face images we can't licence. If a test on real data ever shows a classifier doing better at the same strictness, it can come later behind its own switch.

About the made-up faces: the impostor set isn't in this release yet. Until it is, the threshold is OpenCV's 0.363 plus 0.05, and the report says "calibrated without the impostor set: run the 'someone else' test".

The report and the grade

After training, and on the Face page later:

  • Recognised: the share of your held-out samples that pass, overall and per step.
  • The scores: your median, the threshold ("needs 0.43") and the "unsure" band under it (0.10 lower): a face in that band counts for nothing either way.
  • Grade: good (95 % or more recognised, a comfortable margin, and all nine steps done), fair (80 % or more), or weak. A weak model is still used, and the card says "weak model: greetings may miss you; add samples".
  • Advice, e.g. "Other light: recognised in 4 of 10 held-out samples. Add more samples other light."

Testing it

On the Face page, Test opens the camera for up to a minute and shows each face's score as a number and a bar, with the two marks (unsure, you), and the decision in words: "You (0.71; needs 0.43)".

  • I'm testing as me: ten seconds; how many frames recognised you, and whether an arrival would have greeted you.
  • Someone else is in front of the camera: ten seconds (ask them first); the best score seen and whether any frame would have counted as you. A stranger's photo on a phone screen works too.
  • The honest one: try a photo of *yourself* on your phone. It will probably say "you". That's why face presence never unlocks or approves anything.

Only the results are kept (when, how many frames, the best score); never anything of the other person. If it took someone else for you, it says: "Raise strictness to strict, or retrain with more samples."

Watching for you

Once you've enrolled, Kurooto looks for you only while you're not using the computer:

  • While you type or use the mouse, the camera stays closed. Your input says you're here. That also leaves the camera free for a video call you start (on Linux only one program can use a camera at a time).
  • After a minute with no input (face.watch.idle_s), right after you unlock the screen or the computer wakes, it opens the camera and looks about once a second (face.watch.fps; 0.33 on battery). When you start typing again, it gets up to 6 seconds to recognise you, then closes. On a computer that can't say how long you've been idle, it looks only after an unlock or a wake, for a few seconds.
  • A dashboard badge "● camera" shows while Kurooto holds the camera, and the card says "Camera: on (looking for you)". The camera's own light is the honest signal: look for it.

Add-ons may ask Face presence to keep looking while you type, or to tell them who's in view (where each face is and whether it's you; never a picture). Then the card and /face say "camera on: an add-on is looking" and why. A hold lasts at most 10 minutes unless the add-on asks again, and everything in the pause list below still stops it. face.addons.hold and face.addons.watch turn those off.

Following faces (face.track.on, on): while the camera is open and a dashboard is in sight, the being's eye follows whoever is in view, not just you: it looks at one person and moves on to another every few seconds, sooner when someone new appears or someone moves more; with one person it follows them closely, small nods and turns included. It only knows where each face is and how much it moves (and which one is you); it never works out who anyone else is. It looks up to 10 times a second then (face.track.fps), which costs roughly a tenth of one processor core while it lasts; it never opens the camera by itself. An add-on can follow faces the same way.

You've arrived when it recognises you in 3 of 4 frames within 6 seconds, after at least 20 minutes with no sign of you (face.greet.after_absent_min; a locked screen isn't a sign of you). Unsure frames and other people count for nothing. A photo propped up in front of the camera doesn't move, so it waits for movement.

It pauses (and says why on the card and in /face) when: Face presence is off; you haven't enrolled; it isn't set up; you paused it (/face pause 1h); Kurooto is paused, or on battery in pause mode; the screen is locked (face.watch.when_locked to look anyway); you're on a call; focus; the night hours (face.watch.night to look anyway); you're enrolling; another program uses the camera (checked again every minute); there's no camera; the system blocks it; it's covered (10 dark frames; checked again every 5 minutes); or its key can't be read.

The greeting and the conversation

When you arrive, Kurooto says, in about three sentences, through your speakers:

Welcome back. Next: Linear Algebra at 14:00, in 40 minutes. Two questions and one approval wait for you. Want to go through them?

("Morning." the first time a day before noon.) What it mentions is face.greet.say: what's next today, how many questions and approvals wait (never what they say), one deadline due within 24 hours, and how many messages wait for the evening (held, off by default). It's made by rules on your computer: no AI request, and it's never sent to Telegram, your phone or your AI.

Then the conversation opens for 12 seconds (face.greet.listen_s), like after you'd said "Kurooto": "what's next?" or "yes" goes to your AI without saying its name. Chatter nearby closes it at once and isn't sent. That needs Voice listening on; without it you hear the greeting only.

It doesn't greet when Voice is off, muted or can't speak; during a call, "no calls till", focus, the night, a class you attend or a calendar event; while Kurooto is paused; within an hour of the last greeting (face.greet.gap_min); after three a day (face.greet.per_day); during exam season's quiet days or a quiet week; or when you turned them off (face.greet.on, "Greet me when I sit down" on the card). Greetings aren't counted against the six messages a day, and each one, said or skipped, is recorded with its reason (never its words).

Someone else at the desk: no greeting at all by default (face.greet.with_others = skip). short says only "Welcome back." with no details and no conversation; full greets as usual. Class and deadline names are never spoken in front of someone else unless you chose full.

When you step away

Off by default: turn on face.away.on (or "Notice when I leave" on the card). With no sign of you for 5 minutes (face.away.after_min) while the camera looks, or with the screen locked that long, Kurooto notes that you've left; you're back when it recognises you or someone types. Two more switches use it:

  • face.away.hold_desktop: desktop pop-ups wait until you're back (at most 20), and answers aren't read aloud to an empty room (they're on the dashboard).
  • face.away.hold_calls: while you're away, calls wait as they do during a class (a no-call time), until you're back or at most an hour (face.away.hold_max_min).

Learning over time

Off by default (face.learn.on, "Learn slowly from confident sightings" on the card). When on, a sighting can refine your template only when it's very sure (well above the threshold), you're alone in view, the picture is good, it was part of an arrival and you typed within a minute after, it's been 6 hours since the last one, and it adds something new. Never from an unsure frame, someone else in view, or a test. At most 30 learned samples (the oldest goes first); your enrolled samples are never replaced, and the threshold is still measured only on your enrolled samples.

The Face page lists what it learned ("Learned 1 sample on 3 Nov"), with Undo the last and Forget what it learned.

Forgetting your face

Any of these deletes your face model:

  • /face forget (Forget / Keep it), "forget my face", or kurooto face forget (it asks; --yes doesn't);
  • Forget everything on the Face page;
  • Delete my face data, offered when you turn Face presence off (until then the model stays, and turning it on again uses it);
  • /forget all (it says so in its question).

It deletes the key first, then the template and its status. With the key gone, any old copy of the template (a backup of the state folder, a synced folder) can't be read by anyone. Then: "Forgot your face model: the key, the template and its status. Nothing of it remains on this computer." Nothing went anywhere else, so there's nothing to forget elsewhere.

Privacy

  • What it sees: the camera's picture, only while it looks (above), at about one frame a second.
  • What it keeps: your template only: numbers that describe your face, never a picture, encrypted (AES-GCM) under a key in your system's keychain, in state/face/template.bin, with a status file of counts and dates. Never in a backup, Profile sync or a diagnostics bundle.
  • What it never keeps: the camera's frames (looked at in memory and dropped), the preview (made in memory for the Face page), and anything of other people: a stranger's face is dropped with the frame, and only a count since start is kept ("faces: you 120, unsure 4, not you 9").
  • What leaves your computer: nothing. The one-time downloads fetch the models from media.githubusercontent.com (OpenCV's model zoo) and the package from PyPI; nothing of yours is sent.
  • Honest limits: without a keychain (no keychain service, starting at boot on Linux, or KUROOTO_HOME set) the key sits in Kurooto's owner-only settings file on the same disk, so the encryption then only protects copies of the state folder made without the settings folder. Deleting a file on an SSD doesn't erase it, which is why forgetting deletes the key first. Python can't promise a frame is wiped from memory or never swapped to disk.

It's not a lock

A face is easy to copy: a photo or video of you on a phone will usually pass, and a sibling or lookalike can be taken for you (strict strictness helps; the "someone else" test shows it). Real liveness checks need a depth or infrared camera, or asking you to blink or turn, which would make a greeting a chore. So the rule is: a face match never authorises anything. It never answers an approval, unlocks your screen or Kurooto, or lets a spoken request do more than it could anyway. The worst a photo of you can do is make Kurooto say good morning and open a conversation anyone in the room could already open by saying "Kurooto".

Licences

  • YuNet: MIT (Shiqi Yu). SFace: Apache-2.0. Both from OpenCV's model zoo, downloaded at setup, not part of Kurooto.
  • opencv-python-headless: Apache-2.0. Its wheel bundles FFmpeg's libraries (LGPL-2.1+), OpenBLAS (BSD-3), libvpx (BSD), libpng, OpenSSL and the GCC runtime (GPL-3.0 with the runtime library exception). numpy: BSD-3.
  • SFace's training data: OpenCV's page doesn't name it; the SFace paper reports training on public celebrity-face datasets (CASIA-WebFace, VGGFace2, MS-Celeb-1M variants), whose own terms are research-oriented. The weights are published under Apache-2.0; whether a dataset's terms reach a model trained on it is unsettled.
