AI brains

The "brain" is the AI that reads your messages and does tasks: answering questions, writing notes, filing files, running skills. Kurooto doesn't come with one; you pick one in the setup page's AI step, and you can change it any time (Dashboard → Settings → brain, or the setup page). Instant requests ("remind me at 9", "what's due?") never need the brain: they run on your computer without any AI.

About agy. Kurooto runs Google's official agy program on this computer, signed in as you, and never touches its sign-in token. Google's answers differ on whether other tools may start agy unattended, so there is some risk to your Google account. For tools like Kurooto, Google recommends a Gemini API key.

About a free Gemini key. On the free tier, Google may use what you send and what it answers to improve its products, and human reviewers may read it. You must be 18 or older. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland you need a paid key (billing on). Free keys have small daily limits; the setup page warns that they run out quickly, so they're best kept for calls and voice notes, with agy or another brain for everything else. Phone calls and voice notes always go through Gemini, whichever AI answers your messages; if you don't want Google's free-tier terms there, use a paid key.

About Claude Code. Each request run by Claude Code gets only what Kurooto gives it, for that request: Kurooto's tools (and your own GBrain's, if you use one), reading your notes, course files, Documents, Downloads and Screenshots, writing in your notes, course-files and add-on skills folders, reading web pages, and harmless commands (ls, cat, grep, ls, pdftotext...; none that can start another program through its options, so not find, git, latexmk, rg or sort). Anything else is refused, and the answer says what was refused.

Your own scripts. To let your AI programs run a script of yours unattended (one that does one thing, such as make-figures), put its full path in agents.scripts (Settings → Skills in other AI programs, or settings.json). It's allowed by its path, and by its name when it's on your PATH. Shells and interpreters (bash, python, node...) are never accepted there: they would run anything. It must be a program: on Windows one ending in .exe, .bat or .cmd (your PATHEXT), elsewhere a file with its execute bit set. Nothing is saved in Claude Code's own settings, and your own Claude Code hooks, projects and history aren't used. The kurooto command itself never runs from a request. Models: brain.models.claude (default haiku for quick requests, sonnet for the rest).

Ollama runs models on your computer. Without a graphics card (or Apple Silicon) it's too slow to be your main brain. With Local AI models on (see your-computer.md), a small model on your computer answers the quick "how hard is this?" question and makes the notes index; your main brain still does the real work. A small model that can't use tools just answers without them.

Retired: Codex is no longer offered. If a setting named it, it's cleared, and the next request says to choose another AI in Settings → AI. An AI set up with an earlier version as "FreeLLMAPI" keeps working and shows as "Another OpenAI-compatible service".

Checking it works: /brain on Telegram lists the main and fallback brain and whether each is ready. The setup page's Test it asks the AI for a reply and says what to do if it fails:

Main and fallback

  • brain.main: the AI that handles requests.
  • brain.fallback: another one that takes over when the main one is at its limit, signed out, refused the request, failed or gave no answer. Empty means none. Set it in Dashboard → Settings → brain.

When the fallback answers, the answer says so in one line. Two more rules:

  • A free Gemini key stretches further: when one Gemini model's free daily limit is used up, the next model of the same family answers (gemini-flash-latest, gemini-flash-lite-latest, gemini-2.5-flash, gemini-2.5-flash-lite). Smaller or older models answer less well, so the answer says which one it was: "(Answered with ...: ...'s free limit for today was used up.)"
  • Last resort: if your main brain is a Gemini key and nothing else can answer, the request goes to an agent program (agy or Claude Code) that is installed and has worked on this computer before. The other way round never happens: an agent program doesn't fall back to your free Gemini key, because its limit is small and kept for calls.

How hard it thinks (effort)

Each request runs at low, medium or high effort. Higher effort uses a stronger or slower model and is allowed more time.

  • Choose it yourself: start a message with low:, medium: (or med:) or high:. Example: high: derive the normal equations for least squares.
  • Otherwise Kurooto picks. Requests about notes, revision, summaries, explanations, derivations, proofs or exam prep always get high. Others are judged by the rules, your AI or a local model (brain.classify: rules, main or ollama; default rules), and medium is the default. Anything that changes, schedules or remembers something is never treated as a quick lookup.
  • Time limits: a request is stopped after brain.timeout_min minutes: 10 for low, 15 for medium, 30 for high.

Free limits and the reserve

Free AI has a daily limit, and Google doesn't say how much is left. Kurooto counts requests itself and keeps the end of the day's limit for what you're waiting on: calls, voice notes, the morning plan and your own requests.

  • It learns the limits. The first time a service answers "limit reached", Kurooto keeps the number it names or, when it names none, how many requests the day had. It learns when the limit comes back the same way; otherwise it assumes Google's day, which ends at midnight Pacific time (/usage shows that in your time). Each model of your Gemini key has its own limit, and agy has one (counted from agy's own logs, so runs you type yourself count too). Until a limit has been hit once, it isn't known and nothing waits.
  • Background work waits past 70%. Once the day's count passes 70% of a known limit, work nobody is waiting for waits: the Classroom digest, the nightly look back, skill changes it suggests, research digests, and book and handwritten pages read by Gemini. The quick "how hard is this" question moves to the model on your computer or to the rules. Nothing is dropped: waiting work runs after the reset. Your own requests never wait; a used-up limit is tried last, after your fallback.
  • One plain line instead of an error: "Google's free limit for today is used up, resets at 12:30; your calls and alarm still work." Calls use a different Gemini model with its own limit.
  • /usage lists AI requests per day over the last 7 days (/usage 14 for two weeks, up to 60), per brain, how often a limit was hit, today's limits ("143 of 250 requests (57%); background work waits from 175. Resets at 12:30.") and how many background requests wait for a reset. If agy is on this computer, it also counts agy's own runs from agy's logs (only counted; nothing in the logs is read out). Quick checks (how hard a request is, grading a quiz answer) and phone calls aren't counted. In a terminal: kurooto usage [days]. Needs Chat on.
Setting (Settings → AI limits)DefaultEffect
quota.ontruefalse: background work never waits (counting and the plain line stay)

Only counts and times are kept (quota.json in Kurooto's state folder). Delete that file to forget what was learned.

Subscriptions (Claude Code, agy) and the concept graph. A Claude plan has a rolling 5-hour window and a weekly cap, shared between Kurooto and your own Claude chats and Claude Code, and Anthropic doesn't publish the numbers. The one big background job, the concept graph reading your books at night, therefore has a budget of its own that learns your plan: it starts small, grows after nights with no limit hit, halves at a limit, waits for its reset, and leaves 40% of a window for you. It starts reading 5 hours before you usually wake, so a window it opens has reset when you get up. With a free Gemini key it takes 60% of the day's limit; with a paid API key, at most $0.50 a night. Details: Notes and the concept graph. /usage shows tonight's budget and the last limit hit.

What the AI can do (its tools)

Your AI doesn't only answer: it can act, the same way in Chat, on a phone call and in background requests. Each feature you turn on adds its own tools; a feature that's off has none. Examples: add a reminder, call you back later, hold calls, add a deadline or mark one done, log study time, create a note or add to one, tick a - [ ] task, edit a note when you ask, read your files and web pages, send you a file from your notes (over 50 MB as a Drive link, with Drive upload on), say how your syncs are doing ("what's the sync status?"), and ask you a question when it needs your answer.

What it may read and write (the same for Kurooto's own tools, agy and Claude Code):

  • Read: your notes folder; your Documents, Downloads and Screenshots folders; Kurooto's copies of Classroom (course files) and mail (the digest's batches). Hidden files and folders (like .git) are left out, and a link leading anywhere else is refused.
  • Write: your notes folder, the folder Classroom's course files go to, and (with Your add-ons on) your add-ons' skills/. Text files only (.md, .txt, .tex, .bib, .csv, .json, .yaml...). Documents and Downloads stay read-only.
  • Your own text: it creates notes and adds to them. It changes existing text only with edit_note, when you asked, one exact passage at a time, and keeps the previous version first (Kurooto's state folder → note-history/, the last 20 per file).
  • The web: a public page as plain text (at most a few MB); never this computer or your local network.
  • Never: send anything to anyone but you, or run the kurooto command.

The AI is told to say plainly when it couldn't do something, never to claim it did. If it says it did something and nothing happened, tell it so and check /jobs.

agy's permission question. agy gets Kurooto's tools once: when you test agy on the setup page (the page lists what it allows), or, if you set agy up before, when you tap Approve on the question Kurooto asks. Deny means agy works without Kurooto's tools, and Kurooto asks again after 30 days at the earliest; it also asks before adding more later (for example when you turn on add-ons). Approving adds Kurooto's rules to agy's own settings file (a backup is kept in Kurooto's state folder → backups/; your own rules stay). To take them away: agy mcp remove kurooto, then delete Kurooto's lines from agy's settings (or put back the backup).

Your own agent programs can use the same tools: add kurooto mcp as an MCP server in any MCP client (it works while Kurooto is running). See add-ons.md.

AI requests and the queue (/jobs)

Requests that may take minutes run in the background; Kurooto replies "On it." and the answer comes back as a reply to your message. Answers longer than about 7,000 characters also come as a file.

  • Two lanes. Your own requests (Telegram, calls, the terminal, the dashboard) have their own lane and never wait behind background work (the morning plan, routines, automatic revision packs, digests).
  • /jobs lists what's running and waiting, each with a Cancel button (and Cancel all when there are several), then the last few that finished with their effort, time and result. /jobs cancel <id> or /jobs cancel all from the keyboard; kurooto jobs --cancel ID in a terminal. A running agent program is stopped with everything it started.
  • Stuck requests. A watchdog stops a request with no AI at work and no program running for jobs.stuck_min minutes (10), or one past its time limit: "It got stuck (nothing was happening), so I stopped it. Send it again to try once more."
  • What failures say:
ReplyMeaning
"... isn't signed in any more (or its key stopped working)"Open the setup page (kurooto open) → AI → sign in again.
"... has hit its usage limit for now"Wait, or set a fallback (brain.fallback).
"The AI stopped without an answer: it tried something unattended runs aren't allowed to do"Rephrase it, or do that part yourself.
"The AI gave no answer this time"Try rephrasing.
"No answer in time; the request was stopped"It ran past brain.timeout_min. Ask with low: or split it up.

When Kurooto stops (shutdown, update), running requests stop where they are and run again at the next start.

What goes along with every request

Every request carries a short context: who you are, the time and time zone, where your notes are, and the rules (plain text, never reword your notes, never claim what it didn't do). Two things of yours go along too:

  • the In brief part of your profile (about-me/profile.md, the section under ## In brief), built in the setup page's About you step; /aboutme shows it;
  • a short version of your how-to-help-me note (what you told it with /remember).

Both are yours to edit: see teaching-kurooto.md. Answers come in the language you write in (you.language = follow); set it to a language name, e.g. German, to always get that language.

What leaves your computer: what you ask, and the notes and files the AI reads for it, go to the AI service you picked, under that service's terms. Ollama and an OpenAI-compatible server on your own computer keep it there. See privacy.md.
