Voice: talk and listen

Kurooto can speak through your computer's speakers, with a voice made on the computer itself, and, if you want, listen to the room all day through the microphone. Everything it hears is judged on your computer first: noise and people talking to each other are dropped on the spot, and only what you say *to Kurooto* goes to your AI. Off until you turn it on, and listening has its own switch on top of that (0.8.8).

Turning it on

Features → Voice: talk and listen → Set up. Setting up downloads what the switches you have need, and says how much first; nothing is downloaded before that:

PartWhat it's forAbout
sounddevice, numpyplaying and recording sound60 MB
Kokoro-82M (the default voice)speaking: a natural voice, made on your computer0.55 GB with its program
Piper (if you pick it instead)speaking: lighter, less natural0.12 GB
faster-whisper and its modelwriting out what it hears (base model: 145 MB; tiny 75 MB, small 484 MB)0.6 GB
Silero VADtelling speech from other sound2 MB
openWakeWord (only if you give your own wake word model)a wake word of your own0.2 GB

They go into Kurooto's own folder (kurooto paths, the app line, then voice/), never into your system. Listening needs a microphone; your system's sound settings decide which one is the default.

Turn listening on with voice.listen.on in Settings, then Set up again: it fetches the listening parts.

Speaking

Kurooto reads its answers aloud, a few sentences at a time (the first piece plays while the next is being made, so it starts quickly). Short sentences are spoken together, up to about 200 characters, because a few words read on their own sound hurried and flat (0.8.13; voice.speak.join_sentences off reads one sentence at a time). Between pieces it leaves the short pause Kokoro itself makes between sentences. By default it reads the answers to what you said aloud; voice.speak.what = all reads every answer from your AI (Telegram and the phone app ones too), and off keeps it silent unless asked.

  • Pick a voice with voice.speak.voice (Kokoro: af_heart, the default, af_bella, af_nicole, af_sarah, am_michael, am_adam, bf_emma, bf_isabella, bm_george, bm_lewis), and the speed with voice.speak.rate.
  • Long answers are read up to voice.speak.max_chars (800 characters), then "The rest is on screen."
  • Code in an answer isn't read out ("code on screen"), and links are read as "a link".
  • Numbers are said as a person would (0.8.13): 21:31 as "nine thirty-one PM", 27/10/2026 as "the twenty-seventh of October twenty twenty-six", ₹3,000 as "three thousand rupees", 3-5 as "three to five", 12 GB as "twelve gigabytes", 0.8.13 as "zero point eight point thirteen", 2026 as "twenty twenty-six". Only what's said changes; the screen shows what was written. Write a count in the year range with a comma (2,026 people) and it's read as a count. voice.speak.numbers_as_words turns it off.
  • /voice say Good morning says something now; /voice stop stops it mid-sentence.
  • An add-on can play Kurooto's voice on another device instead of these speakers (0.8.12); when that device can't, the computer's speakers play it, and /voice stop and mute stop it there too.

Listening

With voice.listen.on, the microphone stays open all day, and everything below happens on your computer:

  1. Voice activity (Silero VAD) finds stretches of speech; anything else is never even written out.
  2. Writing out (faster-whisper) turns each stretch of speech into words.
  3. The gate decides: noise, chatter (people talking to each other), or talking to Kurooto.

Its name wakes it. Say "Kurooto" (or the name you gave it, you.assistant_name) first or last: "Kurooto, what's due?", "hey Kurooto, set a timer", "what's due, Kurooto?". Saying the name alone ("Kurooto?") gets a "Yes?", and what you say next is for it. Nothing is downloaded for this and no ready-made wake word is used.

Whisper often spells an unusual name wrong, so Kurooto is generous about it:

  • Whisper is given the name as a hint, so it writes "Kurooto" more often.
  • Known mishearings of "Kurooto" count: Kuroto, Kurouto, Kuruto, Curoto, Kurato, "kuro to", "coo roto", Kirito, "guru to", Kuroo, Kuro ("hey Kuro") and more.
  • Near misses count too: a word that sounds the same (the same consonants: k/c/g, r/l, t/d) and is spelled within two letters of the name ("Kurroto", "Kurotto"). Ordinary words don't: "cool", "auto", "curry", "Kurt", "router", "kurta", "Kyoto".
  • Add spellings it keeps getting wrong with voice.listen.name_variants (["crouton"]).

A wake word model of your own is an option: train one with openWakeWord (for "hey Kurooto", say) and put the path of its .onnx file in voice.listen.wake_word. Set up then fetches openWakeWord. Its ready-made wake words aren't offered, because their licence (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) doesn't fit.

How it tells you're talking to it

In this order, first match wins:

What it heardCounts as
no speechnoise
your own wake word model (if you set one)talking to it
no real words (or what transcribers invent from silence: "thank you", "[music]")noise
"stop", "not you", "never mind" ... right after it opened a conversation or spoke (see Telling it off)it stops
its name, first or last, as Whisper may spell it (careful when someone else may be talking: below)talking to it
the first words after its name said on its own ("Kurooto?")talking to it
two or more voices, and none of the above (when the listener can tell; today's can't, so the model judges this from the words and from how much talk there's been nearby)chatter
anything the voice it just answered says within 20 seconds of the answer ending, statements tootalking to it
a question or request while the conversation is opentalking to it
a question or request within 6 seconds of it speaking (a yes or no too, if it asked something)talking to it
anything elsethe model on your computer decides

Then, with Face presence on: anything above that counts as talking to it, except your wake word, needs a face close to the camera and facing it (below, "It only answers someone in front of it"); else it's chatter.

The model on your computer (Ollama, the one Local AI models set up, or the offline helper's) reads the words and answers noise, chatter or talking to it, with how sure it is. Below voice.listen.threshold (0.7) it counts as not talking to it. With no local model, only its name, your wake word and the rules above open a conversation.

It only answers someone in front of it

With Face presence on and enrolled, Kurooto answers only someone close to the camera and facing it. Any turn that would count as talking to it (its name, a follow-up, the model's judgement, cutting in) also needs the camera to have seen, while it was said or within about a second and a half after, a face:

  • close: at least half as tall as your face usually is at your desk (voice.listen.face_near, 0.5; about twice your desk distance), and
  • facing it: turned at most about 35 degrees away from the camera (voice.listen.face_yaw_deg).

Otherwise it's chatter ("no one facing it nearby") and nothing is sent: your roommate on a phone call across the room, the TV, someone talking to you from the door. Your wake word and "stop" / "never mind" work without a face, so stopping it always works; typed /voice commands are never affected.

While it listens, Voice keeps Face presence's camera looking, even while you type (the Face card says "camera on: voice: who's talking"). Face presence's own pauses still come first: a call, a locked screen, /face pause, focus, the night, another program using the camera, a covered camera. When the camera can't see, voice.listen.need_face_fallback decides: voice (default) answers by the voice rules alone, as before; none answers nothing by voice until the camera is back. /voice says which applies now, e.g. "needs a face: camera paused (screen locked), using voice rules".

Only the time of each look and how many faces were close and facing are kept, in memory; no picture, no face numbers. voice.listen.need_face (on) turns it off. With Face presence off or not enrolled it does nothing. Two things to know: the camera's light stays on while Voice listens, and on Linux a video call app can't use the camera then (/voice mute or /face pause frees it).

When someone else may be talking: its name has to be a call

Mentioning Kurooto to a friend shouldn't wake it. So when someone else may be talking (Voice ID isn't sure the voice is yours or knows it isn't, people talked here in the last minute without talking to Kurooto, or Face presence sees two or more faces), its name counts only said first as a call: "Kurooto, what's due?", "hey Kurooto, ...", or just "Kurooto". Its name at the end ("can you check this, Kurooto") or in the middle, or a word that only sounds like it, is then only a mention: the other rules and the model on your computer decide, and the model is told it may be a mention. With you alone and no one talking, its name first or last wakes it as before. Switches: voice.listen.careful_name (on) and voice.listen.chatter_s (60 seconds).

Telling it off

If it wakes when you weren't talking to it, say "stop", "not you", "I wasn't talking to you", "shut up", "be quiet", "go away" or "never mind" within about 10 seconds of it opening the conversation or speaking: it stops speaking at once, closes the conversation and says nothing. You can say it while it's still speaking (below, "Cutting in"); with that switched off, say it as soon as it stops (or /voice stop, the dashboard). It notes the misfire (which rule woke it, never your words) for the night's learning. voice.listen.told_off (on).

Cutting in while it speaks

You don't have to wait for a long answer to finish: talk over it. Kurooto keeps listening while it speaks, stops at once when it hears you, and then:

  • "Stop", "never mind", "not you" and the like: it just stops and closes the conversation (as above).
  • A question or a request ("wait, what about Sunday?", "set it for six instead"): it stops and answers that, in the same conversation, so it knows what you were talking about.
  • Anything else ("hmm, hold on"): it stops and listens; what you say in the next voice.listen.follow_s seconds goes to it without its name.

It never takes its own voice coming back through the room for you. It counts as you cutting in when:

  • Voice ID (on and enrolled) recognises your voice: at least about 0.6 seconds of speech that matches your voice print and is clearly closer to it than to Kurooto's own voice. Kurooto's own voice print is made once, in memory, from a phrase its voice says silently; it's never played or saved.
  • Or you're clearly louder than its echo. It knows what it's playing and when, and learns how loud its own voice comes back to the microphone; speech well above that whose words, written out here, aren't what it's saying, counts.

Limits:

  • Speakerphone or a high volume: its echo can drown you out. Speak up, come closer to the microphone, or turn it down. The same goes for the phone app playing its voice next to the computer: the computer's microphone hears that too, a little late, and it allows for that.
  • A single short word like "stop" is too short for the voice check: say it clearly louder than it, or say a little more ("stop talking", "Kurooto, stop").
  • A guest can't cut in by voice: with Voice ID only your voice passes the voice check, so someone else has to be clearly louder than Kurooto (and what they then say goes through the usual guest rules).
  • Without Voice ID only the loudness check works, so a loud TV or someone shouting nearby may stop it; turn the switch off if that happens.

voice.listen.barge_in (on). Off, the microphone is dropped while it speaks, as before; /voice stop always works. Nothing extra is kept: the sound stays in memory as for anything it hears, and the event log only says how it noticed you and what came of it, never your words.

Learning at night

Every night at 04:10 it learns from how its voice turns went: ones you carried on talking to it (good) and ones you told off (misfires). Only flags and numbers are kept, under Kurooto's state in voice/ (the rule that decided, how its name was said, how many voices, Voice ID's decision and score, seconds since other talk, faces in view; never words or sound). Once it has 20 turns with some of each, a small model can hold back a borderline opening (its name heard only as a near miss, a follow-up, the model's judgement) that looks like the misfires. It can never stop "Kurooto, ..." said first. /voice and /health show what it learned, as numbers. voice.listen.learn_nightly (on); forgetting your voice (/voice id forget) deletes these too.

The conversation (the "portal")

When the gate decides you're talking to Kurooto, a conversation opens: your words go to your AI like a chat message (answers worded to be spoken), and the answer comes back on the dashboard and aloud. The conversation carries context like a typed chat: your AI sees your turns before (a guest's turns stay apart). Keep talking and it follows the conversation: you don't have to say its name again. Within 20 seconds of its answer ending (voice.listen.follow_s; counted from when it finishes, so a phone playing its voice doesn't eat the time), whatever the same voice says goes to it, statements too, not only questions. Another voice, two voices at once, or telling it off ends that. It closes:

  • at once when the next thing it hears isn't to it (you turn to someone else);
  • after voice.listen.close_s seconds (8) with nothing said to it, or voice.listen.follow_s (20) after an answer;
  • when you mute it, a call starts, Kurooto pauses, or (with voice.listen.night off) the night begins.

The dashboard shows each step (listening, hearing, talking to it, thinking, speaking, closed) and a waveform. Speech that wasn't for Kurooto (people talking to each other, noise) moves the waveform too: only its loudness reaches the page, never its words. voice.listen.levels off stops both. On the being board the YOU lane is live while it listens: the microphone's loudness scrolls through it as you speak (look.being_live_waveform).

When it doesn't listen

  • Muted: /voice mute, kurooto voice mute, or the dashboard's mute. It stays muted (across restarts) until /voice unmute. Muting also stops it speaking.
  • On a call: while a phone call is on (voice.listen.calls to keep listening).
  • At night: it keeps listening by default; turn voice.listen.night off and it stops during your night hours (availability.night).
  • Paused: while Kurooto is paused, and on battery in saver mode.

Privacy

  • What it hears: everything near the microphone, while listening is on and nothing above stops it.
  • What it keeps: nothing. Sound stays in memory (a rolling buffer of under a second, and the stretch being written out) and is never written to disk. The words of noise and chatter are dropped as soon as the gate decides: never stored, logged or sent. It counts how many stretches were noise, chatter or for it, in memory only.
  • What leaves: only the turns the gate decided were said to Kurooto, as text, to the AI you picked, like a chat message (and kept in the chat history like a typed one). While the conversation is closed, nothing leaves.
  • Downloads: setting it up fetches packages from PyPI and models from GitHub and Hugging Face; nothing of yours is sent.

Voice ID: only your voice can make it act

Off by default (voice.speaker_id.on). Once it's on and knows your voice, every turn said to Kurooto is checked against your voice print, on this computer, and gets one of three answers: you, not sure or someone else.

  • You: everything works as before: commands, requests, your AI with its tools, settings, calls.
  • Someone else, or not sure: chat only. They get a real answer from your AI (or only the model on this computer, voice.speaker_id.guest_brain: local), but with none of your data (no notes, calendar, mail, deadlines, memory, profile, history or files) and no actions (no commands, reminders, alarms, calls, messages or settings). Once per conversation the reply says so: "You can chat with me, but only my owner's voice can make me do things." Not sure counts as not you ("I couldn't tell it was you, so I'm only chatting: say it again closer to the microphone, or type it.").
  • voice.speaker_id.mode: owner_only_at_all: others get "I only answer my owner's voice." and nothing else.
  • Typing always works: Telegram, the phone app's chat, the dashboard and the terminal are yours, so they aren't checked.

Turning it on and enrolling

  1. Dashboard → Features → Voice: tick Voice ID on the card, then Set up (it fetches a 28 MB speaker model and a small package, once, plus listening's parts if they aren't there yet; after that it works with no internet). The switch alone doesn't download anything: the Your voice page says so and has its own Set up now button. No internet on that computer? Manage → Install from a file on the page, or kurooto voice parts add FILE: the file is checked against its pinned checksum. Voice ID only checks what Kurooto hears, so listening (voice.listen.on) must be on too; the Your voice page says when it's off and has a Turn listening on button.
  2. Open Your voice (Voice ID) → on the Voice card (the page /voice-id), or type /voice enrol.
  3. Capture, about five minutes at the computer, through the microphone Kurooto listens with (never the browser's). It asks, aloud and on the page, for about twenty short sentences in six ways:

| Step | What you do | Samples | |---|---|---| | Normal | your normal voice, at your usual distance | 5 | | Quiet | a quiet voice, same place | 3 | | Far | step back about two metres | 3 | | Noise | with music, a fan or the TV on low | 3 | | Short requests | the way you really ask it: "Kurooto, what's on tomorrow?" | 4 | | Free speech | ten seconds about anything | 1 (three samples from it) |

Each sample is checked before it counts, and a rejected one says why with its number: too short, too loud (clipped), too quiet, too much noise, not the sentence, not the condition ("That sounded as close as before: step back about two metres."; quiet and far count when your voice is 4 dB lower than your normal one, or 4 dB less above the room's noise, so a microphone that evens out loudness still lets them pass), not sounding like your other samples, or identical to an earlier one (a recording). Say stop, skip, repeat, pause or continue during it. Any step but the first can be skipped. Speak another language? Tick "Say it in my own words".

  1. Train builds it (automatically at the end): good, fair or weak, with the thresholds, how often it would turn you away in each condition, and how many of about 1,750 strangers' voice prints would pass. A weak one is saved only when you press Use it anyway.
  2. Test: press, then say a sentence or two, starting within 15 seconds (it waits for you to finish). Try me (should be You), Try as someone else (ask someone; should be Someone else), Try a synthetic voice (Kokoro's ten voices, if Kokoro is set up). The page shows the score on a dial with the "not sure" and "accept" marks.

Later: Manage → Add samples adds a few in one condition (a new microphone, a cold), then Retrain. Manage → Start over (or /voice enrol again) records all six steps again from nothing; your voice print keeps working until the new one is built, Keep for later keeps the new samples and continues with the same plan next time, and Undo on the Train step puts the previous voice print back (until Kurooto restarts). Forget my voice deletes it.

What "training" means here

No neural network is trained or fine-tuned on your computer. A pretrained speaker model (CAM++) turns each sample into a list of numbers; training makes your template (the average of your samples) and calibrates your thresholds: each of your samples is scored against the others, and compared with the scores of about 1,750 strangers' voice prints that ship with Kurooto, so the threshold sits where strangers almost never pass. Short turns get their own, lower thresholds. A classifier trained on top of your samples was considered and not used: with 12 to 60 samples it learns the room you enrolled in more than your voice.

Improving over time, and undo

Off by default (voice.speaker_id.adapt; the Improve step, or the card's switch). Turns it was very sure were you (well above the threshold, at least two seconds, one voice) slowly refine your voice print: at most 10 a day and 40 in all, never more than a quarter of its weight, never from a turn it wasn't sure of. If your voice print would drift too far from your enrolment, it stops and says so. Undo today and Undo all (or /voice id undo today|all) put it back.

Strictness and the threshold

voice.speaker_id.strictness: relaxed, normal or strict (strict turns you away more often; strangers almost never pass; a short reply right after your turn no longer carries). Press Retrain after changing it. voice.speaker_id.threshold (0 = worked out from your samples) sets your own, held to 0.30 to 0.95 whatever is typed (below that most strangers would pass); the page shows the calibrated one beside it. voice.speaker_id.margin is held to at most 0.2.

How often it gets it wrong, measured on recorded speech (LibriSpeech's 146 dev and test speakers: each enrolled with the real enrolment, from clean audiobook reading with quiet, far and noisy versions made from it, then tried against the others, the strangers never in the threshold's calibration; tools/voiceid_eval.py):

relaxed99.3 %98.5 %89 %about 1 in 70 (1.4 %)2.5 %you 95 %, a stranger 1.9 %
normal99.3 %98.4 %89 %about 1 in 180 (0.3 to 0.9 %)1.0 %you 92 %, a stranger 0.8 %

Real rooms, colds and other microphones lower your numbers (more "not sure"); the strangers' numbers depend mostly on how much someone sounds like you: a few look-alike voices carry most of the misses, so a relative may pass more often. Strict is the one to use when strangers are around often.

Short replies

A "yes" is too short to tell. Right after a turn it knew was you, in the same conversation and within 15 seconds (voice.speaker_id.carry_s), a short reply counts as you unless it sounds clearly like someone else (voice.speaker_id.carry, off in strict mode). That's a deliberate weakness: someone saying "yes" just after you could pass.

In a conversation you opened (0.8.13, voice.speaker_id.carry_talk, on), it goes further: while the conversation that Voice ID knew you opened is open, any turn it's only "not sure" of (inside the margin, not clearly someone else, one voice) stays yours, within 15 seconds or the conversation window (20), the longer, of its last answer to you. So your history, notes and tools carry instead of the turn dropping to "only chatting". Before 0.8.13 such a turn went the guest way. Off in strict mode.

With your face (0.8.13, voice.speaker_id.face_assist, on): when Face presence's last look (a few seconds old at most) shows exactly one face and it's yours, a voice it's only "not sure" of counts as you. A voice clearly someone else's, two voices, two faces or strict never do. With "improve over time" on, such a turn that went well (you carried on, nothing told off) may refine your voice print like a very sure one, under the same daily cap and undo.

Other people talking

With Voice ID on, the voice is checked before the words are written out (voice.speaker_id.gate): someone else's talk that isn't calling Kurooto by name is dropped as chatter without being written out (only its first and last two seconds are, to hear its name). With the gate off, everything is written out as before, but conversation mode still checks each voice so it knows someone else is talking. While another voice has been heard in the last minute, Kurooto is in conversation mode (voice.listen.conversation_mode): it stays out of people talking to each other, you included, and only its name with a question or request after it ("Kurooto, …") or a reply right after its own answer opens the conversation. "Kurooto told me…" only mentions it. The dashboard's ear says "conversation: staying out".

Your voice print

  • Kept: only your voice print (numbers, condition names, dates, thresholds, quality numbers and the microphone's name), encrypted (AES-256-GCM) in Kurooto's state folder, with its key in your system's keychain (or the owner-only secrets file where there's none). Never your recordings: sound stays in memory and is dropped as soon as it's checked.
  • Never: backed up, synced to another computer, in a diagnostics bundle, logged, on the live stream, or shown.
  • Forget: /voice id forget, the page's Forget my voice, kurooto voice id forget, or /forget all: the file and its key are deleted. On an SSD the deleted file's blocks may linger until reused, but with its key gone they're unreadable.
  • Can never be done by voice: enrolling, changing Voice ID's settings, forgetting.

Honest limits

  • A recording of your voice will usually pass. It checks whose voice it is, not whether it's live. A recorded plain-English request ("wake me at 6", "remind me at five") runs at once, as when you say it yourself; what the AI would do on its own (sending, calls, alarms, deleting) still asks for your tap (Approve / Deny) as always. Enrolment refuses identical samples.
  • A cloned voice (made from a minute of you on video) can pass too. The synthetic test only covers Kokoro's stock voices.
  • A close relative with a similar voice may pass at normal strictness: use strict, and try "Try as someone else" with them.
  • A cold, or just woken up: scores drop and you may get "not sure"; add samples while you're ill (Manage → Add samples).
  • A new microphone or headset changes scores: it warns "enrolled with A, listening with B"; add a few samples with it.
  • Two people at once in one turn give a mixed score (usually not sure); it can't separate voices inside one turn.
  • Someone at your keyboard isn't checked: typing is yours.
  • A guest's chat turn runs on your AI only when it can be held to plain text (an API key, or Claude Code with no tools). agy and Codex load their own MCP servers and instructions on every run, which Kurooto can't switch off for one turn, so with one of them as your AI a guest is answered by the model on this computer, or told plainly that Kurooto only chats with its owner when there's none. guest_brain: local always uses this computer.
  • A guest's words go to the AI as a request; once the answer is spoken, the request's words are blanked (the request list keeps only a row titled "Guest"), so nothing learns from them later.

Greeting you at your desk

With Face presence on, Kurooto can greet you through Voice when you sit down after a while, then open the conversation so you can answer without saying its name ("what's next?", "yes"). It opens it like after a reply, not like after its name alone, so people talking nearby close it instead of reaching your AI.

What it can't do yet

  • Cutting in while it speaks works best near the microphone at a normal volume: a loud echo (a speakerphone, the volume up high) can drown you out, and a guest can't cut in by voice (see "Cutting in while it speaks"). /voice stop or mute always stops it.
  • Without Voice ID it doesn't know who is speaking; with it, it knows you from anyone else, but can't tell two voices apart inside one turn, and can't tell a recording of you from you.
  • Its name has to be heard and written out first, so waking takes a moment longer than a dedicated wake word model would, and a name Whisper mangles in a new way needs voice.listen.name_variants.
  • Tested with fakes only so far: real microphones, speakers and the models haven't been tried on many computers.
