Voice: talk and listen

Kurooto can speak through your computer's speakers, with a voice made on the computer itself, and, if you want, listen to the room all day through the microphone. Everything it hears is judged on your computer first: noise and people talking to each other are dropped on the spot, and only what you say *to Kurooto* goes to your AI. Off until you turn it on, and listening has its own switch on top of that (0.8.8).

Turning it on

Features → Voice: talk and listen → Set up. Setting up downloads what the switches you have need, and says how much first; nothing is downloaded before that:

PartWhat it's forAbout
sounddevice, numpyplaying and recording sound60 MB
Kokoro-82M (the default voice)speaking: a natural voice, made on your computer0.55 GB with its program
Piper (if you pick it instead)speaking: lighter, less natural0.12 GB
faster-whisper and its modelwriting out what it hears (base model: 145 MB; tiny 75 MB, small 484 MB)0.6 GB
Silero VADtelling speech from other sound2 MB
openWakeWord (only if you give your own wake word model)a wake word of your own0.2 GB

They go into Kurooto's own folder (kurooto paths, the app line, then voice/), never into your system. Listening needs a microphone; your system's sound settings decide which one is the default.

Turn listening on with voice.listen.on in Settings, then Set up again: it fetches the listening parts.

Speaking

Kurooto reads its answers aloud, sentence by sentence (the first sentence plays while the next is being made, so it starts quickly). By default it reads the answers to what you said aloud; voice.speak.what = all reads every answer from your AI (Telegram and the phone app ones too), and off keeps it silent unless asked.

  • Pick a voice with voice.speak.voice (Kokoro: af_heart, the default, af_bella, af_nicole, af_sarah, am_michael, am_adam, bf_emma, bf_isabella, bm_george, bm_lewis), and the speed with voice.speak.rate.
  • Long answers are read up to voice.speak.max_chars (800 characters), then "The rest is on screen."
  • Code in an answer isn't read out ("code on screen"), and links are read as "a link".
  • /voice say Good morning says something now; /voice stop stops it mid-sentence.

Listening

With voice.listen.on, the microphone stays open all day, and everything below happens on your computer:

  1. Voice activity (Silero VAD) finds stretches of speech; anything else is never even written out.
  2. Writing out (faster-whisper) turns each stretch of speech into words.
  3. The gate decides: noise, chatter (people talking to each other), or talking to Kurooto.

Its name wakes it. Say "Kurooto" (or the name you gave it, you.assistant_name) first or last: "Kurooto, what's due?", "hey Kurooto, set a timer", "what's due, Kurooto?". Saying the name alone ("Kurooto?") gets a "Yes?", and what you say next is for it. Nothing is downloaded for this and no ready-made wake word is used.

Whisper often spells an unusual name wrong, so Kurooto is generous about it:

  • Whisper is given the name as a hint, so it writes "Kurooto" more often.
  • Known mishearings of "Kurooto" count: Kuroto, Kurouto, Kuruto, Curoto, Kurato, "kuro to", "coo roto", Kirito, "guru to", Kuroo, Kuro ("hey Kuro") and more.
  • Near misses count too: a word that sounds the same (the same consonants: k/c/g, r/l, t/d) and is spelled within two letters of the name ("Kurroto", "Kurotto"). Ordinary words don't: "cool", "auto", "curry", "Kurt", "router", "kurta", "Kyoto".
  • Add spellings it keeps getting wrong with voice.listen.name_variants (["crouton"]).

A wake word model of your own is an option: train one with openWakeWord (for "hey Kurooto", say) and put the path of its .onnx file in voice.listen.wake_word. Set up then fetches openWakeWord. Its ready-made wake words aren't offered, because their licence (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) doesn't fit.

How it tells you're talking to it

In this order, first match wins:

What it heardCounts as
no speechnoise
your own wake word model (if you set one)talking to it
no real words (or what transcribers invent from silence: "thank you", "[music]")noise
its name, first or last, as Whisper may spell ittalking to it
the first words after its name said on its own ("Kurooto?")talking to it
two or more voices, and none of the above (when the listener can tell; today's can't, so the model judges this from the words and from how much talk there's been nearby)chatter
a question or request while the conversation is opentalking to it
a question or request within 6 seconds of it speaking (a yes or no too, if it asked something)talking to it
anything elsethe model on your computer decides

The model on your computer (Ollama, the one Local AI models set up, or the offline helper's) reads the words and answers noise, chatter or talking to it, with how sure it is. Below voice.listen.threshold (0.7) it counts as not talking to it. With no local model, only its name, your wake word and the rules above open a conversation.

The conversation (the "portal")

When the gate decides you're talking to Kurooto, a conversation opens: your words go to your AI like a chat message (answers worded to be spoken), and the answer comes back on the dashboard and aloud. Keep talking and it follows the conversation. It closes:

  • at once when the next thing it hears isn't to it (you turn to someone else);
  • after voice.listen.close_s seconds (8) with nothing said to it;
  • when you mute it, a call starts, Kurooto pauses, or (with voice.listen.night off) the night begins.

The dashboard shows each step (listening, hearing, talking to it, thinking, speaking, closed) and a waveform.

When it doesn't listen

  • Muted: /voice mute, kurooto voice mute, or the dashboard's mute. It stays muted (across restarts) until /voice unmute. Muting also stops it speaking.
  • On a call: while a phone call is on (voice.listen.calls to keep listening).
  • At night: it keeps listening by default; turn voice.listen.night off and it stops during your night hours (availability.night).
  • Paused: while Kurooto is paused, and on battery in saver mode.

Commands

CommandWhat it does
/voicewhat it's doing, and how much it heard since it started (counts only)
/voice mute, /voice unmutestop and start listening and speaking
/voice stopstop speaking now
/voice say <text>say this now

The same from a terminal: kurooto voice, kurooto voice mute, kurooto voice say Hello.

Privacy

  • What it hears: everything near the microphone, while listening is on and nothing above stops it.
  • What it keeps: nothing. Sound stays in memory (a rolling buffer of under a second, and the stretch being written out) and is never written to disk. The words of noise and chatter are dropped as soon as the gate decides: never stored, logged or sent. It counts how many stretches were noise, chatter or for it, in memory only.
  • What leaves: only the turns the gate decided were said to Kurooto, as text, to the AI you picked, like a chat message (and kept in the chat history like a typed one). While the conversation is closed, nothing leaves.
  • Downloads: setting it up fetches packages from PyPI and models from GitHub and Hugging Face; nothing of yours is sent.

What it can't do yet

  • It can't be interrupted while it speaks: it doesn't listen while speaking (so it doesn't hear itself). /voice stop or mute stops it.
  • It doesn't know who is speaking; "several voices" is the rule's best guess, not speaker recognition.
  • Its name has to be heard and written out first, so waking takes a moment longer than a dedicated wake word model would, and a name Whisper mangles in a new way needs voice.listen.name_variants.
  • Tested with fakes only so far: real microphones, speakers and the models haven't been tried on many computers.
