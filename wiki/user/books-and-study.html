Books and study

Your textbooks read into text your AI can cite by page, a say in how much books, class material and your own notes count when the AI writes notes, a study-time tracker, study packs to take a subject to another AI, and handwritten pages written out as notes.

Books and PDFs

What it does. Keeps your reference books and other PDFs per subject, reads them at night into text with page numbers, writes an index note for each book in your notes, and lets your AI use the right pages (and cite them, "book: Linear Algebra, p. 123") when it writes course notes, exam prep, revision packs and study packs.

Turn it on. Dashboard → Features → Books and PDFs.

Adding a book, three ways:

  • Telegram: send the PDF with a caption like book for linear algebra, textbook for stats or linear algebra textbook. It's moved out of your notes' inbox into that subject's books folder. Telegram lets bots fetch files only up to 20 MB: put bigger books in the folder. Only PDFs are taken ("Books must be PDFs").
  • The dashboard: Features → Books and PDFs: give the PDF's place on this computer and its subject (a copy is made).
  • The folder: drop PDFs into the subject's books folder, in any subfolder; .pdf in any case. /books folder linear algebra says where it is (and makes it).

Books are never kept inside your notes folder. They go to <files folder>/<subject>/books/ when you've set a files folder, else to Kurooto's data folder (books/<subject>/); books.folder picks your own place (a folder per subject inside it). Files over 400 MB are left alone.

Reading. At 01:00, on the charger, Kurooto reads every new book page by page and picks the best way for each page:

  1. the PDF's own text, when the page has a real text layer (Poppler's pdftotext if it's installed, else Kurooto's built-in reader);
  2. a scanned page: Tesseract OCR, if installed (/books setup installs it). Tesseract writes plain text, so equations come out garbled;
  3. without Tesseract, or for pages full of maths whose text came out in pieces: a vision model on this computer (Local AI models, on graphics cards of 8 GB or more), which writes the maths in LaTeX;
  4. Gemini with your key, only if you turn on books.gemini: pictures of pages nothing on this computer could read go to Google. Books may be copyrighted, so this is off by default.

It stops at 07:00, when you unplug, or with /books stop, and carries on the next night from the page where it stopped. Pages no reader could handle wait, and are read once a reader is there. Encrypted PDFs need Poppler's PDF tools: poppler-utils from your package manager on Linux, brew install poppler on a Mac, or poppler.freedesktop.org on Windows. When a book is done you get one message: "Read "Linear Algebra" (412 pages: 398 by text layer, 14 by Tesseract). Its chapters are in …".

What it makes:

  • the text with page markers (## [p. 123]) in Kurooto's data folder (book-text/<subject>/<book>/text.md), outside your notes;
  • an index note in your notes, <subject>/books/<book>.md: the title, the chapters with their pages (from the PDF's bookmarks, or "Chapter 3 …" headings), how each page was read, and links to the original. Kurooto writes only between its two markers; your own notes around them stay;
  • with the Notes brain on, the text in the brain too, so /notes finds book pages;
  • with the Concept graph on, the book's concepts woven into your notes.

Page numbers are the PDF's own (p. 1 is the first page of the file, often the cover).

Turn it off / undo. Features → Books and PDFs → Turn off: reading stops after the current page; books, their text and the index notes stay. To remove a book, delete its PDF from its folder; its index note and its text are then yours to delete (Kurooto never deletes them). Tesseract installed by /books setup is removed like any program (winget on Windows, Homebrew on a Mac, your package manager on Linux).

The source mix

When your AI writes course notes (/study-log, Classroom → notes, /exam-prep, /pack, /add-to-notes), it asks for a subject's material from three sources:

  • book: the pages of your books on the topic;
  • classroom: Classroom posts and the files Classroom → notes downloaded;
  • own: notes you wrote yourself (not the AI), the class log, and the "My notes" part of lecture notes.

Only the sources a subject has are used, and their shares grow to fill the room the others leave. The notes cite each part: (book: Linear Algebra, p. 123), (Classroom: Problem set 3), (your notes: [[linear-algebra/2026-10-12-eigen]]). Your own words are quoted, never reworded.

CommandWhat it does
/books mix linear algebrashows the subject's mix
/books mix linear algebra book 30 classroom 30 own 40shares (any numbers; they're scaled)
/books mix linear algebra prefer own book classrooman order instead: the first counts most (3:2:1)
/books mix linear algebra resetback to the general mix
/exam-prep linear algebrabrings that subject's exam-prep note up to date from all three sources (also without Classroom); the Quiz asks from it

Settings (Settings → Sources for answers)

SettingDefaultEffect
sources.mixbook 0.3, classroom 0.3, own 0.4the general shares
sources.prefer[]an order instead of shares, e.g. ["own", "book", "classroom"]
sources.capbook 12000, classroom 10000, own 12000characters at most from each source

Undo. /books mix <subject> reset; reset sources.mix in Settings for the general one.

Study tracker

What it does. Counts how long you spend on each subject from the window you're working in, and what you log by hand. A window whose title names a subject (its name or a short name: a PDF called linear-algebra-week3.pdf, a note in that subject's folder) counts while you're at the computer: not idle for 5 minutes and the screen not locked. Only minutes per subject are kept, never window titles.

Turn it on. Features → Study tracker (needs Subjects). Works on Windows, macOS (it asks once for permission to see window titles; say no and it can only log by hand) and Linux on X11, Hyprland or Sway. Other Linux desktops can only log by hand.

/study la 50starts a 50-minute session away from the computer (library, paper); it ends by itself with "Session done. Linear Algebra: 50 min logged. Take a break."
/study lastarts an open session
/study stopends the session and logs it

While a session runs, the window doesn't count (no double counting). A timed session counts at most the time you planned. A session that runs past midnight goes to the day it started. Starting a new session ends the old one first. You can also tell the evening check-in call what you studied.

In your notes. At 23:45 the day's hours go into your daily note as "## Study hours", with how much was away from the computer. If the computer was asleep then, it's written for that day when it wakes.

How it tells you're away. Windows and macOS from the system's last-input time; Linux from your desktop (X11, GNOME, KDE, logind's idle hint). On Hyprland, Sway and other Wayland desktops without an idle API it watches for change instead (keyboard and touchpad activity, the cursor on Hyprland, the focused window; nothing you type is read). A locked screen never counts. kurooto doctor says which way this computer uses.

Your idle tool can tell it (more exact on Wayland): kurooto idle away when you go idle, kurooto idle back when you're back. For hypridle (~/.config/hypr/hypridle.conf):

listener {
    timeout = 300
    on-timeout = kurooto idle away
    on-resume = kurooto idle back
}

For swayidle: swayidle timeout 300 'kurooto idle away' resume 'kurooto idle back'.

Research time. A window naming one of study.research_paths (words, or folders matched by name) counts as Research; with Research on, its projects and repository folders count too.

On the dashboard. The STATUS pane shows "study today"; kurooto top too. The weekly summary and the Sunday review call include study hours. Study mode (Alt+M, /study mode on) keeps only your notes and a focus and break timer: a focus holds calls and messages and logs its minutes here.

Settings that matter (Dashboard → Settings → Study tracker)

SettingDefaultEffect
study.idle_min5minutes without input before you count as away
study.daily_note23:45when the day's hours go into the daily note; empty: never

What leaves your computer. Nothing. Window titles are read here and thrown away.

Turn it off / undo. Features → Study tracker → Turn off. Empty study.research_paths; remove the idle hook lines (and run kurooto idle back once, or counting stays paused).

Study packs

What it does. Bundles one subject's notes and course material into a zip with a ready-made prompt, so you can hand it to another AI (any chat AI that takes file uploads) and ask it for deeper study notes. Nothing here uses your own AI: Kurooto only collects files and fills in the prompt.

Turn it on. It's part of Skills: Features → Skills.

From Telegram. /studypack linear algebra for everything, or add what it's for: /studypack la quiz on thursday. Naming a topic narrows the pack to it: /studypack la quiz on eigenvalues (or an aim that names one, such as /studypack la eigenvalues; several: on eigenvalues, diagonalisation) takes only the class files that mention it, the pages of each book about it (as text, with a page either side, up to 150 pages per book) and the notes about it, including what the notes brain finds. Class material and books are told apart by length, because professors post books on Classroom too: over 80 pages, or a title with "Ch 8", "edition" or a download site's name, is a book; 40 pages or less, or a lecture, notes, slides, cheat sheet, quiz, assignment or exam paper, is class material. Class material always comes first: in the zip (class/, then books/, then notes/) and in the prompt, which tells the other AI to follow the class where a book disagrees. A whole book never goes in: it would flood the other AI's context. Without a topic, books are only listed in INDEX.md; name a topic to get their pages. A book Books hasn't read yet can't be cut to its pages, so it stays out too. The AI can do the same when you ask in plain words ("send me everything on thermodynamics for my POTA quiz").

Which pages of a book go in, in order: first the readings your class material cites ("Reference: Salby Chapter 2", "Wallace and Hobbs Chapter 3 Section 3.2, 3.3"), then whole chapters and sections whose titles name the topic (from the book's own bookmarks), then other pages that mention it, those mentioning it most first. All the books together keep to 250 pages, about what another AI can hold at once; INDEX.md lists what didn't fit. Lecture files Books hasn't read are judged by Classroom's text of them, and a file that's both in Classroom and in your books folder goes in once.

Your own prompt. /studypack prompt puts the prompt every pack gives the other AI into your notes (kurooto/study-pack-prompt.md) to edit; {name}, {topics}, {aim} and the other words listed at its top are filled in for each pack. /studypack prompt reset brings Kurooto's back. The subject can be its name or a short name; your AI can make one for you too ("make me a study pack for statistics").

What's in the zip:

Folder or fileWhat
class/what the class gave out: lecture slides and notes, cheat sheets, problem sets, exam papers
books/books for the course (whole, or with a topic only its pages, as text)
notes/your notes for the subject, and any note that links to them
PROMPT.mdwhat to say to the other AI, with the aim you gave
INDEX.mdevery file in the pack, and what was left out and why

Class material and books are told apart by length, since books are often posted to Classroom too: over 80 pages, or a title that says "edition", "Ch 8" and the like, is a book; 40 pages or less, or a lecture, slides, notes, quiz, assignment or exam paper, is class material.

What comes to Telegram. The zip ("Study pack: Linear Algebra. Upload it to your AI and paste the prompt below."), then the prompt as its own message, ready to copy and paste (it's also PROMPT.md in the zip), and a line saying how many notes and files went in.

Size limits. Telegram takes files up to 50 MB.

  • With Drive upload on, nothing is left out: a pack over 50 MB goes to your Google Drive and you get a link. That upload is temporary: it's deleted from your Drive after drive_upload.temp_days (7) days, unless you move it out of the Kurooto folder in Drive first.
  • Without it, the zip is kept under 50 MB (notes first, then class material, newest first, then books); what didn't fit is listed in INDEX.md and the reply says how many files were left out.
  • Notes over 400 KB and files over 40 MB are always left out; hidden files and folders never go in.

What leaves your computer. Only what you choose to upload to the other AI yourself. With Drive upload, a pack over 50 MB goes to your own Google Drive. The zip is kept in Kurooto's cache, which the Space keeper clears after 14 days.

Turn it off. Turn off Skills, or just don't use /studypack.

Handwritten notes

What it does. Drop a PDF or photo of handwritten pages into a folder and Kurooto writes it out as a Markdown note with the maths in LaTeX, saved in handwritten/ in your notes, linked to the original.

Turn it on. Features → Handwritten notes. By default the folder is handwritten-inbox in your notes (or in your files folder); point handwritten.folder at a folder your tablet's notes app syncs to (Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox). Photos (.png, .jpg, .jpeg, .webp, .heic) and PDFs work.

Who reads the pages.

  • This computer, with Local AI models on: the vision model on 12 GB graphics cards, or the helper model on 8 GB cards; handwritten.local_model names another Ollama model that reads pictures. Nothing leaves your computer then. Photos are read right away. PDFs are read page by page at night on the charger (handwritten.pdf_hours, 23:00 to 07:00), each page kept as it's done, so unplugging or the morning only pauses it. You get one message when a PDF is waiting for the night ("Got notes.pdf (12 pages). I'll write it out … at night; /handwritten starts it now."). It waits while the graphics card is busy with something else, and writes out up to 60 pages per PDF. Drawing a tablet's PDF pages as pictures needs Poppler's PDF tools (scanned PDFs don't).
  • Gemini, with your key, when there's no model here. handwritten.cloud = false keeps everything on your computer: pages then wait for a model here.

It looks in the folder every 15 minutes. When pages are written out you get "Written out: …"; a file that fails gets a message saying why.

Your own notes in it. Kurooto's part of the note sits between `` markers. When you change the original on your tablet, the same note is written again between the markers; what you wrote around them stays. Machine transcriptions aren't checked: look at the original for anything that matters.

Linked like any other note. Once a note is written out, one request to your AI (the concept graph's second-look AI when that's on, else your main AI) links it into the rest of your notes, and you get one line: "Linked your handwritten notes 'Phase change thermodynamics' to POTA lecture 20 and 9 concepts".

  • A real title. When the heading is still the scan's file name ("21 October 2026_261021_105812"), it becomes a title from what's written, and the old name stays as an alias. A heading you changed, or anything you wrote above the transcription, is left alone.
  • The subject and the lecture. Kurooto looks at when the pages were written (the PDF's own dates, a time stamp in the file's name, else the file's time) and at your timetable: written during a class, or later that day, the notes belong to that class. Otherwise the AI picks the subject from what the pages say, against your subjects' course pages. The note's header gets the subject's tag and course: <slug>.
  • ## Links right after the transcription's closing marker, in a block of Kurooto's own (`): that day's lecture note, the course page and the concept notes the pages cover, each as - [[path]]: reason`. The candidates come from the concept graph's own search (no AI); the AI picks the ones that really relate and says why. A link to a note that doesn't exist, or with a vague reason, is never written.
  • ## Doubts: lines you marked DOUBT: … or ???, with their page, so you find them before the next class.
  • The course page lists the note under its lecture (or under "Handwritten notes" when no lecture matched), in a `` block. Your own text there isn't touched.

Notes written out before 0.5.8 get the same with /handwritten link (once: a note that has a Links section is skipped, so running it again changes nothing). Every change keeps a before-copy; /handwritten undo puts the last linking back (a file you edited since is left as it is).

Into the concept graph (opt-in). Add "handwritten" to integrate.sources (or tick "Read your handwritten notes" on the Concept graph card) and each linked note is then read into the concept graph like a book: its concepts become notes (or lines on the ones you have) whose "Seen in" points back at the handwritten note. Each note is read once; a page you change on your tablet is read again. Notes with no subject are left out.

What leaves your computer. Nothing when a model here reads the pages. Otherwise the pages go to Google (Gemini, your key); on the free tier Google may use them to improve its products. Turn it off / undo. Features → Handwritten notes → Turn off; the notes and copies stay. handwritten.local = false sends everything to Gemini instead. /handwritten undo takes back the last linking; handwritten.link = false stops linking.
