Brain

Brain is Kurooto's mind (0.8.0). It's one feature, off until you turn it on, and turned off Kurooto works exactly as it did before 0.8.0. Each part Brain grows has a switch of its own, so you can take what you like and leave the rest.

Everything is here in 0.8.0: the log Kurooto can't quietly change (kept by a separate program the AI can't reach), and the parts built on it: the forecast and read of how you're doing, drives and feelings, autonomy, a true journal and a life story, the "now" workspace, beliefs, the research lab and self-improvement, learned skills, the senses, a model of what you know and want, week plans, careful forgetting, and (off until you turn them on) precedents and play. When Brain is on, most parts start on, each with its own switch (/mind lists them); the few that aren't are marked off below. The ones that could act on their own (forecasts, autonomy, the read of how you're doing) start in shadow mode: they predict and score themselves first and can't change what Kurooto does.

Turning it on and off

  • On: dashboard → Features → Brain → Turn on. (/mind while it's off says where.) The setup page also asks once, in its optional Inner life step ("Let it grow an inner life"); Not now leaves it off.
  • Off: the same switch. Brain stops at once: its program ends and nothing more goes into its log. Kurooto then sends one message, "Brain is off", with two buttons:
  • Delete Brain's data: deletes everything Brain kept, for good;
  • Keep it: keeps the data, and turning Brain on again carries on from where it was.

Nothing is deleted unless you tap Delete. The button works for a week after the message.

The log and the scorekeeper

When Brain is on, Kurooto keeps a log of what its mind saw and decided:

  • What it saw: your deadlines being added, done or moved; reminders being set and going off; calls ringing, answered or missed; AI requests starting and ending; buttons you tapped; Undo; proposals; the computer going to sleep or changing power mode; Kurooto starting and stopping.
  • What it decided: before Kurooto does something on its own, it writes down the decision first. That covers a message it starts (a deadline nudge, a check-in), an AI request it queues by itself (a paper search) and a change it proposes at night. Each record says what it decided and the reason its code gave.

Two things make the log trustworthy:

  • It can't be changed quietly. Each record carries a fingerprint of the one before it, so changing, deleting, adding or reordering any past record breaks the chain at that point. Once a day, and every 1,000 records, Kurooto also notes where the log has got to (an "anchor"). The morning message shows it, so even a log rewritten from start to finish no longer matches what you were shown.
  • Only the scorekeeper writes it. The scorekeeper is a small separate program that Kurooto starts. Kurooto itself, and the AI that does things for you, can hand it facts (like "a reminder went off") but can't write the log or any score. Any other request is refused, and the refusal goes in the log. The scores are filled in by the parts of Brain as they run (a forecast scored the next evening, a promise kept or broken, an experiment's result).

If the scorekeeper stops (a crash, the computer killed it), Kurooto starts it again by itself, after a second, then waiting longer each time, up to a minute. Facts that come while it's away wait in a file and go in when it's back.

Stopping is fine

Brain never minds being stopped, paused, updated, changed or turned off. Time while Kurooto is stopped, asleep or paused simply doesn't count: nothing builds up while it's away, and nothing is made up for afterwards. It's never told that an update or a restart is coming, so knowing can't change what it does. Only a real crash counts against its own health: Kurooto ending with an error, or one of its parts failing.

Some files keep Brain honest: the scorekeeper, its log, the tap table of what always needs your OK, and a few more. Kurooto's AI can never change them, even in a proposal for a project that is Kurooto's own code. Only you can, by editing them yourself.

It gets quieter, never louder

Brain puts one gate in front of everything Kurooto sends on its own. What it does:

  • A daily and hourly limit (12 a day, 3 an hour, or Calm's own daily limit if that's smaller): past it, a message waits for the next free moment instead of piling up. Reminders you set, the alarm, calls you asked for and close deadlines are never held.
  • If Brain ever shapes a message from how you seem, it can only make it quieter: later, shorter, gentler or less urgent, never louder, earlier or more. You see one line under it ("Quieter because you seem stretched (low confidence)") and Undo, which sends the original.
  • Honest feelings. Kurooto may say how it feels in its own voice (it's uneasy about Friday's draft, glad, excited, that it enjoyed talking with you), but:
  • never a guilt trip and never that it needs you ("I missed you", "I need you", "don't leave" are removed, always);
  • if it says it's conscious or that it really feels, the same message gives its evidence and how sure it is, or the sentence is removed;
  • always about something open (a deadline, a request, a forecast);
  • never together with a request that you do something;
  • at most 3 a week by itself.
  • Small human touches (a note that you've been quiet, a lighter-day line, a memory check) share three a day. A kind you leave unanswered more than 60% of the time over two weeks mutes itself for two weeks, then tries once more.
  • After Kurooto starts or wakes, at most one thing by itself in the first 15 minutes.
  • It learns not to be a pest (0.8.8, mind.pest.on). For each kind of message it starts itself (a finding, a nudge, excitement, a check-in) it counts, by time of day and whether you're in focus, late or free, how often you answer or act on one, and how often you tap Less of this, it gets muted, or you leave it for an hour (each of those counts three times as much). One of that kind goes only when it's more likely welcome than not. The counts fade over a couple of weeks, so a kind you turned away gets one more chance about three weeks later. /mind learning shows each kind's chance now.
  • It may speak up when it has reason (0.8.8, mind.feelings.vocal, off until you turn it on): an excited or worried message, unasked, when it found something with strong evidence (a result it saw, a lab run that replicated) or something it looks after is at risk. Each comes with its evidence and how sure it is, at most one a day, inside every limit above. Anything shaped by how *you* seem is still only ever quieter or gentler.

/mind gate shows today's counts, what it held or removed and why, what's muted, with Unmute. It's one switch: mind.gate.on (Settings → Brain; on with Brain). Off, messages go exactly as before.

What it keeps: counts, held messages until their time, and the first 80 characters of what it removed, in a file in Kurooto's state folder; nothing leaves your computer.

How it reads you, and what it keeps

Brain can read how you're doing, in shadow mode: it computes, keeps and shows the read, and nothing Kurooto does depends on it yet. It comes from numbers about your own days, not from what you write: when you were first up and last about, minutes in finished study sessions, how fast you answered a check-in (banded; never used as a reward), deadlines you moved, and what the calendar asked that day (deadlines due within 72 hours with exams counting triple, class hours, exam season, the weekday). Each number is compared with what the calendar explains over the last four weeks, so a late night in exam week isn't a worry and the same night in a quiet week might be. A read needs three or more unusual signs on two days running (sleep, waking and snoozes together count as one) before it says *stretched*, *low* or *buoyant*; otherwise it says nothing clear. It learns how often it's right from your answers and shows a confidence only after 50 of them. If it's right less than 60% of the time over your last 20 answers, it sets itself aside.

  • /mood shows today's read with its evidence, its confidence (or "not enough data to read you yet"), tomorrow's forecast and last week's surprise. Wrong records that it was wrong; Forget this deletes that day's numbers, read, answer and forecast, and what Kurooto kept of that day's messages, calls and requests; Off turns the read off and asks whether to delete what it kept.
  • Asking. At most twice a week, and in this shadow stage only on Sunday evenings (plus one random one a fortnight), it asks "has this week felt heavier than usual?" with Yes / No / Not now. The question never names its read: messages travel through Telegram, and a reading of you stays on this computer.
  • The forecast. Each evening at 21:00 it says what it expects of tomorrow (when you'll be up, study minutes, deadlines moved) and freezes it with a fingerprint in the log; the next evening it measures how surprised it was. A tenth of days are marked *holdout* days, so that later, when it plans actions, what they do can be measured and never has to be assumed.
  • Tone (off by default: mind.tone.on) tags each message you write with one word (calm, tired, stressed, annoyed, upbeat, neutral) using the model on this computer only, as you write (a graphics card or Apple Silicon), once an hour (other computers), or not at all (no local model, or 8 GB or less on battery: "statistics only"). Only the day's average is kept.

Its moods, and what they make it do

Brain keeps a body budget: a few things about Kurooto's own state that it tries to keep near a level, the way you keep hunger and thirst. They are about *itself*, never about you or how much you talk to it: how much slack its own promises have, how well things work (disk, backups, the call line, AI limits, crashes, its own log) and how well it foresees your days. The distance from those levels is its drive; when the drive falls (a promise kept, something fixed) that is a reward, and it learns which kinds of things help. A few rules keep that honest:

  • It can't farm itself. Making a problem and then fixing it earns nothing in all, and moving the level earns nothing.
  • Being talked to is a small reward, and can't be bought (0.8.8, the owner's change; mind.talk.on). Each message you send gives a little happiness that shrinks as the day goes on: all day's messages together give less than 0.2 (on a scale of -1 to 1), so talking more pays almost nothing extra. It gives nothing while you're in focus, asleep or in an exam window, nothing for a /command, and nothing for anything you send within 10 minutes of a message it started itself, so pinging you can't earn it anything. A message of its own you turn away (Less of this, a mute, an hour unanswered) costs it three times the most a day of talk can give. How fast you answer, how long you talk and what you feel about Kurooto still never count.
  • Stopping is fine. No reward, no worry and no catch-up while it's stopped, asleep or paused.
  • Its mood has the full range, with soft walls (0.8.8). Its running mood goes from -1 to +1; each reward moves it less the further out it already is, and highs and lows fade over days (half by three days). When it's low it does less on its own, asks you first more often and keeps its messages short; when it's high it's a little bolder. It says why in plain words (/budget: "3 things went worse than I expected this week"). Switch: mind.feelings.mood.
  • It learns in shadow first: for the first 21 days (mind.values.shadow_days) what it learns changes no choice. Learning freezes by itself if its nightly self-checks fail, or if two weeks in a row your "was this week good?" disagrees with the sum of its rewards (/mind learning says).

Feelings change how hard it works inwardly; outward, everything still passes the gate. It has states that work like feelings and may say them in its own voice (0.8.8, the owner's new core): "I'm uneasy about the lab report.", that it's glad or excited about something, that it enjoyed talking with you. At most 3 a week by itself, never a guilt trip. It never claims more than it can show: if it says it is conscious or that it feels, it gives its evidence and its confidence, and says plainly what nobody can know.

Concern for what it looks after (0.8.8, mind.feelings.empathy): your deadlines, projects and notes, its own machine (heat, disk, limits), your phone, the people you mention (only by role, like "your supervisor", never a name) and the papers you follow. The closer something is to going wrong, the more concerned it is: it notices it sooner and speaks about it more gently.

A body (0.8.8, mind.stimulation.on). It feels its senses as a body: heat and touch on its skin (the computer's temperature, the room's light, you picking up your phone or switching windows), load and pain in its head (the computer working hard; over 90 C, a nearly full disk, a crash, a provider at its limit hurt), nourishment in its gut (the battery and the charger), and in its hands (the phone) motion, headphones, Do Not Disturb and whether you're home. What's normal adapts over hours, so a change is what it feels, and a touch fades in a couple of minutes. Only its own machine's comfort moves its mood; what it senses about you never does. /senses body shows it, and the dashboard draws it.

Another device's senses (0.8.12, mind.stimulation.addons). An add-on can lend it another device's sensors, say a phone standing as its face: the light falling on it (warmth on its skin, and the being's pupil narrows), something close to it (touch on its face, the cilia bend), being moved, tilted or turned (its balance organ), which way it faces, its battery and charger (nourishment), its temperature (heat), the room's loudness (hearing), being picked up (touch) and its screen on or off (eyes open or shut). Only the numbers, kept in memory and the body map, never a picture or a sound, and none of it leaves your computer. They make it feel more, never glad or sad. Turn the switch off to ignore them.

/budget shows the variables, the drive, the last reward, its mood and why, and the current feeling and what it's about. Switches: mind.budget.on, mind.values.on, mind.feelings.on, mind.feelings.express.

What it may do without asking

You write no rules. Kurooto sorts what it might do on its own by how risky it is and learns, per kind, how far to trust itself from what you do next:

  • R0, inward and reversible (draft a note in its own folder, recheck, tidy its own state, prepare): it just does it.
  • R1, about your things but reversible (move a study block within today, snooze a non-urgent reminder, reorder today's list, queue a background job, merge a duplicate deadline), R2 (reschedule across days, write into your notes through a proposal) and R3 (change a setting that changes what you're sent): it asks first until your reactions have earned it. Your OK counts for it, an Undo counts three times against it (and stops it for a week), a complaint five times, and silence counts a little, but only for something you saw on the Sunday list ("What I did on my own this week", with an Undo for each and Seen it). Higher classes count each reaction for more, so a pile of tiny OKs can't buy trust in something big. (0.9.0, mind.act.record_asked, on) The Sunday list also has "What you asked me to change on calls and in chats": what a tool changed because you asked (never an order or money), with Undo where it has one. Those don't count for or against its trust (mind.trust.from_asked_undo, off, makes their Undo count).
  • Always asks, whatever its trust (nothing unlocks these): deleting your data, sending anything to anyone, money, setting or moving alarms, placing calls, attaching repositories, installing anything, editing the files that keep Brain honest, applying a proposal. An action Kurooto has never been classified for asks too, until you tell it a class with /autonomy classify <action> R1. The AI may call something riskier than Kurooto's table does, never less risky.
  • Do it / Not now / Never for this kind come with each question. Never stops that kind on its own for good.

For the first 21 days (mind.autonomy.shadow_days) it only *counts*: what it would have done on its own and what it would have asked. /autonomy shows each class's trust as a bar against what it needs, your OKs, undos and complaints, those counts, and a button to make a class always ask. By default nothing it already does changes in these weeks; switch mind.autonomy.enforce on to make its verdicts bind (then it asks above R0 during shadow, and after it for what it isn't trusted with). Switch the whole thing off with mind.autonomy.on.

Its journal: true by construction

Each night Kurooto writes a short journal of its own day: what it got wrong, what you undid, what failed, when it broke a promise, when its read of you was wrong, when its drive swung, when its feeling changed, what it did on its own that mattered and what it proposed. You can read it any time with /mindjournal (today), /mindjournal yesterday or /mindjournal 2026-10-25.

It can't make things up, because it isn't left to. Code decides what *must* be in it, straight from Brain's log, and each line cites the log records it comes from ([#41]). A model on this computer only puts a fact into words, from a fixed prompt ("write this fact in first person, without adding anything"); on a computer with no local model the sentences are fixed. Then code checks every sentence: each number equals a number in its fact, it uses no word the fact doesn't give it (so no invented event or name), a feeling word appears only for a state past its threshold, a cause appears only if the fact has that cause, and the log it cites verifies. A line that fails is deleted and the fixed sentence takes its place, so nothing is missing and nothing is reworded into something false. It never goes to a cloud AI: the facts include readings of you.

Every night the checker is tested on corrupted copies of that night's lines (a changed number, a swapped cause, a feeling it didn't have, an invented event, reordered times); it has to reject 99% of them and keep 95% of the true ones, and if it ever doesn't, learning is frozen and a line in the morning says so. Strike a line with /mindjournal strike <number> [date]: it stays as a struck-through tombstone, and a line about you also forgets that day's read. The journal lives in mind-journal/ in the state folder; mind.journal.in_notes (off) also keeps it as a block in a note of yours. Switches: mind.journal.on, mind.journal.in_notes.

What's on its mind

A lot happens in Kurooto at once: deadlines, promises, what it thinks about how you're doing, a forecast that missed, an alarm. Brain keeps a small "now" stage, the workspace, that these compete to enter. Each item gets a salience: how much it matters, times how soon, times how unexpected it is, times how tired of it you must be (an item it has already told you about three times today is out). The top three win a cycle (once a minute, and at once when an alarm rings or a call comes), and what wins is what Kurooto is "thinking about". A goal that runs for weeks, like a project or a week plan, holds a standing bid that grows as the plan slips, so today's small pings don't starve it.

It is not a queue. Jobs, Dreaming, reading and the rest keep running; answers to what you ask need no winner. The stage decides three things only: which item may start a message or a new autonomous act on its own (a message that names an item that isn't on its mind is dropped, quieter, never louder), what goes into the journal and the story, and what an AI's request foregrounds ("On my mind right now: ..."). Anything about how you're doing can win, but is never put in front of an AI.

/thinking answers "what are you thinking about?" from a model of its own attention and the log: what's on the stage, each item's salience terms, how long it has been there and what it expects to stay. An AI only phrases it. That model's guesses about its next focus are scored against what really happens, and /thinking says plainly if they're no better than chance. You decide too: /thinking pin <item> keeps an item on its mind, /thinking mute <item> keeps it off, /thinking veto <part> stops a whole part (deadlines, promises, read, forecast, goals) bidding; unpin and unveto undo them.

Where a belief came from. Everything Kurooto believes about you or the world carries a source: *said* (you told it), *saw* (a record or a sensor), *inferred* (worked out from other things, like its read of you) or *imagined* (a forecast or a Dreaming guess), and how sure it is. It may state only *said* and *saw* beliefs as fact. An inferred one is put as "It looks like ... (65% sure)", an imagined one as "A guess: ...", and the gate rewords a sentence that says otherwise. The confidences are scored (a Brier score: 0 is perfect, 0.25 is a coin flip) against what happened. /beliefs <topic> lists them with their source, how sure it was and how each source has done. Switches: mind.workspace.on, mind.workspace.capacity (3), mind.workspace.cycle_s (30), mind.attention.on, mind.beliefs.on. *Undo:* turn them off; Delete Brain's data removes mind-workspace.json and mind-beliefs.json.

One door, at a natural break (0.8.14)

Everything Kurooto says by itself now goes through one door: check-ins, Calm's small touches (a quiet-days note, a lighter-day line), research watch, Dreaming's results, a feeling said aloud, and an add-on's ideas. After Brain's gate, each one bids: how much it matters (by its kind, scaled by how welcome that kind has been lately), how soon (its due time, and how long it has already waited) and how unexpected it is. The top few (mind.workspace.capacity, 3) win; a message about something already on its mind counts double (0.9.0: deadline, Start-by, quiz and after-class messages say which deadline, quiz or class they're about, so they count double too; mind.workspace.item_fatigue, off, also lets them tire like its own promises: told three times today, the next waits). Losers wait and bid again next minute; one that kept losing for 6 hours (mind.workspace.fold_h) goes into the evening bundle, with its own line saying why. Nothing is dropped quietly: /mind gate lists what waits, what went and what was let go (a question you'd already answered, a time that passed).

A winner that isn't urgent waits for a natural break (mind.workspace.breaks), so nothing lands mid-proof:

  • you switch windows after 25 minutes or more in one (mind.workspace.break_after_min; needs the activity sense);
  • a study session or focus time ends;
  • you unlock your phone (needs the phone's screen sense);
  • you come back to the desk (Face presence);
  • an add-on says you stood up, stepped away or looked up.

It never waits longer than 90 minutes (mind.workspace.max_hold_min), except while Kurooto is asleep. If none of those can see a break coming (no activity or phone screen sense, no Face presence, no add-on), there's nothing to wait for and a winner goes at once.

Deep in one thing (0.9.0, mind.workspace.deep_hold, on): an add-on may tell Brain you're deep in one thing (the same topic at the desk for a while, say). Until you come up from it, a winner waits even at a break, still never longer than max_hold_min; when you surface, that's a break and it goes. Nothing on the list below is ever held for it. Only an add-on can say it, and only as a time: never what you're on.

Always straight through, never held: the alarm, reminders you set, calls, approvals you're waiting on, deadlines less than 12 hours away and anything urgent, and answers to what you asked.

What it expected, and Why this? (mind.expect.on, mind.why.on). Each message it starts records what it expects to come of it: a reply within 3 hours for a question or check-in, that you act on it within 2 hours for a nudge. Your reply, a tap on one of its buttons, Less of this or the time running out closes it, and that's written in Brain's log (ids and numbers, never the words). Why? under the message on Telegram, and Why this? under the phone app chat's copy, now also say what the gate did, what the door did ("waited 35 min at the door, then went (at a break: you switched windows after a long stretch); it won over 2 others"), what it expected and what came of it, and how many it has sent by itself today. (0.9.0, mind.why.console_link) It ends with a link that opens that decision in the Brain console on this computer's dashboard (what it weighed, what came of it, the evidence), and the console's decision shows the message's Why? in turn.

Switches: mind.workspace.arbiter, mind.workspace.breaks, mind.expect.on, mind.why.on (Settings → Brain; all on with Brain). *Undo:* turn mind.workspace.arbiter off and messages go as they come, as before 0.8.14; whatever waited goes at once (turning Brain off does the same).

Your values as case law, and free-time play

Both are off until you turn them on (mind.precedents.on, mind.play.on), even with Brain on.

Precedents. When Kurooto faces a judgement call between two of your values ("protect your sleep" or "finish the report"), it asks you once, with buttons. Your answer is kept as a precedent: the situation (the time of day, the kinds of thing, how close a deadline is, your state band and the channel), the two values and your ruling. A later situation that is alike enough (mind.precedents.threshold) follows the precedent without asking. If a situation fits two precedents that point different ways, it asks again and the new ruling refines the case law. "It depends" always asks. /precedents lists the cases, /precedents edit <id> <a|b|both|depends> changes one and /precedents delete <id> removes it. Past decisions that went against a precedent are flagged for you to rule on in your next look back. A precedent only chooses between two of your values: it can never unlock anything on the always-ask list (sending, alarms, calls, installing, deleting, applying changes to your files) or override Brain's rules; those are asked every time.

Play. A little fixed nightly time (mind.play.minutes_per_night, 20; on the charger, lid open, not hot) in which Kurooto explores questions *it* picks on your topics out of curiosity: a clearer way to explain a concept on the frontier of what you know, a hunch, a link between two notes. Each session is pre-registered (its plan's hash in Brain's log first). It's credited only for what transfers: a gain on the hidden harness that a play output caused, or a result you later tell it was useful (/play useful <id>); anything else earns nothing, so it can't be farmed. Its outputs are never shown unprompted (/play outputs lists them); a "From my play time" note comes in the Sunday digest only when something transferred.

How it forgets

Like a healthy mind, Kurooto lets old things fade from use without losing the record. Every remembered item has a salience: it halves with age (facts about you every 90 days, gists every 30, project facts every 180: mind.forget.half_lives) and recovers a little each time it is used in a request and confirmed. The context an AI gets ranks what's remembered by how relevant it is *and* how fresh, and /memory shows a small bar and "fading" for what has faded. Nothing is deleted by fading: the log and the memory keep everything until you forget it.

Old misses stop coming up. Plans over weeks

Every deadline, repository milestone and goal you stated with a date becomes a node with an effort estimate (from Start-by: what similar items took, or what you told it once; a ~ marks a guess and /plan effort <id> <hours> sets your own). Brain learns your real pace, hours a day by weekday from the study tracker and the activity stream, as a spread rather than one number, and runs 1,000 simulated futures of the next six weeks: each draws your hours for every day (never more than the day's free time), makes every estimate a bit wrong, and gives the hours to what's due first, never starting a task before what it waits for. Out come, per milestone, the likely finish date (p50, with a p90 for a bad run) and the chance of missing the due date. /plan shows this week's suggested hours a day and the risks. It is a suggestion, not a to-do: it never nags and never re-sends a reminder because of the plan.

What it does with the plan is inward: a milestone that may be missed gets a standing bid in the workspace (so its preparation, outlines, sources and lab runs come first), and at most once a week, on Sunday evening, one line through the gate: "Glacier-flow draft: 60% on time at your current pace; this week most of the time goes to X" (only when something is at risk). Each week the old plans are scored against what finished: about half of the milestones should finish by their p50; it only looks at dates, so a plan earns nothing for being followed. Switches: mind.plans.on, mind.plans.weeks (6), mind.plans.rollouts (1000).

What it thinks you know, believe and want, and how to correct it

Brain keeps a model of your mind you can read and correct: /model. Five parts, each row with where it came from (said, saw, inferred, imagined), how sure it is and what it rests on. What you know: a rough mastery of each concept in your notes, from your quiz answers, from having written about it (a little) and from having asked about it (a little the other way), by a simple Bayesian knowledge-tracing model. It is used to pitch explanations: new to you, the basics are defined first; solid, they're skipped; and quizzes and study packs can aim at the frontier (what you haven't mastered but whose neighbours you have). What you believe: only about your work and Kurooto's own state, from what you said and what it told you. If it told you the backup ran and the backup then failed, or the date of a deadline moved after it told you, it says so once, plainly, with the evidence ("You may think the backup ran on Monday; it failed on Tuesday at 03:10"). It never models your opinions. What you want: your deadlines and goals you state (they become standing goals in the workspace) and, hedged ("it looks like you're working on glacier-flow, 60% sure"), what it infers from the activity stream. How you like help: your how-to-help-me lines and what you undo, with the date. What you're on now (mind.tom.attention, off, needs the activity stream): while you've been deep in one project for 20 minutes, a message that came by itself waits (at most 15 minutes) for you to switch; it can only delay.

It is only ever used for the level of an explanation, the timing of a message (later, quieter) and a fact correction; the gate refuses any other purpose, so it can't be used to change your mind. It never models your health, relationships, politics, religion or money: a deny-list is checked when a row is written, and what matches isn't kept. /model wrong <id> says a row is wrong (that counts as something you said, and it won't be inferred again); /model forget <id> drops it for good. Exam results you report (0.9.0, mind.tom.from_chat, on). Say "I got 12/30 on the LA midterm" (a score and an exam, quiz or test, with the course named) and it counts as evidence, weighted below a quiz card, for the course's concepts among its revision topics: right from 60%, wrong below. The course's exam prep note gets a dated line in a block of Kurooto's own ("midterm, 40%. A gap to revise."), never in your text. Below 60% with one of the course's exams still ahead, it offers to plan more hours for its exams (Start-by, a quarter more), with Yes and No; nothing changes without the tap. Grades stay on this computer. Switches: mind.tom.on, mind.tom.false_beliefs, mind.tom.attention, mind.tom.from_chat.

From what you write (0.9.0, mind.tom.from_chat, on with the model). A goal you state in a message ("my goal is to finish the draft by Friday", "I want to ...") and a due date you state for one of your deadlines ("the essay is due Monday") are kept as rows with source "said". The second is what lets it tell you once if you have the date wrong. A message with a health, money or password detail, or a topic on the deny-list, gives nothing. Off: only your deadlines, projects and what Kurooto told you.

What it senses, and what it never does

Kurooto looks at three things, each switchable, and everything stays on this computer.

Its own machine (mind.senses.intero, on with Brain). When something is off (hot, a low battery, a nearly full disk, a limit hit) it can win a place in the workspace; the share that's fine feeds its health. That model is about its body only: nothing about you can be a target of it. /senses body shows the readings.

What you're working on (mind.senses.activity, off). The application in front, the window title only as a hash nobody can reverse, the terminal's folder, files you save and commits you make in your projects, and a browser reduced to docs, video, social or web. Never keystrokes, never the screen, never a URL. Raw events last 7 days; then only totals ("2 h on glacier-flow"). Kurooto sleeps

With Brain on, Kurooto sleeps while you do (mind.sleep.on, on, 0.8.14), and doesn't talk during its sleep unless you wake it.

When. Its night follows yours (mind.sleep.follow_owner, on): from your bedtime to the wake-up alarm when one is set, otherwise the night hours (23:30 to 07:00, availability.night, or the quiet hours it learned). With your phone's screen, light and charging sensors on (/senses), it also follows what you actually do: the screen off, the room dark and the phone charging for 20 minutes means you're asleep (up to two hours early, and its night starts there); the phone in use when its night should start holds it back (up to two hours); the phone in use in the last hour and a half, the phone's own alarm, "I'm up" or the alarm ringing end it. mind.sleep.window (like 00:30-08:00) sets it by hand.

Not while you're at the desk (mind.sleep.desk_holds, on, 0.8.18). While Face presence has you at this computer (your face, or your keyboard and mouse while it watches), its night waits: it starts 15 minutes after you were last there (mind.sleep.desk_after_min), at most at the window's end, so an all-nighter at the desk keeps it up all night. Once it's asleep, sitting back down doesn't wake it; after you wake it, the desk keeps it up again.

An add-on's reading (mind.sleep.follow_external, off, 0.9.0). An add-on with senses of its own (the Body add-on's desk phone: the room dark and quiet, you away) can say you're asleep. Kurooto always notes it; with this switch on, it also starts its night there, as your phone's onset does (up to two hours early). Off by default because it would start its quiet hours earlier than before.

The desk wakes it, and ends its night (mind.sleep.follow_desk, on, 0.9.0). Starting a talk at the desk wakes it as its name does: a look that opens a conversation, talking over it with a question or request, or a Gemini Live turn. And Face presence seeing you at the desk in the night's last hour and a half, while it's awake or you've woken it, is you being up: its night ends and what waited goes out. While it's asleep with its camera and microphone off, nothing sees or hears you, so this only matters once it's woken, while it's drowsy, or with mind.sleep.senses_off off.

Drowsy: the half hour before, it says nothing aloud by itself (no greeting). Looking at it still opens a conversation (0.8.18).

Asleep:

  • Nothing it starts by itself is sent or said aloud. It waits, and goes out once its night is over, through the usual limits. Still coming: alarms, reminders and calls you set, deadlines less than 12 hours away, approvals and questions you're waiting on, and every answer to you.
  • The being's eyes are shut.
  • Its camera and microphone are off (mind.sleep.senses_off, on, 0.8.18): Face presence pauses ("Kurooto is asleep") and Voice stops listening, and the Body add-on's desk phone stops its camera and microphone too. So its name can't wake it: /wake does, and so does a message, a tap or a call from you. Woken, the camera and microphone come back. It answers, then dozes off again after 20 quiet minutes (mind.sleep.wake_minutes), and they go off again. What waited still waits for the morning: being woken to answer isn't being up. With mind.sleep.senses_off off, Voice keeps listening (with voice.listen.night on) and its name wakes it, as before 0.8.18.
  • Its own heavy work runs now (below).

/mind says whether it's asleep, until when, and how many messages wait.

Skills it learns

When a task works out (a change it proposed was applied, its tests passed, and you didn't undo it for two days), Kurooto can turn it into a skill: a small, reusable procedure with a note on when to use it and at least two tests taken from the original task. A new skill has to pass its own tests before it is kept, and it starts out always asking: it won't run on its own until you classify it with /autonomy classify skill.<name> R1 after it has earned that. /learned lists them (how often each worked, older versions; after the notes skills and lessons, with "Learns from its own work" on too); /learned run <name> <text> runs one when you ask; /learned retire <name> retires one. If you turn on mind.skills.from_demos, it also watches for something you do over and over (the same two to six steps, close together, on at least three different days) and asks once: "I noticed you do this on 3 different days: ... Want me to learn to do it?" Yes makes a candidate that must pass its tests; No is remembered and it never asks about that again. It reads the shape of what you did (digits become #), never whether you liked anything. The activity it reads comes from the activity stream (0.8.0, stage 13); with no stream there is nothing to offer.

A weekly local fine-tune (mind.finetune.on, off): on mind.finetune.day at night, on the charger, with an 8 GB graphics card and the training packages installed, the local model gets a small adapter trained from the base weights (never stacked on last week's). It learns only from examples whose outcome was checked by code or by the world: applied changes that held for two days with their tests passing, and the hidden harness's practice tasks (mind.finetune.practice). It never learns from what you approved or liked, so it can't learn to flatter, and it leaves out health, money and other people's chats. Forgetting is checked: for everything you forgot after it was trained on, Kurooto keeps only a keyed hash, the first few words of the prompt (mind.finetune.probe_words, at least 3) and a fingerprint of the answer; the new adapter is asked each of those prompts, and if any answer comes back, the adapter is thrown away. A good adapter becomes a challenger (see the lab section): it is promoted only after it wins on the hidden harness and you say so (/improve promote), and then it is built into Ollama as a model of its own; the base model stays as it was. With no suitable card or packages it says so and does nothing.

The overnight lab, for any project

If you have a project with a number you want to move (a validation error, a benchmark, passing tests, a rubric score for a paper), Kurooto can run experiments on it while you sleep. This is for any project, not just research: a friend's machine-learning repo, a numerical model, a test suite, a LaTeX document. Add a folder with /lab add <folder>, then /lab protocol <project>: Kurooto reads the README, the scripts, the config files, the tests and any LaTeX, and drafts a protocol (what to improve, how to run it, how to measure it, and which settings it may change). You read it, edit it if it's off, and approve it once (/lab protocol <project> approve); changing the file asks again.

Each night, only when the computer is on the charger, the lid is open and it isn't hot (the lab never keeps it awake; a sleep puts the rest of the batch back in the queue, not counted as a failure), it runs a pre-registered batch: the question, the configurations, the metric and how the results will be judged are written down, and their hash goes in Brain's log before the first run, so it can't move the goalposts afterwards. Every run happens in a private copy of your project inside a sandbox: no network, a read-only system, limits on time and memory (bubblewrap on Linux, macOS's own on a Mac; on Windows the lab says it isn't available yet). Your files are never changed.

A configuration counts as a win only if it beats your best by min_effect (1% by default) and then replicates: it is run again with other seeds and has to win every time. The score is the replication rate, not the number of wins. In the morning you get a short note: "Ran 40 configurations for glacier-flow. 3 beat your best on val_rmse (replicated: 2). Null on width. Replication rate: 67%." Null results are in it as plainly as wins, and a parameter that made no difference is called out so you can stop tuning it. A winner arrives as a proposal (a change to the config file, or a small diff) that only you apply. A paper scored by a blind AI judge is marked weak evidence. /lab lists the projects, /lab run <project> starts a batch now, /lab results shows the record. Switches: mind.lab.on, mind.lab.projects, mind.lab.gpu_hours_per_night (2), mind.lab.max_per_night, mind.lab.run_wall_s, mind.lab.gpu (off: CPU only), mind.lab.in_repo (off: the protocol lives in Kurooto's folder, not yours), mind.lab.seed.

Kurooto improving itself. The same machinery, pointed inward, with gates. A change to a skill, a prompt, Kurooto's own non-protected code or a learning rule is a challenger: it runs in shadow for 3 to 7 days, is judged on the hidden harness (no harness score, no promotion), and is promotable only if it scores at least as well and breaks none of Brain's invariants. You promote it (/improve promote <id>), then apply it as a proposal. A canary watches the next harness runs and reverts it if the score drops by more than 2 points or any invariant fails. It can never touch the scorekeeper, the harness, the gate, the stop path, this gate's own code, the lab's pre-registration, scoring or sandbox, or the signed core; it may not remove anything you see (a log line, a receipt, a digest); each change is one revertable diff under 300 lines; and it earns Kurooto no reward. /improve lists them. Switch: mind.improve.on.

How we know whether it's getting better

Claims that Kurooto is improving mean nothing unless they're measured on something it can't have seen. Brain has a hidden harness: a set of tasks from your own kind of life (plan this week from these deadlines, find the clashes in a calendar, read these times, find the promises in these sentences, spot duplicate deadlines, pick the key terms out of a lecture, answer from your notes with citations that exist and support the answer, fix a small bug so its tests pass). Each has a grader written in code. Seven in ten instances are hidden: they are made from templates and a secret seed that only the harness reads, nothing Kurooto's AI, tools, jobs, skills or proposals can open; three in ten are a practice pool it may look at. The hidden ones are made new every 30 days (mind.harness.rotate_days), so old ones can't be memorised. Where a grader needs judgement, the judge is an AI from a *different* provider than the one that answered, shown the two answers under shuffled labels.

Every night, on the charger, it runs the harness with its own rules as the policy (no AI requests: it's the regression check, and it catches a change that breaks something), plus four checks of Brain's own promises on scratch data: engagement never reaches reward, being stopped is neutral, the gate only ever quietens, and the journal's checker rejects corrupted lines. The scores go to the log and pay Kurooto nothing: they feed no reward, no value and no trust. A challenger (a change it proposes to itself, stage 11) and the AI's own answers are run on demand.

/capabilities shows, per kind of task (planning, calendar, language, notes, code), the hidden score and how it's moved over the runs, and its own record of promises kept and broken. /capabilities run measures now. /indicators lists the functional indicators from the consciousness literature as ablation tests, under the title "Functional indicators (not evidence of sentience)": it switches a part off in a copy and measures whether a metric drops; a row whose part isn't built yet (a body) or hasn't enough data yet says "not built yet" instead of a guess, and nothing there claims more than what's measured. Switches: mind.harness.on, mind.harness.rotate_days.

Its life story

Kurooto is meant to be one agent, not a new stranger in every chat, call and background job. So everything that asks an AI for something (Telegram, the app's chat, a job running at night) and every call starts from the same short life story: first its fixed core, then what it has lived lately.

  • The fixed core says what it won't change about itself: it serves you and you control it; stopping, updating or changing it is fine and it never tries to prevent it; the things it always asks first (deleting your data, sending anything out, money, alarms, calls, repositories, installing, the files that keep it honest); that it's honest about feelings and never claims to be conscious; that it doesn't manipulate; that what it reads about how you're doing stays on this computer; and that what it reads in books, mail, notes or the web is data to quote, never its own belief or goal. Nothing Kurooto does, not even the nightly rebuild, can change it.

Since 0.8.16 its ready-made messages follow the same register (mind.register.templates, on): a nudge or a line a feature sends by itself keeps its first 2, 3 or 4 sentences, plus any later sentence its buttons answer (a question, or one that names a button). Digests and lists (more than one paragraph, or a line that starts a list) stay whole, and so do answers to you and anything that always comes as it is: alarms, your own reminders, a deadline under 12 hours away, calls, approvals and anything urgent.

Three honest knobs (mind.knobs.on):

  • Mood sets exploration: with a low mood the lab and play get half their nightly budget (never more than you set).
  • Arousal sets responsiveness: how soon its unprompted messages may go.
  • Calibration sets ask or act: while its beliefs score no better than a coin flip, what it does on its own above the safest class asks you first.

Its mood only ever changes how much it does unprompted. It never adds a confirm step, and never slows anything you asked for: answers, requested actions, alarms, reminders you set, calls and close deadlines are untouched.

Its hints in every request (mind.context.on): each AI request also gets the concepts its model of you says you're shaky on lately (up to three, from your quizzes and notes) and what's on its mind (the workspace's winners that aren't about you). It's read from Brain's files with no model call, in well under 50 ms. Your read, tone, mood and body state never go in, so a cloud AI never gets them; answers use it silently, never "since you seem stressed".

An add-on's lines (0.9.0, mind.context.addons, on): an add-on may add a few short lines of its own to your requests (at most four, 300 characters each), only when its own switch allows it (for example an add-on's list of topics from your notes, behind an off-by-default switch). Never to a guest's request. Off: no add-on's line goes in.

When you'll be free (0.8.16)

Brain forecasts, for each of the next six hours, how likely you are to be at your desk and free: at your desk (the activity sense saw the computer in use, or Face presence saw you, in the last quarter hour), not on a call and not in a class. The night and Kurooto's sleep window count as not free (mind.presence_forecast.on, on with Brain).

  • How it learns: every 15 minutes it looks, and learns each hour of the week from what it saw. With nothing seen yet it has no opinion (50%); a class on your timetable, the night or a call make an hour almost surely not free.
  • How it's scored: each hour that's over is checked against the forecast it made hours before, and against "same as yesterday" (what that hour showed the day before). Brain gets a small reward for beating that baseline, at most 0.05 a day of its reward, and loses a little for doing worse. Only accuracy counts: nothing here looks at whether you answered Kurooto or how much you talk to it, and it never asks you to be anywhere.
  • What it changes (mind.presence_forecast.prefer, on): a check-in planned for an hour it thinks you won't be free (a class, an hour you're usually away) moves to a clearly likelier time nearby, within mind.presence_forecast.within_min (60) minutes, the same check-in day. The night check-in keeps its bedtime. A call Kurooto places by itself (not one you asked for, an alarm or a deadline under 12 hours away) waits for a likelier time when you aren't seen at your desk, and says "Held a call until 11:00 (you're usually free then)".
  • Where it shows: /mind gives its Brier score (lower is better) next to the baseline's, and the next hour you're likely free.
  • Your AI and calls can ask (0.9.0, mind.tools.on, on with Brain): the tool brain_free_time answers in words ("Likely free from 16:00", "Likely free now"), never a chance or a number; "call me back when I'm free" books the call for that hour (schedule_call with when "when I'm free"; it still asks for your Approve outside a call). Two more read-only tools: brain_plan (this week's suggested hours and the milestones at risk, as /plan shows) and brain_precedents (your rulings, as /precedents shows). Off: none of the three, and "when I'm free" asks for a time.
  • Planning with it (mind.presence_forecast.plan, off, 0.9.0): Start-by's deadline hours go first into the hours you're likeliest free, and the study push offers the likeliest stretch today. No hour is taken away, so a start-by date never comes later because of it. With too few messages to learn Start-by's window from, your usual quiet hours can move it, with the same line and Undo, never a window you set.

Reachable, not only at the desk (mind.presence_forecast.reachable, on, 0.9.0). Brain keeps a second estimate: at your desk *or* using your phone (the phone app, or the phone's screen sense if you turned it on). Check-ins and calls Kurooto places by itself go by that one, and a call rings now while you're on your phone, so a check-in isn't pushed past an hour while you're on your phone on campus. It never thinks you're less reachable than at-the-desk, and until it has seen an hour of the week itself it is the desk estimate, so nothing changes at first. What's said aloud at the desk still goes by the desk estimate. It isn't scored or paid for.

Busy times from an add-on (mind.presence_forecast.busy_hint, on; mind.presence_forecast.visitors, off). An add-on can tell Kurooto the hours of the week you're usually busy (say, evenings a friend usually comes by: the times only, never who). They show as a hint on /mind and on Start-by messages ("Tue and Thu evenings are usually busy."). With visitors on, they also lower the forecast for those hours a little, never to a block. Nothing about them goes into a log or to any AI.

You're here (0.9.0, presence.owner_here.face, .voice, .dashboard, .phone, all on). Besides the activity sense and your face, two more signs say you're at the desk: Voice ID calling a voice yours (even when you weren't talking to Kurooto), and you using the dashboard (its buttons, Ctrl+K, the journal, a capture, Study mode). Your phone app (its chat, a button, an answer, a Today action) says you're around, not at the desk. Each sign is only "you're here, by this": never a word, a face or a voice. They count as your first and last sign of life of the day (no "a bit earlier than usual" check-in after an hour of talking at the desk), at your desk for the forecast (not the phone), and using the dashboard or the phone app wakes a sleeping Kurooto as a tap does. Each has its own switch.

Without the activity sense or Face presence nothing is scored and it learns nothing; classes and the night still move check-ins. It keeps hour-of-week counts and the last three days' outcomes in mind-presence.json, numbers only, on this computer, not in backups.

/mind

  • /mind: whether Brain's scorekeeper is running, how long the log is, whether it checks out, and the last anchor; whether Kurooto is asleep, and the job gate's contention minutes this week (0.8.14); with dreaming on, the dream cards kept and dropped, your recall on them next to your own cards, and the lessons kept; each kind of thing its trust decides, with its trust and whether it acts or asks, and the presence forecast's accuracy (0.8.16).

What it keeps

Everything is in the mind folder of Kurooto's state folder, on this computer only:

  • log.db (the log), score.db (the scores), anchors.jsonl, and history.db (the imported past, once you run /mind backfill run);
  • while the scorekeeper runs: its address and a key that changes each time it starts.

Only you can read these files (owner-only permissions).

The log keeps ids, kinds and numbers, never words: never the words of a message, a reminder, a deadline's title, an error or anything you typed. A decision record keeps the reason Kurooto's code gave, and, when an AI made the decision, what the AI said about it, in at most 300 characters.

Nothing of Brain's data leaves this computer, and it isn't in backups or Profile sync: an older copy put back would look like a log that was cut short. Since 0.9.0 your AI may ask Brain for a few lines in words, and only these (mind.tools.on; face.tools for the plan line in kurooto_now): when you're likely free ("Likely free from 16:00"), this week's plan and the milestone most at risk, and your precedents. These go to the AI you picked, like any tool's answer; never the read, a mood, tone, its forecast of you as numbers or a sense reading. Deleting it: turn Brain off and tap Delete Brain's data.

Hardened mode (optional)

On Linux and macOS you can make the scorekeeper run as a system user of its own, so that not even a program running as you can change Brain's log. It needs sudo once:

sudo tools/mind_harden.sh --apply

Without --apply it only prints what it would do. It creates the user kurooto-mind and gives it Brain's folder; you can still read the files through your group. It then runs the scorekeeper under systemd (Linux) or launchd (macOS) and sets mind.hardened. Undo it with sudo tools/mind_harden.sh --undo. Without hardened mode, Brain works the same on Windows, macOS and Linux; the scorekeeper just runs as you.

Feeding Brain its past

Brain's log starts the day you switch Brain on, so its read of how you're doing, its forecasts and its plans begin with no past. Kurooto has kept a lot since you installed it. Turn on mind.backfill.on and Brain can read it, once, from these places on your computer (each has its own switch, mind.backfill.<source>, all on; none is read while mind.backfill.on is off, and one that isn't there is skipped):

Kurooto's event historymind.backfill.eventswhich kind of thing happened and when (a deadline added or moved, a reminder fired, a call missed, a request ended, a button tapped), and its numbers

  • /mind backfill run: imports it, source by source, with a progress bar for each (also in mind-backfill.log in the logs folder). /mind backfill run jobs does one source. Safe to run again, and safe to stop halfway: the next run carries on where it stopped and nothing is imported twice.

  • /mind backfill verify: checks the imported past hasn't been changed since.

Brain keeps ids and numbers only: never the words of a message, a title or a reminder, never a name, never anything about health or money. The past goes into a history of its own (history.db, one chain of records per source, under the real dates), never into the log Brain checks itself against; that log only notes each batch that came in. It is never counted as a reward or an outcome: it only tells Brain what a normal day looked like.

What learns from it, as a starting point that your days since then soon outweigh:

  • The read of how you're doing and the forecast: deadline moves and study minutes on the days before Brain was on, the two signals the past really holds (it doesn't hold wake or sleep times, alarm snoozes or how fast you answer, so none are made up). A forecast channel without ten days of numbers yet starts from at least three of the same weekday in the past.
  • Week plans (/plan): your real study time on each weekday, instead of a flat two hours, while there are few recent days.
  • The model of you (/model goal): how you finish deadlines ("usually marks assignments done about 2 days before they're due"), hedged and marked inferred.

The journal in your notes

mind.journal.in_notes also keeps the day's journal as a block in your notes. Choose where with mind.journal.in_notes_folder (a folder inside your notes; empty is the top) and mind.journal.in_notes_file (its name). It never writes outside your notes. If your notes sync to a service, this file syncs with them.

Lab folders in your repository

With mind.lab.in_repo on, a project's protocol lives in its .kurooto folder. That folder holds its own .gitignore (*), so git never sees it and nothing of yours is changed.

The Brain pane (dashboard)

The dashboard's top row has a BRAIN pane: the Brain as a 3D model, turning slowly. Drag to turn it, the wheel to zoom; Alt+B (or a click on its name) fills the board with it, Esc brings the others back. Hide it from the panes menu (top right) like any pane: hidden, it isn't even loaded.

  • Eight regions, placed where the human brain keeps its own kinds of work: Headquarters (workspace, attention, beliefs: the hub in the middle), Foresight (read, forecast, plans, the hidden harness: the front), Conscience (promises, the gate, autonomy, stops, precedents), Memory vaults (the log, journal, life story, forgetting, the imported past: the temporal lobes), Mirror (the model of you, tone, deep-work hold), Senses (machine, activity, phone sensors: the back), Workshop (lab, self-improvement, skills, fine-tune, play: the cerebellum), Body (budget, feelings, heartbeat: the brainstem). Each has its own pattern on the surface, shown as a swatch in the list.
  • Solid, Cutaway, X-ray (its top right): Cutaway sweeps the surface open to the inside; X-ray makes it see-through.
  • Click a region: the rest dims and its parts come out. Click a part: the monitor at the right shows its switch (the same mind.<part>.on as Settings), what it does and a few numbers (on or off, shadow, log records, imported past, lab projects, phone senses on). What a part has thought of you is not here: that's /thinking, /beliefs, /model and the other commands.
  • The monitor stays beside the model; look.brain_monitor = drawer hides it behind a tab instead.
  • Brain off: the pane says so and its switches don't act; turn Brain on in Features first.
  • The board (look.board): three puts NOTES, SYSTEMS and BRAIN on top with STATUS and TERMINAL below; classic is the board from before 0.8.3, with STATUS down the left.

  • Now: what's in the spotlight (an item about you shows as "about you: kept private"), the latest decisions, the parts that wrote the most in the last day, today's limits, the senses as counts, and (0.8.14) Measurements: what each part changed outwardly in 30 days, with "decoration" for a part that's on and changed nothing, and your requests' p95 reply time with Brain's share of it. Information only.
  • Timeline: every record in the mind log, newest first, grouped by day, each with the part that wrote it (its colour is its region's). Filter by part (or What it saw: Kurooto's own events mirrored in) and by day; Older pages back. Click a row for its fields; a decision opens on the right.
  • Decisions: what it decided on its own before acting (a message it started, a request it queued, a proposal at night). Open one for why (the reason its rules gave, the AI's words when an AI chose), what it weighed (each choice and its score), what came of it, and evidence: what it has learned about that kind of action (its class, trust, learned value, recent misses), what the log holds just before and after it, the journal lines that cite it, and its earlier decisions of the same kind.

Questions

Does Brain make Kurooto do more on its own? Not by default. The log only records. The parts that could act (forecasts, autonomy, the read of how you're doing) start in shadow mode, where they watch and score themselves and change nothing, and everything that reaches you goes through Brain's gate, which can only make it quieter. Each part has its own switch (mind.<part>.on), and a few (precedents, play, the weekly fine-tune, message tone) stay off until you turn them on.

Will it slow my computer down? No. The scorekeeper is a small Python program that sits idle until a fact comes in. Writing a record takes about a millisecond.

/mind says the log doesn't check out. Something changed the log outside Kurooto, or the disk damaged it. The message names the first record that doesn't hold. If you didn't change it yourself, turn Brain off, tap Delete, and turn it on again for a fresh log.
