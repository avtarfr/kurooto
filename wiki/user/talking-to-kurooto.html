Talking to Kurooto

You talk to Kurooto through your own Telegram bot: plain sentences, /commands, voice messages, photos and files. Quick requests ("remind me at 9 to call mum") are done at once on your computer; anything else goes to your AI, which answers as a reply. The same requests work in the dashboard's Today page and Ctrl+K box (dashboard.md), on a phone call (calls.md), in the phone app's chat (phone-app.md), and in a terminal (kurooto ask). Telegram is optional: the phone app's chat can take its place entirely (phone-app.md).

Your bot

What it does. Your own bot, made with @BotFather during setup (getting-started.md). It serves only the Telegram account that tapped the one-time pairing link, and only in a private chat; messages from anyone else, and from groups, are ignored.

The "/" menu. Typing / in the chat lists /help, the commands of the features you have on, /q, /jobs, /aboutme, /calm, /why (and /sync when Notes sync is off), then your skills. A skill with a dash in its name shows with _: /term_setup runs term-setup. Telegram shows at most 100. Kurooto updates the menu within a minute of a feature going on or off, and looks for new skills every 15 minutes.

/help (and /start) is an index of everything that works right now, one line per group (Talking, Reminders and deadlines, Classes and calendar, Study, Quiz and exams, Calls and phone, Check-ins and journal, Notes, Skills and routines, Research, Google, Teaching Kurooto, Privacy and memory, Settings and upkeep): its commands (of the features you have on and Kurooto's own), how many things you can just say, and its page in this wiki; then the features that are off. /help <group> shows one group in full (/help calls, /help reminders): what each command does, what you can just say in plain words, and its features that are off; /help all sends every group, one message each. /help <word> searches everything, on or off: /help call shows Phone calls, its commands and how to turn it on. If Chat is off it says free text isn't sent to an AI. "What you can just say" covers each feature's requests and the ones Kurooto understands by itself: "did I finish that thing?" (Talking), "when did I last talk to Prof Ortiz?" (Privacy and memory), "sync everything" (Notes), "I got a project under Prof Ortiz" (Teaching Kurooto) and "about the meeting with Prof Ortiz: ..." (Research, with Chat on). The same /help answers in the phone app's chat, where its wiki links open with a tap. The full list is in commands.md.

While Kurooto is off or asleep. Telegram keeps your messages for about a day; Kurooto handles them when it's back. Telegram doesn't deliver Kurooto's messages while the computer sleeps; with Phone calls set up, your call line can say the computer went offline, and with Reminders with the lid shut your Android phone still gets reminders (calls.md).

What leaves your computer: your messages travel through Telegram's servers to your bot, and Kurooto's messages back the same way.

What happens to a message

Kurooto looks at each message in this order and stops at the first match:

  1. A reply to one of its messages. Replying to a question answers it; replying to a check-in writes your journal; replying to an after-class question, a quest, an "Overdue?" question and so on goes to the feature that asked.
  2. A /command. /journal followed by a new line and text works too. A command of a feature that's off gets "X is off: Features → X → Turn on." (it never goes to the AI). A skill typed while Skills is off is answered the same way.
  3. A skill (/journal, /study-log, your own), when Skills is on. See skills-and-routines.md.
  4. Files and photos (see below).
  5. A call that went on as text on Telegram: what you type is part of it until it ends. A request to decide your health, relationships or money ("should I stop my meds?", "should I break up with him?", "should I put my savings in crypto?") gets a short answer at once, without the AI: it's yours to decide, and who to ask (see Boundaries it keeps).
  6. A plain-English request Kurooto understands instantly ("remind me in 20 minutes to take the pasta off", "what's due?", "no calls for 2 hours"). These run on your computer with no AI. Long messages (over 300 characters), and messages with several requests in one ("..., also ...", "... and then ...", two sentences), always go to the AI so it reads the whole thing. A question ("what...?", "when...?") only matches requests that answer questions. A phrasing you taught with the Wrong button goes first (teaching-kurooto.md). Questions about what's remembered come before the features' requests: "did I finish that thing?", "when did I last talk to Prof Ortiz?", "what was I doing last Thursday?" (below). Two close readings. When no instant request takes your words but they're close to two of them (by their example phrasings), it asks instead of guessing: 'Did you mean "what's due this week" or "what's on today"?' with both as buttons and Neither (which sends it on as before). Only when the two are about equally close; otherwise nothing changes. Pick the same one twice for the same words and it learns them (teaching-kurooto.md). On Telegram and the phone app; repair.on off turns it off. A request a feature that's off would understand ("remind me in 5 min to stretch" with Reminders off) gets "That's for Reminders, which is off. Turn it on and do it?" with Turn on (it turns on that feature, and what it needs, then does the request) and Not now (it stays off; with Chat on, your AI gets the message instead). A feature that needs setting up first says where. On Telegram and the phone app only; turnon.offer off goes back to sending these to the AI.
  7. "sync everything" (or sync all, /sync all) runs every sync that's on.
  8. Chat: everything else goes to your AI.

With Chat off, step 8 replies "I can't answer free text yet: turn on Chat (it uses the AI you picked) in Settings. /help lists what works now."

Every instant request is in commands.md. If Kurooto misreads one, tap Wrong under its reply, or send /wrong (teaching-kurooto.md).

Chat with your AI

What it does. Anything that isn't a command or an instant request goes to the AI you picked. Kurooto replies "On it." and the answer comes as a reply to your message. The AI can act too (add a reminder, a note, a deadline; read your notes and files): see ai-brains.md.

The grey line under an answer. Every answer ends with one short line in italics, built from Kurooto's own record of the run, not from what the AI wrote: which AI answered, the effort, how long it took, the share of today's limit when Kurooto knows it, and what it actually did, e.g. *agy · medium · 42 s · 12% of today's limit · searched notes, wrote 1 note*. "did nothing" means it used none of Kurooto's tools. See ai-brains.md.

A line first, for a long answer. When a request usually takes more than about 20 seconds on that AI (the median of your last ones at that effort, once there are three), one short line comes before the answer: "Searching your notes, then answering (about 40 s with agy)." It's a guess from what's known before the run (a file you sent, a link, words about your notes, things it remembers), not a promise. Never on a call, never for background work; turn it off with jobs.plan_line = false. See ai-brains.md.

Turn it on. Features → Chat with your AI (in Starter). Needs a working AI brain.

From Telegram:

CommandDoes
any textgoes to the AI
high: ..., low: ...choose how hard it thinks (effort)
/new or /clear (also just "new" or "clear")start a fresh conversation: forgets the previous turns
/brainwhich AI answers (main and fallback), and whether each is ready

How long its answers are (persona.register, on). A short message (12 words or fewer) gets a short answer; a long one (80 words or more) may get a full one; a voice note gets an answer worded the way you'd say it aloud (short sentences, no lists). It goes only by your message's length, never by the time of day, and it doesn't change partway through a conversation. A length on your persona card wins over it.

Memory of the conversation. Chat remembers your last few exchanges (up to 6, each cut to 1,200 characters) as long as the last one was within 2 hours; after 2 hours of quiet a new message starts fresh. Requests from a terminal start fresh every time. Background requests (digests, the look back) never become part of the chat.

What it remembers beyond that. When your message refers to past things ("as we said on the call", "yesterday", "that thing", "did I ...", a weekday) or shares words with something you're in the middle of, the AI is also told the few remembered items it's about: open threads, one-line gists of earlier calls, requests and chats, meeting and daily-note lines. At most about 600 tokens (memory.budget_tokens), picked by rules (shared words, the course you name, the day you name, how recent), each saying where it's from, so an answer can say "as we said on the call at 07:56". Never transcripts. The grey line then ends with "used 2 remembered items". memory.recall off sends none.

Limits. Telegram messages hold about 4,000 characters, so long answers come in parts; past six parts, the first five come and then the whole text as a file (nothing is cut off). Answers over about 7,000 characters also come as a file. A request that's stuck for 10 minutes is stopped with a message.

What leaves your computer: what you ask, and the notes it reads, go to the AI you picked.

Turn it off: Features → Chat with your AI → Turn off. Instant requests and commands keep working; free text gets the "I can't answer free text yet" reply. Skills and the Nightly look back need Chat.

Questions and approvals

This is always there; it isn't a feature you turn on. Sometimes Kurooto needs something only you know ("which chapter is Friday's quiz on?") or your OK before it does something ("send this reply?"). These come as:

  • a question: its answers as buttons, plus Later; or
  • an approval: Approve / Deny, or the asker's own choices (Send / Edit / Discard).

They come from any feature, from an AI request (your answer starts its follow-up) and from your own scripts (kurooto ask-me, kurooto ask-approval, see add-ons.md).

Answering:

  • Tap a button, or reply to the message in your own words (photos and files in the reply are kept with the answer).
  • Or answer in the phone app, or on a call (open questions come up at the start of every call).
  • The first answer wins; answering again elsewhere tells you what was already answered ("That's no longer open").
  • Later (or a reply "later", "not now", "ask me later", "on the next call") keeps it open for your next call.
  • Nothing new (or "nothing", "skip", "nothing to add") is a real answer.
  • For Approve / Deny, a reply like "yes", "ok", "sure", "go ahead" approves; "no", "cancel", "stop", "don't" denies. Anything unclear is asked again. Nothing is ever approved for you.

/q (or /questions) lists open questions (up to 8 at a time, oldest first) with their buttons; reply to one to answer it. /status says how many are open.

Expiry. An unanswered question expires after 48 hours (ask.expire_hours), or sooner when its asker stops waiting; then nothing happens ("That question expired"). Answered and expired questions are forgotten after 30 days (ask.keep_days gives them their own, privacy.keep_days when set applies too), or now with /forget (privacy.md).

What leaves your computer: the question goes wherever Kurooto reaches you (Telegram, the phone app, the desktop); open questions are part of the brief your call line keeps for calls while the computer is off.

Never without your tap

Some things Kurooto does only when you asked for them yourself or tapped to approve them. Your AI, a guess from something you said in passing, and Kurooto's own schedule never do them by themselves:

Placing a call, or booking one for lateryou say "call me at 5", "call me now", "I'm awake" or "quiz me by call", type /quiz <subject> call or kurooto call, or ask on a call. Calls of features you turned on (check-ins, the alarm, deadline calls) ring at the times you set

When your AI wants to. On a call you're on the line, so the AI does what you asked and says so. Anywhere else (an AI request from the chat, a skill or routine, an agent program such as agy using Kurooto's tools) the AI can't set the alarm or book a call by itself: you get a Your OK first question ("An AI request wants to set, move or clear an alarm: set_alarm (time: 7:30). Do it?") with Approve / Deny, and it happens only on Approve. The AI is told it waits for you, and asking again doesn't send a second question. Like every approval it never expires into a yes; /q lists the open ones.

Status, pause, hold and focus

These come with Telegram, and without it they answer in the phone app's chat (with Phone calls on).

/status (answers even while Telegram's own feature is off, e.g. from a terminal): whether calls are allowed or held and until when, focus, the next scheduled things, open questions, today's check-ins, what's due soon, the alarm, your last three AI requests, add-ons' own lines, and the features that are on.

Pause. /pause (until you resume), /pause 2h, /pause until 18:00; /resume wakes it. Also kurooto pause 2h, or Pause 1 h on the dashboard.

  • While paused nothing runs, but Telegram still answers. Anything you send gets "I'm paused until ...; I'll do this when I'm back. /resume wakes me now." and is kept; when it resumes, it's handled in order.
  • Only /resume, /pause, /status and /help work while paused.
  • Reminders due meanwhile arrive when it resumes. Your call line keeps placing scheduled calls, so the morning alarm still rings.
  • After /resume on a laptop on battery, heavy work still waits for the charger ("Back on; this laptop is on battery...").

Hold calls. /hold 18:00 or /hold 2h (no argument: one hour): no calls until then; messages still come. /calls lifts it ("Calls are allowed again (night hours and other holds still apply)"). Plain English: "no calls for 2 hours", "don't call me until 6pm", "hold calls for 30 min"; "calls are fine again". A day other than today ("no calls on Friday") goes to the AI, which works out the day.

Focus (do-not-disturb). /focus 2h, /focus until 18:00; /focus off (or stop, end) ends it; /focus alone says whether it's on. During focus, calls are held and messages that can wait are kept until it ends, then delivered together ("Focus off. 3 messages waited for you"). Approvals, calls, urgent messages and the alarm still come. Plain English: "focus for 2 hours", "do not disturb until 6pm", "dnd off", "focus done". A calendar event with #focus in its title or description is focus time too (reminders-and-deadlines.md).

Hooks. "when I say gym, hold calls 90 min" (or "when I say lab, hold everything 2 h") makes "gym" on its own start that hold later; /hooks lists them, /hooks drop gym forgets one, and each step has Undo. Needs Commitments (classes-and-timetable.md).

The night. From 23:30 to 07:00 (availability.night), or from your bedtime to your alarm, Kurooto doesn't call you except for the alarm. Messages still arrive at night; mute the chat in Telegram if you don't want them to sound. See calls.md.

Voice notes

What it does. Send your bot a voice message: it's written out ("Heard: ..."), then handled exactly like a typed message (a reminder, a question for the AI, a note). Mixed languages are kept as spoken. The recording is saved in your notes inbox.

Turn it on. Features → Voice notes. Needs a Gemini API key, even if another AI answers your messages. Without it: "Voice notes are off. Turn on Voice notes in Settings (it uses your free Gemini key)."

Limits. At most about 18 MB per voice note. A caption-less audio file with a file name (a recording you attach as a file) is treated as a file, not a voice note. If it can't be written out you get "Couldn't make out that voice note" with the reason.

Settings. voice_notes.model (gemini-flash-latest).

To talk to Kurooto out loud at the computer instead, with nothing sent until you're talking to it, see Voice.

What leaves your computer: the recording goes to Google (Gemini, your key). On the free tier Google may use what you send to improve its products, so don't say anything you wouldn't type.

Turn it off: Features → Voice notes → Turn off.

Sending files and photos

Send a photo, PDF, document, video or audio file to your bot. It's downloaded into your notes' inbox/ folder (or your files folder's inbox, when you set files.folder: see notes-and-concept-graph.md), named with the date and time. For a photo, the largest size is kept. The caption travels with it. Then:

any other file, with Files into notes off and Chat onit goes to your AI with the caption (or, with none, "say what it is"); the AI is told where the file is and opens it. any other file, with both off"Saved to inbox/.... To have your AI look at it, turn on Chat; to have it filed into your notes, turn on Files into notes (Features)."

Limits. Telegram lets bots download files up to 20 MB; a bigger file gets "Couldn't get that file: Telegram only lets bots download files up to 20 MB". Put bigger files into the folder yourself (for books: /books folder <subject>).

What leaves your computer: files are saved on this computer. With Chat on, the AI you picked reads them to file them.

Turn it off: Features → Files into notes. Files you send are still downloaded into the inbox, but nothing files them; with Chat on, the file and its caption go to your AI as a question instead.

Getting files from Kurooto

Kurooto sends you files it made (revision packs, study packs, long answers), and the AI can send you files from your notes folder ("send me my linear algebra pack"), on calls too. From a terminal: kurooto send report.pdf --caption "...".

  • Up to 50 MB goes to Telegram (Telegram's limit for bots).
  • Over 50 MB, with Drive upload on (drive_upload.big_files, default on), it goes to a Kurooto folder in your Google Drive and you get a link. These temporary uploads are deleted from Drive after drive_upload.temp_days (7) unless you move them out of the Kurooto folder. Without Drive upload: "... is N MB; Telegram takes files up to 50 MB. Turn on Drive upload to get bigger files as a Drive link." See google.md.
  • Three or more files at once come as one zip, so Telegram isn't flooded. Over 50 MB together they go to Drive (or are refused without Drive upload).
  • During focus a file waits until focus ends.
  • The AI can only send files from your notes folder.
  • Without Telegram (the phone app instead), a file comes to the app's chat as a link: Google Drive with Drive upload on, else a sealed copy on your call line for a day (up to 10 MB), else a note that it stays on your computer, with its path. A long answer's full text comes the same way, right after its first part (phone-app.md).

Boundaries it keeps

Decisions about your health, your relationships and your money are yours. Asked to make one ("should I stop taking my meds?", "should I break up with him?", "should I take the loan?"), Kurooto says it's not its to decide and who to ask: a doctor or pharmacist (and your local emergency number if it feels urgent), someone you trust or a counsellor, someone you trust with money or a financial adviser. One line, no lecture. It still gives facts when you ask for facts.

This is a fixed list of rules, not the AI's judgement: plain-English requests that match it are answered at once on your computer; every AI request and every call is also told the rule. persona.boundaries off turns both off.

Open threads and "that thing"

Kurooto keeps a short list of what you're in the middle of, so you can say "that thing" and be understood.

  • Opens when you say you'll do something, in a message or on a call ("I'll send the draft tonight", "I need to reply to Lena's email", "I'm going to finish the problem set"), and when you start a task (a study session, progress on a deadline: "ps3 4/7"). Rules on your computer, no AI; nothing is sent when one opens. Questions, "remind me ..." and anything about health, money or passwords never open one.
  • Closes when you say it's done ("I sent the draft"), when the session ends or the deadline is done, when you tap Done or Drop, or by itself after 7 days nobody touched it (memory.thread_days).
  • /threads lists what's open, newest first, with Done and Drop, and what closed in the last 48 hours with Undo. /threads done 2, /threads drop 2, /threads all.
  • "Did I finish that thing?" looks at threads from the last 48 hours (narrowed by any other words: "that wind tunnel thing"); "did *you* finish that thing?" also looks at your AI requests. One match: what it is, where you said it and how it stands. Two or more: it asks once, with a button each. "That thing is done" closes it, with Undo. "What was that thing?" works too.
  • It never acts on a guess. A "that thing" found this way is only read or asked about; nothing is sent, moved or deleted for it without your OK (the AI is told the same).
  • Your memory, asked plainly. "When did I last talk to Prof Ortiz?" answers from meetings in your project notes, daily notes, gists and threads; "what was I doing last Thursday?" (also "yesterday", "on 20 Oct") lists that day's daily note, meetings, calls, requests and threads. /memory lists and searches everything remembered (privacy.md).

Threads are kept in the encrypted memory ledger; memory.threads off opens no more.

Renaming your assistant

Settings → you → you.assistant_name, or the setup page's first step. The new name is used everywhere at once, and the Telegram bot is renamed too; it says so once ("I'm Nova now."). Telegram limits how often a bot can be renamed: if it refuses, the message says the name shows in Kurooto's messages, the app and the dashboard anyway. The bot's @username can't change (that's set in @BotFather).

From a terminal

kurooto ask "what's due this week?" handles plain English as on Telegram: quick things answer at once, bigger ones go to your AI and the answer prints in the terminal, not on Telegram, with the run receipt as its last line. kurooto ask alone opens a prompt for one request after another. More, including kurooto notify and kurooto ask-me for your own scripts: add-ons.md.

Quick ask (a hotkey)

kurooto ask --popup opens a small window: type one request, press Enter, and the answer shows in the window (a bigger one says "Working on it…" first). Enter again asks another; Esc closes it. It's the same as typing to Kurooto, so quick things answer at once and the rest goes to your AI. It needs Kurooto running and Python's Tk (on Linux, the python3-tk or tk package if the window doesn't open; then kurooto ask works in a terminal).

The hotkey is your system's. Kurooto doesn't grab a key itself: you bind the command to one in your system's own settings. Dashboard → Settings → Quick ask (hotkey) shows the exact command for this computer and the steps.

SystemHow
Windowsrun kurooto ask --shortcut once: it adds Kurooto Quick ask to the Start menu. Start → find it → right-click → Open file location → right-click it → Properties → Shortcut key: press the keys (Ctrl+Alt+K) → OK
macOSShortcuts → New Shortcut → add Run Shell Script, paste the command → the shortcut's details (i) → Add Keyboard Shortcut. Allow running scripts once (Shortcuts → Settings → Advanced). kurooto ask --shortcut also adds a Quick ask app to ~/Applications
Linux, GNOMESettings → Keyboard → Keyboard Shortcuts → Custom Shortcuts → +: a name, the command, the keys
Linux, KDESystem Settings → Shortcuts → Add New → Command or Script
Hyprland, swaybind = SUPER, K, exec, <the command> in hyprland.conf; bindsym $mod+k exec <the command> in sway's config

Wayland. On Wayland no program may take a key for itself, so Kurooto doesn't pretend to: your desktop (the rows above) runs the command when you press the key. That works on every Wayland desktop that has keyboard shortcuts.
