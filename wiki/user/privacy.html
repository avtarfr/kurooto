Privacy: what stays and what leaves

Kurooto runs on your own computer. Nothing is ever sent to Kurooto's author: no analytics, no crash reports, no "phone home". Everything that leaves your computer goes to a service you chose, under your own account, and only for a feature you turned on. Every feature is off until you turn it on, and each one says on its card in Dashboard → Features what it sends where.

This page is the full list. Each feature's own page repeats its part under What leaves your computer.

The principles

  • It runs here. Your notes, journal, deadlines, reminders, call transcripts and settings live on your computer.
  • Nothing to the author. The only thing Kurooto fetches from its own release site is updates and the list of withdrawn versions, both checked against a signature. Nothing about you goes the other way.
  • Your own accounts. The AI, Telegram bot, call line (Cloudflare), Google project, GitHub repository and backup folder are all yours. Kurooto signs in to each as you; no middleman server holds your access.
  • Nothing goes to anyone but you without your tap. Kurooto never emails, messages or shares on its own. An email reply is sent only when you tap Send; a class pack you make comes to your own Telegram for you to forward; approvals are never given for you.
  • The AI sees only what a request needs. What you ask, the notes it reads to answer, what its tools return, and two short notes about you that go along with every request: the "In brief" part of your profile (teaching-kurooto.md) and your how-to-help-me note.
  • Some things are never read by any cloud AI: mail marked confidential, calendar events marked private (only their time is kept), and anything Kurooto refuses to keep (below).

What leaves, feature by feature

"The AI you picked" means the brain you chose (ai-brains.md): Google for a Gemini key or agy, Anthropic for Claude Code, the service you entered for an OpenAI-compatible one, or nothing at all for local models.

Where requests go

Every request Kurooto makes on the internet goes through one place that counts it: the host, the feature, how many requests, and how many bytes went out and came back, per day. /egress shows the last 7 days. Only the counts are kept (egress.json in the state folder, 30 days), never what was sent.

Each host is checked against Kurooto's list, which is the "Where requests go" table in docs/PRIVACY.md: Telegram, Google's APIs (Gemini, sign-in, Classroom, Gmail, Drive and Calendar), GitHub, Cloudflare and your call line (.workers.dev), ntfy.sh, Node.js and Ollama downloads, Voice's models (GitHub and its release file server, Hugging Face), Face presence's models (media.githubusercontent.com, OpenCV's model zoo; its optional eye model from storage.googleapis.com, MediaPipe's model store), arXiv, bioRxiv and PubMed, and Kurooto's release site. Addresses you gave are yours and always allowed: your calendar's link, class calendars you subscribed to, your Research feeds, your own AI service, your call line, your ntfy server, and pages your AI reads for a request. This computer (127.0.0.1) is never counted.

Programs that go online by themselves show as handed to them, with the feature: git (Notes sync, Profile sync, add-ons by link, projects from repositories), gh (a project's issues and milestones), npm and wrangler (setting up the call line), agy and Claude Code, Ollama (a model download), GBrain, Tectonic, uv or pip (Voice's and Face presence's Python packages) and installers. Kurooto doesn't see what they send; the tables above say where it goes.

privacy.egress decides what happens to a host that isn't on the list:

  • record (the default): it's counted and marked "not on the list", and the request goes ahead. It's the default because an add-on or an address changed in settings can reach hosts Kurooto can't know of.
  • enforce: it's refused, and the feature says "Kurooto didn't connect to <host>". Add hosts you trust to privacy.allow_hosts (["feeds.example.org"]; ".example.org" allows every host under it).
  • off: nothing is checked or counted.

Add-ons by link are fenced apart in every mode: their code reaches only the hosts on the card you agreed to and starts only the programs on it; anything else is refused and listed by /egress as blocked for "add-on <name>" (add-ons.md).

Free AI tiers and their terms

A free Gemini API key (Google AI Studio's free tier):

  • Google may use what you send and what it answers to improve its products, and human reviewers may read it. Don't say or type anything there you wouldn't want read.
  • You must be 18 or older.
  • In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland you need a paid key (billing on).
  • Phone calls and voice notes always use Gemini, whichever AI answers your messages (a turn-by-turn call can use the model on your computer instead).

On a paid key Google doesn't use what you send to improve its products. Or keep those features off, or use local models where they're offered.

Antigravity (agy): Kurooto runs Google's own agy program on your computer, signed in as you, and never touches its sign-in. Google's answers differ on whether other tools may start agy unattended, so there is some risk to your Google account; for tools like Kurooto, Google recommends a Gemini key.

Claude Code and OpenAI-compatible services work under their own terms and your own account. Claude Code gets its reach for each request only; nothing is saved in its own settings. To tell which account hit a limit, Kurooto reads the account's id from Claude Code's .claude.json and keeps only a hash of it. An extra Claude account (Two Claude accounts) keeps its sign-in in a folder of Kurooto's (claude-accounts/ in the data folder), whose sign-in Kurooto never reads or copies; the folder stays out of backups unless backups.secrets is on, and it sends to Anthropic like your usual one.

Gists: what settles

Conversations settle the way memories do: as the words of a call, an AI request or a chat fade (privacy.keep_days or /forget), it settles into a one-line gist of what it decided or did. The nightly job lets the words go only once their gist exists. Gists are made by rules from Kurooto's own records, never by rereading the words with an AI:

WhatIts gist keeps
A callwhat it was for, and what its tools did ("set reminder: I'll remind you Fri 23 Oct at 09:00: book the lab slot"); tools that only read are named, never what they read
An AI request of yoursits title (the first 80 characters of what you asked), how it ended and its run receipt; never the answer
A chat turnits request's name, once

A line that looks like a health, money or password detail keeps only its kind ("an AI request about something money"). They stay in the memory ledger (encrypted, never your notes) for good, or privacy.gist_days days when you set it. /gists shows the latest, /gists lab finds them, /gists forget 3 drops one. /forget 7d keeps them; /forget all asks: Forget all, keep gists or Forget gists too. privacy.keep_gist = false makes none. With privacy.gist_model on and Local AI models on, a model on this computer adds one sentence of what a call decided; a cloud AI never does.

The memory ledger

Everything Kurooto remembers is in one place on this computer, ledger.db in the state folder, and it's always encrypted, whether or not you use Profile sync:

RowWhere it comes from
Gistsconversations whose words faded (above)
Threadswhat you said you'd do, in a message or on a call, and tasks you started (Talking to Kurooto)
How-to-help-me linesthe dated lines Kurooto keeps in your how-to-help-me note
Daysyour daily notes' headings and lines (a health, money or password line is left out)
Meetingsthe dated lines under a project note's "## Meetings"
  • Encrypted at rest. Each row's words and where it came from are sealed (AES-256-GCM) with a key made on first use and kept in your keychain (Windows Credential Manager, macOS Keychain, the Secret Service on Linux; the owner-only secrets.json where there's none). On disk stay only each row's kind, its times, a thread's open or done, and keyed hashes. Searching decrypts into memory, so no index of your words is written. If the key can't be read (a keychain still locked at boot), nothing is shown or written until it can.
  • Your notes stay the source of truth. Days, meetings and how-to-help-me lines are read from your notes when they change; gists and threads live only here. /memory export writes the gists and threads to a note in your inbox.
  • Find and forget. /memory lists it, newest first and numbered; /memory glacier finds rows with those words; /memory forget 3 forgets one (a how-to-help-me line comes out of that note too; a daily-note or meeting line stays in your note, which is yours, and stays forgotten here).
  • Ask it. "When did I last talk to Prof Ortiz?" and "what was I doing last Thursday?" are answered from it, by rules on this computer.
  • Covered by /forget. /forget 7d drops the threads from those days, /forget all all of them (and the gists if you choose); privacy.keep_days expires threads like the rest.

What it never touches:

  • Your notes folder. A voice note's recording is saved in your notes' inbox, so it stays; delete it there.
  • Test cases you made, open questions, a call still going.
  • The event log (events.jsonl): times, counts and request names, never what you typed or what was answered. Features learn your rhythm from it over weeks.
  • Messages on Telegram or your phone: they're in those apps; delete them there.
  • Backup zips you made before.

Your call line holds a call's transcript only while the computer is off and hands it over when it's back, so nothing waits there to be forgotten.

Things Kurooto refuses to keep

  • Remember asks "Still true?" about a line it kept that hasn't come up in 90 days, at most two a week (Teaching Kurooto); only its own dated lines, never ones you wrote.
  • Remember won't store health, money or password details: they'd travel with every AI request. It says so and keeps nothing. Skills are told to keep health, money and other people's private details out of your notes too.
  • Learns from its own work never keeps a fact about health, money, passwords or other people (a line naming a relative or friend, a phone number, an email address).
  • Wrong button and test cases never keep long messages, several requests in one, or anything about health, money or passwords. Cases you didn't review are forgotten after 30 days (cases.keep_days).
  • Mail marked confidential is never read by the AI you picked, never summarised by it, and never answered with an AI draft. Kurooto reads its text on your computer only to spot the marking, then drops it.
  • Open threads never open from a sentence about health, money or passwords.
  • Private calendar events keep only their time.
  • Events (the activity log) record counts and request names, never what you typed or answered.
  • Voice never keeps sound (only a rolling buffer of under a second, in memory) and never keeps the words of noise or of people talking to each other: they're dropped as soon as the gate decides. Only counts are kept, in memory.
  • Voice ID keeps only your own voice print: numbers, condition names, dates, thresholds, quality numbers and the microphone's name (0.9.2, voice.speaker_id.per_device: also other microphones' names, the numbers of turns proven yours there and those microphones' own prints; voice.speaker_id.claim: a "not sure" turn's sound waits in memory for 10 seconds so a tap can say it was you, then is zeroed), encrypted with AES-256-GCM in state/voiceid/template.bin under a key in your keychain (or the owner-only secrets file where there's none). Never your recordings (each sample's sound is dropped as soon as its numbers are made), never anyone else's voice (scored in memory, then gone), and never a score in a log. It's never backed up, carried by Profile sync, put in a diagnostics zip, logged or shown. Where the key sits in the secrets file, it's on the same disk as the voice print, so the encryption then only protects copies of the state folder.
  • Face presence never keeps a picture: frames and the enrolment preview stay in memory; a stranger's face is dropped with the frame (a count since start is all that's kept; with voice.listen.look_anyone, 0.8.18, also where it points, its head's turn and eyes as a few numbers, dropped with the frame too, never who it is). Your template is sealed under face.key; forgetting deletes the key first, so an old copy of it anywhere can't be read. Licences: YuNet (MIT), SFace (Apache-2.0), opencv-python-headless (Apache-2.0; it bundles FFmpeg's libraries, LGPL-2.1+), numpy (BSD-3). See Face presence.

What the AI may read and write on your computer

  • Read: your notes folder; your Documents, Downloads and Screenshots folders; Kurooto's copies of Classroom and mail; the reserve; the files you attached on the dashboard's Chat page (0.9.0). Hidden files and folders (like .git) are left out, and a link that leads elsewhere is refused.
  • Write: your notes folder, Classroom's course files folder, and (with add-ons on) your add-ons' skills/. Text files only. Documents and Downloads stay read-only.
  • Your text: it adds notes and lines; it changes existing text only when you asked, and that change comes as a proposal you apply (Undo puts it back). Projects and proposals.
  • Change requests (your project, a change to your notes, a fix for a skill): it writes in a scratch folder of its own in Kurooto's state folder, nowhere else; your files change only when you tap Apply. Keys and passwords (.env, *.pem, id_rsa ...) are never copied there or proposed. Checks you turn on for a project run in a sandbox with no network.
  • The web: public pages, read only, a few MB at most; never this computer or your local network.
  • Everything it reads goes to the AI you picked, like the notes it reads to answer.

Removing your data

  • Conversations: /forget 7d or /forget all (How long it's kept).
  • Your voice print (Voice ID): /voice id forget, Forget my voice on the Your voice page, kurooto voice id forget, or /forget all: the file and its key are deleted. On an SSD the file's blocks may linger until reused; with the key gone, they're unreadable.
  • One feature: turn it off (Dashboard → Features). Its data stays until you delete it; your notes are never deleted.
  • A paired phone: /phones, then /unpair <id>. A lost phone: kurooto rotate gives your call line new keys and forgets every paired phone (phone-app.md).
  • Google access: /google forget <name>, or myaccount.google.com/connections (google.md).
  • Leaving for good: kurooto uninstall --revoke first deletes your call line from your Cloudflare account, revokes each Google sign-in and clears your Telegram bot's webhook and menu, then removes Kurooto's keys from your keychain and Kurooto itself. Your notes folder is never deleted. It lists what's left for you: the GitHub token(s), the Gemini key, the Google Cloud project, Profile sync's kurooto-profile branch, the bot itself (/deletebot to @BotFather), backup zips in the folder you picked.
