Getting started

Paste one line into a terminal, follow the setup page that opens, make a Telegram bot (or pair the Kurooto phone app instead), then send /help to it. That takes 15 to 30 minutes the first time. This page walks through each step and says what to do when something looks wrong.

What you need

Always:

WhatWhy
A computer with Windows 10 or 11, macOS 12 or newer, or LinuxKurooto runs on it. No graphics card is needed: the AI runs in the cloud unless you choose otherwise.
About 1 GB of free disk space, plus what your features needThe installer checks for at least 2 GB free. The setup page shows the total before installing anything, and Kurooto always leaves some space spare (it skips local AI models rather than fill your disk).
Telegram on your phone or computer (free), or the Kurooto phone appThis is where you talk to Kurooto. The phone app instead of Telegram needs Phone calls (a free Gemini key and a free Cloudflare account); see step 7.
A Google account (free)For an AI brain: agy signs in with it, and a Gemini API key is made with it.

Kurooto only works while the computer is on and awake. If you want it reachable all day, leave the computer on and plugged in (see Stay awake on charger).

Only for some features:

FeatureNeeds

Notes sync, Profile synca free GitHub account

Nothing costs money unless you choose a paid plan (a paid AI plan you already have, or a paid Gemini key).

Install

The installer works for your user only: no admin password. It sets Kurooto to start when you sign in, starts it, and opens the setup page in your browser.

Windows: press Start, type PowerShell, open it, and paste:

irm https://avtarfr.github.io/kurooto/install.ps1 | iex

macOS: open Terminal (Spotlight, then "Terminal") and paste:

curl -LsSf https://avtarfr.github.io/kurooto/install.sh | sh

Linux: open a terminal and paste the same line as macOS.

What the installer does, in order:

  1. Checks there are at least 2 GB free.
  2. Installs uv (a small Python manager) and Python 3.12, just for your user.
  3. Downloads Kurooto, checks its signature and every file's checksum, and refuses anything that doesn't match.
  4. Turns on start-at-sign-in, starts Kurooto, and opens http://127.0.0.1:8770/setup.

Run the same line again any time to repair or update an installation.

The beta channel (testers). To get releases before everyone else, put KUROOTO_CHANNEL=beta in front:

curl -LsSf https://avtarfr.github.io/kurooto/install.sh | KUROOTO_CHANNEL=beta sh
$env:KUROOTO_CHANNEL = 'beta'; irm https://avtarfr.github.io/kurooto/install.ps1 | iex

The install then keeps following beta (the setting updates.channel becomes beta). To go back: Dashboard → Settings → updates → updates.channel = stable; you get the next stable release once it's newer than your beta.

If something looks scary:

What you seeWhat it means
Windows Defender or an antivirus warns about a scriptThe line downloads a PowerShell script from Kurooto's release page. You can open the address in a browser and read it first.
macOS asks whether Terminal may control other apps or reach filesOnly when a feature needs it (the Study tracker reads window titles). You can say no; that feature then says it can't.
The setup page didn't openOpen http://127.0.0.1:8770/setup yourself, or run kurooto open. If another program already uses port 8770, Kurooto takes the next free one; kurooto paths shows the state folder, where web.json names it.

127.0.0.1 means "this computer". Nothing outside your computer can open the setup page or the dashboard.

The setup wizard, step by step

The setup page saves as you go: close it and come back any time (kurooto setup, or setup in the dashboard's corner), and it opens on the first step you haven't finished. The steps are listed down the left on a wide screen, or as numbered dots on a phone; tap any step to open it. Finished steps have a tick, skipped ones a dash, and a step you passed but didn't finish (an AI that doesn't answer yet) shows !. Words with a dotted underline (API key, token, repository, webhook...) explain themselves when you tap them.

Skip for now. Every step except You and AI has a Skip for now button: Kurooto needs your name and an AI that answers, the rest can wait. Skipped steps aren't forgotten: the dashboard lists them under Features → Finish setting up (the corner link then says finish setup (2), say), kurooto doctor shows each as a note, and each links back to its step (kurooto setup about opens About you). A step leaves that list once it's done, wherever you did it.

Kurooto also notes how long you spend on each step and whether you finished or skipped it, so someone helping you can see where setup got stuck. That stays on this computer; it's only in the zip kurooto doctor --bundle saves for you (see your-computer.md), never what you typed.

If a button says "Kurooto isn't answering", Kurooto stopped or is restarting: start it from your app menu (or run kurooto on), then press the button again; what you typed is kept. "This page is out of date" means Kurooto restarted: reload the page.

1. You

  • What should it call you? Your name, as it should greet you.
  • Your time zone. Filled in from this computer. Reminders, check-ins and calls go by it. Start typing a city or region and pick from the list (Europe/Berlin, America/New_York), or leave it empty to use the computer's own. An old name for a zone is saved under today's name (Europe/Kiev becomes Europe/Kyiv).
  • Your assistant's name. "Kurooto" by default. Change it later in Settings (you.assistant_name); the Telegram bot takes the new name too (see talking-to-kurooto.md).

The first page also has I've used Kurooto before (see below).

2. Your notes

Where Kurooto writes its notes (the daily note, a folder per subject, quick notes), and how. Already keep notes, in Obsidian or any folder of Markdown files? Type that folder's full path (a Windows path like C:\Users\you\Documents\Notes works, spaces too) and press Look at this folder. Kurooto reads the folder's names, dates, links and headers, with no AI, and proposes a place for each kind of note: daily notes, the inbox, subject folders, concept notes and research projects. Each line says how sure it is and why ("3 daily notes like Daily Notes/2026-10-20.md"), and you can change any line. The preview under it shows where notes will go ("Daily notes → Daily Notes/2026-10-20.md").

  • Use this layout saves it. Nothing in your notes changes before you press it.
  • Keep Kurooto's own layout keeps (or goes back to) Kurooto's folders: journal/, about-me/, a folder per subject.
  • Skip for now leaves it for later. Kurooto's own layout is used until then.

You can come back to this any time from Dashboard → Settings → notes → Fit Kurooto to your notes, or with kurooto setup notes. If Kurooto has already written notes, the step also shows which of them would move. Starting fresh, with the notes folder still empty? There's nothing to fit in with: the step says so, folds all of the above under Change where each kind of note goes, and Next is all there is to do. More: notes-and-concept-graph.md.

3. Your computer

Kurooto reads the computer: system, processor, memory, graphics card, free space, and what kind of setup it suits (no local models, small local models, bigger ones, Apple Silicon). It lists what it recommends, marked ★. Nothing is installed here: local models come with the features that use them (Local AI models, Notes brain) if you turn those on in step 6.

  • Use more than this computer suggests: pick another setup, add an Ollama model of your own (gemma4:12b) or another program (tesseract), then Save or Save and install now. Kurooto says plainly what you're going past (a model bigger than your graphics memory runs partly on the processor, slowly) but never refuses. See your-computer.md.
  • When this laptop is on battery (laptops only): ★ Save battery (Telegram keeps working; heavy work waits for the charger), Keep everything running, or Pause everything. This is power.on_battery.
  • Colours: Kurooto's own greens (kuro), the older pinks (rose), or this computer's own colours (system, marked ★ when Kurooto finds them). Only how Kurooto's pages look. See dashboard.md.

4. AI: pick the AI that answers you

Pick a card, then follow its panel. The first card is marked ★. Each panel ends with a test: Kurooto asks the AI to reply and tells you whether it worked, and if not, what to do.

CardWhat you do
Antigravity (agy)Install agy (Google's official installer), Open the sign-in window, sign in with Google, close the window, Test it.
Gemini API keyOpen Google AI Studio → Create API key, paste the key (it starts with AIza), Save and test.
Claude Code (under Advanced)Install Claude Code, Open the sign-in window, sign in with your Claude account, Test it. Needs a paid Claude plan.
Ollama on this computerInstall Ollama. Private and offline, much weaker; best as a helper next to another AI.
Another OpenAI-compatible serviceIts address (usually ending in /v1), key and model name, then Save and test.

If the test fails, the panel says what to do. You can go on without a working AI and finish this step later. Details of each brain, and what each costs: ai-brains.md.

Try it now. Once the AI answers its test, a chat box appears under it. What you type there is handled exactly like a Telegram message, before Telegram is set up:

  • remind me in 2 minutes to stretch pops up on this computer two minutes later;
  • a question goes to your AI and its answer comes back in the box (it can take up to a few minutes).

Chat and Reminders answer there even though you turn them on later; other features answer once they're on. Nothing said in the box goes to Telegram or your phone. A reminder set there still comes after a restart; cancel it with /reminders once Telegram is connected.

5. About you (recommended, optional)

Your other AI accounts (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot and the rest) already know a lot about you. This step brings that into Kurooto once:

  1. Type which AI and account you're starting with ("ChatGPT personal"), then press Copy the prompt on Your main AI (the account you use most, the fullest audit) or Every other account.
  2. Paste it into a new chat in that account. Turn on its memory or "search past chats" first if it has them.
  3. Copy its whole answer (or download it), paste it in the box and press Add this report, or upload .md / .txt files. Repeat for as many accounts as you can.
  4. Press Build my profile. Your AI merges the reports into about-me/profile.md in your notes.
  5. Read it in the editor under the button and fix anything that's off. Save keeps the old version beside it; Preview In brief shows the part that goes along with every AI request.

The reports stay in Kurooto's private folder (not your notes, never synced); building the profile sends them to the AI you picked. Skip for now (or Next with no reports added) skips this step. More: teaching-kurooto.md.

6. Features: what should it do?

Every feature is off until you tick it. First, What do you do? adds what suits it to Starter (each such card says "★ suits what you do"); you can still untick anything:

AnswerAdded to Starter
StudentSubjects and timetable, Start-by dates, Quiz, After-class notes, Doubt queue, Weekly summary
WorkingCommitments, Start-by dates, Calendar, Weekly summary
ResearchResearch, Commitments, Start-by dates, Weekly summary
Something elsenothing: Starter as it is

Then three buttons pick a set:

ButtonTurns on
★ Starter (recommended)Telegram, Chat with your AI, Reminders, Check-ins, Deadlines
Everythingevery feature offered (Exam season is not in any set: turn it on by hand later)
Pick myselfonly what you tick

The cards for you come first: Starter's, what suits your answer, and what you've ticked. The rest (about 45) wait under More features: open it to pick from all of them. Each card says what the feature does and what leaves your computer. Some ask for a detail right on the card once ticked: a calendar link, a repository and token, a Google client file, a backup folder. Phone calls shows what it needs in order (a Gemini key, then a free Cloudflare account) and a Set up now button that makes your call line right there; a Cloudflare sign-in page opens in your browser. Phone calls, Voice notes and the Morning alarm need a Gemini key even if another AI answers your messages: a key box appears under the first of those cards you tick.

The Notes brain is a 0.5 GB download, so it's not in Starter. You can change any of this later on the dashboard (Features, Alt+F).

How many messages a day. Under the cards, one line says it plainly: "Kurooto sends at most 6 messages a day on its own; the rest wait for one evening bundle at 20:00. Your own reminders, alarms and approvals always come." Change the number in the box beside it (notify.calm.messages); more about the limit in messages-it-sends.md.

7. Chat: Telegram or the phone app

Pick where you talk to Kurooto: Telegram (your own private bot) or The Kurooto phone app instead.

The phone app instead. Its chat does what the bot does: commands, reminders, check-ins, questions with buttons, your AI's answers; files come as links. It needs Phone calls: tick it on the Features step and press Set up now (a free Gemini key, then your call line on a free Cloudflare account). Back here, Pair my phone now shows a QR code (or pair after Finish setup). Telegram can be added later from the dashboard. More: Using the app instead of Telegram.

Telegram. You talk to Kurooto through your own private bot. It takes about two minutes:

  1. In Telegram, open @BotFather (Telegram's official bot-maker, with the blue tick) and send /newbot.
  2. Answer its two questions: a display name, then a username ending in bot.
  3. BotFather replies with a token like 123456789:AAH…. Copy the whole token, paste it on the setup page and press Check the token.
  4. The page says "Found your bot @yourbot" and shows Open @yourbot. Tap it, then press Start in Telegram.
  5. The page says it's connected, and the bot greets you: "This chat is now ours; I'll ignore everyone else."

From then on the bot answers only your Telegram account; anyone else who finds it is ignored.

If it doesn't connect:

What the page saysWhat to do
"That doesn't look like a bot token"Copy the whole token line from BotFather's message (a number, a colon and a long code), not the bot's name or link.
Telegram didn't accept the tokenCopy it again, or send /token to BotFather to get it again.
"Got a /start without the code"You pressed Start without the code. Send the message the page shows (it starts with /start and a code); there is a Copy button.
"This link expired: get a new one"The link works for one hour. Press Get a new link.
"That token was revoked"Get the new token from @BotFather and paste it.
"This bot is connected to a web address (a webhook)"Another service was set up to receive this bot's messages. Press Disconnect it.
"Another program is reading this bot's messages"Maybe another Kurooto using the same bot. Stop the other one, or make a new bot with @BotFather.
"Can't reach Telegram right now"Check the internet; it keeps trying.

You can Skip for now and connect it later (Next without a connection skips it too); the step waits on the dashboard's Finish setting up list until Telegram is paired or, with the phone app picked, Phone calls is set up.

8. Almost done

The summary lists your AI, Telegram (or the phone app), the features you picked, the steps you skipped (each with Go back to it) and your notes folder (by default Documents/Kurooto, or ~/Kurooto on Linux without a Documents folder). Tick or untick Start Kurooto when I sign in to this computer (★ recommended), then Finish setup. Kurooto installs what your features need (with progress for each), turns them on, and starts. Anything that isn't ready is listed as "Not turned on yet" with the reason; finish it later from the dashboard. If you chose Phone calls, a QR code appears to pair your phone (it works for 15 minutes; /phone on Telegram makes a new one). Then Open the dashboard.

Choosing an AI brain

Short version:

  • Antigravity (agy) is free with a Google account and can read your notes and act on your computer within limits. There is some risk to the Google account, because Google's answers differ on whether other tools may start agy unattended.
  • A Gemini API key is the simplest and the way Google recommends for tools like this. The free tier has small daily limits, and Google may use what you send to improve its products. You need one anyway for calls, voice notes and the alarm.
  • Claude Code if you already pay for Claude.
  • Ollama only as a private, offline helper on a computer with a good graphics card.

Set a fallback too, so a second AI takes over when the first hits its limit. Everything about brains: ai-brains.md.

Your first 15 minutes

Send these to your bot, one at a time:

TypeWhat happens
/helpLists the commands of the features you have on, in groups, and what you can just say.
remind me in 2 minutes to stretchA reminder arrives in two minutes, with +10 min and +1 h buttons.
what's due?Your deadline list (empty at first).
/due fri 17:00 essay draftAdds a deadline; you'll get a nudge a day before.
what is a vector space?A question for your AI (needs Chat). It replies "On it." and the answer follows.
/checkinsToday's check-ins. The evening one comes at 22:00 by default.
/statusWhat's going on: calls, focus, what's coming, open questions.
/brainWhich AI answers, and whether it works.

Then, if you like:

  • Reply to the evening check-in when it comes: your reply goes into your journal.
  • Add your subjects and classes: /subject add Linear Algebra (la), /class mon 09:00-09:55 la (classes-and-timetable.md).
  • Open the dashboard (kurooto open, or http://127.0.0.1:8770) and look at Today (Alt+T).
  • Turn on more features: dashboard → Features (Alt+F). First-week quests sends one small thing to try each day for a week.

Used Kurooto before?

On the first page, press I've used Kurooto before. This works if Profile sync was on before. Give:

  • the private repository Profile sync used (owner/name);
  • a token for it (lost it? make a new fine-grained token for that repository with Contents: Read and write);
  • your profile's passphrase.

Press Restore. Your settings, keys and saved state come back, and with Notes sync your notes too. The page then lists what's still to do on this computer (sign your AI in again, install what features need, pick folders that were on the old computer), and Continue walks you through it. Turn Kurooto off on the old computer first (kurooto off): two computers can't share one Telegram bot or one call line. Details: updates-and-backups.md.
