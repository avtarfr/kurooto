Notes and the concept graph

Kurooto keeps everything it writes for you in one folder of plain Markdown files: your notes folder. You can open it in any editor, or as an Obsidian vault. This page covers that folder and the features that work on it: saving files and quick notes, searching by meaning, syncing to GitHub, and the concept graph that weaves books into linked notes.

Skills that write notes for you (/journal, /study-log, /add-to-notes and the rest) are on Skills and routines. Books and handwritten pages are on Books and study.

Your notes folder

What it is. A normal folder of Markdown (.md) files. Links between notes are [[wikilinks]], the same kind Obsidian, Logseq and many other note apps use, or markdown links ([Name](../path.md)) if your notes use those (notes.links, below). Nothing is stored in a database you can't read: if you stop using Kurooto, the notes stay exactly as they are.

Where it is by default.

ComputerNotes folder
WindowsDocuments\Kurooto
macOS~/Documents/Kurooto
Linux~/Documents/Kurooto, or ~/Kurooto if you have no Documents folder

To use another folder (an existing Obsidian vault, say), set Dashboard → Settings → you → you.workspace to its full path. Empty means the default above.

Using it with Obsidian. Open the notes folder as a vault ("Open folder as vault"). Everything works both ways: notes you write in Obsidian are read by Kurooto (search, deadlines from - [ ] tasks, the term note, and so on), and notes Kurooto writes show up in Obsidian. With Notes sync on, Obsidian's per-device window layout files (.obsidian/workspace*.json) are left out of the repository so two devices don't fight over them.

Opening a note from the dashboard. The dashboard's NOTES map opens a note on double-click; see Dashboard for notes.open_with.

What it does. Kurooto knows two ways to arrange a notes folder. Pick one in Dashboard → Settings → notes → notes.layout: kurooto (the default) or zettelkasten. The AI is told which one you use, and skills written for the default layout are translated to yours when they run (yours included).

Note headers. A note Kurooto or the AI writes starts with a short header ("frontmatter"):

  • kurooto layout: made_by (your assistant's name) and date.
  • zettelkasten layout: id (the note's path), aliases, tags and trust_tier: agent_written. Your daily note and your quick notes don't get trust_tier, because they're your own words.

Your own places (notes.paths). Put any kind of note somewhere else, for example {"daily": "diary/{date}.md", "ideas": "plans/ideas.md"}.

Kinds you can moveParts you can use in braces

A place that doesn't work (a part that kind doesn't have, or ..) falls back to the layout's own, and /health says so.

Limits and edge cases.

  • Changing notes.layout or notes.paths in Settings affects new notes only: nothing is moved. To move Kurooto's own notes along, use Fit Kurooto to the notes you have instead.

Undo. Set notes.layout back to kurooto and empty notes.paths. What was written meanwhile stays where it was.

Fit Kurooto to the notes you have

What it does. Kurooto reads your notes folder and proposes where each kind of note goes, so it fits in with how you already keep notes instead of bringing its own folders. It's the setup page's Notes step; open it any time from Dashboard → Settings → notes → Fit Kurooto to your notes, or with kurooto setup notes.

What it reads (no AI, nothing leaves your computer): the top-level folder names; how daily notes are named and where (journal/2026-10-20.md, dailies/, Daily Notes/2026-10-20.md, 2026/10/20.md); whether your notes link with [[wikilinks]] or markdown links, with or without .md; the header (frontmatter) keys in use (id, aliases, tags, title...); where subject or course folders live (Courses/<subject>, sources/<subject>, or a folder with course pages); an inbox folder. Hidden folders such as .obsidian and .git are left out.

What it proposes. A layout (kurooto or zettelkasten) and a line for each of: daily notes, quick notes (the inbox), subject folders, concept notes and research projects. Each line says how sure it is and why. Change any line, or switch the layout; the places that follow from a line follow it (a course page and lecture notes go in the subject folder, a quick note in the inbox). The preview shows where notes will go, with today's date and an example subject:

Daily notes -> Daily Notes/2026-10-20.md A course page -> Courses/linear-algebra/course.md

Links between notes shows how your notes link, and Kurooto writes its own links the same way: [[wikilinks]] (the default) or markdown links, [Name](path.md) with the path relative to the note they're in. That's the setting notes.links (wiki or markdown; also in Settings → notes). Kurooto reads both kinds back, so its concept graph, link cards and hub lists keep working after a switch; links it wrote before stay as they were.

Use this layout saves notes.layout, notes.paths and notes.links (and the notes folder, if you looked at another one). Keep Kurooto's own layout keeps, or goes back to, Kurooto's folders.

Moving Kurooto's own notes. When Kurooto has already written notes, the step shows a plan before anything happens: which of its notes move where, how many links in its own text are updated, and what stays. Only notes with Kurooto's header move (made_by, or trust_tier: agent_written in the zettelkasten layout). These always stay where they are:

  • your own notes, and anything without Kurooto's header;
  • daily notes, because they hold your own words (new ones go to the new place);
  • your profile.

Links to moved notes are updated only in Kurooto's own text: its notes, the `` blocks it keeps in your notes, and the course lists in the home note. Links in your own words that point at a moved note are listed, never changed.

Limits and edge cases.

  • Nothing in your notes changes until you press Use this layout, and only as the plan you were shown says. If a note changed since the plan was shown, nothing moves and you see the new plan.
  • If a note's new place is already taken, the plan says so and nothing moves until you rename that note or change the line.
  • Looking at another folder switches Kurooto to it: its notes in the old folder stay there.
  • notes.links changes how new links are written, not the ones already there. Messages on Telegram still show note paths as [[path]].
  • A very big folder (over 20,000 notes) is read in part, and the step says so.

Undo. After a move, the step shows Undo: it puts the notes back, undoes the link updates and sets the layout back to what it was. A note you changed since keeps your change, and the step says which. In a notes folder that is a git repository, the moves also show up as renames in its history.

The files folder: keeping photos and PDFs out of your notes

What it does. By default, photos, PDFs and other files you send go inside your notes folder. If you'd rather keep big files out of your notes (and out of their Git history), set Dashboard → Settings → files → files.folder to a folder outside your notes, for example Documents/Kurooto files or a folder your cloud drive syncs.

With it set, these go there instead:

WhatFolder inside files.folder
Photos, PDFs and documents you send (Telegram, the phone app)inbox/
After-class photos and files<subject>/
Revision packs (LaTeX and PDF)<subject>/
Other LaTeX documents skills makeinbox/
Originals of handwritten pageshandwritten/
New handwritten scans to readhandwritten-inbox/ (unless you already have that folder in your notes, or set handwritten.folder)
Classroom's downloaded course files<subject>/
A research project's files<project>/

Without it (the default), those files stay inside your notes: inbox/, <subject>/files/, research/<project>/files/ in the kurooto layout, or under attachments/ in the zettelkasten layout. Classroom's downloads then stay in Kurooto's own data folder.

How notes link to the files (files.links): relative (a normal relative link; images show inside the note) or file (a file:// link). Empty means the layout's own choice: relative for kurooto, file for zettelkasten.

Limits and edge cases.

  • A files.folder inside your notes folder changes nothing (files still go into the notes), and /health says so.
  • Notes sync and Backups don't include the files folder. Pick one your cloud drive syncs, or back it up yourself.
  • Changing it affects new files only; nothing is moved.

Undo. Empty files.folder.

Home and hub notes

So your notes don't end up as separate islands, Kurooto keeps two hub notes:

  • Home (index.md, or maps/index.md in the zettelkasten layout): a "Hubs" list (the research hub) and a "Courses" list (each subject's course page).
  • Research hub (research/index.md or maps/research.md): your projects, and what you work on if you told the Research feature.

Each course page gets one line at its end linking back to Home and Research. Kurooto only ever adds lines here, and a line that's already there isn't added twice. They're brought up to date after a book has been read; the research hub is also made when your first project gets a log line.

What Kurooto never does to your text

These rules hold for Kurooto itself, for every skill and for the AI's tools:

  • It adds; it doesn't reword or delete. New notes and new lines, yes. Your existing sentences stay as you wrote them, and your own words go in verbatim (only obvious typos fixed).
  • Its own parts are marked. Where Kurooto keeps a part of a note up to date (a book's chapter list, a concept's "Seen in" list, exam prep, a handwritten transcription and its links), that part sits between markers like ` and `. Only what's between them is ever replaced. Write anything you like above or below.
  • Editing only when you ask. The AI changes existing text only with its edit_note tool, when you asked it to, one exact passage at a time. Before each edit the previous version is saved in Kurooto's state folder, note-history/, the last 20 versions per note. With Notes sync on, your repository's history has every version too.
  • Text files only. The AI writes only text files (.md, .txt, .tex, .bib, .csv, .json, .yaml, ...).
  • No private details. Skills keep health and medical details, money, passwords and other people's private details out of your notes, and say what they left out.

What the AI may read and write on your computer is on AI brains.

Files into notes

What it does. Send a photo, PDF, document, video or audio file to your bot and it's saved in your notes. With Chat on, the AI then files it properly: a note in the right place that says what the file is and links to it.

Turn it on. Dashboard → Features → Files into notes. Needs Telegram. Filing by the AI needs Chat.

From Telegram.

  • Send the file. It's saved as <date>-<time>-<name> in your inbox (inbox/ in your notes, or in your files folder when files.folder is set).
  • Without Chat: the reply is "Saved to inbox/…". Nothing else happens.
  • With Chat: the reply is "Saved; filing it into your notes…", then a one-line answer saying where it went. The caption tells the AI what the file is for ("slides from today's Linear Algebra lecture").
  • With Skills on, a link sent on its own (with at most a few words around it, not a question) is filed the same way, by the /add-to-notes skill: "https://example.com/paper.pdf for statistics".

Which feature takes a file. A file is checked in this order, and the first match wins:

What you sendWhat happens
A caption that starts with a command (/note, /hint la, /journal, /study-log...)That command or skill gets the file
A PDF with a caption like book for linear algebra (Books and PDFs on)It becomes a book for that subject (Books and study)
A .kurooto-course fileA class pack to review (Classes and timetable)
A voice messageWritten out and handled like typed text (Talking to Kurooto)
Anything elseFiles into notes, as above

On the phone. The Android app's share menu sends files to your notes too (Phone app).

Limits and edge cases.

  • Telegram lets bots download files up to 20 MB. A bigger file gets "Couldn't get that file: Telegram only lets bots download files up to 20 MB". Copy it into the folder yourself instead.
  • With files.folder set, the AI is told never to copy the file into the notes folder; it links to it where it is.
  • With Files into notes off, a file you send is still saved in the inbox, but nobody files it.

What leaves your computer. The file travels through Telegram to your computer. With Chat on, the AI you picked reads it to file it.

Turn it off. Features → Files into notes → Turn off. Saved files and notes stay.

Quick notes and prompts

What it does. /note saves a thought straight into your inbox, word for word. The prompt library keeps prompts you reuse, ready to copy. No AI is involved.

Turn it on. Features → Quick notes and prompts. Needs Telegram.

From Telegram.

CommandWhat it does
/note buy graph paperSaves inbox/<date>-<first words>.md with your text as written. Never overwrites: a second note with the same name gets -2.
A photo or file with /note (and optional text) as its captionThe note links the file.
/promptsLists the prompts in your prompts/ folder (subfolders allowed; a README.md is skipped), and your add-ons' prompts as add-ons/<name>. A prompt that's also a skill shows "(also /name)".
/prompt <name>Sends that prompt back so you can copy it. Matches the name, file name or title in any case, or a unique part of one. Several matches: it lists them.

Example of saving a prompt:

/prompt save summarise
Summarise this in 5 bullet points, keeping every number.

A prompt whose header has skill: true (or skill: <name>) is also a skill you can run as a command; see Skills and routines.

From a terminal. kurooto note "an idea" saves a quick note the same way; pipe text in with some-command | kurooto note --title "output". It works even when Kurooto isn't running.

What leaves your computer. Nothing.

Turn it off. Features → Quick notes and prompts → Turn off. Notes and prompts stay.

Notes brain (search by meaning)

What it does. Builds an index of your notes so you can search them by meaning, not just exact words (/notes what is a compact space), and gives your AI a memory of them: it uses the same search when it answers you, on calls too. Kurooto installs GBrain for this and keeps its index in Kurooto's own data folder.

Turn it on. Features → Notes brain (GBrain) → Set up. It installs Bun and GBrain (about 0.5 GB, which is why it isn't in the Starter set), creates the brain and reads your notes into it once.

How the index is made (gbrain.embeddings, default auto):

ModeWhen auto picks itWhere note text goes
ollamaComputers with a graphics card or Apple Silicon (a small model on this computer)Stays on your computer
geminiComputers without one, when you have a Gemini keyNote text goes to Google to make the index
offNo graphics card and no Gemini keyKeyword search only; nothing leaves

From Telegram.

  • /notes <what you're looking for>: the best matches from your notes.
  • /timeline <note>: a note's dated log, oldest first, e.g. /timeline research/reading-group.
  • Ask your AI in plain words ("what did I write about eigenvalues?", "when did I last work on the reading group?"): with Chat on, it searches the brain itself.

Timelines (gbrain.timelines, on). Every night, dated log lines in your notes, written like - **2026-10-19** | source — what happened, become that note's timeline. Kurooto's own skills and features write their log lines this way (journal, research project logs). No AI is involved and nothing leaves your computer.

Course pages (gbrain.course_pages, on). Every night each subject's course page (stats/course.md, or maps/courses/stats.md in the zettelkasten layout) gets one block of Kurooto's own, between ` and `:

## Statistics at a glance
Kept up to date by Kurooto each night; the rest of the page is yours.

Classes: Mon 09:00-09:55, Wed 11:00-11:55.

### Key concepts
- [[stats/bayes-theorem|Bayes' theorem]]

### Timeline
- **2026-10-18** | lecture — [[stats/2026-10-18-priors|Lecture 4: priors]]
- **2026-10-27** | deadline — Problem set 3 at 17:00

Key concepts are the concept notes the course's own notes link to most (up to 10). The timeline holds its lecture notes, its deadlines (done ones too) and the term note's key dates for it, 120 days back and ahead. Its lines are in the timeline format, so after the next night /timeline stats/course shows the course's timeline and your AI (and a call, through note_timeline) can ask it "what's coming up in stats?". Only the block is ever rewritten; a page that isn't there yet is made. Rules only, nothing leaves the computer. *Undo:* turn it off; the block stays until you delete it.

Nightly upkeep (gbrain.upkeep, off). GBrain's maintenance at 03:00 with light steps only: backlinks, facts, the index and orphaned pages (timelines are part of it when it's on). Steps that would rewrite your note files or call an AI model are left out. It's heavy, so on a laptop it waits for the charger. If it fails you get one message ("The notes brain's nightly upkeep didn't finish ... Search still works"), with where its log is, and not again until it has worked once. Logs are kept two weeks.

Choosing the upkeep's steps (gbrain.upkeep_steps). Empty means the light steps above. You can list GBrain's own phase names instead, for example ["lint", "backlinks", "sync", "extract", "extract_facts", "consolidate", "embed", "orphans"]. Know what you add: lint and the repair steps rewrite note files, and consolidate, synthesize or edge_contradictions call the AI model GBrain is set up with (on a paid key, they spend money). Kurooto never runs purge (it deletes pages for good), and unknown names are skipped. *Undo:* empty the list.

Weekly checkup (gbrain.checkup, off). GBrain's own advisor, Sundays at 19:30. You get a message only when something is a warning or worse, listing up to 8 findings, with buttons:

  • Ignore these: adds them to gbrain.checkup_ignore, so the checkup doesn't mention them again.
  • Update the brain (only when the brain needs a schema update, and only for Kurooto's own brain): runs GBrain's update. "The brain is busy" means try again later.

A button on an old checkup says "That checkup is old; the next one comes on Sunday."

Weekly checkup line (gbrain.checkup_line, on). At the same time, one line on how the brain is, from GBrain's doctor (read only: it never updates the brain's schema): "Notes brain checkup: all well (score 95/100), 31 checks passed." or "Notes brain checkup (score 85/100): 2 things to look at; first: run gbrain embed --stale". With the checkup above on and findings to show, the line opens that message instead of coming on its own. It has Calm's Why? and Less of this; *Undo:* gbrain.checkup_line = false.

Contradictions (gbrain.contradictions, on). If you run GBrain's contradictions probe yourself (gbrain eval suspected-contradictions; it uses the AI model GBrain is set up with, so Kurooto never runs it), the real disagreements it found (not renamings, not things that changed over time) join Dreaming's "Worth a look in what I remember" section in the morning (Dreaming), with Keep A / Keep B / Both true. Both sides are your notes, so Kurooto only says where each one is; nothing is changed. Kurooto only reads the last report.

On calls. With the notes brain on, a call's search_notes searches it by meaning (a whole question works: "when is the stats quiz?"), and note_timeline reads a note's or a course page's dated entries.

Your own GBrain. If you already run GBrain yourself, Kurooto can use that brain instead of making its own:

Then press Set up; it only checks that your brain answers. From then on Kurooto searches it, adds pages through gbrain's own commands (books, for example), runs the nightly steps you turned on, and, if you named gbrain.source, runs gbrain sync and gbrain embed --stale for that source every 15 minutes. Limits and edge cases.

  • "The notes brain is busy with its upkeep; try again in a while": the upkeep or a sync is using it.
  • "Nothing in your notes about that": no match.
  • Kurooto pins GBrain's version and turns off GBrain's own update check.
  • On battery (in the default saver mode) the 15-minute re-reading, timelines and upkeep wait for the charger; /notes still works on what's indexed.

What leaves your computer. Nothing with a graphics card or Apple Silicon. In gemini mode, note text goes to Google (your key) to make the index; on Google's free tier, Google may use it to improve its products.

Turn it off / undo. Features → Notes brain → Turn off. The index stays in Kurooto's data folder; your notes are untouched. For your own GBrain: set gbrain.managed back to true; agy mcp remove gbrain takes its tools away from agy.

Notes sync (GitHub)

What it does. Keeps your notes folder in your own private GitHub repository. Every change is kept in its history (so nothing written is ever lost), and edits made elsewhere (another computer, GitHub's website, a phone app that syncs with the repository) come in too. With Phone calls on, your call line can read whole notes from the repository while your computer is off.

Turn it on (about 5 minutes).

  1. Make a repository: any name, Private, nothing ticked.
  2. Make a fine-grained token: Repository access → Only select repositories → that one; Permissions → Contents → Read and write.
  3. Features → Notes sync: paste the repository as owner/name and the token, press Save.

Git is installed for you if it's missing (MinGit on Windows; on a Mac, Apple's installer opens: click Install, then press Set up again). Kurooto checks the repository first and refuses:

  • a public repository ("anyone could read your notes");
  • a token that can only read ("it needs Contents: Read and write");
  • a repository the token can't see (pick it under Repository access).

When it syncs.

  • Every 10 minutes (notes_sync.every).
  • About 2 minutes after an AI request finishes, so what it wrote goes up soon (notes_sync.after_jobs; one push for a burst of requests; 0 waits for the timer).
  • When Kurooto stops (a shutdown, an update), quickly, unless Kurooto is paused.
  • When you ask: /sync.

Each sync saves what changed here (one commit, "notes: <date time>"), takes in what changed elsewhere, then sends it up.

A notes folder that's already a Git repository. Its origin is used as it is when it's the same repository (HTTPS or SSH). An origin that points at another repository is left alone, and Kurooto adds a remote of its own called kurooto. A .gitignore is written if there's none (system junk files, Obsidian's workspace files, half-downloaded files).

When a note changed in two places at once (notes_sync.on_conflict):

  • keep both (default): the note keeps the version from elsewhere, and this computer's version goes beside it as <note> (conflict, this computer, <date time>).md. You get a message naming both. Merge them when you like and delete the copy. A note deleted on one side and changed on the other is kept, changed. Notes changed on one side only merge as usual.
  • stop: sync stops, changes nothing, and tells you (at most every 6 hours) until you've sorted it out yourself in the notes folder (git pull --rebase, fix the files, git add, git rebase --continue). It never forces anything.

A merge or rebase left half done in the notes folder is left alone; sync waits until it's finished. A notes folder that's not on a branch ("detached HEAD") also stops sync until you check your branch out again.

From Telegram. /sync syncs your notes now ("Notes synced." or "Couldn't sync: <why>"). /sync all syncs everything (below).

Settings that matter (Dashboard → Settings → notes_sync):

SettingDefaultEffect
notes_sync.everyevery 10mHow often it syncs
notes_sync.after_jobs2Minutes after an AI request before its notes go up; 0 = wait for the timer
notes_sync.on_conflictkeep bothkeep both or stop
notes_sync.share_with_callsonYour call line may read notes from the repository while the computer is off

Messages it sends by itself. "Notes sync: N note(s) changed here and elsewhere at once..." (a clash kept as copies), and "Notes sync: <problem>." at most every 6 hours while something is wrong (can't reach the repository, can't push, unfinished merge).

Limits and edge cases.

  • On battery (saver mode) the 10-minute sync waits for the charger. /sync works any time.
  • The files folder (files.folder) isn't synced.
  • /health warns when the GitHub token expires within 10 days (GitHub also emails you 7 days before).
  • Two computers can sync the same notes repository; conflicts are handled as above.

What leaves your computer. Your notes, to your own private GitHub repository. With Phone calls on, your call line can read them from there.

Turn it off / undo. Features → Notes sync → Turn off. The repository and its history stay on GitHub; delete the repository there if you want them gone. notes_sync.on_conflict = stop undoes the keep-both behaviour. Conflict copies are ordinary notes: delete them once merged.

Sync everything

What it does. Runs every sync that's on, one after another, in the background, then sends one line per feature, for example "Calendar: ok, 23 events". The order:

  1. your notes (to take in what changed elsewhere),
  2. Google: Classroom, Classroom → notes and the mail digest,
  3. your calendars,
  4. research project logs,
  5. the notes brain's index,
  6. a backup (with Backups on; skipped, not failed, when the backup folder isn't there, e.g. the drive is unplugged),
  7. your notes again (to send up what the others just wrote).

A feature that's off is left out. Only one runs at a time: "A sync is already running; you'll get its summary."

From Telegram. /sync all, /sync everything, or in plain words "sync everything", "sync it all", "sync all the things". With Notes sync off, plain /sync does the same.

From a terminal (Kurooto running):

CommandWhat it does
kurooto syncRuns it and waits, then prints the summary
kurooto sync --no-waitStarts it and returns; the summary comes as a message
kurooto sync --statusWhether one is running and how the last one went (works with Kurooto stopped)

Concept graph

What it does. When a book has been read (see Books and study), or Classroom has filed a lecture's slides or handouts into your notes, its concepts are woven into your notes:

  • a concept note for each new concept (its definition, formulas, links, and where it came from);
  • typed links to notes you already have, each with a relation and a one-sentence reason, e.g. - special-case-of:: [[linear-algebra/eigenvalues]] — the diagonal case of the spectral theorem;
  • a "Seen in" list on each concept: the book and pages, plus "Added from the books" lines for what an existing note lacked;
  • in the book's own note, which concepts each stretch of pages introduces;
  • on the subject's course page, a "From the books" list of the concepts its books introduced.

Your own text is never changed. Kurooto adds lines and keeps its parts between `` markers; a write that would touch anything else is refused.

The relation words are: needs, generalises, special-case-of, example-of, contrasts-with, equivalent-to, part-of, used-in.

What gets read (integrate.sources, default ["books", "classroom"]):

  • books: each book Books and PDFs has read.
  • classroom: each file Classroom → notes files with real text: lecture slides, handouts, PDFs, solutions, 1,500 characters or more (a one-line announcement isn't read). Its course is the subject, so its concept notes go where that course's do. The "Seen in" line points at the lecture note the file was filed into (else the course page), and that lecture note gets a "Concepts in <file>" list of its own. A textbook filed from Classroom (0.6.1: a course's "Books" post with the PDF attached, say) is read like a book and gets a note of its own, like a book's: sources/<subject>/books/<its title>.md, titled with the file's name, with Kurooto's part (what it is, where it was filed, the concepts each stretch of pages introduced) between markers. Its concepts' "Seen in" points there, and the class note it came with gets one line pointing at it, instead of every concept becoming a link to that note. Class material or a textbook is told apart the way study packs do it: more than 80 pages is a book; 40 or fewer, or a title that says lecture, slides, notes, handout and the like, is class material; in between, the title decides (a title with "edition", "textbook" or "handbook" is a book). Textbooks read before 0.6.1 move over by themselves the first time 0.6.1 starts: only Kurooto's "Seen in" blocks change, each file's before-copy is kept, and it's one Git commit per textbook.
  • handwritten (add it to turn it on): your handwritten notes, once written out and linked; "Seen in" points at the handwritten note. Notes with no subject are left out.

Each item is read once: it's known by the hash of its text, so the same file filed again (downloaded a second time, say) isn't read again, and an edited one is. Class files and handwritten notes are cut the same way as books: a PDF by its pages, slides by slide, anything else in parts of about 3,000 characters; "Seen in" gives pages or slides, not parts. A copy of each item's text is kept in Kurooto's state folder (integrate/texts/).

One queue, one budget. Books and the rest share the queue and the night's budget (below): the newest class material first, since it's what you're studying now, then the books in their order. /graph shows what's waiting by source: "Waiting to be read: 2 books, 5 class files, 1 handwritten." The feature's card on the dashboard has a switch for each source.

Class files filed before 0.5.19 aren't read on their own: that would be a whole semester in one night. /graph backfill classroom (or Also read the Classroom files filed before on the card) queues them, and /graph backfill handwritten the handwritten notes linked before (/graph backfill all does every source that's on): the oldest course first, each course's files in date order, after whatever is filed from now on, so they're read night by night within the budget. It finds them in the folder Classroom → notes downloads to; run it again and it queues nothing new.

Turn it on. Features → Concept graph. Needs Books and PDFs on (and at least one book read). It works best with an agent program (agy or Claude Code) installed; see "Which AI" below.

How one batch goes.

  1. The book is cut into batches of about 12 pages (3 to 20) that follow its sections: the PDF's bookmarks, numbered headings, the running headers at the top of pages, or "Chapter 3 ..." lines. A margin note such as "Section 9.1" is never taken for a heading. (A book the graph began before 0.5.8 keeps the batches it began with.) A batch waits until its pages are read; a page the reader skipped (waiting for OCR, say) doesn't hold it back once the book's reading is over.
  2. On this computer, without an AI, Kurooto finds the few existing notes that may be related: matching names and aliases, keyword ranking, the notes those link to, and, where there's a model for it, meaning (Ollama's nomic-embed-text, or your Gemini key). With a helper model on this computer, a reranker then orders them by how likely each belongs with the new concept (no extra download, no AI requests).
  3. One AI request is shown the pages and those notes only, and answers with the concepts the pages develop, how each relates to your notes, and what an existing note lacks.
  4. Plain checks run on the answer, without trusting the AI's opinion of itself:
  5. the quote it gives must really be in those pages;
  6. a link's reason must say something specific ("related to", "see also", "connected" are refused);
  7. a link must point at a note that exists, or one made in the same batch;
  8. "this is your existing note X" needs the names or the meaning to agree;
  9. only the relation words above; two notes can't each generalise the other;
  10. a concept with the same name as an existing note is never made a second time.
  11. Anything doubtful goes to a second look by the other AI program (or the same one, told to be strict), and is dropped unless that confirms it; "this is your existing note X" that isn't confirmed becomes a note of its own and leaves X alone. There's no queue for you to review.
  12. What's left is written and, if your notes are a Git repository, committed: one commit per batch, so git revert <commit> undoes exactly that batch.

When it runs.

  • Each night from 01:30 (integrate.at), batch after batch, and right after a book has been read. If you wake early, it starts earlier: 5 hours before you wake (your alarm, else the night hours in Settings → availability), so a Claude 5-hour window it opens has reset by the time you get up; never before your bedtime, never later than integrate.at.
  • Every 3 hours it carries on if something was waiting (a limit that has reset). When the AI was at its limit or signed out, it tries again after 20 minutes. A batch that fails 3 times is marked failed.
  • How much a night is fitted to your AI by itself (see How much it reads a night below), so it never uses up what you need yourself.
  • Books with the fewest pages go first (integrate.order), so the most books get done soonest; list subjects to do first in integrate.first.
  • Effort: the small, quick model by default; the next one up for a batch that failed before, or for a book whose recent batches needed many second looks.

How much it reads a night (integrate.per_night = auto, the default). A night runs from one night's start to the next, so batches read during the day count toward the night before.

  • Claude Code or agy (a subscription): your plan's usage is shared between Kurooto and your own chats, and the numbers aren't published, so Kurooto learns them. The first night it reads 30 batches (or as many as it ever read in one night before, if that's more). Each night that read its whole budget without hitting a limit, the next night reads 50% more (up to 300). When a limit is hit, the budget is halved and nothing more is read until that limit resets. A reset more than about 6 hours away means Claude's weekly limit rather than the 5-hour window: it waits for it, and the next week starts lower. Once a limit has shown how many batches fit in one window, a night reads at most 60% of that (integrate.share_pct), so the rest of the window is yours. What fit is forgotten after 14 days without a limit, so a bigger plan is noticed.
  • A free Gemini key: 60% of its daily limit (learned the first time it's reached). If that's under 10 batches a night, /graph and the feature's card say so: agy (free with a Google account) or the model on this computer reads books much better.
  • An API key (another OpenAI-compatible service): a money budget, integrate.max_cost_per_night ($0.50), from the cost each answer reports, or estimated from its tokens at integrate.price_per_mtok (set it to your model's prices).
  • The model on this computer (Ollama): no budget; on battery it waits for the charger as before.
  • A number in integrate.per_night (say 60) is a hard ceiling on all of that: it never reads more in a night.

/graph shows tonight's budget and how it was decided ("Tonight's reading budget: 15 batches (learned: 15 batches, 60% of the 25 that fit last night); 4 read so far."), and the last limit ("Last limit: the 5-hour window on Fri 23 Oct 03:10 after 25 batches in it; reset at 06:30."). /usage and the feature's card on the dashboard show the same.

The daily second pass ("garden"), at 06:20 (integrate.garden): the notes the reading touched, then notes that nothing links to, are checked once more against everything that's there now (up to 40 a day, integrate.garden_per_day). It also takes out links Kurooto wrote to notes that don't exist any more (a concept dropped after a second look, or a note you deleted). Your own links are never touched.

Look-alike concept notes (integrate.duplicates, on). Each morning at 06:25, up to three pairs of concept notes that look like one concept come as one message: the same name ("Back-propagation" and "Backpropagation"), a name they share ("Brownian motion" is an alias of the "Wiener process" note), or text that means nearly the same (embeddings at least 0.92 alike, where this computer has them). Lecture notes, course pages and the books' notes are never paired. Each pair has three buttons (two pairs at most when the phone app's chat is your only channel, as it shows six buttons):

  • Merge: the first note (the one more notes link to) keeps everything: the other's links, its "Seen in" lines, its names as aliases, and its notation, plus a "Merged here" part saying what came from it. The other note becomes a short redirect ("Merged into [[...]]") under its title. Anything you wrote in it stays; only a note Kurooto wrote all of (and you haven't changed since) shrinks to the redirect, its text having moved into the kept note. Links Kurooto wrote elsewhere to the old note point at the kept one; new reading under the old name lands there too.
  • Keep both: they're different; that pair is never asked about again.
  • Add alias: both notes stay; the second name becomes an alias of the first and each links the other as equivalent-to.

Every answer comes back with Undo, which puts the notes back as they were (a note you changed since stays as it is, and the message says so). A pair you don't answer comes back after two weeks; /graph same asks now, those included. Nothing is merged without your tap, and nothing you wrote is deleted.

Notation side by side (integrate.notation, on; a switch on the Concept graph card in Dashboard → Features: tables come whenever the graph reads books, class files or handwritten notes). The reading also notes how the pages write each concept: the symbol (B_t, W_t, \hat f(\xi)) or a convention in words (where the 2π goes in a Fourier transform), each with a short quote from the pages. A row whose quote isn't in those pages, or doesn't hold the symbol, is dropped, like any other claim. When a second source (another book, a class file, your handwritten notes) writes the concept differently, its note gets a Notation table in Kurooto's part of the note:

SourceNotationWhat it stands for
Øksendal: Stochastic Differential Equations, pp. 11–20$B_t$Brownian motion at time t
Lecture 4 slides, slides 3–9$W_t$Brownian motion at time t

One source, or sources that agree, give no table.

Handwritten notes join the graph too. Each written-out handwritten note is linked with the same candidate search and one request to the second-look AI: to the lecture it was written in, the course page and the concept notes it covers, with a real title and its doubts listed (/handwritten link for older ones; details on Books and study).

Review the links. With Quiz on, every link between two concept notes is also a link recall card: "How is Itô's lemma used in Fokker-Planck?", answered in your own words, with the reason and the book pages (and the subject's own notes and Classroom files) to read after. Links with a reason in your course notes become cards too, and new cards about what you met in class lately come first. /card for one now, /cards on for up to five a day.

The morning line (integrate.summary), at 07:10 when anything happened: "Concept graph: 14 batches, +22 concept notes, +61 links, 9 notes updated; 30 AI requests (about $0.40 at API prices); 5 doubtful, 2 dropped after a second look." The cost is what the requests would cost at API prices; a subscription plan isn't billed per request (its own limit is what runs out).

Gaps you keep asking about. With Dreaming on, a topic you asked about 3 or more times this week that has no note, or only a stub, gets one line in the morning message, with Make a stub: a concept note where your layout keeps them (the subject's folder, or zettel/), linked from the course page under "Concepts", for you (or the next book) to fill in. A note you changed 10 to 30 days ago may come back there too ("You last worked on Itô's lemma 12 days ago"). See Dreaming.

Which AI (integrate.roles, e.g. {"bulk": "agy", "check": "claude"}). Empty (the default) chooses for you:

  • agy and Claude Code both installed: they split the work (bulk and second look) and run side by side;
  • one of them: it does both;
  • neither: your main AI does both. With a free Gemini key's daily limit that's slow (the budget keeps 40% of the day for you): set integrate.first to your current subjects, or read with agy or the model on this computer.

Without a graphics card nothing changes except that the meaning-based matching comes from your Gemini key or is skipped.

Limits and edge cases.

  • It's heavy work: on battery (saver mode) it waits for the charger.
  • After /graph stop, a batch already sent still finishes, but nothing new starts (not the retries either) until the next night's start (integrate.at). To stop it for longer, turn the feature off.
  • A restart (an update, say) during the night (0.6.1): a batch whose request the restart cut off is asked again at once, not three hours later, and when Kurooto starts again inside tonight's reading window (from the night's start until you wake, by your alarm or night hours; else until 6 hours after integrate.at), the reading carries on a minute later, within tonight's budget, unless /graph stop holds it.
  • Page numbers are the PDF's own (page 1 is the first page of the file, often the cover).

What leaves your computer. A few pages of a book's text (or of a class file, or of your handwritten notes when you add them) at a time, and the titles and opening lines of the related notes, go to the AI you picked, like any request. The matching, the checks and the reranker run on this computer.

Turn it off / undo.

  • Features → Concept graph → Turn off stops it. Notes already written stay.
  • git revert <commit> undoes one batch (each batch is one commit, named like books: <title> pp. 41-52 (+3 notes, +7 links, 2 updated), or classroom: <file> (...)).
  • Take classroom (or handwritten) out of integrate.sources, or untick it on the card, to stop reading it; what was queued waits until it's back.
  • Each batch also keeps a before-copy of every file it changed in Kurooto's state folder, integrate/undo/ (the last 60 batches; the Space keeper removes copies older than 30 days).
