Quiz and exams

Practice questions from your own notes, spaced so you see each one again just before you'd forget it; a stronger routine in the two weeks before a quiz or exam; an optional exam-season mode for the whole exam period, with a revision plan before each exam; revision packs as PDFs; and a hint ladder for problems you're stuck on.

Quiz

What it does. Turns lists in your notes into questions, asks a few at a time on Telegram or on a call, and reschedules each by how well you knew it (items you know come back less often; ones you forgot come back tomorrow).

Turn it on. Dashboard → Features → Quiz (needs Telegram). With Deadlines on it follows your quizzes and exams; with Phone calls on it can quiz you by call; with Chat on your AI grades your answers.

Where the questions come from. Any note whose file name contains exam-prep or quiz (for example linear-algebra/exam-prep.md). Every bullet line under one of these headings is one question:

## Must know
- The rank-nullity theorem: dim V = rank T + nullity T for T: V → W linear.
- A matrix is invertible exactly when its determinant is non-zero.

Headings that count: Must know, Typical questions, Key results, Key theorems, Key definitions, Definitions, Theorems, Formulas. A bullet needs at least 12 characters. The note's folder name is the subject. Classroom → notes keeps such an exam-prep note for each course, and /exam-prep <subject> (see Books and study) brings one up to date. Edit the note any time; new bullets join at the next quiz.

With Phone calls on, /quiz first asks Here or by call? (buttons Here / By call). With quiz.by_call on as well, /quiz and "quiz me" call you straight away; add "here" to keep it on Telegram.

Answering. Reply to the question with your answer. In the Android app, quiz questions are cards under Waiting for you with a box for the answer; self-grading comes as the next card (phone-app.md).

  • With Chat on, your AI gives the key idea and a grade from 0 to 5, and the next question follows.
  • Without Chat (or if the AI fails), Kurooto shows what your note says and you grade yourself: Forgot (0), Hard (3), Good (4), Easy (5).
  • A session is quiz.per_session questions (3; 6 in exam mode). It ends with "That's the session. /quiz for more." A session left unfinished is forgotten at midnight.

The evening before a quiz or exam. From 19:00 (quiz.offer_at) the evening before something on your deadline list that's a quiz or exam: "Linear Algebra quiz is tomorrow at 10:00. Practise? 7 questions are due." with Quiz me now, Call me for a quiz (with Phone calls on) and Not tonight. On the day itself, if it's at least 2 hours away and wasn't offered yet, you get the same with No thanks. If you have no material for it yet, it says where to put some. Never during your night hours. Once per quiz or exam.

On calls. Say "quiz me" on any call for a spoken quiz: the assistant asks a question, listens, grades it and moves on. In exam mode the call's brief says how many questions are due. A missed quiz call becomes a message: "Missed the quiz call. /quiz la practises here instead."

What leaves your computer. With Chat on, the question and your answer go to your AI to grade it. Otherwise nothing.

Turn it off / undo. Features → Quiz → Turn off; your progress stays in Kurooto's state folder (quiz/). quiz.by_call off brings back Here or by call?.

Link recall cards

What it does. The links between your concept notes become questions. Each link the concept graph writes (or one you write the same way), like - used-in:: [[zettel/fokker-planck]] — its generator gives the drift and diffusion terms, is a card: "How is Itô's lemma used in Fokker-Planck?". You answer in your own words, see what the note says and where to read, and grade yourself. Spacing is the quiz's: a card you knew comes back later, one you forgot comes back tomorrow.

Where the cards come from.

  • The concept notes' links, whatever the concept graph read them from: books, Classroom files, your handwritten notes. A concept read from a class file belongs to that file's course.
  • Your course notes (with Subjects on): a lecture note or course page linking a concept note with a line like the one above, or a plain - [[itos-lemma]]: the professor proved it with a second-order Taylor step (a reason of five words or more; "see also" or a bare link isn't asked about). That card asks "How does Diffusions relate to Itô's lemma?" and shows "Diffusions → Itô's lemma" with your reason.

New cards about what you met in class in the last 14 days (a concept from a class file dated then, or a dated lecture note) come before other new ones.

Turn it on. It's part of Quiz. /card gives a card any time; for cards that come by themselves, /cards on (or quiz.links in Settings → Quiz).

A card, step by step.

  1. "Link card (Stochastic Calculus): How is Itô's lemma used in Fokker-Planck? Reply in your own words."
  2. Reply to it on Telegram, or with Reply in the phone app.
  3. You see the link's reason, then Where to read: the book pages where the two concepts are met (from the concept graph's "Seen in"), and the best match in that subject's own notes and Classroom files.
  4. Grade it: Forgot (0), Hard (3), Good (4), Easy (5). After Forgot, the first place to read is repeated. In the phone app the grade buttons come as their own message.

When they come. With quiz.links on, up to quiz.links_per_day cards (3; never more than 5) at the times in quiz.links_at (11:00, 16:00 and 20:00), one at a time. They reach the phone app too (they're questions). Never during a class, focus time, a call or the night: a card that falls then comes 5 minutes after it ends (the same day). A card you haven't answered holds back the next one that day. Cards you've seen come before new ones.

From Telegram.

Local check (optional). With quiz.links_check on and Local AI models on, the small model on this computer says in one sentence how close your answer came, before you grade. It's never a cloud AI, and you still grade.

Exam mode

What it does. Two weeks before each quiz or exam on your deadline list, that subject is in exam mode until the exam has passed. You're told once when it starts. While it's on:

  • /exam (or "exam mode", "when's my next exam?", "when is my next quiz?") lists each subject's next quiz or exam with how many questions are due;
  • calls start knowing it, and a spoken quiz picks that subject;
  • quiz sessions are longer (quiz.exam_per_session), and items you know still come back at least once before the exam;
  • with quiz.exam_daily set, a practice check-in every day: "Exam mode: Linear Algebra midterm in 5 days. 9 questions to practise. Now?" with Quiz me now, Call me and Skip today (not the day before the exam, when the evening offer takes its place). With quiz.by_call on it calls instead.

Exams come from whatever feeds your deadline list: /due, your notes, Classroom, your calendar, recurring quizzes, the term note, a class pack.

Settings. deadlines.exam_mode_days (14; 0 turns exam mode off). The full deadline settings are in Reminders and deadlines.

Exam season

This is optional and off by default. It isn't part of the Starter or Everything choices in setup: turn it on by hand in Dashboard → Features → Exam season. It needs Deadlines.

What it does. One card for the whole exam period, on top of exam mode. It does nothing without exam dates: the exam periods in Subjects (/term exams 2026-12-07 to 2026-12-18, or your term note, a class pack) and the exams on your deadline list whose title says exam, midterm, final, mid-sem, end-sem, viva or oral. Quizzes and tests count only inside an exam period, unless you turn on exam_season.quizzes. A season runs from the day before its first exam to the end of its last; exam days up to three days apart are one season. You're told once when one starts, with what it will do ("Exam season: Mon 7 Dec to Fri 18 Dec, 5 exams. Until it's over: …").

Its parts. Each has its own switch: on the card in the dashboard's Features tab, with /examseason on Telegram (tap a part to switch it on or off), or in Settings → Exam season. All are on when you turn the feature on.

On calls. On the exam day and the day before, every call's brief has the exam and what you said about it.

The revision plan

What it does. From revision_days (14) before an exam, the exam's course gets a revision plan: its topics, weakest first, laid into your free time before the exam, one topic per block, with what to read. Plain arithmetic on this computer: no AI. It needs the exam to have a course (/due 2026-12-08 09:00 Linear Algebra final exam, with the course in the title or set on the deadline).

Where the topics come from. The course's exam prep note (<subject>/exam-prep.md): each line under Covered so far (the exam-prep skill writes it), then the short lines under Gaps; Topics or Syllabus work too. A line's link is the topic's note; "Lecture 5: Eigenvalues" is the topic "Eigenvalues". Without an exam prep note, the course's lecture notes (<subject>/2026-11-05-....md), in date order; without those, its concept notes (the concept graph's for the course, and the other notes in its folder).

How they're ranked. Four groups, in this order: topics with no note yet; a stub (a note with under 50 words of your own; a missed-class stub counts until you fill it in); weak recall (its quiz grades and your answers to the evening question average under 3 of 5); the rest. In each group the weakest recall comes first, then the course's order. A topic's quiz items are the quiz questions that name it or link to its note. A topic you said Yes to isn't laid again for revision_days.

When the blocks go. Into Start-by's free time: each day's window (startby.window, so never at night) less your classes and calendar events, and here also your commitments and the exams themselves. revision_share (50%) of each day's free time goes to blocks of block_min (60) minutes. Each block goes to the soonest exam still ahead that has topics left, and ends before you leave for it (leave_min). An exam gets at most as many blocks as Start-by's size for it (10 hours until you say: /startby exam la a week); /revise says when topics didn't fit.

What to read. With each topic: its own note, then the pages of class material that name it (Classroom's downloaded files, your files folder: "Lecture 5 slides.pdf, pp. 3-7"), then book pages (the pages the concept graph found it on, and pages of books Books has read that name it). Class material always comes first.

Every day. Today's blocks are fixed the first time they're worked out that day; later days are worked out again as your notes and answers change. They're in the morning plan with Start-by's lines (the morning page, the wake-up call, the drafted plan; with Start-by dates on) and on the Exam season card. At the evening check-in's time (night_at, 21:30, without Check-ins) one message asks about the day's topics: "Revision: did you cover Jordan form (Linear Algebra) today?" with Yes, Partly and No. The answer counts like a quiz answer: the topic's quiz items are graded (Yes as Good, 4 of 5; Partly 2; No 1, so they come back tomorrow) and the topic keeps the grade too. Tapping another answer replaces the first; /revise undo takes the last one back.

From Telegram. /revise shows today's blocks and the next days'; /revise la one course's topics, ranked, with why; "what should I revise today?" today's blocks.

Turn it off / undo. Features → Exam season → Turn off: its timers, leave-by alarms (also on your call line) and the update hold go at once, and calls during exams are allowed again. Or switch off just the part you don't want. Debriefs already written stay in your notes; your answers are kept in Kurooto's state folder (exam_season.json) for 30 days after each exam. The revision plan writes nothing in your notes; its days and your answers are in revision.json (14 days of plans), and /revise undo puts a topic's quiz grades back as they were.

Revision packs

What it does. /pack reads a subject's notes (course page, exam prep, lecture notes, solutions, and your books' pages in the source mix) and builds a 2 to 6 page revision pack: what's on it, definitions, theorems with their hypotheses, formulas, worked problems, pitfalls and a self-test with answers. It comes to Telegram as a PDF and is saved in your notes (<subject>/revision-pack-<date>.tex and .pdf; revision-packs/ when the subject has no folder; your files folder when you set one).

Turn it on. It's part of Skills (needs Chat): Features → Skills.

From Telegram. /pack linear algebra, or say what it's for: /pack linear algebra midterm friday. It takes a few minutes. /revision-pack is the same skill. Your AI can also send you a pack you made before ("send me my linear algebra pack").

Making the PDF. Kurooto uses Tectonic (a small LaTeX engine) if it's installed, else your computer's own LaTeX (latexmk or pdflatex). With neither, the pack comes as a .tex file and a message asks Install Tectonic / Not now. Install fetches about 25 MB from Tectonic's official releases; the first PDF then takes a few minutes while it fetches about 50 MB of LaTeX packages into Kurooto's cache; later ones take seconds. After Not now it doesn't ask again for 30 days. If a pack doesn't compile, the AI gets one try to fix its LaTeX; otherwise you get the .tex with the first error line.

Packs before exams. With Deadlines on, the evening before a quiz or exam that has a subject (12 to 36 hours ahead, from 16:00) you're asked "Linear Algebra midterm is tomorrow 10:00. A revision pack for it tonight?" with Build it / No thanks, once per quiz or exam. With skills.auto_pack = auto it's built without asking and comes like a /pack (in the background, never ahead of your own requests).

Settings that matter (Dashboard → Settings → Skills)

What leaves your computer. The notes and book excerpts the AI reads go to your AI. Tectonic's install and its LaTeX packages are downloaded from Tectonic's servers.

Turn it off / undo. skills.auto_pack = off stops the offers; packs already made stay where they are. Tectonic lives in Kurooto's own folder and goes with kurooto uninstall (see Updates and backups).

Hint ladder

What it does. Stuck on a problem? You get one step at a time: the idea first, then the setup, then the key step. The full solution comes only when you ask for it. Every step names the lecture note the problem belongs to.

Turn it on. Features → Hint ladder (needs Subjects; uses your AI).

From Telegram.

  • Send a photo of the problem with /hint la as the caption, or type it: /hint la show that the trace is basis-free. The subject's short name goes first (with only one subject, it's used anyway).
  • Next hint (or reply "next", "more", "go on") gives the next step.
  • After the key step, Show the solution (or reply "solution", "answer", "show me the answer" to any hint) gives the full solution.

The course's AI policy. The first time you use /hint for a subject, Kurooto asks what the course allows: Hints and full solutions, Hints only, no full solutions, No AI help, or your own words. Your answer becomes a line in the subject's course note: AI policy: Hints only, no full solutions (your answer to Kurooto, 2026-10-20). After that it reads the line from the note, so change it there; a line you wrote yourself (AI policy: ...) counts too. A policy set for the course itself (/subject policy la hints, or the AI help box in the dashboard's Study pane: allowed, hints, off) comes first, and then nothing is asked.

  • "No full solutions" or "hints only": the ladder ends at the key step.
  • "No AI help", "not allowed": no hints for that subject, and nothing is sent to the AI.

How it's made. The AI works out the whole ladder once and Kurooto shows it a step at a time. An answer that doesn't come in steps is never passed on (it could give the solution away): you get "No hints this time" and can /hint again. If the photo can't be read, it asks you to send it sharper or typed. It's the shipped hint skill: copy it into your skills folder (/skills copy hint) to change how hints are written.

Limits. Photos need an AI that opens files itself (agy or Claude Code). With a Gemini key or another API service, type the problem instead.

What leaves your computer. The problem (photo or text), the subject's AI-policy line, and the notes the AI reads to find the lecture note go to your AI.

Turn it off / undo. Features → Hint ladder → Turn off. The "AI policy" lines stay in your course notes; delete them there if you like.
