Your computer

Kurooto runs on your own computer, so what it can do depends on whether that computer is on, awake, online and plugged in. This page says what happens in each case, how to pause or turn Kurooto off, how to keep it reachable, what your hardware allows, where its files are, and how to check that it's healthy.

On, paused, off: the four states

StateHow you get thereTelegramReminders and scheduled workCalls from your call line
Running (plugged in)normalanswersrun on timering
Battery saveron battery, power.on_battery = saveranswerslight work runs; heavy work waits for the chargerring
Paused/pause, kurooto pause, Pause on the dashboard, or on battery with power.on_battery = pausesays it's paused and keeps what you sendwait until you resumestill ring (the alarm too)
Offkurooto off, or Turn off on the dashboarddoesn't answer; Telegram keeps your messages up to a daynothing runsstop, unless you used --keep-calls

Kurooto checks every 30 seconds whether the computer is plugged in or a pause has ended, and switches by itself.

On battery

What it does. By default Kurooto saves your battery: everything that leans on the laptop waits for the charger, and Telegram keeps working.

Keeps working on batteryWaits until it's plugged in
Telegram: messages, commands, plain-English requestsLocal AI models (unloaded, freeing the graphics card)
Reminders, check-ins, deadlines, the calendarThe notes brain's indexing and upkeep, Notes sync, Backups
Answers to what you ask the AIHandwritten notes, Books reading, the concept graph, Research, Classroom → notes, the nightly look back
Phone calls and the morning alarm; Profile sync (it's small)AI work nobody asked for (the mail and Classroom digests, the papers digest's picks)

Nothing is skipped: waiting work runs the moment you plug in. Your own requests never wait.

Settings that matter (Dashboard → Settings → power, or the setup page's "Your computer" step):

SettingDefaultWhat it does
power.on_batterysaversaver: as above. normal: everything keeps running, as if plugged in. pause: everything pauses until you plug in

Edge cases.

  • A desktop computer (no battery) is always "plugged in".
  • Some commands still run on battery when you ask for them directly: /backup, and /books read force.
  • /resume while on battery says "Back on; this laptop is on battery, so heavy work still waits for the charger."

Pausing by hand

What it does. Nothing runs while paused, but Telegram still answers: it replies "I'm paused until ...; I'll do this when I'm back. /resume wakes me now", keeps what you send, and handles all of it, in order, when you resume. Reminders due meanwhile arrive when it resumes. Your call line keeps placing scheduled calls, so the morning alarm still rings.

WhereHow
Telegram/pause (until you resume), /pause 2h, /pause until 18:00; /resume to wake it
Terminalkurooto pause, kurooto pause 2h, kurooto pause 18:00; kurooto resume
DashboardPause 1 h in the header

While paused, only /resume, /pause, /status, /help and /start are answered at once; everything else waits.

Turning it off, stopping and starting

CommandWhat it does
kurooto off (or Turn off on the dashboard)stops Kurooto completely (and the local models it started) and keeps it from starting when you sign in. Your call line stops placing scheduled calls
kurooto off --keep-callsthe same, but your call line keeps placing scheduled calls (for example, the alarm)
kurooto on (or open Kurooto from your app menu)turns it back on: start-at-sign-in again, starts it now, opens the dashboard
kurooto stopstops it just for now; it starts again at your next sign-in
kurooto startstarts it in the background now
kurooto openopens the setup page or dashboard in your browser
kurooto runruns it in this terminal window (for troubleshooting)
kurooto superviseruns it and restarts it if it crashes

Edge cases.

  • Telegram keeps messages for about a day. Messages sent while Kurooto is off are handled when it's back, if it's back within that day.
  • kurooto off while Kurooto isn't running can't tell your call line to stop; it says so.
  • A clean stop sends "Kurooto is going offline" on Telegram; coming back sends "back online". See Messages it sends for the full rules (notify.presence.*).

When the computer sleeps or is off

  • Telegram messages wait until it's back.
  • Reminders due while it slept arrive when it wakes, marked late. With Reminders with the lid shut on, your Android phone gets them at their time instead.
  • Calls and the morning alarm still ring: your call line places them from your Cloudflare account. Anything that needs the computer waits.
  • Questions can still be answered in the phone app; the answer waits on your call line.
  • Scheduled work that came due runs when it's back. A routine missed while off runs once, if that's still one of its days.
  • "Back online" comes when it starts, wakes or the network returns, with what it caught up on. Without Phone calls, there's no "going offline" message for sleep, a crash or a lost network.

To keep it reachable all day, leave it on and plugged in, with Stay awake on charger on. To have the morning plan drafted overnight, turn on alarm.keep_awake (see Calls: Morning alarm).

Stay awake on charger

What it does. While your computer is plugged in, it doesn't go to sleep by itself, so Kurooto keeps answering. On battery it sleeps as usual. The screen can still turn off.

Turn it on. Dashboard → Features → Stay awake on charger.

Closing the lid:

SystemWhat happens with the lid closed
Linuxturn on awake.lid and it keeps running with the lid closed while plugged in. If your system doesn't let a background program hold the lid switch, it holds the rest and kurooto doctor says so
Windowsfollows Power Options: Control Panel → Power Options → "Choose what closing the lid does" → "Do nothing" when plugged in. An app can't change this without admin rights
macOSa closed lid sleeps a Mac unless an external display is attached

Settings that matter:

SettingDefaultWhat it does
awake.lidfalseLinux: keep running with the lid closed while plugged in

Edge cases. The hold ends when Kurooto stops, even if it crashes. On Linux, if the lid still sleeps the laptop, allow the lid hold in polkit (org.freedesktop.login1.inhibit-handle-lid-switch) or set HandleLidSwitchExternalPower=ignore in logind.conf.

What leaves your computer. Nothing.

Turn it off. awake.lid off, or turn the feature off; the computer sleeps as usual at once.

Starting at boot

By default Kurooto starts when you sign in (Windows: a Run entry, no window; macOS: a LaunchAgent; Linux: a systemd user service, or an autostart entry without systemd). It restarts by itself if it crashes.

On Linux with systemd it can start when the computer does, before anyone logs in, so Telegram and calls work after a power cut or a remote reboot:

CommandWhat it does
kurooto autostart boot (or Dashboard → Settings → Starting)turns on lingering for your account (loginctl enable-linger; it may ask for your password, or tell you to run that yourself). Because your keychain stays locked until you log in, Kurooto's keys move to its owner-only settings file (secrets.json, readable only by your account)
kurooto autostart loginback to starting at sign-in: lingering is turned off again only if Kurooto turned it on, and the keys go back to the keychain
kurooto autostart statussays on or off
kurooto autostart on / offturns start-at-sign-in on or off

The setting is autostart.when (login or boot).

Edge cases.

  • Windows and macOS start at sign-in only: starting before would need admin rights. On a Mac that restarts by itself, automatic login brings Kurooto back.
  • If your home folder is encrypted and only unlocked at login, starting at boot can't reach Kurooto's files.
  • "Linux didn't allow that from here": run loginctl enable-linger in a terminal, then kurooto autostart boot again.

Undo. kurooto autostart login (or kurooto uninstall, which also turns off lingering Kurooto turned on).

Hardware profiles

The setup page reads your computer (processor, memory, graphics card, free space, battery) and recommends a setup, marked ★. The main answers always come from the AI you picked; local models only take quick decisions, make the notes index and, on bigger cards, read handwriting.

ProfileExampleLocal modelsNotes brain index
lightno usable graphics card, or under 8 GB of memory, or an Intel MacnoneGemini (your key) or keywords
apple-8Apple Silicon, 8 GBembeddings only (0.27 GB)on your Mac
appleApple Silicon, 16 GB or morea small helper model (2.5 GB) + embeddingson your Mac
small-gpuan NVIDIA card with 4 to 8 GBa small helper model (2.5 GB) + embeddingson your computer
big-gpuan NVIDIA card with 8 GB or morea larger helper model (6.6 GB, also reads handwriting) + embeddingson your computer
big-gpu-12an NVIDIA card with 12 GB or morethe above + a vision model (8 GB) for handwriting and imageson your computer

Only NVIDIA cards and Apple Silicon count for local models; other graphics cards get the light profile.

Going past what's recommended. You know your computer better than the detection:

CommandWhat it does
kurooto hardwarewhat it detected, what it recommends, and any warnings
kurooto hardware --profile big-gpu-12use another setup's models (auto goes back; also light, apple-8, apple, small-gpu, big-gpu)
kurooto hardware --add gemma4:12badd an Ollama model of your own (repeatable; its size is looked up in Ollama's registry); --remove MODEL takes one off
kurooto hardware --add-prereq tesseractadd another program for Kurooto to install; --remove-prereq ID takes it off
kurooto hardware --installinstall Ollama, every model on the list and those programs now, after checking there's room

The setup page's computer step has the same choices and an install button. Kurooto never refuses: if a model is bigger than your graphics memory, it says part of it will run on the processor, slowly, and goes ahead.

SettingDefaultWhat it does
hardware.force_profileempty (detected)another profile's models (Settings → This computer, kurooto hardware or the setup page)
hardware.extra_models[]Ollama models of your own, e.g. ["gemma4:12b"]
hardware.extra_prereqs[]other programs to install, by id (e.g. tesseract, poppler, tectonic, git)

Dashboard → Settings → Your computer shows what was found and lets you change it; kurooto doctor checks again.

Local AI models

What it does. Small AI models on your computer, through Ollama, picked for its hardware: they decide quickly how hard a request is, make the notes brain's index, read handwriting on bigger cards, and can summarise confidential mail on this computer. Private and offline. The main answers still come from the AI you picked (see AI brains).

Turn it on. Dashboard → Features → Local AI models. It installs Ollama and the models for your profile. Not offered on computers without a usable graphics card or Apple Silicon.

From Telegram: /models says whether Ollama is running and lists the models with a tick or cross for each, its size and what it's for.

Limits and edge cases.

  • On battery (saver mode) or when paused, the models are unloaded and an Ollama Kurooto started is stopped, freeing the graphics card; they come back when you plug in.
  • One graphics card runs one big model at a time, so handwriting waits while the card is busy.
  • A small model may not support the AI's tools; it then answers without them.

What leaves your computer. Nothing; the models run here.

Turn it off. Features → Local AI models → Turn off. Ollama and the models stay installed (remove them with Ollama if you like).

Voice notes don't use local models: they use your Gemini key.

Your notes folder can be anywhere (you.workspace; see Notes and the concept graph).

Secrets (bot token, API keys, sign-ins) are in your system's keychain: Windows Credential Manager, macOS Keychain, or the Secret Service on Linux. Where there is none (or when starting at boot on Linux), they're in secrets.json in the settings folder, readable only by your user account.

The dashboard runs at http://127.0.0.1:8770. If another program uses that port, Kurooto picks the next one; web.json in the state folder says which.

The reserve

Kurooto keeps plain Markdown copies of its own state in the state folder, under reserve/, so an AI program can just open a file instead of running a command it may not be allowed to run:

FileWhat it holds
alarm.mdthe next alarm and the daily one
deadlines.mdwhat's due, nearest first
calendar.mdthe next two weeks of events
settings.mdevery setting that differs from its default, and which features are on (never a secret)
addons.mdyour add-ons and whether each loaded
agent-guide.mdwhat an AI program may read, write and run here, its tools, and the chat commands

They're rewritten every 10 minutes and when an alarm changes, only when their text changed. kurooto reserve writes them now. AI programs may read this folder, never write to it, and Kurooto never reads it back.

Checking that it works (diagnostics)

CommandWhat it does
kurooto doctorchecks Kurooto, your AI, every feature you have on and free space; each line says what's wrong. It also asks Telegram whether your bot answers, asks your call line whether it's up, checks the skills copied into agy and runs your add-ons' own checks. The exit code is the number of problems (lines marked -- are notes, not problems; a setup step you skipped is one, with the command that opens it: kurooto setup about)
kurooto doctor --fix <id>runs the fix a problem offers, inside the running Kurooto (needs it running)
kurooto doctor --jsonthe same checks as JSON, for programs
kurooto doctor --bundlesaves a zip to send someone helping you: the checks, the log, your settings, recent events and how long each setup step took (finished or skipped; never what you typed), with secrets, names, email and web addresses and your folders blanked out. Never your messages, notes, journal, transcripts or what you asked the AI. Nothing is sent: look inside, then send it yourself. If Kurooto itself can't start, there's no zip and it says so: send the checks it prints instead
kurooto topa live status board in any terminal: this computer (processor, memory, graphics card, network, disks, battery, processes) beside Kurooto (running or not, battery mode, the call line, today, what's due, AI requests, recent events). q quits, r refreshes, c and m sort processes by cpu or memory; --once prints one screen; NO_COLOR=1 turns colours off
kurooto logsthe end of Kurooto's log (-n 200 for more lines); kurooto logs -f keeps following it (Ctrl+C stops)
kurooto selftestchecks that this version starts
kurooto --versionthe version you run

On Telegram, /status says what's going on and /health what isn't working, with Fix buttons (/health now checks online too). Kurooto also runs its own daily health pass and fixes what it safely can; see Messages it sends.

Lost phone or keys that got out

kurooto rotate gives your call line new keys after a lost phone or a backup zip with keys that got out. Every phone then has to pair again. See Phone app.

Local models stay on the graphics card

A model that runs partly on the processor is several times slower and heats the laptop. Every 10 minutes Kurooto looks at what Ollama has loaded: if models together don't fit your graphics card, it unloads them, so the next request loads its one model fully on the card; if one model doesn't fit even alone, you're told once, with what to do (a smaller model in Features → Local AI models). /models shows each loaded model and how much of it is on the card.
