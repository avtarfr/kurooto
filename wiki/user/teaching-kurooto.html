Teaching Kurooto about you

Kurooto gets better at helping you in seven ways, and you can see and change every one of them:

Nothing changes without your say: the look back asks before every change, taught phrasings need your tap, and the two notes are yours to edit (your edits win). The one exception is the optional Learns from its own work, which you turn on by hand: it acts, tells you in one line each time, and every change has an Undo. Paths are the default notes layout's; in the zettelkasten layout the about-me/ notes are in master/ and kurooto/ideas.md is master/ideas.md.

Some times it also learns by rules alone, each told in one line with Undo: check-in times (check-ins-and-journal.md), Start-by's day window, and your quiet hours at night and the morning things that follow them (calls.md). Settings you set yourself are never changed.

About you (your profile)

What it does. Your other AI accounts (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, Perplexity and the rest) already know a lot about you: your projects, how you work, what you keep asking for help with. The setup page's About you step brings that into Kurooto once, so it starts out knowing you.

How to build it.

  1. Open the setup page (kurooto open, then setup; or the dashboard's About you tab, Alt+Y). Type which AI and account you're starting with (e.g. "ChatGPT personal") and copy a prompt: Your main AI (the fullest audit) for the account you use most, Every other account for all the rest.
  2. Paste it into a new chat in that account. Turn on its memory or "search past chats" first if it has them: it can only report what it can see.
  3. Do this in as many accounts as you can, including school, work and old ones. Each knows different things.
  4. Each AI writes a long report and ends with a few questions. Answer them there if you like and ask it to update the report. Copy its whole answer (or download it as a file) and add it on the page: paste it, or upload several files at once. A report can be up to 2 million characters.
  5. Press Build my profile. Your AI merges the reports into one profile. It's a big request: a free AI may hit a limit; try again later or with a stronger AI.
  6. Read it and fix anything that's off, right there in the editor under the button.

The editor.

ButtonWhat it does
Savesaves your edits and keeps the previous version beside it (profile-<date>.md)
Revertgoes back to what's saved
Preview In briefshows exactly what goes along with every AI request: the section under ## In brief

Without a ## In brief heading (or with it empty), nothing from your profile goes along with your requests; Kurooto warns you but saves. A profile can be up to 200,000 characters. You can also edit the file in your notes.

Building again never replaces your edits silently. If you changed the profile since it was built, Build my profile again asks first; say yes and the new one replaces yours, with yours kept beside it as profile-<date>.md. If you edit it while a new one is being built, yours stays and the new one goes beside it as profile-new-<date>.md.

What Dreaming adds. With Dreaming on, the morning message can offer lasting things you said for your profile ("For your profile", see Dreaming); a line goes in only when you tap Keep, into Kurooto's own block (``, at the end of the In-brief part), with where and when you said it. Those lines go along with every request too. They don't count as your edits, building the profile again keeps them, and you can edit or delete them like any line. With no profile yet, the first Keep starts one with just that block.

From Telegram. /aboutme shows the In-brief part and a link to the editor (it opens on your computer).

What leaves your computer. Building the profile sends the reports to the AI you picked. After that, the In-brief part goes with every AI request. The reports stay in Kurooto's private data folder (not your notes, never synced): remove anything you'd rather not keep before adding a report.

Undo. Edit or delete about-me/profile.md; delete its ## In brief section to stop sending it.

Your persona card

What it does. Four short lines on how Kurooto talks to you: what to call you, tone, length and things to avoid ("plain and direct, no pep talk", "short unless I ask for detail", "emojis"). They go along with every AI request and every call, so it words things the same way on Telegram, the phone app and calls.

It starts empty. No name, voice or tone is picked for you; empty means plain answers.

Where to set it. The dashboard's About you tab (Persona card, with a preview of exactly what's sent), Settings → persona, or Telegram:

You sendWhat happens
/personashows the card
/persona tone: plain, no pep talksets one line (several lines in one message work: call me: Sam on the next)
/persona clear tone · /persona clearclears one line, or the whole card

The lines are call me, tone, length and avoid, up to 200 characters each.

It sets wording only. Kurooto has no feelings, backstory or life of its own, so a part of a line that asks for one is left out, and you're told which: "Left out "say you missed me" (saying it missed you)". That covers feelings ("tell me you love talking to me", "I'm so happy to see you"), a backstory ("you grew up in Pune", "you're 23"), and a relationship or being human ("be my girlfriend", "pretend you're human"). A tone like "friendly" or "dry humour" is fine. The same rules apply to a card edited by hand in the settings file, and to calls.persona.

What leaves your computer. The card goes to the AI you picked with each request, and to your call line's voice model with each call, like your profile's In brief.

Undo. /persona clear, or empty the lines on the About you tab.

Remember

What it does. Tell Kurooto how you like to be helped and it keeps it. The line goes into about-me/how-to-help-me.md, under "You told me", with the date. A short version of that note goes along with every AI request Kurooto makes, next to your profile's In brief.

Turn it on. Features → Remember. It needs Telegram.

From Telegram.

/remember I hate calls before 8"Noted in about-me/how-to-help-me.md: I hate calls before 8."
remember that I like a plan before 9 · keep in mind that I prefer short answers · remember: no emojisthe same
/rememberwhat it keeps

On a call: "remember that I study best in the morning" (the remember tool).

Rules.

  • One line, under 300 characters. Longer things belong in your notes.
  • Health, money and password details are refused ("I'd rather not keep that: health details would go along with every AI request"), because they'd travel with every request. Reword it if it isn't one.
  • "Remember to buy milk" is a reminder, not this: it's left for Reminders or your AI.
  • A line it already has: "I know that already."
  • The note is yours: read it, edit it, add your own lines. Kurooto never rewords or removes a line, and what you write there wins. The nightly look back adds lines only when you approve them (under "Learned in the nightly look back"), except the facts Learns from its own work picks up, if you turned it on (under "Picked up from your chats and calls", each with an Undo that takes only that line out again).

Still true? People change. A line Kurooto wrote (under "You told me", "Picked up from your chats and calls" or "Learned in the nightly look back", with its date) that hasn't come up in 90 days (not in what you asked, said on a call or told it again, and not kept with Keep) gets one quiet message: 'Still true? "I study topology on Sundays"', with Keep, Change and Forget. At most two a week, three days apart.

  • Keep: it stays, and isn't asked about for another 90 days.
  • Forget: the line comes out of the note, with an Undo that puts it back.
  • Change: reply with the new wording (or reply to the question itself); same rules as /remember, with an Undo. Replying "yes" keeps it, "no" forgets it.
  • Lines you wrote yourself, or anywhere else in the note, are never asked about or touched. Editing a line counts as new. remember.recheck_days (90; 0 never asks); Less of this on the message works too.

What leaves your computer. The short version of the note goes to the AI you picked with every request.

Turn it off. Features → Remember. The note stays; while it exists, AI requests still read it. Delete the note to stop that.

What it remembers, and when it uses it

Everything Kurooto remembers sits in one encrypted store on your computer, the memory ledger (privacy.md): the gists of faded conversations, the threads you're in the middle of (talking-to-kurooto.md), the lines it keeps in your how-to-help-me note, and what your daily notes and project meetings say. Your notes stay the source of truth; the ledger reads them when they change.

  • /memory lists it, /memory lab finds rows with those words, /memory forget 3 forgets one (a how-to-help-me line comes out of the note too), /memory export writes the gists and threads to a note.
  • Ask it plainly: "when did I last talk to Prof Ortiz?", "what was I doing last Thursday?".
  • What an answer is told. Only when your message refers to past things or to something you're in the middle of, your AI gets the few items it's about, at most about 600 tokens (memory.budget_tokens), each saying where it's from ("call at 07:56 today"), never a transcript. The grey line under the answer counts them ("used 2 remembered items"). Calls get your open threads and the last day's gists in their brief, so a call can pick up what you said in a chat. memory.recall off: none of it goes.
  • Nothing it isn't told. It never guesses how you feel or keeps health, money or password details; a thread found from "that thing" is only read or asked about, never acted on without your OK.

Nightly look back

What it does. Once a day (23:50) the AI looks at how the day went and suggests at most three small improvements, each with the evidence behind it. Nothing changes without your tap, except data fixes it is sure of (below), which it makes and tells you with Undo.

Before the AI, by rules on this computer:

  • Routines it sees in your records. The same thing at the same time of the week on 3 or more different weeks (in the last six): a study session for one course, or calls held with /hold. If it isn't a commitment or a class yet, one message a night: "From your records: you started studying Linear Algebra around Tue 18:00 on 3 different weeks. Add it as a routine (study linear algebra, Tue 18:00–19:30), so calls wait then?" with Add / No. Add makes it one of your commitments (how long it usually took, else an hour; Commitments is turned on if it was off). No keeps it away for 30 days.
  • Data fixes it is sure of, made at once with one line and Undo: two copies of one deadline (Classroom's and the one you said, or yours moved onto Classroom's day) become one, when they're due the same day, from different places, for the same course, with the same words and numbers. The one kept is the one Classroom (or your notes) brings back; it takes the other's time if it only had "end of the day", and the copy's buttons still work. "Problem set 3" and "Problem set 3 prep" are left alone. Anything that sends, an alarm or a call still waits for your tap.

Turn it on. Features → Nightly look back. It needs Telegram and Chat.

What it reads.

  • what happened: the questions Kurooto asked you and your answers (how fast, on which channel), check-ins you moved or skipped, calls answered or missed, after-class prompts, deadlines, AI requests, study sessions, quizzes, focus;
  • today's call transcripts and journal note (each can be left out: retro.transcripts, retro.journal);
  • your how-to-help-me note and the settings it may change.

Answering. Each suggestion comes as a question with Apply, Observe longer and No (and Later). Tap a button, reply ("yes", "observe 14", "no"), or answer on your next call.

  • Apply does it at once.
  • Observe longer watches it for a week first (reply "observe 14" for 14 days, up to 60); when the watching ends, the next look back tells you what it found.
  • No drops that idea, however it's worded next time, for 30 days.
  • Later keeps it for your next call. Unanswered suggestions stay open for three days.

Limits and edge cases.

  • On a laptop it waits for the charger (battery saver), and past 70% of a known free AI limit it waits for the limit to reset (ai-brains.md). Most days the right answer is no suggestion at all.
  • A skill change that's identical, or that broke the skill's header, isn't saved ("nothing changed").

What leaves your computer. Once a day, the day's events, your answers, today's transcripts and journal (unless turned off), your how-to-help-me note and the settings it may change go to the AI you picked.

Turn it off. Features → Nightly look back. Waiting suggestions stay answerable for three days.

Learns from its own work

What it does. Kurooto keeps what its own finished work teaches it, in four parts, each with its own switch. It doesn't ask first: it acts and tells you in one line, with an Undo button. (The idea comes from Hermes Agent, by Nous Research; none of its code is used.)

Skills from experienceafter a request that went well and took 5 or more steps (tool uses), the AI looks at how it was done. Turn it on. Features → Add-ons → Learns from its own work (it's in no preset). It needs Chat. Learned skills and lessons need Skills on; picking up facts needs the Nightly look back on. The card on the Features page has a switch for each part.

From Telegram.

Undo. Every message has an Undo button, and /learned lists the last ten changes with theirs.

  • A new skill is removed. Tips or a lesson are taken out again: the skill file goes back to how it was (a copy of it is kept in Kurooto's state folder, learning/before/). For a skill that comes with Kurooto, the change was made in your own copy (kurooto/skills/<name>/SKILL.md), and Undo removes that copy.
  • If you edited the skill since, Undo leaves it alone and says where the earlier version is.
  • A fact is taken out of your how-to-help-me note, unless you changed that line.

Rules it keeps.

  • Only your own requests are looked at, not background work (digests, routines, the concept graph). At most five a day are looked at as possible skills.
  • A learned skill's file says so in its header: learned: true, the request it came from (learned_from) and the date (learned_on). Edit or delete it like any of your skills.
  • Facts follow Remember's rules: one short line, never health, money or password details, and never anything about other people (a line about your sister, a phone number or an email address is left out).
  • A reply that only answers a skill's question ("week 3") isn't a correction.

What leaves your computer. The search index is built and kept on this computer. When the AI searches it during a request, the passages it finds go to that AI with the request. Learning a skill sends the finished request, its answer and the steps it took to the AI that takes second looks (learning.brain; by default the concept graph's second-look AI, else your main one); a lesson sends your correction and the skill. Picking up facts sends the day's requests to the AI the nightly look back uses.

Turn it off. Features → Learns from its own work, or one part on its card. Learned skills, lessons and facts stay where they are (they're yours now); the search index stays in the state folder until the Space keeper trims it (it forgets what's older than space.jobs_days, 60 days, like finished requests).

Wrong button and test cases

What it does. When Kurooto gets a plain-English request wrong, you tell it, and it keeps the request as a case. Reviewed cases become test cases, and they can teach Kurooto your own words for things ("whats on my plate" taken as "what's due").

Turn it on. Features → Wrong button and test cases. It needs Telegram.

Telling it.

  • Tap Wrong under a reply to a plain-English request (or under the AI's answer to one), or send /wrong right after it. The case keeps what you wrote, what Kurooto did with it, the time and the settings that decided it.
  • Short requests no instant request understood (they went to the AI, or nowhere with Chat off) are kept as cases too, so you see what Kurooto keeps missing.
  • Never kept: messages over 120 characters, several requests in one, and anything about health, money or passwords.

Teaching it your words.

  • At once: /wrong what's due right after the reply: "what you meant" in words Kurooto already understands. /wrong ai says the AI was the right place for it.
  • Once a month (a Sunday evening): the look back lists the phrasings that came up most (up to 5), each with Make it a test case. Tap it, then reply with what you meant, or tap The AI.
  • By itself, when you say it again in other words (cases.learn_rephrased, on): "whats on my plate" goes to the AI, and within 3 minutes you write "what's due this week". The second time that same phrasing is followed by the same request, it's learned, under the same two rules below: "You said "whats on my plate" and then "what's due this week" 2 times, so: Learned: ...", with Undo.

<a id="two-readings"></a>Two readings. When no instant request takes a short message but two of them are about equally close to it ("whats my plan" between "what's my plan today" and "what's my day plan"), Kurooto asks with both as buttons instead of guessing (see talking-to-kurooto.md). Tap one and it runs. The second time you pick the same request for the same words, they're learned, under the same two rules below: "You picked "what's my day plan" for "whats my plan" 2 times, so: Learned: ...", with Undo. A phrasing that's a test case already is taken as it says, without asking. Needs this feature on to learn; the question itself works either way (repair.on).

From then on that phrasing is taken as the request you named, at once, with today's time ("skip gym tmrw" taught as "skip gym tomorrow" always means the day after you say it). A taught phrasing is matched whole (capitals and punctuation aside) and goes before the usual patterns.

Two rules keep this safe.

  • A phrasing is learned only if every test case that passed before still passes with it. Otherwise: "Saved as a test case, but not learned", and which case it would break.
  • Requests that send, delete, call, ring or approve anything are never learned this way (calls, holds on calls, quizzes by call, the alarm, an add-on's "send" or "delete"). They keep needing their usual words; the test case is still kept.

Call lessons

After every call, Kurooto looks back at the transcript with a stronger AI: it does what was asked but slipped through (a reminder misheard, a note forgotten, a tool that failed) and writes down lessons for later calls. Every later call is told them. /lessons lists them; /lessons forget 2 removes the second, /lessons clear all of them. Details are in calls.md.

"By POTA I mean Physics of the Atmosphere", "LA is short for linear algebra", "I call stochastic calculus stoch"gives that subject the short name, so "POTA quiz friday" lands on it (only your subjects; never a name another one has)
"Every Tuesday and Thursday at 7pm I go to the gym", "I work a shift every weekend from 9 to 5", "gym Tue Thu 7am"adds it to your commitments (an hour unless you say the end), so calls wait for it; turns Commitments on if it was off. The short way needs two days or more (or "every", or "on Tuesdays") and a clock time like 7am or 19:00: "dentist Tue 7am" is a one-off, so nothing is set up
"I have lab every Thursday 2-5" (no course named)adds "lab" to your commitments, Thu 14:00–17:00, and says how to make it a class instead (/class thu 14:00-17:00 <course>)
"I've got a lab on Mondays at 9", "I go running every morning at 6""on Mondays" counts as every Monday and "I've got" as "I have"; "go running" is a commitment too (every day). A meeting or a call with someone ("a weekly call with Jun on Fridays") is never a commitment: it's a meeting, and only with someone a project is With
"There's a quiz on Linear Algebra every Monday at 10", "LA has a test every other Friday from 2026-10-30"adds the row to your recurring quizzes table, so each one becomes a deadline. Only when the course, the day and (every other week) a date are all said; a message that is only that request ("la has a quiz every thursday") is answered by the request itself, which asks for what's missing
"LA problem set" in one message and "LA notes" in another, when Linear Algebra is the only subject whose initials are LAtakes "LA" as Linear Algebra's short name ("you've called it that twice"). Only names written in capitals, two messages within 60 days, and never one that fits two subjects or is a common word (OK, PM, PDF)

It only acts on what you say about yourself: "my friend got a project", "I didn't get it" or "maybe I'll ask" set nothing up, and a line is looked at once even when it reaches both the chat and the journal. A question sets up nothing, with or without its question mark: "do I have lab every Thursday", "is there a quiz every Monday". It works by rules on this computer (the one pass below can ask an AI about a line the rules can't read). It never sets alarms, places calls, sends anything or attaches code (/repos add does that).

The one pass (infer.sweep, on). Besides what you send, Kurooto reads what never came as a message: your chats with it and your journal and daily notes as they are in your notes folder (your lines, not its own blocks). It does that once a few minutes after it's first on, over the last 30 days (infer.sweep_days), and then each night at 04:20 over what's new. The rules come first. A line they can't read but that looks like one of the cases above ("Prof Lena Ortiz said yes, so I'm on her glacier team now as my project") gets one more try with infer.sweep_ai: local (the default) asks the model on this computer, when there is one, to restate it in one of the forms in the table, and the rules read that; a restatement that brings in a name, day or number your line doesn't have is dropped. main asks your own AI instead (only that line goes to it); off leaves it to the rules. What one pass sets up comes in one line:

Set up 2 things from your recent chats and journal (1 project, 1 class). /inferred shows each, with its Undo.

with Undo all. /inferred scan runs it now.

/inferred lists what was set up, newest first, including the pieces of a project you set up from its repository (/project add); /inferred undo <id> (or the Undo button) takes one back. Turn it off with infer.on, or a part with infer.projects, infer.meetings, infer.classes, infer.aliases, infer.commitments, infer.quizzes, infer.short_names; infer.sweep = false stops only the pass.

Dreaming

Turn on Dreaming (Features) and Kurooto's night comes to you as one message in the morning (dreaming.when, 07:30 by default, or half an hour after you're usually up once that's learned, calls.md) instead of several. The day after an exam or a heavy day it starts with one line saying the day will be lighter, with Not today:

  • While you slept: how much of your books was read into your notes (new concept notes, links), new books taken in, broken links tidied, handwritten notes linked, lessons the calls learned (/lessons), skills and facts learned (/learned), things set up from what you said (/inferred).
  • Loose ends: your own open doubts from the last two weeks (the Doubts of your handwritten notes), notes nothing links to yet, links that point at notes that don't exist, tasks past due, AI requests that failed overnight.
  • The self-check (dreaming.self_check, on): with Wrong button and test cases on, it replays your test cases and names any that went right yesterday but go wrong now (after an update or a settings change), and counts the requests it didn't understand and the Wrong taps since the last message (the Sunday look back shows them).
  • Tuning its own messages (dreaming.tune_messages, on): it replays the messages it sent you by itself (the last 45 days) under a few variants of when they come, and when one clearly beats things as they are, it makes that one change, with Undo: "Learned while you slept: questions about quiet projects wait for your evening bundle once 2 in a row go unanswered; over the last 14 days that would have saved 8 ignored pings and delayed none you answered." One change a night at most, never to what always comes or what you set yourself. Messages it sends has the details; /dream policy shows the rules and the last decisions.
  • Tuning the rest (Dream RSI, part three): the same nightly replay on three more of its decisions, each with its own switch, all on:
  • effort (dreaming.tune_effort): how hard the AI thinks about your requests, per what picked it (the quick check's low, medium or high, or the study-from-notes rule). It counts your requests of the last 45 days that you asked again within 15 minutes, marked Wrong, followed with "that's not what I asked" (or "you misunderstood", "no, I meant ..."), or that ran out of time, and how long they took. One step at a time; a level you type ("high: ...") is never changed;
  • which AI goes first (dreaming.tune_fallbacks): with a fallback AI set, at an effort level where your main AI keeps hitting limits or timing out, the fallback is tried first (brain.fallback_first);
  • Start-by sizes (dreaming.tune_sizing): how long each kind of deadline takes (startby.sizes), from the study time on its subject between its start-by date and when you marked it done, "4/7" progress, and sizes you tapped. A size you set yourself is never changed.

Today's rules are always among what it compares, and it changes something only when the replay clearly wins (dreaming.tune_margin). Still one change a night across everything (messages included), said in the morning message with Undo: "Learned while you slept: Start-by sizes a report at about 5 h (a day), not 12 h; the last 4 took about 5 h (4 from study time)."

  • It improves the way it improves: every change's outcome is kept. A kind of change you undid needs a clearer win next time (up to four times the margin), one you kept earns some of that back, and what you undid is left alone for 30 days. /dream policy shows all of it.
  • Proposed fixes to skills (dreaming.propose_prompts, off): when a skill's answers were corrected, marked Wrong or asked again twice or more in two weeks, the night asks Learning's second-look AI for the skill with the failing step rewritten. If, by that AI's replay, it fixes at least half of those cases, the morning message offers it as a proposal with Apply, Change and Discard (/dream proposal shows it whole, /proposals as a diff). Nothing changes without your tap; Apply replays your test cases and puts the skill back at once if one that passed goes wrong; Undo in /learned. Change has the AI rework it in a scratch folder; Discard leaves that skill alone for 30 days.
  • Curious (dreaming.curious, on): a topic you asked about 3 or more times this week (your requests to the AI, what calls searched your notes for, quiz questions you got wrong) with no note on it, or only a stub, gets one line: "You've asked about the Fokker–Planck equation 3 times this week and there's no note on it. Want one?" Make a stub writes a short concept note in that subject's folder (where your notes layout puts concept notes) and links it from the course page under "Concepts"; Not interested puts the topic on your not-interested list (/unignore takes it back). It's careful about what counts as a topic: the name of one of your concept notes, or a phrase that ends in a word like equation, lemma, theorem or distribution ("Itô's lemma"), or a pair of names ("Navier–Stokes").
  • From the past (dreaming.resurface, on): one line about a note you last changed 10 to 30 days ago, or a subject you last practised in a quiz then ("You last worked on Itô's lemma 12 days ago"; one with an exam in the next two weeks first), or an anniversary ("A month ago today you started work on L96": a project's first Log line, or a repository's first commit). Open it opens the note on your computer (Practise starts a quiz); Less of this turns these lines off, with Undo.

Each comes at most once a day, and the same topic or note not again for 30 days.

  • Worth a look in what I remember (dreaming.consolidate, on): each night (04:45) it looks for the same thing remembered twice and for lines that disagree, and lists a few (dreaming.consolidate_max, 3) as one short section:
  Worth a look in what I remember (nothing changes until you tap):
  1. These disagree (different dates): A "Stats quiz 3 postponed, due 02 Nov now" (journal/2026-10-22.md, Thu 22 Oct);
     B "Statistics: Stats quiz 3, due Fri 30 Oct" (your deadlines).
  2. The same thing twice: A "No calls before 8" (about-me/how-to-help-me.md, 12 Oct); B "Don't call me before
     8am" (about-me/how-to-help-me.md, 20 Oct).

Each has Keep A, Keep B and Both true (Keep both for a duplicate). What's compared: gists (two of the same kind from the same day that say the same thing), the lines Kurooto keeps in your how-to-help-me note, your daily notes and project meetings from the last 30 days, your open deadlines, and, with the notes brain on, its contradictions report. Two lines disagree when they're about the same thing (the same key words, the same "Quiz 3") and give a different date, weekday, time or number ("Quiz 3 is due Fri" and "Quiz 3 is due Mon"); a line that only says when you did something ("studied for quiz 3 on Thursday") doesn't count.

Nothing changes until you tap. Keeping one side takes the other out of your how-to-help-me note (a line Kurooto wrote), drops the other gist, or moves the deadline to the date your note says; a line in your own notes is never changed: Kurooto says which note it's in, for you to change. Every change has Undo. Both true keeps both and the pair isn't asked about again; one shown on two mornings without a tap is let go. /dream check looks now.

It's rules first. A pair that's close but not clear can get a second opinion (dreaming.consolidate_ai): local (the default) asks the model on this computer when Local AI models has one, main asks your own AI (only how-to-help-me lines and note lines go to it, never a gist), off asks nobody; at most 8 a night.

  • For your profile (dreaming.fold, on): each night (04:50) it reads what you said since the last time (your chats with Kurooto, your lines on calls, the AI requests you made, your journal and daily notes) for lines that say something lasting about you or how you like to be helped, and offers a few (dreaming.fold_max, 3):
  For your profile (what you said that seems to last; nothing is written until you tap Keep):
  1. "I'm a second-year geology student" (a chat, 23 Oct) → your profile
  2. "Please don't call me before 9" (a call, 23 Oct) → how to help you

Each has Keep and Not that. Keep adds it, in your words with where it came from ("- Please don't call me before 9 (from a call, 2026-10-23)"): a fact about you into Kurooto's block of your profile, how to help you into your how-to-help-me note; both go along with every AI request. Each Keep has Undo (the line comes out and is offered again). Not that drops it for good; a line shown on two mornings without a tap is let go. It only picks lines in the first person that state something (not a question, not "maybe" or "if", not only about today), not already in your profile or note, and never about anyone else (a "he", "she", "my friend" or a phone number, so a message you forwarded isn't learned from), health, money or passwords. With dreaming.fold_ai = local (the default) the model on this computer, when there is one, takes a second look and drops what isn't lasting; main asks your AI (only those lines go to it); off leaves it to the rules. /dream fold looks now.

Apart from that second opinion, nothing new runs for it: it reads Kurooto's own log, its memory and your notes, so it uses no AI and nothing leaves the computer. A quiet night with no loose ends sends nothing. /dream shows the same any time. With it on, the concept graph's own morning line comes inside it. dreaming.loose_ends = false leaves the loose ends out (the curious and past lines have their own settings). What calls searched your notes for is kept 8 days at most, and /forget clears it.
