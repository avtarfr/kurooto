Research

For anyone who reads papers or writes code for a project: a digest of new papers in your fields, a note per project that keeps up with your code, a log of experiment runs, and a message when a long job finishes.

Turn it on

Dashboard → Features → Research. Its card asks for "What I work on" (a sentence or two) and a few keywords to start with; then pick your fields and sources on the dashboard's Research tab. It needs at least one source or one repository before it counts as ready. Nothing to install. With Chat on, the AI picks the papers that matter to you and says why.

The papers digest

What it does. New papers from the sources you choose, as one message, weekly by default (Monday 08:00).

On the dashboard: the Research tab (Alt+R).

  1. What I work on: a sentence or two ("graph neural networks for molecules"). With Chat on, the AI uses it, and your notes, to pick the most relevant papers and say in one line why each matters to you.
  2. Your sources, each with a remove button. Start from a quick pick (machine learning, language models and NLP, computer vision, robotics, security and privacy, algorithms, probability and statistics, quantum physics, astrophysics, materials, earth and climate, economics, quantitative finance, neuroscience, genomics, ecology and evolution, epidemiology, psychology and psychiatry), or add a source:

| Source | What you pick | |---|---| | arXiv | any of its 155 fields (maths, physics, computer science, statistics, quantitative biology and finance, economics, electrical engineering); type in the box to find one ("number theory", "galaxies") | | bioRxiv | biology preprints, by subject area (neuroscience, genomics, ecology...) | | medRxiv | medical preprints, by subject area (epidemiology, oncology, psychiatry...) | | PubMed | published biomedical papers for a search; PubMed's own search words work (sleep AND adolescents) | | A feed | any journal's or blog's RSS or Atom address (https://...); most journals have one |

Add keywords to any source (except PubMed, whose search already narrows it) to keep only papers that mention one of them in the title or abstract. Preview shows what a source would find this week (the first 10, and how many) before you Add it.

  1. The digest: weekly (which day) or daily, at what time, up to how many papers (1 to 30), and whether the AI picks and says why. Save keeps it; Send a digest now tries it at once ("Looking now; the digest comes on Telegram in a minute or two").

What a digest looks like.

  • With Chat on and more new papers than your limit: the AI's pick of the most relevant, each with its title, link and one line on why it matters to you, then one line listing others worth a glance.
  • Otherwise: the newest papers as they are (title, first authors, link), and "...and N more."
  • A paper is sent only once: a paper you've had before never comes again, from any source.
  • A source that couldn't be reached is named at the end ("Couldn't reach: ...").
  • No new papers: nothing comes, unless a source failed, in which case you get "No new papers this time" with which one failed.

A copy in your notes (research.save_note, off). On: each digest is also kept as inbox/papers-<date>.md, every new paper grouped by source with its first lines, and the message says where. A second digest the same day adds to the same note. Keep what matters as a note of its own; the digest note is yours to delete.

arXiv in its own words. A source in research.feeds (Dashboard → Settings → research) can be a search written the way arXiv's API takes it, with a name that heads its part of the digest:

{"type": "arxiv", "name": "Molecules", "query": "abs:\"graph neural network\" AND (abs:molecule OR abs:protein)"}

ti: searches titles, abs: abstracts, all: everything, cat: a category; combine with AND, OR, ANDNOT and brackets, and add "categories" to narrow it. A search with an unclosed bracket or quote is refused. Kurooto asks arXiv once every 3 seconds, as arXiv asks, so several arXiv sources take a little longer.

Limits. Up to 30 sources, 20 keywords per source, 30 arXiv fields per source. Each digest looks back 7 days (research.window_days). On battery (saver mode) the scheduled digest waits for the charger. A paper counts as sent once its digest reaches you: if the AI's pick fails, those papers come again in the next digest (while they're still in its window).

Zotero

What it does. Each paper in the digest comes with a Zotero button: one tap puts it in your Zotero library (title, authors, abstract, date, link, arXiv id or DOI), and a second tap doesn't add it twice. Your reading notes that link the paper (its arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv, PubMed or feed link) get a Kurooto block with a zotero://select link that opens the item in Zotero, at once and in a nightly pass; nothing of yours in the note changes.

Two ways in (research.zotero, auto):

  • The Zotero app on this computer, when it's running: through its connector at 127.0.0.1:23119, as the browser extension does. Nothing leaves your computer from Kurooto. For notes to link the item, turn on Zotero's local API (Zotero 7: Settings → Advanced → "Allow other applications on this computer to communicate with Zotero").
  • Zotero's web API with a key: make one at zotero.org/settings/keys with library and write access, then /zotero key <key> (or Research's setup form on the dashboard). It's kept with your other secrets, never in settings.

auto uses the app when it's running, else the web API when there's a key; app or web only that one; off no buttons. Without either, the digest has no Zotero buttons.

Projects and their logs

What it does. Each research project gets a note in your notes, research/<project>/index.md (projects/<project>/index.md in the zettelkasten layout), and the research hub research/index.md lists them all. Every night at 23:30 (research.log_when), each day's new commits in your code repositories become one line under ## Log in their project's note:

- **2026-10-20** | git:my-model — 3 commits: fix the loss; add the sweep; tidy plots

At most three commit messages are shown per day, then "+N more". Kurooto only reads your repositories (it never changes them), and in your notes it only adds lines: your own text is never changed. The first time, only the last day's commits are logged.

Adding repositories.

WhereHow
Dashboard → Research tab → Projectstype the folder, Add; remove takes one off
Telegram/repos add ~/code/my-model

Telegram/repos remove ~/code/my-model; /repos alone lists them with their project

A repository's project is its folder's name unless you name it; the mapping is the setting research.projects. The folder has to be a Git repository when you add it ("That folder isn't a git repository"). A folder that was added and isn't a Git repository yet (only a proposal, say) stays a project with no commits to follow: kurooto doctor notes it ("isn't a git repository (no commits to follow)"), which isn't a problem; git init it to follow it.

Logs from 0.1.0 in research/<repository>-log.md are left as they are.

/sync all also brings the project logs up to date (Sync everything).

Set up a project from a repository

What it does. Give Kurooto a project's code repository (a GitHub address, or a folder on this computer) and your AI works out what the project is and sets everything up from it: the project note, its commit log, its docs in the concept graph, and its dated milestones as buttons.

WhereHow

In plain words"set up my project from github.com/ana-ruiz/glacier-flow", or "add ~/code/glacier as a project": it says what it would do, with Set it up / Not now; nothing is fetched before your tap

/project lists your projects, these with the rest; /project update <name> reads one again. With Help with your project on, the AI can work on one set up from a folder of yours (/project help on <name>); a GitHub address's copy is Kurooto's own and read only.

Getting it. A GitHub address is copied into Kurooto's data folder (<data>/repos/<owner>__<name>): only the default branch, its last 50 commits, and of its files only the ones worth reading: documentation, manifests, notebooks, a paper PDF (up to 20 MB) and other text files up to 1 MB each, 50 MB in all (repo_projects.max_mb). Pictures, archives, data files and anything that looks like it holds a secret (.env, keys, credentials, token files, .ssh/, secrets/) are never copied or read. A folder on this computer is read where it is and never changed. Nothing in a repository is ever run: no install, no script, no build, no submodules.

Private repositories work when the git on this computer can already reach them: sign git in once (gh auth login, then gh auth setup-git, or an ssh key and the git@github.com:... address). Kurooto never asks for a token and never keeps one. If git can't reach it, the message says so.

Understanding it. Kurooto reads the README, docs/, the list of files, the languages, the package manifests (pyproject.toml, package.json, requirements.txt...), the licence, CITATION.cff, a paper link, and the recent commits (their messages and the authors' names only). With the gh program signed in, also the repository's description, topics, open issues and open milestones (repo_projects.github_extras; skipped quietly otherwise). With Chat on, your AI reads all that in one request and says what the project is, its goal and field, its stack, where it stands, how to run it, open problems and next steps, who works on it, and which of your courses and projects it relates to. Its answer is checked: a person is kept only if the repository names them, a supervisor only if the repository says so, and only your own courses and projects are linked. Without Chat (or if the AI fails), the same is filled in from the files alone: the README's title and first paragraph, its install section, the manifests, the commit authors and the open issues.

What gets set up. One message lists it, with an Undo button per piece (also in /inferred):

Set up Glacier Flow from github.com/ana-ruiz/glacier-flow (your AI read it):
- Project note research/glacier-flow/index.md: what it is, its stack, how to run it, status and next steps; with
  Dr Lena Ortiz (supervisor, as the repository says).
- Its commits go into the project's Log each night (/repos).
- Its README and docs (3 files) are queued for the concept graph; it reads them at night.
Dated milestones:
- Glacier Flow: v1.0: Fri 14 Nov
Nothing is added until you tap.
/inferred lists these, with Undo.
[Undo note] [Undo commit log] [Undo graph]
[Add: Glacier Flow: v1.0 · Fri 14 Nov] [None of these]
  • The project note is the same kind of project note "I got a project under Prof X" makes, listed in the research hub. What Kurooto writes from the repository sits in its own block under the title (``): what it is, goal, field, topics, stack, licence, how to run it (the commands as its docs write them), open problems and next steps, people, and links (the repository, a paper, your related courses, projects and concept notes). Reading it again rewrites only that block; your own text in the note is never changed. A supervisor the repository names also gets a "With:" line and a Meetings section, so meeting briefs and minutes work as for any project.
  • The commit log: the repository joins /repos under the project's name (Research is turned on if it was off), so each day's commits become a line in the project's Log.
  • The concept graph (when it's on): the README, docs, notebooks' text and the paper are read in batches like a book, and the concepts they introduce link back to the project note ("Seen in"). It adds "repos" to what the graph reads (integrate.sources; its card has the switch). If the graph is off, the message says so.
  • Dated milestones come as Add buttons (each becomes a deadline only when you tap it).
  • GBrain, when it's on, picks up the new notes at its next sync.

Again, and keeping up. Adding the same repository again (in any of its address forms) updates it: the block is written again, changed files go to the concept graph, new milestones are offered; nothing is made twice. Every night at 04:20 (repo_projects.refresh_at, while Research is on and the repository is watched), Kurooto looks again: for a GitHub copy, one small request asks whether there's a new commit or tag, and only then is a fresh copy made. If the README or docs changed, the note's block is written again and only the changed files are queued for the graph; a new release (tag) and new dated milestones are said in one line, the milestones with Add buttons (repo_projects.refresh = false stops this).

Undo. Undo note removes the note if nothing of yours is in it, else only Kurooto's block (and the "With:" line it added). Undo commit log takes the repository out of /repos (and turns Research off again if it turned it on and has nothing else). Undo graph takes back the files not read yet (concepts already written stay, like any other). The copy in the data folder stays until you delete <data>/repos/<owner>__<name>; backups leave it out (it can be fetched again).

Quiet projects

When a project has had no commits or runs for 4 days (research.idle_days), Kurooto asks once, at 18:00 (never in your night hours): "No commits to my-model for 5 days. What's the plan?" with these buttons:

ButtonWhat happens
BusyNoted in the project's log
Working off-gitNoted in the project's log
Remind me tomorrowWith Phone calls on, a call tomorrow at 18:00 about it (a missed call becomes a message); otherwise a message then. research.remind_by_call = false always makes it a message
Pausing itNoted; it won't ask again until there are new commits

You can also reply in your own words; whatever you answer goes into the project's log as - **<date>** | you — <your answer>. It asks again only after the next quiet stretch. Unanswered, the question expires after 3 days. Like any question, you can answer it on the phone app or on a call.

research.idle_days = 0 never asks. Less of this on the message offers Fewer (asks half as often) and Stop (Messages it sends).

Meetings with someone: briefs, minutes and action items

Any meeting you have with someone a project note is With, whatever they are to you (supervisor, guide, manager, coach): the project note keeps the meeting's lines, a brief comes the evening before, and what you send after it becomes minutes.

Setting it up, by saying it (in the chat or your journal; one line back with Undo, /inferred lists them):

You sayKurooto
"I got a project under Prof Lena Ortiz on glacier flow"starts the note *Glacier flow* with "With: Prof Lena Ortiz (supervisor)"
"I'm doing my thesis under Prof Ana Ruiz", "My supervisor is Dr Ken Ito"starts *Thesis with Prof Ana Ruiz*, *Project with Dr Ken Ito* the same way
"I meet my manager Mira Castell every Monday at 10 about onboarding"puts "With: Mira Castell (manager)" and "Meets: every Mon 10:00" in the project note *Onboarding* (made if missing; without a topic, *Meetings with Mira Castell*)
"We meet my guide Dr Okafor every Tuesday at 4"the weekly time for the project that's With Okafor already ("I have a weekly call with ... on Fridays at 5" works too)
"Met Okafor today, we fixed the drift"a dated line under the note's Meetings, and "Last met: <date>"
"I have a meeting with Ortiz on Friday at 4"a "Meeting: Prof Lena Ortiz" deadline, for a one-off meeting

Someone unknown with no role or title ("I meet Sam every Friday") sets nothing up. The role is your own word, from "my <role>"; you can change it in the note.

In the project note, these lines live in Kurooto's own block, the only part of the note it rewrites:


With: Mira Castell (manager)
Last met: 2026-10-19
Meets: every Mon 10:00

A note from before 0.5.20 keeps its own "With:" and "Last met:" lines (they're yours now, and never changed); the block goes under them, and the newest "Last met" date in the note is the one that counts.

The evening before (20:00, deadlines.meeting_brief), for a weekly meeting or a "Meeting:" deadline, a meeting brief: what the project's Log has since you last met, its open "## Next steps", the last Meetings line and the last minutes' first points. Once per meeting.

After the meeting (needs Chat on: your AI writes the minutes, in one request), any of these becomes minutes:

  • a voice note sent within 3 hours of the meeting's start (meetings.after_h);
  • a typed note of 20 words or more sent then, which nothing else (a reminder, a /command) answers;
  • a message that starts "about the meeting with Okafor:", "notes from today's meeting:" (any time; a colon or dash after it).

The reply says which meeting it took it for, with Not about the meeting: that takes the minutes out of the note and sends your message to your AI as a plain message. The minutes go under the note's ## Meetings, in a block for that day (a second note the same day is added to the same minutes):


**Minutes, Mon 19 Oct 2026, with Mira Castell**
- Went through the onboarding plan
Decisions:
- Start with the support queue
Action items:
- Write the access request (by 2026-10-21)

If your AI can't answer, your notes go into that block as you sent them, so nothing is lost. "Last met" moves to the meeting's day.

Action items. They're added as - [ ] lines in Kurooto's block under ## Next steps (made if missing; tick or edit them there, they're kept as you leave them), and come back as buttons, on Telegram and in the phone app's chat:

ButtonWhat happens
Add 1: ...that item becomes a deadline: on the date said in the meeting (17:00), else at your next meeting with them, else a week on. Nothing is added without this tap
Noneno deadlines; the items stay under Next steps
Laterthey come back the next evening, with the briefs

Four items get buttons at a time (the rest are in the note). meetings.minutes = false turns minutes off; meetings.after_h sets the window after a meeting.

Experiment runs (kurooto runlog)

What it does. Logs a training or simulation run into the project's notes, with the code's exact state, so you can always tell which code produced which numbers. It works from any terminal, without Kurooto running.

kurooto runlog start my-model --name lr-sweep --config '{"lr": 0.001}'
kurooto runlog metric loss=0.31 acc=0.88 --step 100
kurooto runlog note "diverged after step 800"
kurooto runlog end                  # or: --status failed / --status interrupted
kurooto runlog ls                   # the runs so far (ls my-model: one project's)
kurooto runlog diff A B             # two runs side by side, into the notes (the Research add-on)
CommandOptions
start <project>--name (default: the repository's name), --config (a JSON object), --repo (the code's folder; default: where you are). Prints the run's id
metric k=v ...--step N, --run ID
note TEXT--run ID
end`--status okfailedinterrupted (default ok), --run ID`
ls [project]

Without --run, the commands use $KUROOTO_RUN, else the run you started last on this computer.

What it writes. A folder research/<project>/runs/<YYYYmmdd-HHMMSS>-<name>/ in your notes with:

FileWhat's in it
run.mdwhat it was (folder, Git commit and branch, config) and, at the end, how it went
config.jsonthe config you gave
meta.jsonGit commit, branch, whether there were uncommitted changes, start, end, duration, status
diff.patchthe uncommitted changes at the start (up to 2 MB), so the exact code can be recovered
metrics.jsonlone line per metric
notes.mdyour notes

Plus a row in runs/index.csv, and a line in the project's Log when it ends.

From Python. A run is a with block:

from kurooto.runlog import Run

with Run("lr-sweep", config={"lr": 1e-3}, project="my-model") as run:
    for step in range(n_steps):
        run.log(step=step, loss=loss)
    run.note("diverged after step 800")

It ends as ok, failed (an exception: its traceback goes into the run's files, and the exception still stops your script) or interrupted (Ctrl+C). With project= (or $KUROOTO_PROJECT) the run goes into your notes as above.

Runs kept in your repository. Without a project, the run stays with your code: a runs/ folder where the script starts ($RUNLOG_DIR to put it elsewhere), with the same files plus the host, Python version and command line, and a row in runs/index.csv. For repositories you added with /repos add, Research reads that file every night, and each new run becomes a line in the project's Log:

- **2026-10-20** | run:my-model/20261020-101500-lr-sweep — ok after 41m10s (step=900, loss=0.12)

The first time, only the last 30 days' runs. research.repo_runs is the folder's name (runs; empty: don't read them).

Long jobs (kurooto watch)

What it does. Runs a command and messages you when it ends.

kurooto watch -- python train.py --epochs 50
kurooto watch --lines 0 -- make all
kurooto watch --log my-model -- python fit.py
  • Its output still shows in your terminal as it runs.
  • When it ends you get "Job finished": done, failed or stopped, the exit code, how long it took and its last 10 lines (--lines N; --lines 0 sends none). Colours and progress-bar redraws are cleaned out of those lines.
  • A job that failed is sent as urgent, so it gets through focus time.
  • --log <project> also logs it as a run of that project; the job gets $KUROOTO_RUN, so it can add metrics with kurooto runlog metric.
  • If Kurooto isn't running, the message shows on this computer only.
  • Every watched job is listed in watch.log in Kurooto's state folder (time, exit code, duration, folder, command).

Research time in the study tracker

With the Study tracker on, a window that names one of study.research_paths (words, or folders matched by their name, e.g. ~/code/my-model) counts as Research; with Research on, its projects' names and repository folders count too.

The Research add-on: run compare and the LaTeX tracker

What it is. The narrower research tools, kept out of the everyday student features in one bundle: run compare and the LaTeX writing tracker, plus the concept graph's notation crosswalk (a Notation table when two sources write a concept differently), and Check my draft (books-and-study.md). It's off until you turn it on, and it doesn't need the papers digest (the Research feature) to be on.

Turn it on: Dashboard → Features → under Add-ons, Research add-on → Turn on. That one switch turns the whole group on. Its card then has a switch for each part (Run compare, LaTeX tracker, Notation crosswalk, and each research tool in the bundle); a part you switch off stays off the next time you turn the add-on on. Turn off turns the whole group off; your notes and files stay as they are.

Run compare

What it does. Puts two experiment runs (logged with kurooto runlog, kurooto watch --log or kurooto.runlog.Run) side by side, and writes the comparison into the project's notes:

  • Config: each key that differs (nested keys as opt.lr), and keys only one run has ("(not set)").
  • Code: the diff between the two runs' git commits (git diff, read-only: nothing is checked out or changed in your repository), how many commits apart they are, and whether either run had uncommitted changes (its diff.patch).
  • Metrics: each metric's last and lowest value in both runs, and the change (with the percentage).
  • Curves: each metric over the steps, both runs on one plot, as a picture when Kurooto's Python has matplotlib; otherwise a sparkline per run (loss A ▇▆▅▃▂▁ 1 → 0.2), on one scale so the two compare at a glance.
WhereHow
Telegram or the phone app/runs: the ten most recent runs, numbered
/runs compare 2 1: those two (by number in /runs, or by id, or a unique piece of an id)
/runs compare: the last two runs of the newest run's project
A terminalkurooto runlog diff 20261020-101500-lr-sweep 20261021-090000-lr-sweep (pieces of the ids work too)

The answer is one line ("2 config changes (lr 0.001 → 0.01); my-model: 3f2a… → 9c1d… (2 commits after A), 1 file changed; loss 0.31 → 0.12 (-61%)") and where the comparison is. In your notes:

  • research/<project>/runs/compare/<A>--<B>.md: the whole comparison (and <A>--<B>.png beside it with matplotlib).
  • In the project's note, a block Runs compared with the last ten comparisons, newest first, each linked.

Kurooto writes only inside its own marked blocks (`); comparing the same two runs again replaces that block, and your own text in these notes is never changed. Runs kept in a repository's runs/ folder (of a repository you added with /repos add`) can be compared too; their comparison goes to that repository's project.

The LaTeX writing tracker

What it does. Every night it builds the LaTeX documents you attached and keeps what each build found:

  • errors, with the line they're on;
  • undefined references (\ref to a label that isn't there);
  • missing citations (\cite keys your .bib doesn't have);
  • words written: today's word count less yesterday's. The count is an estimate, like texcount's: the prose of the document and the files it takes in with \input, \include or \subfile, without the preamble, comments, maths or commands.

In the morning, one line comes only when something broke or you wrote something, e.g. "LaTeX: thesis: 1 error: Undefined control sequence. (line 12); missing citation smith2020; +412 words. (/latex for the rest)". Quiet nights send nothing.

Attaching a document (you have to type it; Kurooto never looks for LaTeX on your disk):

CommandWhat it does
/latex add ~/papers/thesisFollows the document in that folder (the .tex with \documentclass; with several it picks main.tex, thesis.tex or paper.tex, or asks you to name it)
/latex add ~/papers/thesis chapter-draft.texFollows that file
/latexEach document's last build: errors, undefined references, missing citations, words and the change
/latex buildBuilds them now; the result comes as a message
/latex remove ~/papers/thesisStops following it (your files stay as they were)
/latex installInstalls Tectonic when there's no LaTeX on this computer

How it builds. With Tectonic (the small LaTeX program revision packs use) in its safe mode, without shell escape; without Tectonic, with your computer's own LaTeX (latexmk or pdflatex, shell escape off). The build goes to Kurooto's own cache folder: no .aux, .log or .pdf appears in your folder. A build that takes over 15 minutes (research_addon.latex_timeout_min) is stopped and counts as an error. On battery, the nightly build (and its morning line) waits for the charger.

A BibTeX entry for a missing citation. Under the morning line (or after /latex), BibTeX for smith2020 asks your AI which work the key most likely is, from the key and the sentence it's cited in. The suggestion comes as a message with Add to refs.bib and Discard: nothing is written until you tap Add, and then the entry goes at the end of the .bib the document names (\bibliography or \addbibresource), with a comment saying your AI suggested it, so check it against the paper. A key that's in the .bib already is never added twice. This needs Chat on.

What leaves your computer, and turning it off

What leaves. Your fields, keywords and searches go to the sources you pick (arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv, PubMed, the feeds), all public; never your notes. With Chat on and the AI picking, the papers' titles and summaries and what you work on go to the AI you picked. Notes you send after a meeting go to the AI you picked, to be written up as minutes (a voice note goes to Google first to be written out, as any voice note does). Commit messages and runs stay on this computer. A project set up from a repository: git fetches a GitHub repository from github.com (with your own git sign-in for a private one), and gh, if it's signed in, reads its issues and milestones from api.github.com; what your AI is shown (README, docs, the file list, manifests, licence, commit messages and authors' names, issues, milestones, the names of your courses and projects) goes to the AI you picked, with anything that looks like a key taken out and secret files never read. kurooto watch messages go through Telegram like any message. A paper you tap into Zotero goes to the Zotero app on this computer, or, with your key, to Zotero's web API (api.zotero.org): only its public details. The Research add-on: run comparisons and LaTeX builds stay on this computer; Tectonic fetches LaTeX packages it lacks from its public server (nothing of yours is sent); a BibTeX suggestion, only when you tap for one, sends the citation key and the sentence it's in to the AI you picked.

Turn it off. Features → Research → Turn off: no digests, logs or questions. Project notes and log lines stay. research.save_note = false stops the digest notes; take a search out of research.feeds to drop it; empty research.repo_runs stops reading runs (lines already in your notes stay). research.zotero = off drops the Zotero buttons, /zotero key off removes the key; delete a kurooto:zotero block from a note if you don't want it. The Research add-on: Features → Research add-on → Turn off (or a part's switch on its card); comparisons in your notes and entries you added to a .bib stay. Projects from repositories: repo_projects.on = false (no /project add), repo_projects.refresh = false (no nightly look); Undo under the message or /inferred undo <id> takes back a piece.
