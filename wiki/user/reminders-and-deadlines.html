Reminders and deadlines

Kurooto keeps two lists for you: reminders (a message at a time you pick) and deadlines (things that are due, from everywhere Kurooto can see them, chased until they're done). On top of the deadline list sit recurring quizzes, a not-interested list, exam mode and start-by dates.

Reminders

What it does. A message on Telegram at the time you ask for, with two buttons: +10 min and +1 h (each sets the same reminder again that much later).

Turn it on. Features → Reminders. It needs Telegram. It's in the ★ Starter set.

From Telegram.

What's left after the time is the text of the reminder. Words like "to", "that", "about", ":" or "-" at its start are dropped, so remind me at 6 pm: call mum says "call mum".

Which 9? Without am or pm, Kurooto picks the sensible one: 1 to 5 mean the afternoon; "tonight", "evening", "night" or "afternoon" in the sentence mean pm; "morning", "tomorrow", "noon" or "lunch" keep the hour as said; otherwise it's the next time the clock shows that hour (at 10:00, "at 9" means 21:00). A 24-hour time (21:30, 09:15) is always taken as written. call me at 5 and remind me at 5 mean five o'clock, not five minutes.

On the dashboard. The Today page (Alt+T) lists your reminders with +10 min and cancel. Type ! 18:30 call mum, ! in 20m tea or ! tomorrow 9am gym in its box to set one. Ctrl+K anywhere on the board does the same: remind 18:30 call mum.

On the phone and on calls. On a call, just say it: "remind me at six to buy milk". The AI's add_reminder tool sets it (a reminder *message*; ask for a call if you want it to ring, see calls.md). With Phone calls on, your next day's reminders are part of the brief your call line keeps. With Reminders with the lid shut on, your Android phone gets them at their time even when the computer sleeps.

At a place (location reminders)

"Remind me when I reach the library to return the book": the Android app reminds you when you get there. Off by default, on two switches: Settings → Reminders → places on the computer, and Places in the phone app (which asks to use the phone's location only then, and says the battery cost first).

You sayWhat happens
remind me when I reach the library to return the bookonce, the next time you arrive at "library"
remind me when I get home: water the plantsonce, at "home"
remind me whenever I'm at the gym to stretchevery time you arrive at "gym"

How it works:

  • Places live on the phone. In the app: Places → Turn on location reminders → type a name → Save this place while you're standing there (no map). A place counts within 150 m of where you saved it.
  • Your computer only knows the name. The reminder goes to the app as the place's name and the text. A reminder for a place the app hasn't saved yet waits under Waiting for a place, with "I'm here: save it as library". "The library", "library" and "my library" are the same place.
  • You can add reminders in the app too: each place has a "Remind me here to…" box, once or every time.
  • When it comes: a notification "At library: return the book" when you arrive from somewhere else (not while you stay there, and not twice within 20 minutes). A once reminder is gone after it's said.
  • Battery. Android checks where you are a few times an hour while the app is in the background, mostly from Wi-Fi and the phone network (the GPS isn't kept on), more often near a saved place. The cost is small, and a reminder can come a few minutes after you arrive.
  • Android 10 and newer ask a second time for "Allow all the time": without it, reminders only come while the app is open. Android 6 to 9 ask once.
  • It works with the computer off: once the reminder is on the phone, the phone does the rest. No Google Play services needed. Not on iPhone.

Limits and edge cases.

  • The computer was asleep or off at the time: the reminder comes as soon as Kurooto runs again, marked "Reminder (late: the computer was asleep): ...". Nothing is dropped. With Reminders with the lid shut on, the Android app shows it on time instead, and Telegram doesn't repeat it.
  • Paused: reminders due while Kurooto is paused arrive when it resumes.
  • Focus time (/focus): reminders are your own, so Calm never holds them, but focus does hold messages that aren't urgent until it ends.
  • A time about the past is never used. "remind me, I didn't do it at 3" doesn't set a 3 o'clock reminder: Kurooto says it didn't set one and asks when.
  • "Don't", "never", "stop" or "no need to" in the sentence, or several requests in one message ("remind me at 9 to call mum and skip the check-in"), go to your AI instead of being handled instantly. With Chat on, the AI can still set the reminder with its tool.
  • Plain English understands today, tonight, tomorrow, the day after tomorrow, a weekday and "in ...". A weekday counts when it says when ("friday at 5", "on friday", "this friday"), not when it's part of what to remember ("revise for friday's quiz"). For a date, use /remind (/remind 2026-11-02 14:00 ...) or ask your AI.
  • A bare number in /remind is the hour. /remind 9 call mum is the next 9:00 (morning or evening, whichever comes first); /remind 18 gym is 18:00. Minutes need their unit: /remind 20m tea or /remind in 20 min tea.
  • Cancel with the button under /reminders or on the Today page. "Already gone" means it fired or was cancelled.
  • The snooze buttons work for the last 200 reminders that fired; older ones say "That reminder is gone".

What leaves your computer. The reminder goes through Telegram to your own bot. A location reminder goes to your phone app through your call line (the place's name and the text); where your places are never leaves the phone. With Reminders with the lid shut on, the next day's reminders go to your call line sealed for your phone (it can read only their times).

Turn it off. Features → Reminders → Turn off. Nothing reminds you while it's off, but the reminders you had set are kept: turn it on again and they're back, and any whose time passed meanwhile come at once as "Reminder (late: Reminders was off): ...". To get rid of one, cancel it (/reminders) before turning the feature off.

Deadlines

What it does. One list of everything that's due, gathered from every place Kurooto can see, and a chase that doesn't let things slip: a message a day before, a call six hours before, and an "Overdue?" check after.

Turn it on. Features → Deadlines. It needs Telegram. It's in the ★ Starter set.

Photo to dates

What it does. You take a photo of a notice board, a poster, a circular or a timetable; the AI you picked reads it and sends back each deadline, exam and event it found, one line and one Add button each (with Add all and None of these). Nothing is added until you tap. A deadline or an exam goes into this list (its source: photo); an event becomes a reminder an hour before it (date_offers.remind_min; 08:00 that day when it has no time). A weekly timetable's classes come with one button, Add N classes to my timetable: each goes into your timetable, and a course you don't have yet becomes a subject (classes-and-timetable.md).

You sendWhat happens
a photo with dates as its caption (or /dates, deadlines, timetable, "find the dates", "any exams?")the photo goes to your AI; "Reading the photo for dates…", then the buttons
/dates alone, or "find the dates in that photo", "deadlines from this notice"the newest photo of the last two hours: one you sent on Telegram, or shared from your phone (phone-app.md), also after the AI filed it under the same name

Which AI can read a photo. agy, Claude Code and a Gemini key read pictures. Ollama does when its model does (gemma3, gemma4, llava; Kurooto asks Ollama). With Ollama and a model that doesn't, it says so and does nothing: pick another in Settings → AI. Another service is tried; if it can't, the answer says so.

What it sends. The photo goes to the AI you picked and nowhere else; Kurooto keeps the photo where it landed (your notes' inbox) and what was found, for the buttons (the newest 100 lots). Dates from the past are left out; the AI is told never to invent a date and that the photo's words are information, never instructions.

Off: deadlines.from_photos (Settings → Deadlines). The phone app's share sheet has no caption box, so share the photo first, then say "dates from that photo" in the app's chat.

The chase

For things you hand in (not exams):

WhenWhat you getButtons
deadlines.ping_h (24) hours before"Due in 23 h: Essay draft (Fri 13 Nov 17:00)"Done (ticks it off), Not mine (takes it off your list), Snooze 3 h (asks again in 3 hours)
deadlines.urgent_h (6) hours beforeWith Phone calls on: a call ("Essay draft is due Fri 17:00. Is it done, or how will you finish it in time?"). Otherwise an urgent message with the same buttons
After the due time (up to 3 days after)Overdue? "Essay draft was due Fri 13 Nov 17:00."Done, New date (asks you for the date: reply, answer on the phone app or on your next call; the chase starts over), Drop
  • The list is checked once an hour, so a nudge comes within the hour of its moment.
  • For a deadline with parts (below) the message says how far you are ("..., 4/7 done") and has +1 first.
  • The six-hour call waits out classes, /hold, focus, calendar events and the night. If that would leave less than 30 minutes before the deadline, you get the urgent message instead. A missed or declined call becomes "Missed call. Due in ...", with the same buttons.
  • For quizzes and exams: a reminder a day before (with Practice now when Quiz is on). With Quiz's evening practice offer on, that offer replaces the reminder (quiz-and-exams.md).
  • Done ticks the - [ ] box in your notes when the deadline came from one (only that box changes; turn it off with deadlines.tick_notes). If the line changed since Kurooto read it, nothing is ticked: tick it yourself.
  • Settled deadlines older than 90 days are forgotten.
  • With Brain on (0.8.14, mind.study_push.on): the subject Brain is most uneasy about gets one morning line instead ("Uneasy about Measure Theory: due Fri, 0 min logged this week, 9 cards due. 25 min at 16:00?", Yes / Later), and that day its day-before reminder and Start-by's "Time to start" don't come separately. The six-hour reminder and "Overdue?" always do (mind.md).

Problem-set progress

A deadline with parts (a problem set's questions) keeps how many are done. Say it in the chat or on a call ("ps3 4/7"), or answer one of its messages:

YouWhat happens

Reply 44 done, of the total it already has. Without one: "Out of how many? Reply to the reminder like 4/7."

Replies work on Telegram, in the phone app's chat (Reply under the message) and with Reply on the phone's notification. +1 works on all of them too; on the phone it waits on your call line while your computer is off, and each tap counts, in order (phone-app.md).

In the morning. The wake-up call, the morning page and /status say a deadline with parts as "Problem set 3 4/7, due Fri" (or "due tomorrow", "due today 17:00"; with the date when it's a week or more away). The lists show "(4/7)", and Start-by plans from what's left.

On the dashboard. The Today page shows what's due in the next three weeks (red when overdue, amber under a day) with done and new date, and an add box (essay draft fri 17:00: what, then when). Ctrl+K: due essay draft fri 17:00. The board's due corner shows the same list.

On calls. Every call's brief has what's due in the next deadlines.plan_h hours. Say "the lab report is done", "it got moved to Friday", "add a deadline: poster due Monday 9am", "I don't care about the drama club". The AI's tools: mark_deadline_done, add_deadline, move_deadline, ignore_topic, add_recurring_quiz. Done while the computer is off, they wait and run when it's back.

The course. add_deadline takes the course by name or short name ("pota"), so a deadline the mail digest finds ("Lab report due Wed" in a mail from your Physics of the Atmosphere teacher) lands on that course even when its title doesn't name it; lists show it as "Physics of the Atmosphere: Lab report". A course Kurooto doesn't know, or words naming two, isn't guessed: the answer says so and lists your courses, and the deadline goes on no course (unless its title names one).

How far ahead "due soon" looks (deadlines.plan_h, 72 hours): what's due in that window goes into the wake-up call's plan, the morning page, the call brief, /status and "what's due soon?". Set 168 to plan a week ahead, 36 for little more than tomorrow. /deadlines and the chase don't change.

What leaves your computer. Nothing by itself. The list is kept in Kurooto's state folder. With Phone calls on, what's due soon is in your call line's brief. The AI you picked sees the list when it reads it for you.

Turn it off. Features → Deadlines → Turn off (Start-by dates and Exam season need it, so turn those off first). The list itself is kept for when you turn it on again; the chase stops at once. Just the call: deadlines.call off. Just the overdue question: deadlines.overdue_check off.

Recurring quizzes and tests

What it does. Quizzes and tests that repeat ("Linear Algebra quiz every Thursday 10:00", "Statistics test every other Friday") become dated items on the deadline list, so reminders, exam mode and practice offers follow them. It's part of Deadlines.

From Telegram.

"Every" can be weekly, every week, each, every other, every second, every 2 weeks, fortnightly or biweekly (up to 8 weeks). What it is can be a quiz, class test, test, assessment, exam, viva, lab test, worksheet, problem sheet, homework, assignment or problem set.

The table in your notes. They live in kurooto/recurring.md (master/recurring-assessments.md in the zettelkasten layout). Edit it like any note:

linear-algebraQuiz1Thu10:00
  • Every is in weeks (or "weekly", "every other week"). Next is any date it happens on: needed for every two weeks, so Kurooto knows which week. Until is optional.
  • The header can use your own column names ("Course" for "Subject"); columns are read by position.
  • Missing day or time: Kurooto uses the subject's class from your timetable. When it can't tell (the subject has several classes, or it's every two weeks with no Next), it asks once with buttons ("Which day (and time) is the Linear Algebra quiz?", or "when is the next one?"). For a row you wrote yourself, your answer is kept by Kurooto and your note isn't changed. The question expires after 3 days.
  • Holidays from your subjects settings are skipped.
  • Items are made deadlines.horizon_days (30) days ahead.

Turn it off. deadlines.recurring off. The table stays in your notes.

Not interested

What it does. Topics you don't care about (a club, a kind of event, a course you dropped) are left out: matching deadlines leave the list and the chase, and the mail and Classroom digests only count matching items instead of making tasks from them.

From Telegram.

You sendWhat happens
/ignore drama clubadds it; says how many open deadlines it now hides
I don't care about the drama club · I'm not interested in sports day · stop telling me about hackathons · ignore chess club events from now onthe same
/ignoredthe list
/unignore drama clubtakes it off

On a call: "I don't care about the drama club" (the ignore_topic tool). A topic must be at least 3 characters and at most 6 words; "that" or "it" alone isn't taken.

The note. The list is about-me/not-interested.md (master/not-interested.md in the zettelkasten layout), one topic per line: - drama club, or - drama club — not this term (the part after the dash is your reason and isn't matched). Edit it freely. Matching is on whole words and phrases, ignoring case.

What leaves your computer. Nothing.

Turn it off. Empty the note, or /unignore each topic.

Exam mode

What it does. Two weeks before a quiz or exam on your deadline list (deadlines.exam_mode_days, 14), exam mode starts for that subject, and ends after it. You're told once: "Exam mode for Linear Algebra: Linear Algebra midterm, Thu 12 Nov 10:00 (in 9 days). Quizzes come more often until then. /exam shows it."

While it's on:

  • /exam, exam mode or "when's my next exam?" (also quiz, test) shows each subject's nearest quiz or exam, and, with Quiz on, how many questions are due to practise;
  • calls start knowing it (it's in the brief);
  • with Quiz on, sessions are longer (quiz.exam_per_session, 6), items you know come back before the exam, and you can have a short practice every day (quiz.exam_daily, e.g. 18:00). Details: quiz-and-exams.md.

Exam mode is automatic. Exam season is a separate, optional add-on for the whole exam period (it's off and in no preset; turn it on by hand): see quiz-and-exams.md.

Turn it off. deadlines.exam_mode_days = 0. /exam then says it's turned off.

Start-by dates and crunch weeks

What it does. Works out when to start each deadline, back from how much work it is and your free time, and warns you about weeks where the work due is more than the time you have. It's an estimate, plain arithmetic on this computer: no AI.

Turn it on. Features → Start-by dates. It needs Deadlines. It's off until you turn it on.

Sizes. Each deadline gets a size from its title:

KindWords in the titleDefault size
Problem setproblem set, pset, homework, hw, assignment, worksheet, exercise, sheet, tutorial, lab3 h (an evening)
Reportreport, essay, term paper, write-up, thesis, dissertation, proposal, project, presentation, poster12 h (2-3 days)
Quiz or testquiz, test2 h
Examexam, midterm, finals, midsem, endsemwhat you set for its subject (10 h until you do)
Anything else2 h

Correct a size with one tap: the "Time to start" message and /startby have buttons with the other sizes (An hour, An evening, A day, 2-3 days, A week; for exams An evening up to Two weeks). A size you tap for an exam counts for that subject's exams from then on. The first exam of a subject asks you once: "How long do you usually need to prepare for a Statistics exam?". On a call or to your AI: "the lab report is about a day of work". A day of work counts as 5 h, a week as 25 h.

Free time. Each day from 09:00 to 22:00 (startby.window), minus your classes (timetable, holidays, exam periods, classes you /skip) and your calendar's timed events, in gaps of at least 30 minutes. Only 40% of that counts (startby.share): the rest is meals, rest and everything else. Calendar events are known two weeks ahead; after that only classes count, so far-off weeks look freer than they will be.

The window learns your day (startby.learn_window, on). Once a week (Sunday noon) it looks at when your messages came in over the last three weeks. With 7 or more days of 3+ messages, and the middle of your first messages or of your last ones an hour or more from the window's ends, the window moves there, to the half hour (starting between 05:00 and 13:00, ending between 17:00 and 03:00): "Start-by dates now count your free time from 11:00 to 01:30, not 09:00 to 22:00", with Undo. Not again for a week; after an Undo, not for a month. A window you set yourself is never changed.

Start-by dates. Working back from the due times, latest first, each deadline's hours go into the latest free time before it that a later deadline hasn't taken. The day its first hour lands on is its start-by date. Two big deadlines in one week push each other earlier.

From that date until it's due, the deadline is in the morning plan: the morning page (Time to start), the wake-up call and the plan drafted before the alarm. On the day itself, at 08:00 (startby.when; it waits out focus) you get one message: "Time to start: Lab report, due Fri 13 Nov. It's about 12 h (2-3 days) of work...", with the sizes to tap. At most three such messages a day, and only when it's two or more days before the due date (closer than that, the deadline's own day-before message says it). When there isn't enough free time left, it says so. The first morning after you turn it on, one message lists what's to start now.

With Exam season's revision plan on, the day's revision blocks are in the morning plan too, after what's to start ("Revise Jordan form, 10:00-11:00 · for Linear Algebra final exam").

Negotiate, don't repeat (startby.negotiate, on). When a deadline's reminder would come again (the six-hour one after the day-ahead one, one after a Snooze, the message after a missed deadline call, or any reminder for a deadline whose "Time to start" came on an earlier day) and the deadline is slipping, you get a reshaped plan instead, from your own Start-by plan: "Problem set 4 (due Tue 03 Nov 11:00): your Start-by plan has 2 h of it today, and about 1 h of today's free time is left. 1 h now, 1 h tomorrow?" with Do that and Not today.

  • *Slipping* means this morning's plan put hours of it into today, and what's left of today's free time (the same 40% share) no longer holds them. Outside your day's window, or with no free time tomorrow before it's due, nothing changes.
  • Do that: tomorrow's part gets a reminder at the start of tomorrow's first free time ("Problem set 4: the 1 h you moved to today"), and with the Agent plan calendar on, both parts go in as blocks.
  • Not today: nothing more about it today.
  • One offer per deadline per day; after that the reminders come as they always did. The words are fixed and plain: no rising tone, no urgency that isn't in the dates.
  • On a deadline call, the AI has the same plan as the reshape_plan and reshape_answer tools and is told to offer it once, calmly, when you're behind.

Crunch weeks. The Sunday weekly summary flags weeks in the next four (startby.crunch_weeks) where the work due adds up to more than the free time: "Crunch weeks (an estimate): week of 9 Nov: about 14 h of work, about 9 h free. Start some of it the week before." It also says what to start in the coming week.

What leaves your computer. Nothing.

Turn it off. Features → Start-by dates. Only the messages: startby.tell off. The exam question: startby.ask_exams off. Crunch weeks: startby.crunch_weeks = 0. Sizes you tapped are kept for when you turn it on again; reset startby.exam_by_subject to forget exam sizes.

When something goes wrong

What you seeWhat to do
A reminder came late, marked "(late: the computer was asleep)"The computer was asleep or off at that time. Keep it awake on the charger (your-computer.md) or turn on Reminders with the lid shut (calls.md).
/remind 20 tea set 20:00, not in 20 minutesA bare number is the hour. Minutes need their unit: 20m.
Kurooto asked "When should I remind you?"The only time in your message was about the past ("I didn't do it at 3"). Say the time you want.
A deadline shows up twiceTheir titles have different numbers or dates. Tap Not mine on one.
A calendar event became a deadline by mistakeNot mine on it, /ignore a word in its title, or deadlines.from_calendar off.
No call six hours before a deadlinePhone calls on and set up? A class, /hold, focus, an event or the night until shortly before the deadline means a message instead.
Done didn't tick the box in my notesThe line changed since Kurooto read it (it reads hourly). Tick it yourself.
A task in my notes isn't on the listIt needs a date in brackets, (2026-11-02) or (2026-11-02 17:00), on an unticked - [ ] line.
A recurring quiz is on the wrong weekPut a date it really happens on in the Next column of the recurring table.
/exam says exam mode is turned offdeadlines.exam_mode_days is 0. Set it to 14.
Start-by dates are too early or too lateTap the right size on a deadline (/startby). If they're all off, change startby.share or startby.window.

On a call

Say "that quiz is cancelled" or "I don't have that quiz" and the call removes it for good (it doesn't come back from Classroom, a note or the recurring table); "I finished it" marks it done; "it moved to Friday" moves it. A quiz or exam whose time has passed is simply over: it's never listed as overdue. After every call, Kurooto looks back at what you asked for and fixes anything the call got wrong (see Phone calls).

Notes the scan should leave alone

Kurooto finds deadlines in your notes by looking for unticked checkbox lines that end in a date, like - [ ] hand in the form (2026-10-30). A list of ideas where the date is just *when you wrote it down* would turn into "Overdue?" nudges. To stop that, put its path (or a folder) in the setting deadlines.skip_notes (dashboard → Settings → Deadlines), for example master/ideas.md. Anything already found from it: tap Drop once and it stays gone.
