The dashboard

The dashboard is a page on your own computer at http://127.0.0.1:8770. It shows what Kurooto is doing, lets you turn features on and off, change settings, pair a phone, and do everyday things (reminders, deadlines, the alarm, your journal) without Telegram. kurooto open opens it in your browser.

This page covers the dashboard, the morning page, the colours, kurooto top (the same board in a terminal) and the two terminal features. For talking to Kurooto in general see talking-to-kurooto.md.

Opening it

  • Go to http://127.0.0.1:8770 in any browser, or run kurooto open (it prints the address too). 127.0.0.1 means "this computer".
  • If another program already uses port 8770, Kurooto takes the next free one (it tries 30 in a row). kurooto paths shows the state folder; web.json there says which port is in use. You can pick the starting port with the web.port setting (default 8770; Settings → Dashboard, then restart Kurooto).
  • The setup link in the top bar goes back to the setup page (/setup), for example to add more AI accounts to About you or to restore a profile. While setup steps you skipped are waiting, it says finish setup (N) and opens the first of them.
  • If Kurooto isn't running, the page can't load. Start it from your app menu (Kurooto) or with kurooto on.
  • Leaving it open costs little: while the dashboard is in a background tab or a minimised window, Kurooto stops reading the computer and your notes for it, and catches up the moment you look again.

Who can open it. Only this computer: the page listens on 127.0.0.1 only. Every request needs a key that only the page Kurooto serves gets, requests from other websites are refused, and the page loads nothing from the internet. On Linux, where several people can be signed in at once, it also checks that the page and every request come from your own user account, and refuses them when it can't tell.

Two designs: new and classic

DesignLooks
new (default since 0.5.19)set in a sans-serif typeface (Public Sans, which comes with Kurooto: nothing is fetched from the internet), with figures that keep their width as they change; monospace only for the terminals, code and file paths. Rounded panes with their name and figures on a header line, a top bar with the name, a status line, study, panes ▾ and the design button; thin-line graphs and bars in the palette's colours
classicthe look of 0.5.18 and earlier: everything in monospace, thin borders with each pane's name set into its top line, dotted graphs

Both have the same panes, pages, keys, search and data, and both follow the palette (Colours).

Switch: the classic look button in the top bar (in classic: panes ▾ → new look, or the top bar in the roomy layout), or Dashboard → Settings → Look → look.design. It changes at once, without a reload, and every browser gets it (it's a setting). *Undo:* switch back the same way.

Narrow windows (a window tiled to half the screen, say 940 px of a 1920 px screen) get roomy. It shows every pane: STATUS down the left, NOTES wide beside it, SYSTEMS and BRAIN under NOTES, and the pages as tabs across the bottom. With the being as your board (look.board = being), the being takes the place of SYSTEMS and BRAIN (it draws both), with NOTES and STATUS beside it. A BRAIN pane under 600 px wide keeps its region panel as a drawer (the ‹ at its right edge). If the window is too short for the panes, the page scrolls instead of squeezing them; under 900 px wide they stack in one column. (Before 0.8.16 the roomy board left STATUS, SYSTEMS and BRAIN no height, and NOTES a narrow strip, on long pages such as Features.)

Going narrow and back keeps what you had: the page open over the dense board shows as roomy's tab while the window is narrow, and comes back over the board when it's wide again; with no page open, the board comes back without one. A tab you pick while the window is narrow opens over the board when it's wide again (Features, Phone, Research, About you and Settings; after another tab the board comes back without a page). (Before 0.8.17 the board came back with Features, or roomy's last tab, open, and a page such as Chat closed.)

Setting: look.layout (dense or roomy), Dashboard → Settings → Look. The change shows at once.

The board (dense layout)

Four panes: STATUS down the left, SYSTEMS and NOTES on top, TERMINAL below. A top bar shows Kurooto's name and version, a short status line ("12 features on · 0 AI requests running · cpu 7%"), a needs a look badge when something isn't working, an update badge when a new version is ready, and the setup link (the new design; classic shows it only in the roomy layout and keeps study and panes ▾ in the board's top-right corner).

The being (the board since 0.8.9)

The board shows Kurooto as one body, drawn from what it's really doing; the panes below are still a click away (the panes ▾ menu: board being, three or classic, or any one pane filling the board, with Esc back to the being).

  • The mind (the brain): each kind of work sparks in its own region: AI requests in the prefrontal cortex, plans beside it, what may reach you in the orbitofrontal cortex, speaking in Broca's area, memory in the hippocampus, and so on (the colours' key is top left). Click the brain to open it; click a region for its Brain parts (each with its switch) and the features that live there. The sparks show through the brain, in their own colours. At rest the brain keeps a low trickle of sparks, each from something Kurooto really does all the time: every reading of its body (every 2 seconds, more the busier your processor) in the insula, its own processor use in the thalamus, a scheduler timer that fired in its feature's region, notes that changed in the hippocampus, sound heard in the auditory cortex. Turn it off with look.being_resting_sparks; Remove animations and your system's reduced motion turn it off too. The fluid round the brain is a pale aqua.
  • The heart beats faster the busier Kurooto has been in the last ten minutes. Its parts: the pacemaker (what runs next), the ventricles (AI requests running), the atria (answers), the aorta (the call line), the coronaries (upkeep: backups, updates, sync). Blood (tinted red) carries work out to the organ doing it and back when it reports. The vessels stay joined to their organs however you turn them; the brain's enter at its base, by the brainstem.
  • The nerves leave the brainstem as the spinal cord, run down behind the heart and split into three: to the heart, the senses and memory. Each is a bundle of fibres with nerve cells along it; a signal (something heard, notes synced, an answer) lights its own fibres in its colour as it passes.
  • The senses: the eye (your screen and activity), the coil (this computer: click it for every core, memory, swap, graphics, disks, battery and temperatures, live), the cilia (your phone in your hand), the cochlea (hearing: the call line and Voice), the canals (your phone's motion).
  • Memory is your notes, in the NOTES map's neighbourhoods, each in a clear colour of its own, laid out by their links (the ones through a "Seen in" or a reading list too, a little fainter). Point at a note for its title; click it for a preview and open it.
  • Points of light are the features, on the organ that does their work; they flare when the feature does something. Click one for its switch and all its settings, editable right there.

Turning it: drag an organ to turn it (a sense on its own); dragging empty space doesn't move anything. Wheel zooms, right-drag pans. Every part focuses: click a region, a heart part, a sense, a neighbourhood, a note or a feature and the view flies to it; it stays solid, glowing in its colour with a fine ring round it, and the rest of its organ turns to faint glass so nothing hides it; Esc (or a click on empty space) goes back one level. The brain's views (top of the board while the brain is open): Solid (the default), Cutaway (the top lifts off to show the inside) and X-ray (the cortex as glass, the thalamus, hippocampus, caudate and corpus callosum inside). H (or hints top right) hides the hints: the lines under each name, the help line and the colours' key (look.being_hints). Mute stops Voice hearing and speaking. look.being_theme: void (white light on black) or theme (the page's palette; on a light palette nothing glows, so the being stays visible).

The voice lanes at the bottom: YOU shows the shape of what you said to Kurooto; speech it heard that wasn't to it (chatter, a call in the room) shows too, fainter, as "heard, not to Kurooto": only its loudness, never words. While Voice listens, YOU is live: the microphone's loudness scrolls through it as you speak (the last six seconds, newest at the right), settles into your turn's outline when the turn ends, and fades out when nothing has come for a moment and a half. Turn the live trace off with look.being_live_waveform; Remove animations and your system's reduced motion keep it still (it sweeps across instead of scrolling). Both lanes are smooth waveforms, older parts fainter.

Brain's glance (0.8.14, with Brain on): the being shows Brain's status. Its eyes shut while focus time is on or it's asleep, a small number by the heart counts the messages it's holding, and a slow glow sits on the one deadline it's uneasy about: click it to open the deadline (look.being_status). Click the held number to open the Waiting page (0.9.1, Alt+W, look.being_held_link): every held message in full, why it waits and since when, with Send now (they go out as the evening bundle would), the "while you were away" card (face.md), and how many questions and proposals wait. Its own mood tints the fluid round the brain and sets the heart's beat: a low mood is only dimmer and softer, never sad (look.being_mood). More: mind.md.

The talk box (bottom right, on every board; Alt+K): type to Kurooto as on Telegram; the answer prints in it. Turn it off with look.talk_box. (0.9.1) What you type there and its answers also go to the Desk chat (Chat page, phone app; calls.chat_desk), so an answer that comes after you closed the page isn't lost; it never goes to Telegram. calls.chat_desk_history (off) lets the talk box follow on from the Desk chat's recent turns.

STATUS: this computer

A monitor like the program btop, the same as kurooto top:

  • Header: the date and time, how long the computer has been up, today's event count, the battery (percent, time left, watts) or "ac", Kurooto's mode ("saving battery" or "paused …"), the update and health badges, and three buttons: pause 1h (or resume while paused), off and setup.
  • CPU: a history graph, every core with its own little graph, speed, load, temperatures and fans (where the computer reports them).
  • MEM: used, cached, available, swap. GPU: use, its memory, temperature, power and a graph (needs NVIDIA's nvidia-smi, or an AMD card on Linux; a sleeping laptop graphics card isn't woken to ask).
  • NET: download above the line, upload below, and totals. DISKS: how full, reads and writes per second.
  • PROCESSES: the busiest programs. Click cpu or mem in the header to sort (this browser remembers it). Kurooto's own processes are marked ✦ (a dot in the new design).
  • STUDY TODAY: minutes per subject, with the Study tracker on.
  • PROJECTS: your git folders with each one's last commit, what isn't pushed yet and what's changed. List the folders in look.projects (default empty: the repositories you gave Research).

pause 1h pauses Kurooto for an hour (Telegram still answers and keeps what you send; see your-computer.md). off asks first, then turns Kurooto off completely: it stops, won't start at sign-in, and your call line stops placing scheduled calls until you open Kurooto from your app menu or run kurooto on. The page then only says that Kurooto is off.

SYSTEMS: the features as living cells

Each feature that's on is a round cell around a beating core labelled with your assistant's name. Pulses run along the lines between the core and the cells as messages, reminders and AI answers come and go. The hours of the day run around the ring.

  • A cell's words sit just outside it: its name and its figures. A feature that runs programs of its own shows their cpu and memory ("running · 2.1% · 140M"); one whose programs aren't running turns grey and dashed ("not running"); one whose programs only run now and then says "idle". Other features say what they did last ("last 4m ago") and when they run next ("next in 2h").
  • Hover over a cell for its card: events in the last 2 minutes, its processes (cpu, memory, threads, uptime), the last event and the next scheduled time. Its nerve lights up and it swells a little. On a very crowded ring a few labels may hide; the card always works.
  • In the new design (0.5.21) SYSTEMS is the largest pane on the board and looks alive: the core is a soft membrane with rims that each heartbeat runs out through, every feature is a small neuron whose branches grow with how busy it was over the last two minutes (oldest to newest, clockwise), and the nerves are tapered fibres, thick at the core and fine at the cell, that flash where a pulse arrives. Classic keeps its round cells and plain lines.

NOTES: a map of your notes

Every note in your notes folder is a dot; links between notes are lines. Notes are grouped into coloured neighbourhoods by folder: each sources/<subject>/ folder is one (in the kurooto notes layout, each subject folder with a course.md), each research project (projects/<name>/ or research/<name>/) is one, the other maps and hubs are the hub ("Maps & hubs"), and any other note joins the neighbourhood most of its links go to (or "Other Notes"). Grouping works best with the zettelkasten notes layout (see notes-and-concept-graph.md).

Names on the map (new in 0.5.19). At the usual zoom the map names its regions, the way a map names places: each course by its course page's title ("MTH201 Linear Algebra") and each research project by its index note's title, in the region's colour at the middle of its notes. A note's own name shows when you point at it, find it, light the recent ones (R) or zoom in; zoomed in, course pages, hubs and projects are named first, then notes that link across courses, then the best-linked. A book's index note (<subject>/books/<book>) is drawn small and named last: once the concept graph reads a book, hundreds of notes link to it from their "Seen in" lines, and before 0.5.19 it took every label and the map read as a list of book titles. The region names step back while you point at or search for a note.

What you know, on the map (0.9.0, look.notes_mastery, on). With Brain's model of what you know on (mind.tom.on), concept notes get a ring over their neighbourhood's colour: green for one you know well, sand for one partly known, and a double peach ring for one you're ready to learn next (its prerequisites are solid). Pointing at a note says it in words ("Brain: ready to learn next"). It's worked out on this computer and read again every two minutes; off, or with Brain off, no rings.

Pointing at a note (0.6.1). The note and the notes it links to (or that link to it) light up in full colour with their names raised above everything else, placed so they never overlap (up to 24 neighbours' names, the course pages, hubs and links you made first); everything else dims and the region names step aside. It eases in over about a tenth of a second (at once with reduced motion). The keyboard does the same: Tab to the map, then the arrow keys move to the linked note in that direction (or the nearest note that way), Enter opens it, Esc lets go.

"Seen in" links don't make hubs (0.6.1). The links Kurooto writes to say where a concept was read (a concept note's "Seen in", a book's or class note's "Concepts in ..." list, a course page's "From the books") are still drawn, but they don't make a dot bigger or get its name shown first: a lecture note or a class post that many concepts were read from stays a normal-sized dot. Its card says how many such links it has: "3 links (+152 from reading)".

Research on the map (new in 0.5.21). A research project's region is its folder's notes, the notes its index links to, and the topics its line in the research hub (maps/research.md, or your layout's research hub) links to, together with the lines indented under it. It's always named, however few notes its folder has: by its index note's title, or the hub's line for it ("L96 parameterization" in - [[projects/l96/index|L96 parameterization]]: ...). Research regions and their names are in the accent colour, their names a little bolder. Concept notes (zettel/) and any other note outside a course or project folder join the region most of their links come from, a course or a project (a tie goes to the course), so the cloud of concept notes splits into named topics; a note with no such links stays unnamed ("Other Notes").

TERMINAL: plain English right in the page

The bottom pane's border holds its tabs. With the Terminals feature on, there are real terminals for your AI's own program (agy or Claude Code, one tab for each that's installed), a shell and Kurooto's log. Without it, there's a + terminals tab that explains the feature.

The tab named after your assistant (kurooto) is always there: type to Kurooto as on Telegram. Quick requests ("push the evening check-in by 30 min", "what's due this week?", "no calls till 6") answer at once; anything bigger goes to your AI, and its answer prints here, not on Telegram. The pane's header (classic: its border) also lists the pages (today, activity, jobs, schedule, features, phone, research, you, settings), each with its key letter underlined, and a reminder of the keys.

Study mode

A calm board for studying: only your notes and the things that help you study. Everything else (STATUS, SYSTEMS, the terminal, the panes menu) waits, untouched, and comes back exactly as it was when you leave.

Switch it on or off: study in the top bar (classic design: the board's top-right corner; it reads leave study while it's on), Alt+M, Ctrl+K → study on / study off, or from Telegram or the kurooto tab /study mode on / /study mode off (plain words work too: "study mode", "leave study mode"). The page follows at once wherever you switch it. It stays on across reloads and in every browser (the setting look.study_mode).

What it shows:

  • NOTES, large on the left: the map, with the chosen subject's notes lit and fitted to the pane. The note you pick from the list is centred with its first lines; double-click opens it.
  • STUDY, on the right:
  • Timer: pick a subject, then start. A focus (25 minutes, study.focus_min) is focus time: calls and messages that can wait are held until it ends, and its minutes are logged to that subject in the Study tracker. Then a break (5 minutes, study.break_min); after it the next focus waits for you. pause keeps what's left (the minutes so far are logged), resume goes on, reset ends it. The digits show in the tab's title while it runs. It keeps time with the page closed, and Telegram says when a focus or a break ends.
  • then leave Study mode: tick it and Study mode ends when the focus ends, instead of a break (study.timer_ends_mode).
  • Today: minutes per subject.
  • Due: quizzes and exams (marked *exam*) and tasks in the next two weeks, and what's time to start (Start-by dates).
  • Recall: with Quiz on, up to three cards due for the subject from its exam-prep notes; click one to see it. /quiz <subject> practises them.
  • Open (0.9.0, look.study_extras, on): the subject's open doubts from the Doubt queue (with it on), and with Brain's model of what you know on (mind.tom.on) the concept it thinks comes next for you (a concept not mastered whose prerequisites are; one of this subject's first, else "another subject"). Click it to find it on the map. Shown only when there's something to show.
  • Notes: the subject's course page, exam prep, doubts and class log, then its lecture and concept notes, newest first (wherever your notes layout keeps them).

How it behaves:

  • As you type, a list shows what Enter would do. ↑ / ↓ pick a line, Enter does it, Tab puts the highlighted verb in front of what you typed.
  • A bare word is completed first: re then Enter gives remind , with a hint of what to type. With something after the verb, Enter runs it and the line clears for the next one.
  • What happened shows under the line ("Reminder set for 18:30", or why not). For an ask that goes to your AI, the answer arrives there too, with which AI answered, its effort and how long it took. It waits up to about 20 minutes; after that, look in the AI jobs page.
  • Times for remind: 18:30, 6pm, in 20m, in 2 hours, tomorrow, tomorrow 9am, friday 9:00. Times for due: a weekday with a time (fri 17:00), tomorrow (with or without a time), a date (2026-11-02, or 2026-11-02 17:00) or in 3 days.
  • Every action is exactly the one the Telegram command and the AI's tool do, so they never disagree. A feature that's off says "… is off: turn it on in Features". The alarm needs Phone calls.

Search, hiding features, bigger panes

Search

Features and Settings each have a search box at the top. Press / or Ctrl+F while the page is open (Ctrl+F a second time is the browser's own find). It filters as you type, ignoring case; every word has to be found somewhere.

  • Features are found by name, description, privacy line and their settings. A card found only by a setting says which ("its settings: deadlines.ping_h").
  • Settings are found by section, name, help text and current value. Each section keeps only the settings that match, laid out as without a search (before 0.8.17 one that wasn't its section's first could end up squeezed down the right edge).
  • The words found are marked, with a count ("3 of 41 features"), or "nothing matches".
  • Esc empties the box.

Hiding features

A feature that's off has hide on its card: it leaves the Features page and nothing else changes (Telegram, the setup page and everything else still know it; it stays off). A feature that's on always shows. Show hidden (N) at the bottom lists hidden ones with Unhide, and a search still finds them, marked *hidden*. Turning a hidden feature on from a search unhides it.

Setting: look.hidden_features (default []). *Undo:* Unhide, or empty the list.

Bigger panes and the panes menu (dense layout)

  • Fill the board with one pane: click its name (⤢) or double-click its border. Esc or its name again brings the others back.

Terminals (in the dashboard)

What it does. Real terminals inside the dashboard: your AI's own program (agy or Claude Code, started in your notes folder as you'd run it yourself; one tab for each that's installed, the one you use first), your usual shell (PowerShell on Windows), kurooto ask (plain English, like Telegram) and Kurooto's log.

Turn it on: Dashboard → Features → Terminals → Turn on. Only turn this on if you're the only person who uses this computer: anyone who can open your dashboard can type into these terminals.

Using them. In the dense layout they're tabs in the TERMINAL pane's border; in roomy, a Terminals tab. Alt+1, Alt+2 … switch between them. Each starts the first time you open it. They keep running when you close the page and show where they were when you open it again. One that ended starts again when you press a key in it, or with restart (ends the program in that terminal and starts it again).

Limits. On Linux a terminal request from another user account is refused on purpose, and if the computer can't tell who is connecting (no /proc/net/tcp), terminals refuse everyone. What you type into your AI program's terminal goes to that program's service, as when you run it yourself.

What leaves your computer: nothing by itself.

Turn it off: Features → Terminals → Turn off (the terminals end).

The Terminals tab says it's off, or a terminal won't typeFeatures → Terminals → Turn on. On Linux, a terminal from another user account is refused on purpose.
Kurooto Terminal opens separate windows instead of tabskitty (Linux, macOS) or Windows Terminal isn't there: kurooto term --install, or Windows Terminal from the Microsoft Store. kurooto term --print says what it found.
"kitty didn't start"kitty needs a graphics driver with OpenGL 3.3 (some virtual machines lack it): set terminal.kitty off. kitty's own message is in terminal/kitty.log in the state folder.
Kurooto Terminal's tabs or colours look old in Windows TerminalClose every Windows Terminal window and open it again.

What the board shows (?)

Press ? anywhere on the board (not while typing) for a card that explains it: the beating core is Kurooto itself (faster when busier); each cell is a feature that's on, breathing faster when busy and flashing when something happens; a dot along a nerve is something arriving (into the core) or Kurooto acting (out to a cell); a ripple is news for no one feature; a grey dashed cell isn't running. In NOTES each dot is a note (bigger with more links), lines are links and colours are neighbourhoods (one per course or subject folder). It also says what Study mode is. ? again or Esc closes it.

Processor numbers (0.5.21). Every processor figure on the board is a share of the whole machine, over a steady window: the CPU total, each process in Processes, "kurooto" in the SYSTEMS header (Kurooto and every program it started: the AI reading your books, the call server, your terminals) and each feature cell's. 100% would be every core busy, so they add up and never pass 100%.
