The dashboard

The dashboard is a page on your own computer at http://127.0.0.1:8770. It shows what Kurooto is doing, lets you turn features on and off, change settings, pair a phone, and do everyday things (reminders, deadlines, the alarm, your journal) without Telegram. kurooto open opens it in your browser.

This page covers the dashboard, the morning page, the colours, kurooto top (the same board in a terminal) and the two terminal features. For talking to Kurooto in general see talking-to-kurooto.md.

Opening it

  • Go to http://127.0.0.1:8770 in any browser, or run kurooto open (it prints the address too). 127.0.0.1 means "this computer".
  • If another program already uses port 8770, Kurooto takes the next free one (it tries 30 in a row). kurooto paths shows the state folder; web.json there says which port is in use. You can pick the starting port with the web.port setting (default 8770; Settings → Dashboard, then restart Kurooto).
  • The setup link in the top bar goes back to the setup page (/setup), for example to add more AI accounts to About you or to restore a profile. While setup steps you skipped are waiting, it says finish setup (N) and opens the first of them.
  • If Kurooto isn't running, the page can't load. Start it from your app menu (Kurooto) or with kurooto on.

Who can open it. Only this computer: the page listens on 127.0.0.1 only. Every request needs a key that only the page Kurooto serves gets, requests from other websites are refused, and the page loads nothing from the internet. On Linux, where several people can be signed in at once, it also checks that the page and every request come from your own user account, and refuses them when it can't tell.

Two designs: new and classic

DesignLooks
new (default since 0.5.19)set in a sans-serif typeface (Public Sans, which comes with Kurooto: nothing is fetched from the internet), with figures that keep their width as they change; monospace only for the terminals, code and file paths. Rounded panes with their name and figures on a header line, a top bar with the name, a status line, study, panes ▾ and the design button; thin-line graphs and bars in the palette's colours
classicthe look of 0.5.18 and earlier: everything in monospace, thin borders with each pane's name set into its top line, dotted graphs

Both have the same panes, pages, keys, search and data, and both follow the palette (Colours).

Switch: the classic look button in the top bar (in classic: panes ▾ → new look, or the top bar in the roomy layout), or Dashboard → Settings → Look → look.design. It changes at once, without a reload, and every browser gets it (it's a setting). *Undo:* switch back the same way.

The board (dense layout)

Four panes: STATUS down the left, SYSTEMS and NOTES on top, TERMINAL below. A top bar shows Kurooto's name and version, a short status line ("12 features on · 0 AI requests running · cpu 7%"), a needs a look badge when something isn't working, an update badge when a new version is ready, and the setup link (the new design; classic shows it only in the roomy layout and keeps study and panes ▾ in the board's top-right corner).

STATUS: this computer

A monitor like the program btop, the same as kurooto top:

  • Header: the date and time, how long the computer has been up, today's event count, the battery (percent, time left, watts) or "ac", Kurooto's mode ("saving battery" or "paused …"), the update and health badges, and three buttons: pause 1h (or resume while paused), off and setup.
  • CPU: a history graph, every core with its own little graph, speed, load, temperatures and fans (where the computer reports them).
  • MEM: used, cached, available, swap. GPU: use, its memory, temperature, power and a graph (needs NVIDIA's nvidia-smi, or an AMD card on Linux; a sleeping laptop graphics card isn't woken to ask).
  • NET: download above the line, upload below, and totals. DISKS: how full, reads and writes per second.
  • PROCESSES: the busiest programs. Click cpu or mem in the header to sort (this browser remembers it). Kurooto's own processes are marked ✦ (a dot in the new design).
  • STUDY TODAY: minutes per subject, with the Study tracker on.
  • PROJECTS: your git folders with each one's last commit, what isn't pushed yet and what's changed. List the folders in look.projects (default empty: the repositories you gave Research).

pause 1h pauses Kurooto for an hour (Telegram still answers and keeps what you send; see your-computer.md). off asks first, then turns Kurooto off completely: it stops, won't start at sign-in, and your call line stops placing scheduled calls until you open Kurooto from your app menu or run kurooto on. The page then only says that Kurooto is off.

SYSTEMS: the features as living cells

Each feature that's on is a round cell around a beating core labelled with your assistant's name. Pulses run along the lines between the core and the cells as messages, reminders and AI answers come and go. The hours of the day run around the ring.

  • A cell's words sit just outside it: its name and its figures. A feature that runs programs of its own shows their cpu and memory ("running · 2.1% · 140M"); one whose programs aren't running turns grey and dashed ("not running"); one whose programs only run now and then says "idle". Other features say what they did last ("last 4m ago") and when they run next ("next in 2h").
  • Hover over a cell for its card: events in the last 2 minutes, its processes (cpu, memory, threads, uptime), the last event and the next scheduled time. On a very crowded ring a few labels may hide; the card always works.
  • The pane's top line says how many features are on, how many aren't running, Kurooto's own cpu and memory, and whether the call line is linked.

Folded panels and legends (new in 0.5.8). To keep the board calm, the four corner panels, the NOTES legend and the NOTES recent list are folded to their first line, with a count of what's hidden (▸ 4). Point at one and it opens while the pointer is over it. Press L to open all of them (or fold them all again). This browser remembers your choice; another browser starts folded.

NOTES: a map of your notes

Every note in your notes folder is a dot; links between notes are lines. Notes are grouped into coloured neighbourhoods by folder: each sources/<subject>/ folder is one (in the kurooto notes layout, each subject folder with a course.md), each research project (projects/<name>/ or research/<name>/) is one, the other maps and hubs are the hub ("Maps & hubs"), and any other note joins the neighbourhood most of its links go to (or "Other Notes"). Grouping works best with the zettelkasten notes layout (see notes-and-concept-graph.md).

Names on the map (new in 0.5.19). At the usual zoom the map names its regions, the way a map names places: each course by its course page's title ("MTH201 Linear Algebra") and each research project by its index note's title, in the region's colour at the middle of its notes. A note's own name shows when you point at it, find it, light the recent ones (R) or zoom in; zoomed in, course pages, hubs and projects are named first, then notes that link across courses, then the best-linked. A book's index note (<subject>/books/<book>) is drawn small and named last: once the concept graph reads a book, hundreds of notes link to it from their "Seen in" lines, and before 0.5.19 it took every label and the map read as a list of book titles. The region names step back while you point at or search for a note.

The pane's top line counts notes, links and neighbourhoods. The legend (top right) lists the neighbourhoods with their counts; the bottom-left list shows the notes you edited last. Both are folded until you point at them or press L.

Opening notes in your own program: Dashboard → Settings → Notes → notes.open_with, for example kitty nvim {path}, code {path} or kurooto term -e nvim {path}. {path} is the note (one argument even with spaces), {folder} your notes folder; without {path} the note goes at the end. It runs as a program with arguments, never through a shell, and the program must open its own window. If the program isn't there, the default app opens the note. Empty (the default) uses your default app.

TERMINAL: plain English right in the page

The bottom pane's border holds its tabs. With the Terminals feature on, there are real terminals for your AI's own program (agy or Claude Code, one tab for each that's installed), a shell and Kurooto's log. Without it, there's a + terminals tab that explains the feature.

The tab named after your assistant (kurooto) is always there: type to Kurooto as on Telegram. Quick requests ("push the evening check-in by 30 min", "what's due this week?", "no calls till 6") answer at once; anything bigger goes to your AI, and its answer prints here, not on Telegram. Its own words:

↑ / ↓what you typed before

An answer that takes longer than 30 minutes stops being waited for here; see it in the AI jobs page.

The pane's header (classic: its border) also lists the pages (today, activity, jobs, schedule, features, phone, research, you, settings), each with its key letter underlined, and a reminder of the keys.

Study mode

A calm board for studying: only your notes and the things that help you study. Everything else (STATUS, SYSTEMS, the terminal, the panes menu) waits, untouched, and comes back exactly as it was when you leave.

Switch it on or off: study in the top bar (classic design: the board's top-right corner; it reads leave study while it's on), Alt+M, Ctrl+K → study on / study off, or from Telegram or the kurooto tab /study mode on / /study mode off (plain words work too: "study mode", "leave study mode"). The page follows at once wherever you switch it. It stays on across reloads and in every browser (the setting look.study_mode).

What it shows:

  • NOTES, large on the left: the map, with the chosen subject's notes lit and fitted to the pane. The note you pick from the list is centred with its first lines; double-click opens it.
  • STUDY, on the right:
  • Timer: pick a subject, then start. A focus (25 minutes, study.focus_min) is focus time: calls and messages that can wait are held until it ends, and its minutes are logged to that subject in the Study tracker. Then a break (5 minutes, study.break_min); after it the next focus waits for you. pause keeps what's left (the minutes so far are logged), resume goes on, reset ends it. The digits show in the tab's title while it runs. It keeps time with the page closed, and Telegram says when a focus or a break ends.
  • then leave Study mode: tick it and Study mode ends when the focus ends, instead of a break (study.timer_ends_mode).
  • Today: minutes per subject.
  • Due: quizzes and exams (marked *exam*) and tasks in the next two weeks, and what's time to start (Start-by dates).
  • Recall: with Quiz on, up to three cards due for the subject from its exam-prep notes; click one to see it. /quiz <subject> practises them.
  • Notes: the subject's course page, exam prep, doubts and class log, then its lecture and concept notes, newest first (wherever your notes layout keeps them).
SettingDefaultEffect

The timer needs the Study tracker (and Subjects); without it, Study mode still shows your notes and what's due. A focus never shortens a /focus of yours that lasts longer. /study stop (or "done studying") during a focus ends the timer and logs the time. *Undo:* leave Study mode; reset the timer.

Ctrl+K: do anything from anywhere

Ctrl+K (⌘K on a Mac) opens one line at the top of the board, from wherever you are (except inside a terminal, where Ctrl+K belongs to the shell). Press it again, Esc, or click outside to close it.

How it behaves:

  • As you type, a list shows what Enter would do. ↑ / ↓ pick a line, Enter does it, Tab puts the highlighted verb in front of what you typed.
  • A bare word is completed first: re then Enter gives remind , with a hint of what to type. With something after the verb, Enter runs it and the line clears for the next one.
  • What happened shows under the line ("Reminder set for 18:30", or why not). For an ask that goes to your AI, the answer arrives there too, with which AI answered, its effort and how long it took. It waits up to about 20 minutes; after that, look in the AI jobs page.
  • Times for remind: 18:30, 6pm, in 20m, in 2 hours, tomorrow, tomorrow 9am, friday 9:00. Times for due: a weekday with a time (fri 17:00), tomorrow (with or without a time), a date (2026-11-02, or 2026-11-02 17:00) or in 3 days.
  • Every action is exactly the one the Telegram command and the AI's tool do, so they never disagree. A feature that's off says "… is off: turn it on in Features". The alarm needs Phone calls.

Today (Alt+T)

The day, and the everyday things done right there.

  • One box for anything at the top. Plain words go to Kurooto as if typed on Telegram (an AI answer shows which AI gave it and how long it took). j … adds a journal line, . … a line to today's inbox note, ! 18:30 call mum a reminder (! in 20m tea, ! tomorrow 9am gym; the time comes first). Press Enter or ↵.
  • Alarm (needs the Morning alarm): what's set, a box to set one ("7:30", "tomorrow 6:45"), skip tomorrow, turn this one off, and daily alarm… / change daily (asks for a time, or off).
  • Classes: today's classes, "(skipping)" on ones you skip.
  • Due: the next three weeks; done ticks one off (and the - [ ] box in your notes when it came from one), new date asks for a date ("tomorrow 23:59"). An add box takes "essay draft fri 17:00" (when it's due goes last).
  • Reminders: each with +10 min and cancel.
  • Check-ins: today's, with +30 min and skip while they're still to come.
  • Waiting for you: open questions, with a button per answer and answer… for your own words.
  • Journal: today's entries and a box; save (or Ctrl+Enter) adds a "Journal HH:MM" section to today's daily note. It also says how many lines went into the inbox note today.

A part whose feature is off says so with a link to Features. Every button does exactly what the command or the AI's tool does.

Where things go: journal lines go into today's daily note (journal/<date>.md, or dailies/<date>.md in the zettelkasten layout). . lines go into one inbox note per day, inbox/<date>-captured.md, one timed line each. This differs from /note on Telegram, which makes a new note per thought.

Activity, AI jobs, Schedule

  • Activity lists the last 80 events as they happen. Events never hold what you typed, only names, counts and times.
  • AI jobs lists requests with Cancel on running and waiting ones (a running agent program is stopped with what it started). The same as /jobs on Telegram.
  • Schedule lists what's coming with its time.

The Mail digest card links the Confidential mail page (mail summarised on this computer; see google.md). Features from your add-ons are listed separately at the bottom, under "Your add-ons".

Phone (Alt+P)

Needs Phone calls on. It shows your call line's address and whether this computer is connected to it.

  • Pair a phone: a QR code to scan with the phone. The page that opens offers the Android app or Add to Home Screen (iPhone), then pairing. The code works for 15 minutes; the page also shows the code and a link. /phone on Telegram does the same.
  • Ring my phone (test): rings every paired phone (only while connected).
  • Paired phones: name, kind, whether notifications are on, when it was paired, and Remove.
  • ntfy account (optional): paste a free ntfy.sh account's access token so Android rings aren't refused for an internet address you share (it says how many were refused this week); Remove goes back to no token. Your call line is updated with it by itself.

Details are in phone-app.md.

Research (Alt+R)

What you work on, your paper sources (quick picks, arXiv fields, bioRxiv, medRxiv, PubMed, any journal's feed, with keywords and a Preview), the digest's schedule and size, Send a digest now, and the code repositories whose commits go into your project notes. All of it is explained in research.md.

About you (Alt+Y)

First your persona card: what to call you, tone, length and things to avoid, with Save card and a preview of exactly what goes along with every AI request and call. It starts empty; a part that would give Kurooto feelings, a backstory or "I missed you" is left out when you save, and the page says which (teaching-kurooto.md). Then your profile (about-me/profile.md in your notes), in an editor: Save keeps the previous version beside it (profile-<date>.md), Revert goes back to what's saved, and Preview In brief shows exactly the part that goes along with every AI request (the section under ## In brief; a warning shows when there is none). With no profile yet, the page links to the setup page's About you step. See teaching-kurooto.md.

Settings (Alt+S)

Settings are grouped by section. The page shows the core sections (you, notifications, availability, updates, AI, questions, look, power, notes, files, AI limits (quota), health checks, AI jobs, sources for answers, skills in other AI programs (agents), this computer (hardware), the dashboard's port (web), and Starting) and the sections of each feature that's on. A feature's settings appear once you turn it on.

  • Change one: tick a box, pick from a list, or type a value and leave the box (Tab or click elsewhere). It saves at once and says "Saved section.key". Lists and groups (like ["status"] or {"night": "23:15"}) are typed as JSON; if what you typed isn't valid, it says so and saves nothing.
  • Go back to a default: type the default value back (every default is in the settings guide), or delete that line from settings.json.
  • Starting has its own choice (when you log in, or on Linux when the computer starts) and says what it did.
  • Settings not on the page (the features that are on, which the Features page changes, and Telegram's pairing: telegram.chat_id, telegram.allowed_users, telegram.api_base) are in settings.json in Kurooto's settings folder (kurooto paths → config), like every other setting. It holds only what you changed; a dotted name like brain.main is {"brain": {"main": "…"}}. Restart Kurooto after editing the file by hand (kurooto stop, then kurooto start).
  • Secrets (keys, tokens) are never on this page or in settings.json.

Search, hiding features, bigger panes

Search

Features and Settings each have a search box at the top. Press / or Ctrl+F while the page is open (Ctrl+F a second time is the browser's own find). It filters as you type, ignoring case; every word has to be found somewhere.

  • Features are found by name, description, privacy line and their settings. A card found only by a setting says which ("its settings: deadlines.ping_h").
  • Settings are found by section, name, help text and current value.
  • The words found are marked, with a count ("3 of 41 features"), or "nothing matches".
  • Esc empties the box.

Hiding features

A feature that's off has hide on its card: it leaves the Features page and nothing else changes (Telegram, the setup page and everything else still know it; it stays off). A feature that's on always shows. Show hidden (N) at the bottom lists hidden ones with Unhide, and a search still finds them, marked *hidden*. Turning a hidden feature on from a search unhides it.

Setting: look.hidden_features (default []). *Undo:* Unhide, or empty the list.

Bigger panes and the panes menu (dense layout)

  • Fill the board with one pane: click its name (⤢) or double-click its border. Esc or its name again brings the others back.
  • panes ▾, in the top bar (classic: the board's top-right corner), hides STATUS, SYSTEMS, NOTES or TERMINAL (one always stays) and the others take the room. It also lists the pages with their keys, and show them all.
  • What's hidden is the setting look.panes (e.g. ["status"]), so every browser shows the same. *Undo:* panes → show them all, or empty look.panes.
  • Only what's on screen is drawn, so a hidden pane costs nothing.

Links to other sites

Links to other sites (Google sign-in, a token page, Cloudflare, AI Studio, the guides) open in this computer's default browser, even when the dashboard is in another browser's app window. Kurooto's own pages stay where they are, and Ctrl-, Shift-, Alt- or ⌘-click still opens a link in the browser you're in. If the default browser can't be reached, the link opens here. On Linux and macOS a $BROWSER you've set is used.

Setting: look.links_in: default (the default browser) or here (the browser showing the page).

The morning page

http://127.0.0.1:8770/morning shows today's plan from your daily note ("## Plan for today"), with your classes (skipped ones marked), calendar, what's due soon, Time to start (from Start-by dates), check-ins, open questions, the next alarm and today's study hours. Open today's note opens the note itself. It is read-only and refreshes every 5 minutes.

The Morning alarm opens it in your browser when it rings, if this computer is awake (alarm.open_page, default on). Without a plan yet it says so: with Chat on and the alarm set, one is drafted 20 minutes before the alarm.

Colours

Kurooto's pages (setup, dashboard, morning page, and the terminal colours) come in three palettes:

Where system looks, best first: on Linux, Material You colours made from your wallpaper by matugen, then pywal, then KDE's accent colour, GNOME's (47 or later) and your GTK theme's; on Windows the accent colour and app mode (Settings → Personalization → Colors); on macOS the accent colour and appearance. When nothing is found, the pages look like kuro until something is. Dashboard → Settings → Look says what was found.

Your own colours file: look.theme_file = the full path of a kitty theme (lines like background #1a1110) or a matugen or pywal colors.json. It's used instead of the desktop's colours and switches the palette to system.

Text always stays readable: a colour that would be hard to read on its background is made lighter or darker until it passes the WCAG AA contrast level (4.5:1). The dashboard is always dark; the setup and morning pages follow light or dark mode. Your call line's pages on the phone stay green.

kurooto top: the board in a terminal

kurooto top shows the same board in any terminal (Windows 10 and later, macOS, Linux), laid out like btop: this computer (processor history and every core, speed, load, temperatures and fans, memory and swap, the graphics card, network down and up graphs, disks, the battery, the busiest processes) beside Kurooto (running or not, the battery mode, the call line and its heartbeat, Kurooto's own process and the features' programs, today, what's due, AI requests, what's next, study today, projects, recent events), with lights for Kurooto, Telegram and the call line, today's event count and a trace that beats with each event.

KeyDoes
q (or Esc, Ctrl+C)quit
rrefresh now
csort processes by cpu
msort processes by memory
  • kurooto top --once prints one screen and exits (for scripts, or a terminal that can't redraw).
  • Its colours are your palette, in true colour where the terminal has it. NO_COLOR=1 kurooto top turns colours off.
  • What a computer can't tell is left out: most Windows and Mac computers give no temperatures or fans; live graphics-card figures need nvidia-smi (or an AMD card on Linux).
  • It only reads, so it's safe to run while Kurooto is busy.

kurooto logs shows the end of Kurooto's log (-n 200 for more lines); kurooto logs -f keeps following it (Ctrl+C stops).

Terminals (in the dashboard)

What it does. Real terminals inside the dashboard: your AI's own program (agy or Claude Code, started in your notes folder as you'd run it yourself; one tab for each that's installed, the one you use first), your usual shell (PowerShell on Windows), kurooto ask (plain English, like Telegram) and Kurooto's log.

Turn it on: Dashboard → Features → Terminals → Turn on. Only turn this on if you're the only person who uses this computer: anyone who can open your dashboard can type into these terminals.

Using them. In the dense layout they're tabs in the TERMINAL pane's border; in roomy, a Terminals tab. Alt+1, Alt+2 … switch between them. Each starts the first time you open it. They keep running when you close the page and show where they were when you open it again. One that ended starts again when you press a key in it, or with restart (ends the program in that terminal and starts it again).

Limits. On Linux a terminal request from another user account is refused on purpose, and if the computer can't tell who is connecting (no /proc/net/tcp), terminals refuse everyone. What you type into your AI program's terminal goes to that program's service, as when you run it yourself.

What leaves your computer: nothing by itself.

Turn it off: Features → Terminals → Turn off (the terminals end).

Kurooto Terminal (a window of its own)

What it does. Kurooto in a real terminal window of its own, laid out like the dashboard's terminals: a tab each for your AI's own program (agy or Claude Code, started in your notes folder; whichever is installed if your AI is an API key), your shell, Kurooto chat (kurooto ask, with a kurooto ❯ prompt; type plain English as on Telegram) and Kurooto's log, with kurooto top as a column on the left of the first tab. Everything is in Kurooto's colours.

Turn it on: Dashboard → Features → Kurooto Terminal. It then appears in your app menu (Start menu, Launchpad, your Linux launcher) as Kurooto Terminal. If there's no terminal program at all, Set up gets one.

Open it:

kurooto term --installgets kitty and fish into Kurooto's own folder

Per system:

What it uses
Linux, macOSkitty: yours if you have it, otherwise kurooto term offers once to get the official kitty into Kurooto's folder (about 45 MB, no admin, checksum checked). Tabs switch with Alt+1 to Alt+9 on Linux, Cmd+1 to Cmd+9 on a Mac. Your own kitty.conf is read first and never changed. Without kitty, each tab opens in a window of your usual terminal.
WindowsWindows Terminal (built into Windows 11; free in the Microsoft Store for Windows 10). Turning the feature on adds Kurooto's profiles and colour scheme (no admin); chat, top, logs and your AI appear in Windows Terminal's new-tab menu. Without Windows Terminal, each tab opens in a console window.
The shellfish if you have it; otherwise kurooto term offers once to get the official fish into Kurooto's folder (Linux 3 MB; Mac the 17 MB fish app, not installed system-wide). If you say no, your own shell (PowerShell on Windows).

What the board shows (?)

Press ? anywhere on the board (not while typing) for a card that explains it: the beating core is Kurooto itself (faster when busier); each cell is a feature that's on, breathing faster when busy and flashing when something happens; a dot along a nerve is something arriving (into the core) or Kurooto acting (out to a cell); a ripple is news for no one feature; a grey dashed cell isn't running. In NOTES each dot is a note (bigger with more links), lines are links and colours are neighbourhoods (one per course or subject folder). It also says what Study mode is. ? again or Esc closes it.
