Settings guide

Every setting Kurooto has, one entry each: what it does, what it can be set to and its default, when you'd change it and what changes when you do, what it costs or sends outward, and what it depends on. Grouped by feature; the contents below lists the groups.

Finding one. On the dashboard, Settings (Alt+S) has a search box that also looks through these entries: type what you want ("night", "calls", "battery") rather than a setting's name. Each setting there shows its one-line tip under its name; click the tip for the whole entry. In the phone app, long-press a setting for its entry. On this page, your browser's find (Ctrl+F) finds a name like brain.main.

Changing one. On the dashboard's Settings page (a feature's settings appear once it's on), or in settings.json in Kurooto's config folder (kurooto paths says where), which holds only what you changed: a dotted name such as brain.main is the key main inside brain, {"brain": {"main": "agy"}}, and deleting a line goes back to the default. Restart Kurooto after editing the file by hand (kurooto stop, then kurooto start). A few settings are set by Kurooto itself (pairing, the call line's address); their entries say so. Secrets (keys, tokens) are never settings: they're in the system keyring.

Times and schedules. A time is 24-hour HH:MM. A schedule is daily 07:30 (several times: daily 07:00,13:00), weekly sun 19:00, hourly, or every 15m / every 3h.

This page is written by tools/gen_settings_guide.py from Kurooto's code and the guide's source (tools/settings_guide/), so every setting is here; the table lists them all on one page.

You and how it talks to you

Who you are to Kurooto (your name, time zone, notes folder) and how its AI words things for you. The persona card's four lines go with every AI request and every call, so the wording is the same on Telegram, in the app and on the phone. They set wording only: a line that would give Kurooto feelings, a backstory or a relationship is dropped, and you're told why. More in Teaching Kurooto about you.

For every setting in this group, unless its entry says otherwise:

  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing by itself. The persona card's lines travel with each request to the AI you chose, like the rest of the request.
  • Depends on: Nothing: these are core settings, always there.

you.name

Your name, as Kurooto uses it in messages and calls.

  • Values: Any text. Default: empty.
  • When to change it: Set it at setup or when it gets your name wrong. Messages, the morning page and calls use it from the next one; empty, Kurooto just doesn't use a name.

you.timezone

Your time zone, for every time Kurooto keeps; empty follows this computer's clock.

Reminders, check-ins, the alarm, night hours and every schedule are worked out in this zone.

  • Values: An IANA zone name such as Asia/Kolkata or Europe/Berlin, or empty. Default: empty.
  • When to change it: When this computer's clock is set to another zone than the one you live in (a server, a travel laptop). The schedule reads it when Kurooto starts, so restart it after a change; times already set keep their wall-clock time in the new zone.

you.workspace

Your notes folder: where Kurooto reads and writes notes, journal and course files.

Everything Kurooto writes (journal, course notes, book notes, its own blocks) goes inside it. Obsidian works on it as it is.

  • Values: A full folder path, or empty for Documents/Kurooto. Default: empty.
  • When to change it: Set it at setup to the folder you already keep notes in. Changing it later points Kurooto at the new folder from its next start; nothing is moved, so move your notes yourself first.
  • Depends on: Read when Kurooto starts: restart after changing it. Notes sync, the notes brain and the sorter all work in this folder.

you.assistant_name

The name your assistant goes by in messages, calls and the dashboard.

  • Values: Any short name. Default: Kurooto.
  • When to change it: When you'd rather call it something else. Your Telegram bot takes the new name at once and says so; messages, calls and the AI use it from the next one.

you.language

Which language answers come in: the one you wrote in, or always one you name.

  • Values: follow (the language of your message), or a language's name such as English or Hindi. Default: follow.
  • When to change it: When you write in a mix and want answers always in one language. Only the wording of AI answers changes; Kurooto's own fixed messages stay in English.

persona.call_me

What the AI calls you: a nickname, your first name, or nothing.

The dashboard's About you page and /persona change it too.

  • Values: Any text; empty uses your name, or nothing. Default: empty.
  • When to change it: When the AI's way of addressing you feels off. The next answer and the next call use it.

persona.tone

The tone you want answers in, in your own words.

  • Values: A short phrase, e.g. "plain and direct, no pep talk". Empty: the AI's usual tone. Default: empty.
  • When to change it: When answers feel too chirpy, too formal or too long-winded. It shapes wording from the next request; facts and actions don't change.

persona.length

How long answers should be, in your own words.

  • Values: A short phrase, e.g. "short unless I ask for detail". Empty: by the length of your message (see persona.register). Default: empty.
  • When to change it: When answers keep coming out longer or shorter than you like. What you write here wins over the length-matching of persona.register.

persona.avoid

Things answers should never have, such as emojis or motivational lines.

  • Values: A short list in your own words, e.g. "emojis, exclamation marks". Default: empty.
  • When to change it: When something in the AI's style keeps annoying you. The next answer and call leave it out.

persona.register

A short message gets a short answer, a voice note a spoken-style one.

By the message's length only: nothing changes with the time of day or partway through a conversation.

  • Values: On or off. Default: on.
  • When to change it: Turn it off if you want the same style of answer whatever you send. A length on your persona card wins over it either way.

persona.boundaries

Health, relationship and money decisions stay yours: Kurooto names who to ask instead.

Every AI request and call is told so, and a request that asks Kurooto to make such a decision ("should I stop my meds") is answered at once, without the AI, with a short line naming a person to ask. It's a list of rules, not a judgement: anything else goes on as usual.

  • Values: On or off. Default: on.
  • When to change it: Leave it on. Turn it off only if the rule keeps catching ordinary questions; then such requests go to the AI like any other.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing: rules on this computer. Off, those requests go to your AI like any other.

Your AI

Which AI answers your requests, what stands in when it can't, how long it may work, how the free limits are kept for what you're waiting on, and the small offline helper. Everything an AI does goes through the AI you chose here; a request's words go to that AI's service, and nowhere else. More in AI brains.

For every setting in this group, unless its entry says otherwise:

  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Requests go to the AI you chose (agy and Claude Code through their own programs; a Gemini key to Google; Ollama stays on this computer). Changing these settings decides which of them is asked, not how much is sent.
  • Depends on: Nothing: core settings. The dashboard's AI step on the setup page and kurooto ai change the main ones with their checks.

brain.main

The AI that answers your requests and does the bigger tasks.

  • Values: agy, claude, gemini (a key), ollama (on this computer), custom, or an extra Claude account claude:<name>. Empty until setup picks one. Default: empty.
  • When to change it: When you switch AI programs or subscriptions. The next request goes to the new one; requests already running finish where they are. The setup page's AI step does the same with a test first, which is the safer way.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Your requests' words, the files you attach and the notes a request needs go to this AI's service (nothing for ollama). Its free limit or subscription is what's used.
  • Depends on: The program or key for that AI (setup installs and signs in). brain.fallback covers it when it can't answer.

brain.fallback

Which AI takes over when your main one can't answer, in order.

  • Values: One AI's id, a list in order such as ["agy", "gemini"], or empty for the automatic order: at a limit, the other agent program, then a Gemini key. Default: empty.
  • When to change it: When you have a second AI you'd rather use as the stand-in, or none you want used. Empty is right for most people.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: A request that falls back sends the same words to the stand-in's service.
  • Depends on: brain.fallback_on decides what counts as "can't answer".

brain.fallback_on

What makes a request move on to the stand-in AI: a limit, a sign-in problem or a timeout.

  • Values: A list of rate_limited, auth, timeout; [] never falls back. Default: ["rate_limited"].
  • When to change it: Add timeout if your main AI is often slow, auth if its sign-in often lapses. Empty it if you'd rather wait than have another AI answer.
  • Depends on: brain.fallback.

brain.classify

Who decides how hard a request is (and so how much effort it gets).

  • Values: rules (no AI), main (asks your AI first), or ollama (a model on this computer). Default: rules.
  • When to change it: rules is quick and free. Pick ollama if you have a local model and the rules misjudge your requests; main costs one small extra request each time.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: main sends each request's text to your AI once more, to rate it; rules and ollama stay here.

brain.timeout_min.low

Minutes a quick request may run before it's stopped.

  • Values: A whole number. Default: 10.
  • When to change it: Raise it if quick requests get cut off on a slow connection; lower it to give up sooner on stuck ones.

brain.timeout_min.medium

Minutes a normal request may run before it's stopped.

  • Values: A whole number. Default: 15.
  • When to change it: Raise it if ordinary requests (reading a few notes, a short write-up) get cut off.

brain.timeout_min.high

Minutes a hard request may run before it's stopped.

  • Values: A whole number. Default: 30.
  • When to change it: Raise it for long jobs (a whole chapter, a big project change) that get cut off; a stopped request says so and can be asked again.

brain.models

Which model each AI uses at each effort level, where you want other than its preset.

  • Values: A JSON object per AI, e.g. {"custom": {"low": "...", "medium": "...", "high": "..."}} or {"claude": {"high": "opus"}}. Default: none.
  • When to change it: When you want a cheaper or stronger model at one level. Settings doesn't show it: the AI step on the setup page and kurooto ai set it, with a test.

brain.base_urls

Another address for an AI's service, in place of its preset.

  • Values: A JSON object, e.g. {"custom": "http://localhost:8000/v1"}. Default: none.
  • When to change it: Only for custom (an OpenAI-style server of your own) or a proxy. Set through the setup page's AI step, which tests it.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Requests go to the address you give, so make sure it's one you trust.

brain.claude_accounts

Your extra Claude accounts, each its own AI you can pick (claude:<name>).

  • Values: Set by Kurooto when you add an account: {"work": {"name": "Work"}}. Default: none.
  • When to change it: Add one on the setup page (AI → Claude Code → Add another Claude account) or with kurooto ai add-claude NAME; rename or remove it in Settings → Claude accounts. Don't edit it by hand.

brain.effort_map

Effort changes Dreaming learned for your requests, e.g. low ones run at medium.

  • Values: A JSON object from one effort to another: {"low": "medium"}. Default: none.
  • When to change it: Set by Dreaming's nightly replay, one change at a time with Undo. Empty it to go back to the efforts as rated.
  • Depends on: Dreaming with dreaming.tune_effort on.

brain.fallback_first

Effort levels at which your first stand-in AI is tried before the main one (learned by Dreaming).

  • Values: A list of low, medium, high. Default: none.
  • When to change it: Set by Dreaming when its replay shows the stand-in does better at a level. Empty it to always start with the main AI.
  • Depends on: Dreaming with dreaming.tune_fallbacks on, and a brain.fallback.

brain.agy.io_mode

How Kurooto talks to the agy program: through a pipe, or a pseudo-terminal.

  • Values: One of: pipe; pty (a pseudo-terminal, where pipes give no output). Default: pipe.
  • When to change it: Only if agy runs but Kurooto gets no output from it (some versions buffer when piped): try pty.
  • Depends on: agy as your main or fallback AI.

quota.on

Keep part of each AI's free limit for what you're waiting on, so background work never uses it all.

Background work (digests, nightly reading) waits once a day's count reaches quota.background_pct of a known limit. Your own requests never wait.

  • Values: On or off. Default: on.
  • When to change it: Turn it off on a paid plan with room to spare: background work then never waits. Counting and the usage line stay.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing: counts and times only, in quota.json on this computer.

quota.background_pct

The share of an AI's daily limit background work may use before it waits for the reset.

  • Values: A percentage, 0 to 100. Default: 70.
  • When to change it: Lower it if you run out before evening; raise it if background work waits while you hardly use the AI yourself.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing: counts and times only, in quota.json on this computer.
  • Depends on: quota.on.

quota.limits

Your own daily limits per AI, which win over the ones Kurooto learned.

  • Values: A JSON object: {"agy": 900, "gemini:gemini-flash-latest": 250}. Default: none.
  • When to change it: When you know your plan's real limit and the learned one is wrong. Kurooto then keeps its reserve against your number.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing: counts and times only, in quota.json on this computer.
  • Depends on: quota.on.

quota.soft

Whether a limit Kurooto only measured makes background work wait, or is just shown.

  • Values: learned (a limit learned from its own count is a measure only), all (Google's numbers too), off (every known limit keeps its reserve, as before 0.7.5). Default: learned.
  • When to change it: Pick off if background work keeps using up your day before you need the AI. A "limit reached" answer always pauses that AI until its reset, whatever this says.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing: counts and times only, in quota.json on this computer.
  • Depends on: quota.on.

quota.local

What a model on this computer takes over to spare the free limit.

  • Values: quick (only the "how hard is this" question), background (queued background work too, instead of waiting), off. Default: quick.
  • When to change it: background keeps digests coming on a tight limit, written by a smaller model (weaker). off if you have no local model.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing leaves: the local model runs here; it uses this computer's memory and graphics card while it works.
  • Depends on: Local AI models on, with a model installed.

jobs.receipt

One quiet line under each answer: which AI, effort, time, limit used and what it did.

  • Values: On or off. Default: on.
  • When to change it: Turn it off if you don't care which AI answered. Nothing else changes; the AI jobs page keeps the same record.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing.

jobs.plan_line

Before a long answer, one short line saying what it's about to do and roughly how long.

Only when your last requests of the same kind took more than about 20 seconds. Never on a call.

  • Values: On or off. Default: on.
  • When to change it: Turn it off if you'd rather get just the answer.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing: worked out from what's known before the request runs.

jobs.stuck_min

Minutes a request may sit with no AI at work and no program running before the watchdog stops it.

  • Values: A whole number. Default: 10.
  • When to change it: Raise it if long-thinking requests are stopped while they're still fine; lower it to free a stuck queue sooner. A stopped request says so.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing.

agents.export

Which other AI programs get your skills as slash commands, so /journal works there too.

  • Values: A list of agy, agents (Copilot CLI and others that read ~/.agents) and claude (Claude Code); empty: none. Default: none.
  • When to change it: Add a program you use directly. Its skills folder gets a copy of each skill (marked as Kurooto's; yours are never touched), kept up to date every hour. Emptying it removes every copy Kurooto made.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing leaves: files written into those programs' own skills folders on this computer.
  • Depends on: Those programs installed. kurooto export-skills on|off changes it too.

agents.prefix

A prefix put before every skill copied into your other AI programs, e.g. k- for /k-journal.

  • Values: Short text, or empty. Default: empty.
  • When to change it: When a copied skill's name clashes with one of the program's own commands. The copies are renamed within the hour.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing.
  • Depends on: agents.export.

agents.scripts

Scripts of your own the AI programs may run during a request (full paths).

  • Values: A list of full paths. Default: none.
  • When to change it: When you want a request to be able to use a tool you wrote (say, make-figures). Everything else stays refused; add only scripts you trust to run unattended.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: The scripts run as you on this computer when the AI calls them; what they do is up to them.
  • Depends on: agy or Claude Code as the AI.

local_helper.on

A small model on this computer (about 1 GB) for quick decisions and as a stand-in while the internet is down.

It rates how hard a request is, picks between close readings of a short message and reads a short yes/no. Never answers your requests while you're online.

  • Values: On or off. Default: off.
  • When to change it: Turn it on (or /helper on) on a computer without a graphics card when the free limit runs short or the internet often drops. It only stays on if it passes a speed test here.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: One download from Ollama's registry (about 1 GB of disk). Nothing else leaves; it uses some memory while it runs.
  • Depends on: Ollama (installed for you).

local_helper.model

Which Ollama model the offline helper uses; empty picks the standard small one.

  • Values: An Ollama model name, or empty. Default: empty.
  • When to change it: Only if you want a different small model; it must still pass the speed test.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Downloads that model from Ollama's registry.
  • Depends on: local_helper.on.

local_helper.timeout_s

Seconds a quick decision waits for the helper before the rules decide instead.

  • Values: A whole number. Default: 4.
  • When to change it: Raise it on a slow computer if the helper keeps timing out; the rules are always the fallback.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing.
  • Depends on: local_helper.on.

local_helper.max_s

How fast the helper must answer in its speed test (seconds), or it stays off.

  • Values: A number. Default: 1.5.
  • When to change it: Raise it to let a slower computer use the helper anyway; decisions will feel slower.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing.
  • Depends on: local_helper.on.

local_helper.offline

With the internet down: quick things answered here, AI requests wait in the queue until it's back.

You're told once that a request waits; when the connection is back, one line says what's running. Nothing is dropped silently.

  • Values: On or off. Default: on.
  • When to change it: Turn it off to have requests fail at once while offline instead of waiting.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing: nothing extra is pinged to find out the connection is down.

local_helper.hold_h

Hours a request waits for the internet before it gives up and says so.

  • Values: A whole number. Default: 24.
  • When to change it: Lower it if old requests coming back much later would be useless; raise it for long trips offline.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing.
  • Depends on: local_helper.offline.

local_helper.offer

Offer the offline helper once if your free limit keeps running short or the internet often drops.

  • Values: On or off. Default: on.
  • When to change it: Turn it off if you never want to be asked. /helper on works either way.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing: one message with Install / Not now, two weeks after setup, and only when it fits.

Messages and where they reach you

Where Kurooto's messages go (Telegram, the phone app, this computer's notifications), how many come by themselves in a day (Calm), the offline and back-online messages, and questions waiting for your answer. Answers to what you asked are never held or counted; the alarm, approvals, your own reminders and deadlines under 12 hours never wait. More in Messages Kurooto sends by itself.

For every setting in this group, unless its entry says otherwise:

  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Messages go out through Telegram's servers to your chat, or sealed through your call line to the phone app; desktop notifications stay on this computer. Calm's records (state/calm.db, 45 days) stay here.
  • Depends on: Nothing: core settings, always there.

notify.calm.on

Calm: each message Kurooto sends by itself says why, has Why? and Less of this, and there's a daily limit.

  • Values: On or off. Default: on.
  • When to change it: Turn it off to have everything come as it happens, with no buttons and nothing recorded, exactly as before Calm existed.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Nothing leaves. On, what was sent and why is recorded on this computer for 45 days.

notify.calm.messages

How many messages a day may come by themselves; the rest wait for the evening bundle.

  • Values: A whole number. Default: 6.
  • When to change it: Raise it if useful things keep waiting until evening; lower it for a quieter day. The first time a day the limit holds one, a line says so with Send them as they come today / Raise to 12 / Fine.
  • Depends on: notify.calm.on.

notify.calm.calls

How many calls a day may come by themselves; one past it waits in the evening bundle as a call link.

  • Values: A whole number; 0: none by themselves. Default: 1.
  • When to change it: Raise it if you like being called for reminders and check-ins; set 0 to be called only when you asked. The alarm and calls you asked for never count.
  • Depends on: notify.calm.on, Phone calls.

notify.calm.evening_at

When the evening bundle comes with everything that waited today.

  • Values: A time of day, 24-hour HH:MM. Default: 20:00.
  • When to change it: Move it to when you usually look at your phone in the evening. After this time, messages come as they are.
  • Depends on: notify.calm.on.

notify.calm.evening

Kinds of message that always wait for the evening bundle.

  • Values: A list of message kinds, e.g. ["research", "weekly"]; filled by Less of this → Another time. Default: none.
  • When to change it: Usually set by tapping Less of this → Another time. Remove a kind to get it as it happens again.
  • Depends on: notify.calm.on.

notify.calm.fewer

Kinds of message limited to a number a day; more wait for the evening.

  • Values: A JSON object, kind → number: {"quiz": 1}; filled by Less of this → Fewer. Default: none.
  • When to change it: Usually set by tapping Less of this → Fewer. Raise or remove a kind's number to get more of it.
  • Depends on: notify.calm.on.

notify.calm.habituate

After this many of a kind go unanswered in a row, they wait for the evening until you answer one.

  • Values: A JSON object, kind → number; set by Dreaming, with Undo. Default: none.
  • When to change it: Dreaming fills it in from what you leave unanswered. Remove a kind to have it always come as it happens.
  • Depends on: notify.calm.on; Dreaming tunes it.

notify.calm.windows

Hours a kind of message may come in; outside them it waits for the evening bundle.

  • Values: A JSON object, kind → "10:00-18:00"; set by Dreaming, with Undo. Default: none.
  • When to change it: Dreaming sets it from when you actually answer; edit or remove a kind to change its hours.
  • Depends on: notify.calm.on; Dreaming tunes it.

notify.calm.stopped

Kinds of message you stopped with Less of this → Stop.

  • Values: A list of kinds. Default: none.
  • When to change it: Remove a kind to start getting it again (or use Undo on the message that stopped it).
  • Depends on: notify.calm.on. Used only for kinds whose feature has no switch of its own.

notify.calm.lighter_after

After an exam or a heavy day, the next day's limit is halved.

  • Values: On or off. Default: on.
  • When to change it: Turn it off if you'd rather have the same flow every day.
  • Depends on: notify.calm.on.

notify.calm.monthly

Once a month, it names a kind of question you hardly ever answer and offers to turn it off.

  • Values: On or off. Default: on.
  • When to change it: Turn it off if you don't want the monthly suggestion. Nothing is turned off without your tap either way.
  • Depends on: notify.calm.on.

notify.presence.on

Tell you when Kurooto goes offline, and again when it's back with what it caught up on.

A clean stop says so before it exits; coming back says how long it was away and what it caught up on. Never a call; at night it waits for the morning.

  • Values: On or off. Default: on.
  • When to change it: Turn it off if you don't care when the computer sleeps or restarts.

notify.presence.after_min

How long Kurooto must be away before going offline or coming back is worth a message.

  • Values: Minutes, at least 1. Default: 10.
  • When to change it: Raise it if short sleeps and network blips cause messages you don't need.
  • Depends on: notify.presence.on.

notify.presence.channels

Where the offline and back-online messages go.

  • Values: A list of telegram and app (the phone app). Default: ["telegram"].
  • When to change it: Add app if you mostly use the phone app; remove telegram to keep them off your chat.
  • Depends on: notify.presence.on; a paired phone for app.

notify.presence.line

While this computer can't speak (asleep, crashed, offline), your call line tells you so.

  • Values: On or off. Default: on.
  • When to change it: Turn it off if you don't want messages from the call line while the computer is away.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Your call line (on your own Cloudflare account) watches the computer's link and sends the message itself.
  • Depends on: notify.presence.on and a call line (Phone calls).

notify.presence.line_telegram

Whether your call line may keep your bot's token, so it can tell you on Telegram while the computer is away.

  • Values: none (asked once), on, or off (off deletes the token there at the next sync, within 5 minutes). Default: none.

face.greet.gap_min

The fewest minutes between two greetings.

  • Values: A whole number. Default: 60.
  • When to change it: Raise it if the greetings feel too frequent.

face.greet.per_day

A daily cap on greetings.

  • Values: A whole number. Default: 3.
  • When to change it: Lower it to one for just a morning greeting.

face.greet.say

What the greeting tells you: what's next today, what's waiting, deadlines in 24 h, what was held while you were away.

  • Values: A list, typed as JSON. Default: ["next", "waiting", "deadline"].
  • When to change it: Trim it to what you want to hear first when you sit down.

face.greet.talk

After the greeting, listen for a reply without its name being said.

  • Values: On or off. Default: on.
  • When to change it: Turn it off if you'd rather start every talk by saying its name.
  • Depends on: Voice listening.

face.greet.listen_s

How long that conversation stays open with nothing said, in seconds.

  • Values: A whole number. Default: 12.
  • When to change it: Shorten it if it keeps listening longer than you'd like.

face.greet.with_others

When someone else is in view too: skip the greeting, say just "Welcome back.", or the full greeting.

  • Values: skip, short, full. Default: skip.
  • When to change it: Leave it on skip so your day isn't read out in front of others.

face.track.on

While the camera is open, follow every face in view (where it is and how much it moves) for the being's eye.

Each face gets a number that stays with it from frame to frame, its place, its size, whether it's you and how much it moves (small nods and turns). The being's eye looks at one person, then another every few seconds, sooner when someone new appears or moves more. Nobody else is identified, nothing is kept, and it never opens the camera by itself.

  • Values: On or off. Default: on.
  • When to change it: Turn it off if you'd rather the eye only finds you, or to save the processor time.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: With a dashboard in sight, roughly a tenth of one processor core while the camera is open.
  • Depends on: Face presence watching; a dashboard page in sight, or an add-on that follows faces.

face.track.fps

How many times a second it looks for faces while following them (1 to 15).

Only finding faces runs this often; recognising you stays at face.watch.fps. On a slow computer it lowers itself when a look takes too long.

  • Values: A whole number. Default: 10.
  • When to change it: Lower it to save processor time; raise it for a livelier eye.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: Each look a second costs about 1% of one processor core.
  • Depends on: face.track.on.

face.addons.watch

Let an add-on see who's in view: each face's place, size and numbers, in memory only, never a picture.

An add-on gets every face in each frame Face presence already looks at: where it is, how big, which way it's turned, whether it's you, someone else or unsure, and its numbers (kept by the add-on only if it says so). With no add-on asking, nothing is worked out for it.

  • Values: On or off. Default: on.
  • When to change it: Turn it off to keep add-ons from seeing faces at all.
  • Depends on: An add-on that uses it; Face presence watching (every pause still applies).

face.addons.hold

Let an add-on keep the camera looking while you type (never past a pause, the night, a call or a switch).

Normally the camera closes while you use the keyboard and mouse. An add-on may ask it to keep looking, for at most 10 minutes at a time unless it asks again; the Face card and /face then say which add-on and why. Every pause reason still comes first. While it looks, other programs (a video call) can't use the camera on Linux.

  • Values: On or off. Default: on.
  • When to change it: Turn it off if the camera's light on while you type bothers you, or you want the camera free for calls.
  • Costs and what leaves this computer: The camera's light on, and a little processor time, while an add-on holds it.
  • Depends on: An add-on that uses it; Face presence watching.

face.learn.on

Slowly refine your face template from times it was very sure it was you (never unsure ones), with Undo.

  • Values: On or off. Default: off.
  • When to change it: Turn it on if your look changes (glasses, a beard, the light at your desk) and you'd rather it keeps up.

face.learn.every_h

At most one new sample this often, in hours.

  • Values: A whole number. Default: 6.
  • When to change it: Raise it to make the template change more slowly.

face.learn.margin

How far above your threshold a frame's score must be before it may refine your template.

  • Values: A number. Default: 0.08.
  • When to change it: Raise it to learn only from the surest frames.

face.learn.max

At most this many learned samples (the oldest goes first); the enrolled ones are never replaced.

  • Values: A whole number. Default: 30.
  • When to change it: Lower it to keep the template closer to your enrolment.

face.recognise.frames

Recognised in this many of the last 4 frames means you're here.

  • Values: A whole number. Default: 3.
  • When to change it: Raise it if it thinks you've arrived when you only passed by.

face.recognise.min_face_px

Faces smaller than this many pixels are ignored: too far away to judge.

  • Values: A whole number. Default: 60.
  • When to change it: Lower it if you sit far from the camera.

face.recognise.strictness

How rarely a stranger may pass: loose (1 in 100 frames), normal (1 in 1,000) or strict (1 in 10,000).

  • Values: loose, normal, strict. Default: normal.
  • When to change it: Strict if others often sit at your desk; loose if it fails to recognise you in poor light.

face.recognise.threshold

0 uses the calibrated threshold; a score from 0 to 1 overrides it (never below 0.363).

  • Values: A number. Default: 0.0.
  • When to change it: Leave it at 0 unless the live test on the Face page shows it gets it wrong.

face.watch.fps

How many pictures a second it checks while waiting for you (0.2 to 2).

  • Values: A number. Default: 1.0.
  • When to change it: Lower it to save processor time; raise it if it's slow to notice you.

face.watch.fps_battery

Frames a second while the computer is on battery.

  • Values: A number. Default: 0.33.
  • When to change it: Lower it (or to 0) to save battery.

face.watch.idle_s

Seconds with no keyboard or mouse before the camera looks; while you type it doesn't need to.

  • Values: A whole number. Default: 60.
  • When to change it: Raise it to keep the camera off more of the time.

face.watch.night

Also look during the night hours (Availability's night); off pauses it at night.

  • Values: On or off. Default: off.
  • When to change it: Turn it on if you work at your desk at night and want the greeting then too.

face.watch.when_locked

Also look while the screen is locked; off pauses it while locked.

  • Values: On or off. Default: off.
  • When to change it: Turn it on if you want it to notice you arriving at a locked computer.
