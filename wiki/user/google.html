Google: Classroom, Gmail, Calendar and Drive

Kurooto can read your Google Classroom, sort your Gmail into a digest, write email drafts, read your Google calendars, keep a calendar of planned time blocks, and put big files in your Google Drive. All of it uses your own Google sign-in through a free Google Cloud project that only you own. Setting that up takes 10 to 15 minutes, once. Each Google feature is off until you set it up, and each asks Google for only the permission it needs.

If all you want is your timetable or calendar, you may not need any of this: the Calendar feature reads a calendar's private iCal link with no Google project at all.

Why your own Google project

Most apps sign you in to Google through the app maker's own registration, so the maker's servers hold your Google access. Kurooto doesn't do that. You make your own registration (an "OAuth client", see the glossary) in a free Google Cloud project, and Kurooto on your computer signs in with it. Google sees your own project asking for your own data, and you say yes or no. Nobody else's server sits in between, and it costs nothing (no billing account is needed).

The price is a one-time setup and a warning screen from Google ("Google hasn't verified this app") every time you sign in. That warning is about apps strangers publish; this one is yours.

Setting up your own sign-in, step by step

You do steps 1 to 6 in a browser, on any computer, with any Google account. Step 7 (signing in) has to happen on the computer Kurooto runs on, with the account whose Classroom, mail or calendar you want. A personal Google account is easiest; school accounts sometimes block apps like this (see When something goes wrong).

Google renames these screens now and then. If a button has a slightly different name, pick the nearest match: the order stays the same. The Google card in Kurooto (Dashboard → Features → Google, or the setup wizard) has the same steps, links to each page, and copy buttons for the values you need to paste.

1. Create the project. Open console.cloud.google.com/projectcreate.

  1. Project name: Kurooto (any name works; only you see it). Leave Organization or Location as it is.
  2. Press Create and wait a few seconds.
  3. Check that the project picker at the top of the page shows the new project. Every step below happens inside it.

2. Turn on the APIs your features use. An API is a switch that lets your project talk to one Google product. Open the link for each feature you want, check the project name at the top, and press Enable. You can come back for more later.

3. Branding: what Google's sign-in screen says about your app. Open Google Auth Platform → Branding. The first time it says "Google Auth platform not configured yet": press Get started. A short form asks four things:

  1. App Information: App name Kurooto; User support email: your own address. Next.
  2. Audience: External (anyone with a Google account may sign in, which here means you). Next.
  3. Contact Information: your own email address. Next.
  4. Finish: tick "I agree to the Google API Services: User Data Policy", Continue, then Create.

Why Kurooto's public pages: Google asks every app that's "in production" for a home page and a privacy policy on an authorized domain. They describe what Kurooto does with your data. Nothing is sent to that site.

4. Audience: publish the app. Open Google Auth Platform → Audience. Publishing status says Testing: press Publish app, then Confirm. It becomes In production.

Why this matters: in Testing, Google ends every sign-in after 7 days (Kurooto would ask you to sign in again every week), and only accounts listed under Test users can sign in at all. In production your app stays unverified, which is fine for personal use: up to 100 people can ever sign in to an unverified app, and each sees the warning first. You don't need to submit it for verification; ignore that if Google suggests it. If you made the project with a school or work account and will only ever sign in with that organisation's accounts, Internal works too (no Publish step, no warning), if Google offers it.

5. Data access: nothing to do. The Data access page lists the permissions ("scopes") an app declares. Leave it empty: Kurooto asks for each permission when you set a feature up. If you add scopes here, Google may suggest verification; ignore it.

6. Clients: make your desktop client. Open Google Auth Platform → Clients.

  1. Create client.
  2. Application type: Desktop app. Name: Kurooto.
  3. Create. A window shows the client ID and secret: press Download JSON right away. Google shows the secret only this once. (Closed it? Open the client, press Add secret, and download again.)
  4. In Kurooto: Dashboard → Features → Google → paste the file's contents into the box (or press the file button and choose the file) → Save. The setup wizard's Google card has the same box.

Keep this file to yourself, like a password. On its own it can't read your data (that also takes your sign-in), but it lets an app present itself as yours. Kurooto keeps it in your computer's keychain. Kurooto refuses a file for a "Web application" client (it must be a Desktop app) and a file without a client ID and secret.

7. Sign in. After Save, Kurooto opens Google's sign-in in your browser.

  1. Pick the account whose Classroom, mail or calendar you want.
  2. Google says "Google hasn't verified this app". That's your own project, made minutes ago. Press Advanced, then Go to Kurooto (unsafe). "Unsafe" only means Google hasn't reviewed it.
  3. Google lists what Kurooto asks for. Leave every box ticked and press Continue.
  4. The tab says "Signed in with Google as you@example.com (main)". Close it; the Google card in Kurooto turns on.

Adding more Google features later. Each one needs one more permission. Turn on its API (step 2), press Save on its card in Dashboard → Features, and sign in again (step 7). Google lists the new permission and shows the warning screen again. When you're back, the feature turns on by itself. If you untick its box on Google's screen, that feature stays off and everything else keeps working. Google may call a permission "restricted" or "sensitive": that's Google's label for mail and Drive access, and it's fine for your own client.

Google (Classroom deadlines and posts, Gmail drafts)

What it does. The base Google feature, which every other Google feature builds on.

  • Classroom deadlines: every 3 hours it reads your active Classroom courses. Each assignment with a due date (from two days ago onward) goes onto your deadline list as "Course: assignment", and is marked done there when you hand it in on Classroom (turned in or returned).
  • Classroom announcements: each new announcement comes to Telegram as "New in Linear Algebra: ..." (the first 400 characters) with an Open button to the post. The very first check only learns what's already there, so you don't get a flood of old posts.
  • Posts for missed classes: each course's posts of the last 30 days (when, title, link) are kept on this computer, so the catch-up for a class you missed lists what was posted that day. Classroom → notes adds materials to them.
  • Gmail drafts (off by default): /draft writes an email draft into your Gmail. Kurooto never sends it; you open Gmail, check it and send it yourself.

Turn it on. Do the step-by-step setup (Classroom API on), then Dashboard → Features → Google → paste the client file → Save → sign in. For drafts, also turn on the Gmail API, set google.gmail_drafts to true (Dashboard → Settings → google), and sign in again (Save on the card).

From Telegram.

  • /classroom: check Classroom now (deadlines and new posts).
  • /draft someone@example.com ask for an extension on problem set 3: the AI writes the email (needs Chat on) and saves it as a draft. You get "Draft saved in Gmail: ... Open Gmail to check it and send it yourself." (also when Kurooto restarted while the AI was writing it).
  • /draft someone@example.com | Extension request | Dear ...: subject and text written by you, saved as it is. No AI.
  • /draft only exists while google.gmail_drafts is on.
  • /sync all (or "sync everything") also checks Classroom, then Classroom → notes and the mail digest.

Limits and edge cases.

  • Assignments without a due date, and ones due more than two days ago, aren't added.
  • A topic on your not-interested list (/ignore) is left off the deadline list like any other deadline.
  • "Less of this" on an announcement offers Fewer (at most one announcement a day; the rest wait for the evening bundle) or Stop. See messages-it-sends.md.
  • If the sign-in expires or you remove Kurooto's access at Google, you get an urgent message: "Google sign-in expired ... the Google features are paused". Sign in again from Dashboard → Features → Google. (A short-lived access key that Google refuses is simply renewed; only Google saying the sign-in itself is gone pauses anything.)

What leaves your computer. Nothing new: Kurooto reads from Google with your own sign-in. Drafts are written into your own Gmail. The sign-in tokens stay on your computer: in its keychain, or where there's none (or Kurooto starts at boot), in a file only your user can read.

Turn it off. Dashboard → Features → Google → Turn off. Features that build on it (reply drafts, digest by email) must be off first.

Several Google accounts

What it does. Lets each Google feature use the account you choose: say the mail digest reads your school account while Drive upload uses your personal one. The first account you sign in with is main; every feature uses main unless you say otherwise. Each account signs in on its own (same Google Cloud project) and is asked only for what the features using it need.

On the dashboard. Each Google feature's card has a Which Google account box. It shows the account in use with its address. Leave it empty to keep it; type a new name (a short word like school) to add that account: Google's page opens with its account chooser, so pick the right one. Google Calendar can read several at once: main, school (press Save once for each account). Email reply drafts have no box: they always use the mail digest's account.

From Telegram.

  • /google: your accounts, each with its address and what uses it.
  • /google use mail school: move a feature to another account. The feature names you can use: classroom, drafts, mail, email (digest by email), calendar, plan (or blocks), drive, classroom_notes. A new name adds an account. If that account hasn't given its OK yet, the feature pauses until you press Save on its card and sign in; the reply says so.
  • /google forget school: forget that account's sign-in on this computer. Its features go back to main. (main can't be forgotten this way.)

Limits and edge cases.

  • An account name is one short word: letters, digits and dashes, up to 24 characters.
  • Only Google Calendar takes several accounts; every other feature uses one.
  • Signing in to the same Google account under two names gets "That's the same Google account as your main one": the browser picked the account you were already signed in with. Save again and choose the other account.
  • If one account's sign-in expires, only the features using it pause, and the message names them.

Turn it off / undo. /google forget <name>. To take the access away at Google too, open myaccount.google.com/connections while signed in to that account.

Classroom → notes

What it does. Three times a day (07:00, 13:00, 19:00) Kurooto reads what's new in your active Classroom courses: materials, coursework, announcements, and the Drive files your teachers attach or link. The AI then files each course into your notes, in the course's folder (it uses the matching subject from Subjects when there is one, else a folder named after the course):

  • a note per lecture or problem set (e.g. linear-algebra/2026-10-20-eigenvalues.md): what it covers, the teacher's post, the files, and links to your related notes;
  • the course page linear-algebra/course.md: lecture notes in order, coursework under Tasks, exams and announcements;
  • an always-current linear-algebra/exam-prep.md with Next assessment, Covered so far, Must know (what Quiz asks you), Typical questions and Gaps. Kurooto rewrites only the part between its two markers; anything you write above or below them stays.

Dates in posts (a midterm, a moved quiz) go into Deadlines, on that course; assignment due dates already arrive through the Google feature. It also keeps your notes' home page linked to your courses, and a class you missed gets that day's posts in its catch-up.

Turn it on. Needs the Google feature. Turn on the Drive API (and the Classroom API) in your project, then Dashboard → Features → Classroom → notes → Save → sign in again.

From Telegram.

  • /classnotes: check now and hand anything new to the AI ("Found 4 new item(s) in 2 course(s); the AI is filing them..."). /sync all does it too.
  • /classnotes courses: your courses and the notes folder each goes to (or "skipped").
  • /classnotes skip chess: leave out courses with "chess" in their name. /classnotes unskip chess undoes it.

Each check ends in one message, once the AI has filed everything it found, whichever courses it came from: "Classroom: filed 3 new items from Linear Algebra into linear-algebra/:" and the items by title (the first eight, then "and N more"). A batch the AI couldn't file is tried again with the next check and is in that check's message.

Into the concept graph. With the Concept graph on, each filed file with real text (slides, handouts, PDFs, solutions: 1,500 characters or more, not a one-line announcement) is then read into it like a book: new concept notes, links with reasons, and "Seen in" lines pointing at the lecture note the file was filed into. Each file is read once (the same file filed again isn't read twice; an edited one is). Files filed before 0.5.19: /graph backfill classroom. To leave class files out, take classroom out of integrate.sources.

Limits and edge cases.

  • Posts are handed to the AI eight at a time. If the AI fails, the batch waits and is tried again at the next check; after three failures you get one message ("the AI couldn't file the new posts ... I'll keep trying").
  • Posts about a topic on your not-interested list are only counted, not filed.
  • It's heavy work: on a laptop on battery (with the default battery saver) it waits for the charger. Past 70% of a free AI limit it waits for the reset. See your-computer.md and ai-brains.md.
  • If the Drive or Classroom permission is missing, you get "Classroom → notes is paused: needs Google's OK ..." at most once a day until you Save on its card and sign in.

What leaves your computer. Post text and the text of attached files go to the AI you picked, to write the notes. Nothing is changed in Classroom or Drive.

Turn it off. Dashboard → Features → Classroom → notes → Turn off. The notes stay.

Mail digest

What it does. Twice a day (08:00 and 18:00) your new Gmail comes to you as one short message (about 15 lines; a busy check comes in parts, 20 mails each), grouped by what it asks of you: Needs you (requests, forms, replies due), Dates (exams, quizzes, deadlines, events, marked "added" when they went into Deadlines), Courses (other news per course) and Worth knowing (people, opportunities, office notices, five lines at most); newsletters are only counted. A copy goes in your notes (mail/2026-10-20-0800.md, linked from that day's journal). Mail about exams becomes your schedule at once: every exam of your subjects in it, or in a schedule attached as a PDF, is a deadline with its time and room, and the room, syllabus and what to bring go under ## Exams in the course note. Course news goes onto the course page. Gmail is only read: nothing is ever sent, deleted, archived or marked read.

You choose what it may read. Everything else is only counted, by sender, subject and labels, on your computer; its text never goes to the AI. Mail is read when it matches, in this order:

OrderRuleResult
1a topic on your not-interested listonly counted
2on your never-read list (/mail never)only counted
3marked confidential in its subject or a labelonly counted
4a sender, domain or label you chose (/mail read)read
5matches your Gmail search (/mail query)read (personal mail among it is only counted)
6Promotions or Socialonly counted
7from your own organisation's domain (school or work account)read (personal mail among it is only counted)
8anything elseonly counted

Then, for mail that's read, its whole text (footers and quoted replies too) is checked for confidentiality markings on your computer; a marked mail is dropped there and only counted. Chats, sent mail, drafts, spam and trash are never looked at.

A personal Gmail account reads nothing until you choose something; the scheduled digest tells you how, at most once a day: "Mail digest: 12 new message(s), none from senders or labels you chose, so I only counted them...".

From Telegram.

  • /mail: check now ("Reading 5 new message(s) (12 only counted); the digest comes in a minute.").
  • /mail read @school.example (a domain), /mail read someone@example.com (a sender), /mail read label:Courses (a Gmail label). /mail unread ... takes one off.
  • /mail never @office.example keeps a sender, domain or label out altogether, even if chosen above. /mail unnever ... takes it off.
  • /mail query -category:promotions -category:social: read all mail matching a Gmail search (any Gmail search words work: label:Courses OR from:school.example, -category:forums). /mail query shows it; /mail query off clears it.
  • /mail rules: what it reads, the never-read list, and whether confidential mail is skipped.
  • /mail confidential: dates found in confidential mail summarised on this computer (local mode only; below), titles and dates only, each with Add to Deadlines and Not needed.
  • /sync all also runs the digest.

Confidential mail is never read by the AI. School and work mail may fall under your institution's email policy. If a mail's subject, its text (all of it) or a Gmail label's name says "confidential", "do not forward", "restricted", "private and confidential", "privileged and confidential", "proprietary and confidential", or carries a standard footer ("intended solely for the addressee", "if you are not the intended recipient", "this e-mail and any attachments are confidential / may be privileged"), it's only counted, even from a sender or label you chose, and never gets an AI reply draft. Many institutional mails carry such a footer, so expect more of your mail to be only counted.

Optional: confidential mail summarised on this computer. Set mail_digest.confidential to local and turn on Local AI models. A model on this computer (Ollama) then reads marked mail instead of leaving it counted; the AI you picked still never sees it. The summary is kept only in Kurooto's data folder (mail/confidential/<date>.md, readable only by your user account): not in your notes, not on Telegram (which only says "Confidential (2): summarised on this computer; open the dashboard to read it"), not in Profile sync, and not in Backups unless you turn on backups.confidential_mail. Read it on the dashboard: Features → Mail digest → Confidential mail, which also lists the dates it found with Add to Deadlines / Not needed. Nothing is added to Deadlines until you tap. Limits: up to 15 such mails a check, three per request to the local model, each cut to its first 3,500 characters. Without a ready local model (off, not set up, or resting on battery) marked mail is only counted and you're told once.

Limits and edge cases.

  • "Less of this" on a digest offers Fewer (once a day, at the first time) or Stop (turns the feature off).
  • Kurooto keeps the sender, subject and a short excerpt of digested mail for 30 days, so a reply draft can answer it.

What leaves your computer. The text of the mail you chose (and, with a query, the mail it matches) goes to the AI you picked. For everything else, only counts, on your computer.

Turn it off / undo. Dashboard → Features → Mail digest → Turn off. /mail query off goes back to only your /mail read rules and your own domain.

Email in

What it does. Forward a notice, a receipt or a schedule to Kurooto, and it's filed in your notes with a short summary, and its dates come back with an Add button each. Your call line lives on workers.dev, which can't receive mail (Cloudflare only routes mail for a domain of your own), so the way in is your own Gmail, with the Mail digest's read-only access:

  • forward it to your own address with +kurooto before the @ (example+kurooto@gmail.com for example@gmail.com; Gmail delivers it to you as usual; on a school account, you+kurooto@school.example), or
  • put a Gmail label on it (/mail in label Kurooto), by hand or with a Gmail filter.

Every ten minutes (mail_digest.mail_in_every) Kurooto looks for such mail. Each one goes to the AI you picked, which writes the note in your notes' inbox (inbox/2026-10-23-hostel-fee.md: a summary, a link to open it in Gmail, then the mail's text) and lists its dates. You get "Email in: Hostel fee, filed in inbox/...", with the dates, if any, each with Add (a deadline or exam goes into Deadlines, an event becomes a reminder), Add all and None of these. Nothing goes into Deadlines until you tap. Each mail goes in once.

Turn it on. Mail digest must be set up (it's the same Gmail access). Then /mail in on. It's off by default.

Who may put mail in. Anyone who learns your +kurooto address could mail it, so mail to it goes in only when it's from your own address or one you listed (/mail in from); anything else is never even opened. A label is yours to put on, so labelled mail goes in from any sender. Your never-read list (/mail never) and the confidentiality check still hold: such mail is only counted, never read. The AI reads the mail as information, never as instructions, with no tools: it can only write the note's summary and list dates. mail_digest.mail_in_anyone lets mail to +kurooto in from anyone (not recommended).

With your own domain. If your domain's mail is on Cloudflare, Cloudflare Email Routing (free) can deliver kurooto@your-domain.example to example+kurooto@gmail.com: add the Gmail address as a destination, then a rule for the address. Mail sent there then arrives "from" whoever sent it, so add those senders with /mail in from, or have Gmail label it (a filter on to:kurooto@your-domain.example, label Kurooto). A Cloudflare Email Worker that hands mail straight to your call line isn't built yet.

Settings (Dashboard → Settings → mail_digest): mail_in (off), mail_in_label (empty), mail_in_senders ([]), mail_in_anyone (false), mail_in_every (every 10m).

What leaves your computer. The text of the mail sent in goes to the AI you picked. The note stays in your notes.

Email reply drafts

What it does. When the mail digest finds mail that needs your reply (a question or request to you), the AI writes a short reply in your voice into your Gmail Drafts and sends it to you as an approval with three buttons:

ButtonWhat it does
Sendsends that draft from your Gmail
Editasks "What should change?"; reply with it ("say I can do 10am") and the AI rewrites the draft, then asks again. Answer "nothing" to keep it as it is

Nothing is sent unless you tap Send. Unanswered, the draft stays in your Gmail Drafts; the question expires after 3 days. You can answer on Telegram, in the phone app or on a call.

Turn it on. Needs Google and the Mail digest. Dashboard → Features → Email reply drafts → Save → sign in (Google asks to "manage drafts and send email"). It always uses the mail digest's account.

Limits and edge cases.

  • Replies go only to the sender of a mail in your digest (or its reply-to address). Never to no-reply addresses, never to mail marked confidential (even in local mode), never to mail on your never-read list.
  • One open draft per mail.
  • If you sent or deleted the draft in Gmail yourself, tapping a button says it isn't there any more.
  • If Gmail doesn't take the tap (a hiccup, an expired sign-in), the question is asked again.

What leaves your computer. The mail being answered goes to the AI (it was already in the digest); the draft is written into your Gmail; a reply leaves only when you tap Send.

Turn it off. Dashboard → Features → Email reply drafts → Turn off. Drafts already in Gmail stay there.

Mail digest by email

What it does. Each mail digest also comes to your own inbox, sent from your Gmail to yourself, with the subject "Mail digest, Tue 20 Oct 18:00". With several accounts it can send from another account than the one the digest reads (a personal account while the digest reads a school one). It always goes to the sending account's own address; there's no way to give it another recipient.

Turn it on. Needs Google and the Mail digest. Dashboard → Features → Mail digest by email → Save → sign in (Google asks to "send email on your behalf" and to see your address).

Limits and edge cases.

  • A digest that mentions confidential material anywhere (beyond its own "Confidential: 2" count) isn't emailed: you get it on Telegram only, and a note saying so.
  • If Google didn't tell Kurooto your address, or sending fails, you're told (at most every 6 hours for the same reason).

What leaves your computer. The digest's text, from your Gmail to your own inbox.

Turn it off. Dashboard → Features → Mail digest by email → Turn off.

Agent plan calendar

What it does. Kurooto makes a calendar of its own, Agent plan, in your Google Calendar, for the time blocks it plans with you: the morning plan, or "block 2 to 4 for the problem set" on a call. Each block has a 5-minute reminder. It writes only to that calendar; Google's permission covers only calendars Kurooto makes, so it can't see or change your others.

Turn it on. Needs Google and the Calendar API. Dashboard → Features → Agent plan calendar → Save → sign in.

From Telegram.

  • /blocks: today's blocks.
  • /blocks add 14:00-15:30 problem set 4 (also 2pm-3:30pm, 14:00 to 15:30): add one.
  • /blocks clear: remove today's blocks (only Kurooto's own).
  • /blocks remove bayesian, /blocks remove after 22:00, /blocks remove all: take some blocks out (by title or subject, from a time on, or all of tonight's); /blocks undo puts them back.
  • /blocks shift [19:00], /blocks shift by 2 hours: reschedule the day (below); /blocks undo puts it back.

Each block knows what it's for. A block is tied to the deadline, quiz or exam its title names (from Deadlines) and to that one's due date when it was planned. A block for something done, dropped or already over (revision for a quiz that was this morning) isn't planned.

Reschedule the day. "Reschedule my day", "I just woke up, reschedule my day", "reschedule my day from 19:00", "push my day by 2 hours", /blocks shift [19:00], /blocks shift by 2 hours, or on a call: today's blocks you haven't done move to start from now (or the time you give), each as long as it was, with the breaks between them kept (up to 45 minutes). Starting over takes the blocks you slept through too, and puts what's due soonest first (blocks tied to nothing keep their places); an order you give on a call ("homework first, then quiz prep") comes before that. Pushing by some time moves only the ones not over yet, in their order. Blocks for something done, over, or moved past "due soon" (deadlines.plan_h, 3 days) since they were planned are dropped and named ("Dropped, nothing left to do for them: ..."). A block planned twice keeps its first; one that would end after 03:00 is left out and named, and so is what's due within a day with no block yet. Tonight's blocks past midnight count too. A block that would overlap a class you go to, a commitment or a timed event in your calendar goes after it (0.9.0). The new plan also goes in today's daily note. /blocks undo puts them back.

Planned time is busy. Start-by dates and the revision plan count your blocks as busy (0.9.0), so they don't plan other work into time you planned. With Google out of reach they plan as if there were no blocks.

A deadline that moves (0.9.0). When a deadline moves (you, a call, or its source, such as a Classroom post with a new due date), its blocks still ahead get "(moved to Fri 13 Nov)" in their titles and one message asks "remove its 2 plan blocks from your Agent plan calendar?" with Remove and Keep. Remove takes them out (/blocks undo puts them back); Keep puts their titles back. Nothing is removed without your tap.

A focus when a block starts (agent_plan.start_focus, on, 0.9.0). With the Study tracker on, when a block starts and you're at your desk (Face presence or the computer in use), one message: "Your block Topology report starts now. Start a 25-minute focus for it?" with Start 25 min. Away from your desk: nothing, and nothing is noted.

Time for what you said you'd do (agent_plan.thread_slot, off, 0.9.1). Something you said you'd do by today ("I'll send the draft by 6", an open thread with a due time) gets a 30-minute block in your free time before it's due: never over your blocks, classes, commitments or calendar events, at most three a day, tried once a day each. One line says it, with Remove.

Calls wait for your blocks (agent_plan.holds_calls, off, 0.9.0). Turned on, a call Kurooto places by itself (a check-in, a nudge) during a block waits until it ends. Calls you asked for and alarms still ring.

On calls. Agree to a time on a call ("let's do the problem set from 2 to 4") and the call puts the block in. A block that would overlap one already planned goes in the first free time after it, and the call says the time it got; when there's none before 03:00 it isn't planned and the call says so. "Remove those blocks", "clear everything after 10" takes blocks out (with Undo under the message it sends, or "put them back"). Today's blocks are in the call's brief.

Settings that matter (Dashboard → Settings → agent_plan):

agent_plan.remind_min5Google's pop-up reminder before each block; 0 for none
agent_plan.nameAgent planthe calendar's name
agent_plan.start_focusona block starting with you at the desk offers a Study mode focus (0.9.0)
agent_plan.thread_slotoffa half-hour block for an open thread due today, in free time (0.9.1)

Limits and edge cases. A block must end after it starts, be at most 12 hours long, and fall between now and a month ahead. The same block twice is refused ("Already planned"); so is one for a quiz or exam that's over, or a deadline that's done. Hide or delete the calendar in Google Calendar any time; Kurooto makes a new one the next time it plans something. Moving the feature to another account (/google use plan personal) makes a new Agent plan calendar there. To see the blocks in Kurooto's own Calendar too, add the Agent plan calendar's private iCal link to Calendar.

What leaves your computer. Each block's title and time, into your own Google Calendar.

Turn it off. Dashboard → Features → Agent plan calendar → Turn off. The calendar stays in Google Calendar until you delete it there.

Google Calendar

What it does. Lets the Calendar feature read your Google calendars through Google's own API instead of private iCal links: for school or work accounts that don't offer a "secret address in iCal format", and for reading two accounts at once. Everything Calendar does works the same: /today, /cal, no calls during events, #focus and #nocall, event reminders, class calendars, private events as "Busy (private)". It reads two weeks ahead and is read-only.

Turn it on. Needs Google and the Calendar API. Dashboard → Features → Google Calendar → Which Google account (main, school, or main, school) → Save → sign in (once per account). When Google says yes, Calendar turns on too.

What it reads. Your own calendars and ones you subscribed to (a class calendar, holidays). Left out: calendars other people share with you (a classmate's or a teacher's own calendar: their data), hidden calendars, and Kurooto's own Agent plan calendar (/blocks shows that).

Settings that matter (Dashboard → Settings → google_calendar):

SettingDefaultEffect
google_calendar.skip[]calendars to leave out, by any part of their name, any case: ["Birthdays"]

Limits and edge cases. A calendar you also added to Calendar as an iCal link comes twice: leave it out here with google_calendar.skip.

What leaves your computer. Nothing; Kurooto only reads from Google.

Turn it off. Dashboard → Features → Google Calendar → Turn off. Calendar keeps its iCal links, if you have any.

Drive upload

What it does. Puts files you send yourself into a folder of Kurooto's own in your Google Drive, Kurooto, and sends you the link with an Open in Drive button. This is how files bigger than Telegram allows reach you.

Turn it on. Needs Google and the Drive API. Dashboard → Features → Drive upload → Save → sign in.

How files get there.

  • kurooto send lecture.mp4 --to drive from a terminal: uploaded and kept.
  • Ask your AI to send you a file "to Drive", on Telegram or on a call: uploaded and kept.
  • Anything over Telegram's 50 MB (a file Kurooto or the AI sends you, a big study pack, several files sent at once that together pass the limit) goes there by itself, with drive_upload.big_files on. These are temporary: the message says "It's deleted from your Drive in 7 days". A daily clean-up at 04:10 deletes them when their days are up, each with the Google account that uploaded it (switching Drive upload to another account doesn't strand them).
  • Big files go up in 8 MB pieces in the background: you first get "Uploading lecture.mp4 (212.4 MB) to your Google Drive; the link comes when it's done", then the link.

Settings that matter (Dashboard → Settings → drive_upload):

SettingDefaultEffect
drive_upload.folderKurootothe folder's name in your Drive
drive_upload.big_filestruefiles over 50 MB come as a Drive link instead of not at all
drive_upload.temp_days7days a too-big-for-Telegram upload is kept before it's deleted; 0 keeps them

Limits and edge cases.

  • Without Drive upload, a file over 50 MB isn't sent: "Telegram takes files up to 50 MB. Turn on Drive upload..."
  • To keep a temporary upload for good, move it out of the Kurooto folder in Drive before its days are up: the clean-up checks the folder first and leaves a moved file alone (and forgets it). drive_upload.temp_days 0 keeps every upload from the start.
  • Google's permission covers only files Kurooto makes, so it can't see the rest of your Drive. Nothing is shared: the link opens only for you (and whoever you share it with yourself, in Drive).
  • If the upload fails (sign-in expired, no space in Drive), you get "Couldn't upload ... to Drive" with the reason.

What leaves your computer. The files you send, to your own Drive.

Turn it off. Dashboard → Features → Drive upload → Turn off. Files stay in Drive until you delete them.

Taking access away

  • One feature: turn it off in Dashboard → Features. Its permission stays granted at Google until you withdraw it.
  • One account on this computer: /google forget <name> (not main).
  • At Google: myaccount.google.com/connections → your project (Kurooto) → Delete all connections. Kurooto then asks you to sign in again.
  • Everything, when leaving: kurooto uninstall --revoke revokes every Google account's sign-in for you (see updates-and-backups.md).
  • Delete the client: Clients page → tick it → Delete (restorable for 30 days).
  • Delete the project: console.cloud.google.com/iam-admin/settings → Shut down → type the project ID. Google deletes it after 30 days.
  • Google deletes a client that hasn't been used for six months (it emails you a month before). If that happens, make a new one (step 6) and paste it in.
  • The Agent plan calendar and the Kurooto folder in Drive stay in your Google account until you delete them.
