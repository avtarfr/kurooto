Voice (modules/voice/, 0.8.8)

Kurooto speaks on the computer it runs on (Kokoro-82M by default, Piper as the lighter choice) and, with voice.listen.on, listens to the room all day. Every decision about what it hears is made on the computer; only turns the gate calls *addressed* reach the AI you picked, through the portal. Off by default (Module off until enabled), with listening a second switch. User page: ../user/voice.md.

The flow

Mic (sounddevice | pw-record | parec), 16 kHz mono, 512-sample frames
  -> Listener (thread "voice-listen"): levels -> voice_level; held while Speaker.holding() or the portal is busy
       wake word (only with your own openWakeWord .onnx, 1280-sample steps) -> woke_at
       Silero VAD per frame: start >= 0.5, end after 700 ms < 0.35, max 15 s; 320 ms pre-roll (a deque in memory)
  -> Voice._heard_audio: queue (max 3, oldest dropped)
  -> worker thread "voice-hear": Voice.hear(Heard(speech, wake, seconds), pcm)
       blocked()? drop.  Whisper.text(pcm) (CPU, int8; the assistant's name as initial_prompt and hotwords) -> del pcm
       Gate.decide(heard, portal.context())
       not addressed: heard.text = "" (dropped), portal.take() closes an open portal
       addressed: NameMatcher.uncall() (the call isn't sent); portal.take(heard, decision, text) -> ask_brain(text) -> Speaker.say(reply)

Voice.background() (the module's long-running coroutine, app.start_background) runs every second: works out blocked(), starts or stops the listener, closes the portal when blocked, and calls Portal.tick(). The listener and the worker are daemon threads; the coroutine never blocks the loop. heavy = True: in saver mode on battery power.may_run keeps it from starting, and blocked() says so.

blocked() reasons, in order: listening is off, muted (state/voice.json), Kurooto is paused, on battery (saver mode), you're on a call (web/tools_api.py:live_call, unless voice.listen.calls), night hours (core/availability.py:Availability.night, only with voice.listen.night off; it's on by default), not set up yet.

The gate

gate.py's docstring is the spec; in short, first match wins:

  1. no speech (VAD) -> noise, 1.0
  2. your own wake word model fired (voice.listen.wake_word, an .onnx path; empty by default) -> addressed, 1.0
  3. empty, or a transcriber's invention from silence (MADE_UP) while the portal is closed -> noise, 0.9
  4. its name as a call, the default wake (first, after an optional "hey/ok/hi", or last; one to three words joined; you.assistant_name and "Kurooto", through names.py:NameMatcher) -> addressed, 0.95
  5. the portal is awaiting (its name or the wake word alone opened it) -> addressed, 0.9
  6. voices >= 2 -> chatter, 0.8 (the listener doesn't count voices yet: Heard.voices stays None)
  7. portal open, within close_s, and a question (?, QUESTION) or request (REQUEST) -> addressed, 0.85
  8. within after_speak_s of speaking, a question or request; REPLY words (yes/no/sure ...) only when what it said ended in "?" -> addressed, 0.8
  9. the local model (Voice.judge: providers.local() only when its base_url host is loopback, else the offline helper's _generate; None otherwise), with PROMPT and context lines (the open conversation, its last reply, how much unaddressed talk in the last minute, a mid-sentence mention of its name). parse() takes the first {...} JSON, else a single class word at 0.5, else chatter at 0. Addressed below voice.listen.threshold -> chatter. No model, no answer, or model_judge off -> chatter, 0.0.

The prompt (gate.py:PROMPT):

You decide whether speech heard near a computer is spoken TO its voice assistant, called {name}.
It was written out by a speech recogniser, so words may be wrong.

Heard: "{text}"
{context}
Answer with JSON only, like {"class": "chatter", "confidence": 0.8}. The classes:
- "noise": not real speech meant for anyone (a TV, music, a cough, words with no sense).
- "chatter": people talking to each other, on the phone, or to themselves; {name} mentioned in passing.
- "addressed": a request, a question or an answer meant for {name}.
When unsure, answer "chatter" with a low confidence.

Its name (names.py)

The default wake is the name heard by faster-whisper; openWakeWord's ready-made models (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) are never fetched. Whisper gets the name as initial_prompt ("Kurooto.") and hotwords (dropped on a TypeError from a faster-whisper before 1.0.2). NameMatcher(names, variants):

  • spellings: each name joined without spaces, MISHEARD[name] for the default "kurooto" (kuroto, kurouto, kuruto, curoto, kurato, "kuro to", "coo roto", kirito, "guru to", kuroo, kuro ...) and voice.listen.name_variants; one to three adjacent words are joined before comparing, so spacing doesn't matter.
  • near misses, against the names themselves only (a mishearing as the target would let "kurt" match "kuroto"): 4+ letters, at least 2 vowel groups and within 1 of the name's, the same skeleton() (consonants with c/k/q/g, t/d, r/l, b/p, v/f, s/z merged; vowels, h, w, y dropped; doubles once), and Levenshtein distance() <= 2.
  • called(): at the start (after one of CALLS) or the end; anywhere() for the model's context line; uncall() removes the call before the turn is sent.

tests/unit/test_voice.py lists mishearings that must match and ordinary words that must not (cool, auto, curry, ghetto, kurt, router, kurta, karate, curator, Kyoto, Toronto, kettle ...). "cool auto" was dropped: it matched an ordinary phrase.

The portal

Portal.take() with a non-addressed decision records the time (never the words) for the "talk nearby" count and closes an open portal at once (voice_state closed, why not addressed). An addressed one opens it (Voice._portal_changed logs voice_portal {open, why}), publishes voice_text {who: "you"}, thinking, calls send (Voice.ask_brain), publishes voice_text {who: "kurooto"}, speaking, speaks, then addressed (waiting). The name or wake word alone (uncall() leaves nothing) says "Yes?" (locally) and sets awaiting; nothing is sent. tick() closes after close_s of nothing addressed (why silence). close() is also called on mute, block and stop().

ask_brain() builds Incoming(text, via="voice", voice=True) and calls app.handle(): commands and instant requests answer through respond; anything for the AI becomes a Chat job. modules/chat.py makes via == "voice" jobs quiet (no Telegram copy) but keeps the conversation history; ask_brain waits for the job (jobs.watchers sets an event, plus polling), and records the turn with Chat.record_turn (quiet jobs skip Jobs._deliver, which normally does it). persona.register_line(voice=True) asks for an answer worded to be spoken.

voice.speak.what = "all": the same watcher speaks every non-quiet, non-background answer in lane "you".

The shape: samples[i] is loudness 0..1 at i / rate seconds. source: "tts" when Voice is on and an engine is set up: the text is synthesised into memory (never played) and each 20 ms window's peak is taken, scaled to the loudest. Otherwise "estimate" (shape.py:estimate, deterministic): vowels 0.55 rise, 0.92 hold (longer for vowel pairs), 0.6 fall; voiced consonants l m n r w v z j 0.45, 0.5; plosives p t k b d g c q 0.0 then 0.78; sibilants and fricatives (s f h x, sh ch th ph zh) three of 0.22/0.31/0.18/0.27/0.24 in turn; 0.05 between words; 8 zeros at , ; : - ( ) and 15 at . ! ? and line breaks; digits 0.5, 0.85, 0.85, 0.5; other letters 0.5, 0.8, 0.6; trailing silence trimmed; voice.speak.rate resamples the length. Text is clean()ed first (Markdown, links, code).

Parts and setting up

Voice.wants() lists the parts the switches need (pkg:audio, pkg:kokoro|piper, model:kokoro|piper; with listening pkg:listen, model:vad, model:whisper-<size>; with your own wake word model, pkg:wake and model:wake-common). ready() is false with the download size until they're all there, so app.enable() refuses and the Features card shows Set up (Module.setup, run by POST /api/modules/setup in the background with progress). Packages go through uv (install.json's, else on PATH) or python -m pip, with --target <app>/voice/py-<X.Y> (never Kurooto's venv; Parts.importable() appends it to sys.path, after Kurooto's own). openwakeword is installed --no-deps because it declares tflite-runtime, which has no Python 3.12 wheels; its real needs are listed beside it. Models are fetched with core/egress.py:urlopen (feature "Voice") to <name>.part, renamed when complete. A ModelFile with a sha256 (0.8.10: Voice ID's two models) is hashed while it downloads; a mismatch deletes the .part and fails with "… didn't match its checksum (a damaged or changed download); nothing was kept". The other files aren't pinned yet. Parts.add_file(path) installs from a file already on the computer: a model file only when its basename matches a pinned ModelFile and its hash matches (unpinned ones: "this part has no checksum pinned yet; download it instead"), copied via .part; a .whl of a package in PACKAGES with the installer and --no-index --find-links <its folder> --target <py> (wheels beside it, numpy's or onnxruntime's, are found too). Surfaces: kurooto voice parts add FILE... (works without the daemon) and the page's Install from a file.

Hosts: github.com (Kokoro, Silero; openWakeWord's feature models only with your own wake word model; Voice ID's model), release-assets.githubusercontent.com (where GitHub's release downloads redirect; urllib follows the redirect, so it's listed since 0.8.10), huggingface.co (faster-whisper, Piper voice); uv/pip are counted as handed to them.

Voice ID (0.8.10)

Only the owner's voice can make Kurooto act; any other voice, or one it isn't sure of, can only chat. Switch voice.speaker_id.on (off by default); its behaviours each have their own: gate, carry, tell_guests, adapt (off), spoken_enrol, and voice.listen.conversation_mode. User page: ../user/voice.md.

The model

CAM++ from 3D-Speaker (Apache-2.0), ONNX by sherpa-onnx (Apache-2.0): campplus-en (VoxCeleb, 512-d, default) or campplus-multi (zh+en, 192-d), parts model:voiceid-campplus-en|multi with pinned sha256, and pkg:voiceid (kaldi-native-fbank). CPU only (CPUExecutionProvider, 2 intra-op threads; the GPU is the local AI's). The front end comes from the model's metadata (sample_rate 16000 only, normalize_samples, feature_normalize_type: global-mean, output_dim); fbank: 80 bins, dither 0, snip_edges, 25/10 ms. About 50 ms per 3 s turn on a Ryzen 7 7435HS by the spec's bench (not re-measured in this build). The model loads on first use and is dropped on Voice.stop() and when the switch goes off.

The flow change in hear()

with voice.speaker_id.on and .gate (default):
  VAD speech -> who = voiceid.check(pcm, speech_s)          # before any transcription
    not owner, no wake word, portal closed:
      Whisper on the first and last 2 s only (_edges); its name not called -> chatter, dropped, never written out
  Whisper (all of it) -> Gate.decide(heard, ctx + conversation) -> portal.take(..., who=who)
with .gate off and voice.listen.conversation_mode on (default): who = voiceid.check(pcm) for every stretch of speech
  (so another voice still starts conversation mode), then Whisper (all of it) -> Gate -> portal.take(..., who)
with both off: Whisper -> Gate -> addressed only: who = voiceid.check(pcm) -> portal.take(..., who)

Heard.speech_s is the VAD's speech time (the listener now passes per-frame flags as a 4th argument to on_speech), not seconds (which includes the pre-roll and tail). The portal counts a session per opening, keeps last_owner_at/last_owner_session for carry, publishes voice_text {who: "you", speaker} (the decision word, never the score), calls send(said, who), and prefixes the guest notice once a session per kind. owner_only_at_all: nothing published or sent; "I only answer my owner's voice." at most once a minute (each call opens a new session). ask_brain builds Incoming(speaker=who.decision, speaker_by="voice").

Conversation mode (Context.conversation): set while biometric.others_present() (a guest voice in the last voice.listen.conversation_s, or a face part's signal) or three guest voices in two minutes. Then the gate takes its name only with a question or request after it (or nothing), the wake word, the turn right after them, and a follow-up within conversation_follow_s of its answer; rules 7 and 9 (open portal, local judge) don't apply.

decide() and calibrate()

decide(score, t, speech_s, ctx): missing / locked / model failed -> unsure; voices >= 2 -> unsure; short turns (< 2.5 s of speech) use t.short; under min_speech_s: owner only by carry (same portal session, within carry_s, score >= unsure; never in strict), else unsure; score >= accept -> owner; >= unsure -> unsure; else guest.

calibrate(samples, bank, synthetic, strictness, margin, threshold): n >= 12; outliers by leave-one-out (g_i < median - 3 * 1.4826 * MAD and < 0.30, at most 10%, named by condition); centroid = normalised mean; held-out genuine scores (long, and the 1.5 s crops for short) against c_-i; impostor scores from the bank (full + 3 s for long, 1.5 s for short, plus Kokoro's 30 synthetic vectors when Kokoro is set up); per set accept = quantile(I, 1 - far) with far 1% / 0.2% / 0 (strict: max(I) + 0.03), clamped to [0.30, 0.85]; unsure = min(quantile(I, .99), accept - margin). Grade: good (FRR_long <= 5%, FRR_short <= 15%, median genuine - accept >= 0.12), fair (FRR_long <= 15%), weak (saved only with Use it anyway). threshold > 0 overrides accept for both, applied at check time from the stored calibration so it takes effect at once; strictness and margin need a Retrain. The override is held to OVERRIDE (0.30 to 0.95) and the margin to 0..MARGIN_MAX (0.2) wherever they're read (override(), band(), margin_of()): a typed 0.05 can't make a stranger "you", and a wide margin can't let any short reply carry.

The bank. tools/voiceid_bank.py over LibriSpeech dev/test (146 speakers x 4 utterances x {whole, 3 s, 1.5 s}, about 1,750 rows; optionally Common Voice's India and South Asia accents with --cv), float16, one file per model with the model's sha256 in its header. Train refuses when it's missing or for another model. Not in this commit: building it needs LibriSpeech (about 1.3 GB) and the two model files downloaded; see "Not verified".

Evaluation (tools/voiceid_eval.py)

uv run --with onnxruntime --with kaldi-native-fbank --with soundfile --with numpy python tools/voiceid_eval.py \
    --librispeech DIR --model <the .onnx> --cache <scratch dir> --groups 12 [--out results.json]

Each LibriSpeech dev/test speaker is enrolled through enrol.Session (stt off) from his largest chapter, the conditions made from the clean reading (quiet -14 dB; far -10 dB and a synthetic room, RT60 0.45 s; noise: pink at 10 dB under the speech, VAD flags from the speech before the noise, as Silero would find it; short requests 1.2-2.5 s; free 11 s), then calibrate() at each strictness and Verifier.check() for 8 held-out utterances per speaker (another chapter where there is one), whole (up to 8 s) and cut to 2, 1.5 and 0.8 s, against every stranger in its group. The impostor set is built with voiceid_bank.py's recipe from the speakers *not* being tested ("halves": dev vs test; "groups": K groups, each against a set from the other K-1, pooled over 4 groupings). Vectors are cached in the scratch folder.

The four groupings alone, stranger passes on a long turn at normal: 0.90, 0.34, 0.46, 0.52 %; a 0.8 s turn alone is never "you" (min_speech_s). campplus-multi: normal 0.51 % (relaxed 1.17, strict 0.16), you 99.2 % on long turns. Grades: 581 good, 3 fair at normal. Enrolment: every speaker finished; 116 samples rejected as too short (of 2,894 tries), 2 as identical, 2 as not sounding like the rest. 30 of the 146 have one chapter only, so their own turns come from the chapter they enrolled from (an easier test for them).

What it says: the targets in FAR (1 %, 0.2 %) are per impostor *row*, but a few look-alike voices carry most of the misses, and a set of 146 speakers can't place a cut-off at 1 in 500 of the whole population (that needs more speakers, not more rows): relaxed and normal pass strangers about 1.4 and 2.8 times as often as their targets. With fewer speakers in the set it gets worse (18, 36, 73 speakers: 2.2, 1.1, 0.58 % at normal, halves). The defaults weren't changed: the miss rate is set by how many speakers the set has, the owner-side cost of a higher cut-off can't be measured on clean audiobook speech, and strict (0.13 %) is there for a room with strangers. A bigger impostor set (more speakers, e.g. Common Voice with --cv) is the real fix. Limits: read speech, mostly clean, one microphone a chapter; simulated conditions; strangers, not relatives.

Adapter

Takes a turn only when: owner by "your voice" (not carried), score >= accept_long + 0.08, speech >= 2 s, not two voices, under adapt_per_day; FIFO up to adapt_max; centroid = normalize(0.75 * enrolled + 0.25 * mean(adapted)); drift guard cos(new, enrolled) >= 0.95, else drift_stopped; thresholds derived again after each take. Undo today / all.

The guest path

App.handle, right after message_in and before tone/pest: if msg.speaker not in ("", "owner"): guest.handle(). guest.handle: owner_only_at_all -> one line; / -> refused; a match (never run) of the enabled modules' intents, threads' and ledger's question patterns, repoproject.asked, turnon.find -> refused as action or personal; the hourly limit; else jobs.submit(prompt, audience="guest", text_only=True, quiet=True, effort="low", brain=<local id with guest_brain: local>). The runner for audience == "guest": no assembler.for_request, no selfdoc or scratch, guest_context(), tools=[], mcp=None, mcp_env={KUROOTO_JOB: id} only (so agy's own registration of kurooto mcp carries the guest's job id), a run_tool that refuses, cwd = <state>/guest (emptied each run), the one AI it was given (no fallback, no last resort, no automatic order), no watchers (nothing learns). For a guest job id, core/tools.tools_for lists nothing (kurooto mcp shows no tools; POST /api/tools/call answers 404) and run_tool refuses. When Voice is done waiting (answered, failed or given up), Jobs.forget_guest(id) stops it if it's still going and blanks its prompt, result and detail: the row stays, titled "Guest", so tuning, gists and the search index never see the guest's words. The guest's last four turns of this portal session live in VoiceID.guest_turns (memory) and go into the next turn's prompt; Chat's history never sees them.

Guarded

core/tap.py: voice.enrol (typed, button); the vid button is data.delete. /voice id … and /voice enrol refuse msg.via == "voice". The settings API is fine (the dashboard and the phone's sealed settings are authenticated).

Portability

No Linux-only call without a guard: playback tries sounddevice first everywhere, then pw-play/paplay/aplay only on Linux, afplay on macOS, winsound on Windows; capture is sounddevice (PortAudio is bundled in its Windows and macOS wheels; Linux uses the system's libportaudio) or pw-record/parec on Linux; microphone() asks sounddevice, then pactl on Linux, else "can't tell". Subprocesses it starts are its own children, stopped by their exact pid.

Not verified

Voice ID (0.8.10): the unit tests use fakes and synthetic data only (a fake embedder, fake banks, a fake microphone and transcriber). The CAM++ models, kaldi-native-fbank's features, the enrolment session, the calibration and the decision ran on real recorded speech through tools/voiceid_eval.py (above: LibriSpeech, every speaker); the impostor banks are built by tools/voiceid_bank.py from the same data. Never run: a real microphone in a real room, Silero's VAD on a live stream, a live voice, the Kokoro synthetic check, and anyone who isn't an audiobook reader.

Written against the published APIs of kokoro-onnx 0.4, piper-tts 1.2/1.3, faster-whisper 1.1, openwakeword 0.6, Silero VAD v5 and sounddevice 0.5, but run only with fakes: no real microphone, speaker or model was used in the tests or the build. Silero's v4 input names are handled too. The first real run should check kurooto voice, /health, and a spoken "Kurooto, what's the time?".
