Morning planning. What they said: $ARGUMENTS

1. **Read** today's daily note `journal/<today>`: the draft under `## Plan for today (draft…)` and
   `## From last night`. If there's no draft, build one now the way the morning-prep skill does: today's classes,
   what's due soon (deadline tools, the `## Tasks` on course pages), yesterday's daily note, and
   `about-me/how-to-help-me`.
2. **Use their answers** to the draft's questions (above). If they gave none, plan from the draft and put its
   questions at the end of your reply; they can answer by replying.
3. **Write** `## Plan for today` in the daily note (leave the draft as it is):
   - **Top 3**;
   - **Schedule**: time blocks from now to the evening, around their classes, with breaks; each block names the
     concrete task and links the note to work from;
   - **Before each class**: one line per class, linking the last lecture note;
   - **If there's time**: smaller tasks.

   If they say their energy is low, plan fewer and smaller blocks (don't write their energy level down).
4. **Apply their answers with the tools you have**: classes they're skipping today (skip_class), when they want the
   check-in call (change_checkin), reminders at fixed times (add_reminder), and the time blocks in their calendar
   (plan_block), if those tools exist.
5. **Reply** with the Top 3 first, then the schedule as a compact list, and one short encouraging line (not cheesy).
