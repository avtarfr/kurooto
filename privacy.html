What leaves your computer

Kurooto runs on your computer, and every feature is off until you turn it on. This page lists, per feature, exactly what goes where. Nothing is ever sent to Kurooto's author (no analytics, no crash reports, no "phone home"). The only things Kurooto fetches from its own release page are updates and the list of withdrawn versions, both checked against a signature.

Everything below goes to services you chose, under your own accounts.

The screenshots in the README and on the website are of a demo with made-up data ("Sam") and stand-in services; none of them shows anyone's real notes, chats or accounts. The pages' colours (look.palette) are a setting on this computer only.

Free tiers and terms

A free Gemini API key (Google AI Studio's free tier):

  • Google may use what you send and what it answers to improve its products, and human reviewers may read it.
  • You must be 18 or older.
  • In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland you need a paid key (billing turned on).
  • Phone calls and voice notes always go through Gemini, whichever AI answers your messages (a call turn by turn can use the model on your computer instead: calls.turn_brain = local).

If you don't want that, use a paid key: on paid tiers Google doesn't use what you send to improve its products. Or keep those features off, or use local models where offered.

Antigravity (agy): Kurooto runs Google's official agy program on your computer, signed in as you, and never touches its sign-in token. Google's answers differ on whether other tools may start agy unattended, so there is some risk to your Google account. For tools like Kurooto, Google recommends a Gemini API key.

Every other AI service you pick (Claude Code, a custom OpenAI-compatible one) works under its own terms and your own account. Kurooto no longer offers FreeLLMAPI, which pools other providers' free tiers; one set up with an earlier version keeps working and shows as "Another OpenAI-compatible service".

Several accounts. Each Google account you add signs in on its own and is asked only for the permissions of the features you give it (/google lists them). Its sign-in is kept in this computer's keychain like the first one's; /google forget <name> removes it from this computer.

Mail marked confidential is never read. Work or school mail may be covered by your institution's email policy (many forbid forwarding confidential messages, and institutional mail belongs to the institution). If a mail's subject, its text (all of it, footers and quoted replies included) or a Gmail label's name says "confidential", "do not forward", "restricted", "private and confidential", "privileged and confidential" or "proprietary and confidential", or carries a standard confidentiality footer ("intended solely for the addressee", "if you are not the intended recipient", "this e-mail ... is confidential / may be privileged"), Kurooto only counts it, even from a sender or label you chose: it isn't sent to the AI, summarised, or answered with an AI draft. (To spot a marking in the text, Kurooto reads it on your computer, then drops the mail in memory without keeping it.) The same goes for everything on your never-read list (/mail never). This is on by default (mail_digest.skip_confidential); tune the list in mail_digest.confidential_words.

Optional local summaries. With mail_digest.confidential set to local and Local AI models on, a model on this computer (Ollama, 127.0.0.1) summarises that mail instead. Only the summary is kept, in Kurooto's data folder (mail/confidential/<date>.md, readable only by your user account); the dashboard shows it on this computer. It isn't in your notes, on Telegram (which gets a count only), in Profile sync, or in Backups unless you turn on backups.confidential_mail. Dates found there are offered to you (titles and dates only) and never added by themselves. No AI reply drafts for it, ever.

Copies kept on your computer: downloaded Classroom files (Kurooto's data folder, classroom/); the text of the mail you chose, only until the digest is written (state folder, mail-digest/); and the sender, subject and a short excerpt of digested mail for 30 days, so a reply draft can answer it.

To withdraw a permission: your Google account → Security → Third-party access → your project.

The AI's tools

Tools add no new destination: what a tool returns to the AI (a note's text, your reminders or deadlines) goes to the AI service you picked, as the notes it reads always did. AI requests can't answer your questions or approvals: those tools exist only on calls, with you.

  • kurooto mcp (how agent programs reach the tools) talks only to Kurooto on 127.0.0.1, with a key that is good for the tools and nothing else; it changes at every start and is kept in an owner-only file in Kurooto's state folder (tools-access.json).
  • What the AI may read on this computer (Kurooto's own tools and agy alike): your notes, Documents, Downloads and Screenshots folders, and Kurooto's copies of Classroom and mail; hidden files (like .git) are left out. What it reads goes to the AI service you picked. It may write only in your notes folder, your Classroom course files and, with add-ons on, your add-ons' skills/; Documents and Downloads stay read-only. Before it changes existing text (edit_note), the previous version is kept in Kurooto's state folder (note-history/, the last 20 per file); Notes sync's history has every version too.
  • The AI may read public web pages (GET only). It never fetches this computer or your local network, so a page or a note can't steer it into your router or other programs here.
  • agy's settings file is changed only by adding (after a backup; your own rules stay): mcp(kurooto/*), read_file and write_file for the folders above, read_url(*), harmless commands (cat, find, git, grep, latexmk, ls, rg and similar; never kurooto or sed), and non-workspace file access if you hadn't set it. Kurooto asks before adding anything (Approve / Deny), except when you test agy on the setup page, which says what it allows.
  • A long agy request is written, for the length of the run, to .kurooto/requests/ inside your notes folder (a hidden folder Notes sync ignores, so it's never uploaded), then deleted.

Questions and approvals

Questions are delivered on the channels you set up (Telegram, the phone app, the desktop) and stored on this computer in the state folder (ask/<id>.json), deleted 30 days after they're answered or expire (ask.keep_days, or privacy.keep_days when you set one). The events log records when and where you answered, not what.

How long it's kept

What Kurooto keeps of your conversations on this computer stays until you say otherwise: set privacy.keep_days (e.g. 30) and it's forgotten after that many days, every night (0, the default, keeps it until you forget it): call transcripts and calls that went on as text, AI requests and their answers (the time, AI and effort stay, without the words), Chat's recent turns, answered questions, Wrong-button cases you didn't make test cases, the words of messages that came by themselves, the search index of past requests, and log lines. /forget 7d forgets the last 7 days now; /forget all forgets everything, after a confirm button. Your notes folder is never touched (a voice note's recording is saved there, so it stays), nor the event log, which holds times, counts and names but never what you typed or what was answered. Your call line holds a call's transcript only while the computer is off and hands it over when it's back. Backup zips you made before keep what they had.

Your own code and terminals

  • Your add-ons run your own Python code inside Kurooto, with its access to this computer. Only use add-ons you wrote or trust; overlay.code off keeps their skills, prompts and settings and skips the code.
  • Terminals in the dashboard are real terminals on this computer: anyone who can open your dashboard can type into them, so only turn them on if you're the only person who uses this computer. The dashboard only answers this computer, needs a key only its own page gets, refuses other websites, and on Linux checks that every request comes from your own user account. What you type into the AI program's terminal goes to that program's service, as when you run it yourself.

Secrets

Your bot token, keys, tokens and links are kept in your system's keychain (Windows Credential Manager, macOS Keychain, the Secret Service on Linux), or, where there is none, in a file only your user can read. They're never written to logs. kurooto doctor --bundle blanks them out (and names, addresses and folders) before saving anything.

  • Starting at boot (Linux, opt-in: kurooto autostart boot or Settings → Starting): your keychain is locked until you log in, so Kurooto's keys are kept in its owner-only settings file (secrets.json, readable only by your account) instead. kurooto autostart login moves them back.
  • Backups: with backups.secrets on, your keys are copied into each backup zip (secrets.json), in plain text; anyone who gets the zip can use them. Off by default.
  • Your call line and Telegram: if you approve "going offline" messages from your call line (asked once), your Telegram bot's token and your chat id go to your call line on your own Cloudflare account, beside your Gemini key. With them the line can send messages as your bot (it only sends "Kurooto has been offline since ..."). Set notify.presence.line_telegram to false and they're deleted there at the next sync.
  • Profile sync: your keys also go to your repository, encrypted with your passphrase (AES-GCM, with a key from scrypt). The passphrase is never stored; this computer keeps the profile's data key in its keychain, like your other keys, so it can save on its own. Never in the profile: this computer's dashboard key, and the reports you added in About you.

Your phone

The Android app keeps its pairing (the call line's address, its device key, the ring messages' key) in its private storage. Nothing personal is built into the app. The iPhone web app keeps its device key in Safari's storage for the Home Screen app. Unpair a phone with /phones → /unpair, or on the dashboard's Phone tab. A lost phone holds that pairing and the ring messages' key: kurooto rotate gives your call line new keys (they go to your own Cloudflare account, as on every setup), and the line then forgets every phone paired before, so the lost one can't call, be called or read new messages.

Answers from the Android app are sealed with the same key as the ring messages. That key keeps them from ntfy, not from your call line: the line holds it too (it seals the ring messages and gives it to each Android phone when it pairs), so your call line, on your own Cloudflare account, could read them. Only what Reminders with the lid shut hands over is sealed with keys that only your phones hold. The iPhone app's answers travel over HTTPS with its device key. Each answer can be used once (it carries a random number and the time it was sent).

Removing it all

kurooto uninstall removes Kurooto's settings and data from your computer (your notes folder is kept). Your call line, Google client, notes repository and the kurooto-profile branch are on your own accounts, and backup zips are in the folder you picked: delete them there (see the manual's Uninstall).

kurooto uninstall --revoke first takes back Kurooto's access, which means asking each service: Cloudflare to delete your call line (with its keys and stored data, the Telegram token and chat id it may hold included) and then to sign Kurooto out; Google's revoke endpoint, sent each sign-in's token, which is how a token is revoked; Telegram to drop your bot's webhook, waiting messages and command menu. Nothing else is sent. It then removes Kurooto's keys from your system's keychain. The GitHub token, the Gemini key, the bot itself and the Google client are yours to delete; it prints where.
